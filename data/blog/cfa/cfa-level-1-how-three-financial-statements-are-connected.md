## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, articulation across income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement via working capital accruals, CapEx/depreciation, and retained earnings links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Financial Statement Analysis. Indirect Method Operating Cash Flow Reconciliation (CFO = Net Income + Non-Cash Charges (D&A) − ΔWorking Capital Assets + ΔWorking Capital Liabilities) establishes the primary relationship (Converts accrual net income into cash flow from operations by reversing non-cash items and balance sheet working capital changes.), while PP&E Roll-Forward & Cash Paid for Suppliers (Ending Net PP&E = Beginning Net PP&E + CapEx − Depreciation − Net Book Value of Disposals) governs Connects investing cash outflows (CapEx) and income statement depreciation to balance sheet net fixed assets.

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Indirect Method Operating Cash Flow Reconciliation

```formula
CFO = Net Income + Non-Cash Charges (D&A) − ΔWorking Capital Assets + ΔWorking Capital Liabilities
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Converts accrual net income into cash flow from operations by reversing non-cash items and balance sheet working capital changes.

### Formula 2: PP&E Roll-Forward & Cash Paid for Suppliers

```formula
Ending Net PP&E = Beginning Net PP&E + CapEx − Depreciation − Net Book Value of Disposals
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Connects investing cash outflows (CapEx) and income statement depreciation to balance sheet net fixed assets.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Deriving Cash Collections from Customers and Cash Paid to Suppliers

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Revenue = $500M, COGS = $300M. Balance sheet changes during the year: Accounts Receivable increased by $25M, Inventory increased by $18M, and Accounts Payable increased by $12M.

```formula
CFO = Net Income + Non-Cash Charges (D&A) − ΔWorking Capital Assets + ΔWorking Capital Liabilities
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Cash Collected from Customers = Revenue − ΔAR = $500M − $25M = $475M. Inventory Purchases = COGS + ΔInventory = $300M + $18M = $318M. Cash Paid to Suppliers = Purchases − ΔAP = $318M − $12M = $306M (negative sign −$306M on direct CFO).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> An increase in an operating asset (AR or Inventory) consumes cash, while an increase in an operating liability (AP) conserves cash.

### Worked Example 2: Equipment Sale Gain and Investing Cash Inflow Reconciliation

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Beginning Gross PP&E = $400M, Ending Gross PP&E = $460M, CapEx = $90M. Accumulated Depreciation rose from $150M to $175M, with Annual Depreciation Expense = $40M. The disposed equipment was sold for a $6M gain on the income statement. Find Cash Proceeds from Equipment Sale (CFI).

```formula
Ending Net PP&E = Beginning Net PP&E + CapEx − Depreciation − Net Book Value of Disposals
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Historical Cost of Sold Equipment = Beg Gross PP&E + CapEx − End Gross PP&E = $400M + $90M − $460M = $30M. Step 2: Acc. Depr. of Sold Equipment = Beg Acc. Depr. + Depr. Expense − End Acc. Depr. = $150M + $40M − $175M = $15M. Step 3: Net Book Value (NBV) sold = $30M − $15M = $15M. Step 4: Cash Sale Proceeds = NBV + Gain = $15M + $6M = $21M.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> On the cash flow statement, the $6M gain is subtracted in CFO, while the full $21M cash proceeds are reported as an investing inflow in CFI.

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Balance Sheet Account Change | Direction of Change | Effect on Operating Cash Flow (CFO) | Economic Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accounts Receivable (AR) | Increase (+ΔAR) | Subtract from Net Income (−) | Credit sales exceeded cash collections |
| Inventory | Increase (+ΔInv) | Subtract from Net Income (−) | Cash tied up in unsold goods |
| Accounts Payable (AP) | Increase (+ΔAP) | Add to Net Income (+) | Supplier credit financed purchases |
| Unearned Revenue | Increase (+ΔUnearned) | Add to Net Income (+) | Cash collected ahead of revenue recognition |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A company reports Net Income of $120 million, Depreciation Expense of $30 million, a $10 million gain on sale of land, a $14 million increase in Accounts Receivable, and an $8 million increase in Accounts Payable. Operating cash flow (CFO) is closest to:

- **[A]** $134 million

- **[B]** $144 million

- **[C]** $154 million

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** CFO = Net Income ($120M) + Depreciation ($30M) − Gain on Sale ($10M) − ΔAR ($14M) + ΔAP ($8M) = $134 million.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** If a company's Gross Wages Expense on the income statement is $85 million and its Accrued Wages Payable on the balance sheet decreased by $5 million during the year, cash paid to employees was:

- **[A]** $80 million

- **[B]** $85 million

- **[C]** $90 million

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option C.**
>
> **Explanation:** A decrease in a liability (Wages Payable) requires an additional cash outflow: Cash Paid = Expense ($85M) + Decrease in Payable ($5M) = $90 million.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** When equipment with a net book value of $40 million is sold for $48 million cash, how is the transaction reflected across the cash flow statement?

- **[A]** $48M inflow in CFI and $8M subtracted from Net Income in indirect CFO

- **[B]** $40M inflow in CFI and $8M inflow in CFO

- **[C]** $48M inflow in CFO

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Full cash proceeds ($48M) appear in CFI, while the $8M non-operating gain is deducted from Net Income in indirect CFO to prevent double counting.
