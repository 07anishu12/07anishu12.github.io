### Formula 1: Commodity Futures Cost-of-Carry & Convenience Yield

```formula
F_0(T) = S_0 × (1 + R_F)^T + PV(Storage Costs) − PV(Convenience Yield)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- Convenience yield is the non-monetary benefit of holding physical inventory during tight supply shortages.

- when Convenience Yield > R_F + Storage, F_0(T) < S_0 (Backwardation).

### Formula 2: Three Components of Commodity Futures Total Return & Roll Yield

```formula
Total Return = Price (Spot) Return + Roll Yield + Collateral Yield

Roll Yield ≈ (S_t − F_t) / F_t
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- In Backwardation (Spot > Futures), rolling expiring long contracts into cheaper deferred contracts generates POSITIVE Roll Yield.

- in Contango (Futures > Spot), Roll Yield is NEGATIVE.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Calculating Futures Roll Yield and Total Return in Backwardation vs. Contango

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
An institutional investor holds a fully collateralized $10,000,000 long crude oil futures position (100% backed by T-bills earning 4.80% annually, or 0.40% per month). At the start of the month, the front-month futures price is $80.00/bbl and the 2-month futures price is $78.40/bbl (Backwardation: Spot/Front > Deferred). Over the month, spot oil prices remain completely flat at $80.00/bbl, so the 2-month contract rolls up to $80.00 at expiration.

```formula
F_0(T) = S_0 × (1 + R_F)^T + PV(Storage Costs) − PV(Convenience Yield)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Spot (Price) Return = ($80.00 − $80.00) / $80.00 = 0.00%. Step 2: Roll Yield (buying the 2-month contract at $78.40 as it converges up to $80.00 spot) = ($80.00 − $78.40) / $78.40 = +2.04% (or ($80 − $78.40)/$80 = +2.00%). Step 3: Collateral Yield on T-Bills = +0.40% for the month. Step 4: Total Monthly Return = 0.00% + 2.04% + 0.40% = +2.44%!

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Even when spot oil prices go nowhere (0.00% spot return), a backwardated curve generates +2.04% positive monthly roll yield plus +0.40% T-bill collateral yield.

### Worked Example 2: Comparing Timberland, Farmland, and Physical Commodities

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Compare the biological growth and cash flow characteristics of Timberland vs. Farmland vs. Commodity Futures.

```formula
Total Return = Price (Spot) Return + Roll Yield + Collateral Yield

Roll Yield ≈ (S_t − F_t) / F_t
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Timberland acts as a 'living warehouse' with biological tree growth (~4%–6% physical volume growth per year) and harvest timing flexibility: when lumber prices fall, the owner simply leaves trees standing on the stump to grow larger! Farmland lacks harvest flexibility (crops must be harvested when ripe) but provides annual cash crop/lease income and inflation protection. Physical commodities pay zero dividends/income and incur storage and insurance costs.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Timberland's unique ability to delay harvest during price downturns (biological storage on the stump) provides embedded option value not available in annual row-crop farmland.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** When a commodity futures market is in 'backwardation' (downward-sloping futures curve where Spot Price > Futures Price), which condition must hold?

- **[A]** Storage costs and financing costs are negative

- **[B]** The non-monetary convenience yield of holding physical inventory exceeds the sum of the risk-free interest rate and storage costs

- **[C]** Roll yield for a long futures investor is negative

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Since F_0(T) = S_0 + Financing + Storage − Convenience Yield, F_0(T) < S_0 (backwardation) occurs if and only if Convenience Yield > Financing + Storage Costs.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** A fully collateralized long commodity futures index investor earns a total return composed of which three distinct sources?

- **[A]** Dividend yield, P/E expansion, and leverage

- **[B]** Spot (price) return, roll yield (from rolling expiring contracts into the next maturity), and collateral yield (interest earned on the T-bill margin collateral)

- **[C]** Cap rate, NOI growth, and depreciation tax shield

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Because commodities pay no cash dividends, a fully collateralized futures position earns Spot Return + Roll Yield + Risk-Free Collateral Return.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** What key operational advantage distinguishes Timberland investments from annual row-crop Farmland investments?

- **[A]** Timberland requires no land ownership

- **[B]** Timberland offers harvest timing flexibility ('storage on the stump')—allowing owners to delay harvesting when lumber prices are depressed while trees continue growing biologically in volume and grade

- **[C]** Farmland can defer harvesting corn or wheat for five years

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Unlike perishable agricultural crops that must be harvested on schedule, timber can be left standing on the stump during recessions, growing biologically into higher-value sawtimber.
