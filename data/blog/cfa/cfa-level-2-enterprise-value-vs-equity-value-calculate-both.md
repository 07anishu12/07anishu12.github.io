## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, calculating Enterprise Value (Market Cap + Preferred Stock + Market Value of Debt + Non-Controlling Interest − Cash & Short-Term Investments) and aligning EV multiples vs Equity Price multiples links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Equity Investments. Complete Institutional Enterprise Value (EV) Equation (EV = Market Value of Common Equity + Market Value of Preferred Stock + Market Value of Total Debt (incl Leases) + Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) − Cash & Marketable Securities) establishes the primary relationship (Represents the total net takeover price an acquirer would pay for 100% of the firm's core operating business.), while Numerator-Denominator Matching Principle for Multiples (EV Numerator => Pre-Interest Operating Denominator (Sales, EBITDA, EBIT, FCFF, Total Invested Capital) ; Equity Price (P) Numerator => Post-Interest Equity Denominator (EPS, FCFE, Book Equity)) governs Never mix an Enterprise Value numerator with an post-interest equity denominator (e.g., EV / Net Income is invalid), and never mix Stock Price with EBITDA (P / EBITDA is invalid).

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Complete Institutional Enterprise Value (EV) Equation

```formula
EV = Market Value of Common Equity + Market Value of Preferred Stock + Market Value of Total Debt (incl Leases) + Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) − Cash & Marketable Securities
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Represents the total net takeover price an acquirer would pay for 100% of the firm's core operating business.

### Formula 2: Numerator-Denominator Matching Principle for Multiples

```formula
EV Numerator => Pre-Interest Operating Denominator (Sales, EBITDA, EBIT, FCFF, Total Invested Capital)

Equity Price (P) Numerator => Post-Interest Equity Denominator (EPS, FCFE, Book Equity)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Never mix an Enterprise Value numerator with an post-interest equity denominator (e.g., EV / Net Income is invalid), and never mix Stock Price with EBITDA (P / EBITDA is invalid).

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Calculating Complete Enterprise Value Including Non-Controlling Interest and Lease Debt

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A consolidated parent has 100M common shares trading at $42.00. Its balance sheet shows: Short-Term Debt = $150M, Long-Term Bonds (Book = $600M, Market Value = $580M), Capitalized Lease Liabilities = $120M, Preferred Stock (Market Value) = $80M, Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) = $70M, Accounts Payable = $200M, and Cash & Marketable Securities = $300M. Consolidated EBITDA = $600M.

```formula
EV = Market Value of Common Equity + Market Value of Preferred Stock + Market Value of Total Debt (incl Leases) + Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) − Cash & Marketable Securities
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Market Cap of Common Equity = 100M × $42.00 = $4,200M. Step 2: Total Interest-Bearing Debt at Market Value = $150M + $580M + $120M (Leases) = $850M (exclude $200M non-interest Accounts Payable!). Step 3: Add Preferred Stock ($80M) and Non-Controlling Interest ($70M), and subtract Cash ($300M): EV = $4,200M + $850M + $80M + $70M − $300M = $4,900M. Step 4: EV / EBITDA = $4,900M / $600M = 8.17x.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Why is Non-Controlling Interest ($70M) added to Enterprise Value? Because Consolidated EBITDA ($600M) includes 100% of the partially owned subsidiary's EBITDA, so the EV numerator must also include 100% of the subsidiary's equity value (including the 20% NCI)!

### Worked Example 2: Why Cash & Marketable Securities Are Subtracted in Enterprise Value

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Suppose Acquirer Corp buys 100% of the company above (Common + Debt + Preferred + NCI = $5,200M Total Firm Value). Immediately upon closing, Acquirer Corp takes the target's $300M Cash and uses it to pay down $300M of the acquisition bridge loan.

```formula
EV Numerator => Pre-Interest Operating Denominator (Sales, EBITDA, EBIT, FCFF, Total Invested Capital)

Equity Price (P) Numerator => Post-Interest Equity Denominator (EPS, FCFE, Book Equity)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Net acquisition cost for the operating business = $5,200M − $300M Cash = $4,900M (Enterprise Value). Moreover, because Cash earns interest income (which is below EBITDA), subtracting Cash from EV ensures both numerator and denominator reflect pure operating assets.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Subtracting cash & marketable securities isolates the net price paid for core operating assets.

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Balance Sheet / Claim Item | Treatment in Enterprise Value (EV) | Reason for Inclusion / Exclusion | Valuation Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Common Equity (Shares × P_0) | Add (+) | Primary equity claim on operations | Market Capitalization |
| Interest-Bearing Debt & Leases | Add (+) | Creditor claim on EBITDA/FCFF | Market Value (Book if unavailable) |
| Preferred Stock & NCI | Add (+) | Senior/Minority claims on 100% EBITDA | Market Value / Fair Value |
| Accounts Payable & Deferred Tax | Exclude (0) | Already netted in operating working capital | Part of operating net assets |
| Cash & Marketable Securities | Subtract (−) | Reduces net takeover cost; non-operating | Carry/Fair Value |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** Why must Non-Controlling Interest (minority interest) be ADDED when calculating the Enterprise Value (EV) of a parent company that consolidates an 80%-owned subsidiary?

- **[A]** Because Non-Controlling Interest is short-term debt

- **[B]** To maintain numerator-denominator consistency, because Consolidated EBITDA and Revenue in the denominator include 100% of the subsidiary's operations

- **[C]** Because NCI reduces taxes

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Since consolidated EBITDA includes 100% of the subsidiary's earnings, EV in the numerator must include 100% of the subsidiary's capital claims—both the parent's 80% and the NCI's 20%.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Which of the following valuation multiples violates the fundamental rule of numerator-denominator consistency?

- **[A]** EV / EBITA

- **[B]** Price / Free Cash Flow to the Firm (P / FCFF)

- **[C]** Price / Book Value of Common Equity (P / B)

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Price (Market Cap) belongs only to common shareholders (post-debt), whereas FCFF belongs to both debt and equity holders (pre-debt). The consistent multiples are EV / FCFF and P / FCFE.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** When comparing two companies in the same industry that have identical operating profitability and identical capital structures (D/E), but use vastly different accounting depreciation methods (Straight-Line vs. Double-Declining Balance), which valuation multiple is LEAST distorted?

- **[A]** Trailing P/E

- **[B]** EV / EBIT

- **[C]** EV / EBITDA

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option C.**
>
> **Explanation:** Because EBITDA is calculated before deducting Depreciation and Amortization, EV/EBITDA is unaffected by differences in accounting depreciation schedules across peers.
