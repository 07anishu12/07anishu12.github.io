## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Post-Merger Combined Firm Value & Acquirer / Target Gains (V_AT = V_A + V_T + Synergies (S) − Cash Paid (C) ; Gain_Target = Takeover Premium = P_T − V_T ; Gain_Acquirer = Synergies (S) − Premium = S − (P_T − V_T)) and Post-Merger Share Price in a Stock-for-Stock Merger (P_AT = (V_A + V_T + Synergies) / (Shares_Acquirer + New_Shares_Issued_to_Target)) govern calculating Post-Merger Combined Value (V_AT = V_A + V_T + S − C), Target and Acquirer Gain splitting in Cash vs Stock deals, Exchange Ratio pricing, and Divestitures vs Spin-Offs vs Equity Carve-Outs.

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Post-Merger Combined Firm Value & Acquirer / Target Gains

```formula
V_AT = V_A + V_T + Synergies (S) − Cash Paid (C)

Gain_Target = Takeover Premium = P_T − V_T

Gain_Acquirer = Synergies (S) − Premium = S − (P_T − V_T)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** In a Cash offer, P_T = Cash Paid (so Acquirer keeps 100% of synergy risk/upside). In a Stock offer, P_T = (N_New_Shares / Total_Post_Merger_Shares) × V_AT (sharing synergy risk with target shareholders!).

### Formula 2: Post-Merger Share Price in a Stock-for-Stock Merger

```formula
P_AT = (V_A + V_T + Synergies) / (Shares_Acquirer + New_Shares_Issued_to_Target)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Notice that the true value paid to Target shareholders in a stock deal must be evaluated using the POST-MERGER share price P_AT, not the pre-merger acquirer share price!.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Comparing Acquirer and Target Gains in a Cash Offer vs. Stock-for-Stock Offer

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Acquirer A has 100M shares trading at $50.00 (V_A = $5,000M). Target T has 40M shares trading at $25.00 (V_T = $1,000M). Merging the firms will create PV of Synergies S = $300M. Compare the Takeover Premium (Target Gain) and Acquirer Gain under: (Option 1) A Cash Offer of $30.00 per Target share ($1,200M cash); vs. (Option 2) A Stock Offer of 0.60 Acquirer shares per Target share (24M new shares issued).

```formula
V_AT = V_A + V_T + Synergies (S) − Cash Paid (C)

Gain_Target = Takeover Premium = P_T − V_T

Gain_Acquirer = Synergies (S) − Premium = S − (P_T − V_T)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. Cash Offer ($1,200M): Target Gain (Premium) = $1,200M − $1,000M = +$200M; Acquirer Gain = Synergies ($300M) − Premium ($200M) = +$100M (Post-merger A share price rises to $51.00).**

- **Step 2. Stock Offer (24M new A shares issued; Total Post-Merger Shares = 100M + 24M = 124M): Combined Firm Value V_AT = $5,000M + $1,000M + $300M = $6,300M. Post-Merger Share Price P_AT = $6,300M / 124M = $50.8065 per share. True Value Paid to Target (P_T) = 24M shares × $50.8065 = $1,219.35M. Target Gain = $1,219.35M − $1,000M = +$219.35M; Acquirer Gain = $300M − $219.35M = +$80.65M ($0.8065 × 100M shares).**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> In the stock offer, Target shareholders own 24/124 = 19.35% of the combined company, so they capture 19.35% of the $100M net gain above the nominal $1,200M pre-announcement stock value (+$19.35M extra)!

### Worked Example 2: Comparing Divestitures (Direct Sale), Spin-Offs, and Equity Carve-Outs

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Conglomerate C trades at a 15% conglomerate discount because its high-growth MedTech division is buried inside a mature industrial parent. Contrast the cash inflow, tax impact, and control impact of a Direct Sale (Divestiture), a Spin-Off, and an Equity Carve-Out.

```formula
P_AT = (V_A + V_T + Synergies) / (Shares_Acquirer + New_Shares_Issued_to_Target)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

• Direct Sale (Divestiture to third-party buyer): Generates immediate 100% cash proceeds to Parent C; triggers capital gains taxes; transfers 100% control to buyer. • Spin-Off (pro-rata distribution of division shares to existing Parent C shareholders): Generates $0 cash inflow to Parent C; typically tax-free to shareholders; creates an independent public company. • Equity Carve-Out (IPO of a minority 20% stake of the division to new public investors): Generates cash inflow to Parent C/Sub while Parent C retains 80% control.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Spin-offs generate ZERO cash inflow for the parent company; if a parent needs cash to pay down debt, it must use a Direct Sale (Divestiture) or an Equity Carve-Out.

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Restructuring Action | Does Parent Receive Cash? | Who Owns New Entity? | Primary Strategic Motivation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct Sale (Divestiture) | Yes (100% cash from buyer) | Acquiring third-party firm | Raise cash to pay debt; strategic exit |
| Spin-Off | No ($0 cash to parent) | Existing parent shareholders pro-rata | Eliminate conglomerate discount tax-free |
| Equity Carve-Out | Yes (IPO proceeds for ~20% stake) | Parent (80%) + New IPO Buyers (20%) | Raise cash + establish public valuation |
| Cost / Balance Sheet Restructuring | No (internal cost cutting / leaseback) | Existing shareholders | Restore ROIC > WACC during downturn |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** Acquirer Inc. (pre-merger value $2,000 million, 50 million shares @ $40) offers 0.50 of its shares for each of Target Co.'s 20 million shares (issuing 10 million new shares) when Target Co. is worth $350 million standalone. If the merger generates $70 million in present value of synergies, the true post-merger share price of the combined firm is:

- **[A]** $40.00

- **[B]** $40.33

- **[C]** $41.40

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Combined post-merger value V_AT = $2,000M + $350M + $70M = $2,420 million. Total post-merger shares = 50M + (0.50 × 20M) = 60 million shares. Post-merger share price P_AT = $2,420M / 60M = $40.33 per share.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** In an M&A transaction, how does paying with Stock vs. paying with Cash affect the allocation of synergy risk and confidence signals?

- **[A]** In a Cash offer, the Acquirer's shareholders bear 100% of the risk (and keep 100% of the upside) of whether projected synergies materialize, and a Cash offer signals high management confidence that the Acquirer's own stock is not overvalued; in a Stock offer, synergy risk is shared proportionally with Target shareholders

- **[B]** In a Cash offer, Target shareholders bear all synergy risk

- **[C]** Stock offers always signal that the Acquirer's stock is undervalued

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** In a cash deal, Target shareholders exit with a fixed cash sum, leaving Acquirer shareholders with 100% of synergy uncertainty; in a stock deal, Acquirer managers often use stock when they believe their own shares are generously valued.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** A conglomerate wishes to separate a non-core division into an independent publicly traded company while raising immediate cash proceeds to pay down parent-level debt. Which restructuring transaction accomplishes this goal?

- **[A]** A pro-rata Spin-Off to existing shareholders

- **[B]** An Equity Carve-Out (selling a stake in the subsidiary to the public via an IPO) or a Direct Sale (Divestiture)

- **[C]** A reverse stock split

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** A Spin-Off merely distributes shares to existing shareholders and raises zero cash; an Equity Carve-Out (or Direct Sale) brings in external cash proceeds.
