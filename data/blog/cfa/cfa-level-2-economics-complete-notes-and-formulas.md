## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Cobb-Douglas Production Function & Solow Steady-State Equilibrium

```formula
Y = A × K^α × L^(1−α)

Output per Worker y = A × k^α

Steady-State Growth Rate of Output per Worker (g_y*) = θ / (1 − α)

Total GDP Growth = [θ / (1 − α)] + n
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- θ = Total Factor Productivity (TFP) growth.

- α = capital share of income.

- n = labor force growth.

- s = saving rate.

- δ = depreciation rate.

- in steady state, Δk/k = g_y* = θ / (1 − α).

### Formula 2: Solow Steady-State Capital-to-Output Ratio (K / Y)

```formula
Steady-State (K / Y) = s / [θ / (1 − α) + δ + n]
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Notice that raising the national saving rate (s) permanently raises the steady-state level of capital per worker (k*) and output per worker (y*), but does NOT permanently change the steady-state growth rate g_y* = θ/(1−α)!.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Calculating Solow Steady-State Growth Rates of Per-Capita GDP and Total GDP

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
An economy is described by a Cobb-Douglas production function where capital's share of income is α = 0.36 (so labor's share is 1 − α = 0.64). Annual Total Factor Productivity growth is θ = 1.60%, labor force growth is n = 1.20%, the physical depreciation rate is δ = 4.30%, and the national saving rate is s = 24.0%.

```formula
Y = A × K^α × L^(1−α)

Output per Worker y = A × k^α

Steady-State Growth Rate of Output per Worker (g_y*) = θ / (1 − α)

Total GDP Growth = [θ / (1 − α)] + n
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1 (Steady-State Growth Rate of Output per Worker g_y*): g_y* = θ / (1 − α) = 1.60% / (1 − 0.36) = 1.60% / 0.64 = 2.50% per year. Step 2 (Steady-State Growth Rate of Total Real GDP g_Y*): g_Y* = g_y* + n = 2.50% + 1.20% = 3.70% per year. Step 3 (Steady-State Capital-to-Output Ratio K/Y): K/Y = s / (g_y* + δ + n) = 0.24 / (0.025 + 0.043 + 0.012) = 0.24 / 0.080 = 3.00x.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> In the neo-classical Solow model, once the economy reaches steady-state equilibrium, capital per worker (k) and output per worker (y) grow at the exact same rate θ / (1 − α) = 2.50%.

### Worked Example 2: Comparing Neoclassical (Solow) Growth vs. Endogenous Growth Theory (Romer)

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Suppose the government permanently increases the national saving/R&D investment rate (s) from 24% to 30%. Contrast the long-run growth rate impact in the Neoclassical Solow Model vs. Endogenous Growth Theory.

```formula
Steady-State (K / Y) = s / [θ / (1 − α) + δ + n]
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. Neoclassical Solow Model (diminishing marginal returns to capital, α < 1): Raising s temporarily accelerates growth during the transition to a higher steady-state level k*, but the long-run steady-state growth rate returns to θ / (1 − α) = 2.50% (unchanged!).**

- **Step 2. Endogenous Growth Theory (Romer — constant returns to broad capital K including R&D knowledge externalities, y = c × k): Because R&D spills over across the economy without diminishing returns, raising the saving/investment rate s PERMANENTLY increases the long-run growth rate of output per worker!**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> This contrast—temporary transition speed-up in Solow vs. permanent growth rate increase in Endogenous Growth Theory when saving rate s rises—is heavily tested at Level II.

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Growth Theory / Regulation Theory | Core Assumption | Effect of Higher Saving Rate (s) | Convergence Prediction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classical (Malthusian) | Fixed land + population explosion | Temporary gain eaten by population | Subsistence wage trap |
| Neoclassical (Solow-Swan) | Diminishing returns to K (α < 1); exogenous TFP | Raises level y*, NOT long-run growth rate | Conditional convergence (same s, n, δ) |
| Endogenous Growth (Romer) | Constant returns to K via R&D spillovers | Permanently raises long-run growth rate! | No automatic convergence (rich can pull away) |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** In the neoclassical Solow growth model, if Total Factor Productivity (TFP) grows at θ = 1.4% per year, the labor force grows at n = 0.8% per year, and capital's share of national income is α = 0.30, the steady-state growth rate of output per worker (y) and total output (Y), respectively, are:

- **[A]** Output per worker = 2.0%; Total output = 2.8%

- **[B]** Output per worker = 1.4%; Total output = 2.2%

- **[C]** Output per worker = 4.67%; Total output = 5.47%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Steady-state growth of output per worker = θ / (1 − α) = 1.4% / (1 − 0.30) = 1.4% / 0.70 = 2.00%. Steady-state growth of total output = 2.00% + n = 2.00% + 0.80% = 2.80%.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** In the neoclassical Solow model, a permanent increase in an economy's saving rate (s) will:

- **[A]** Permanently increase the steady-state growth rate of output per worker

- **[B]** Permanently increase the steady-state LEVEL of capital per worker (k*) and output per worker (y*), and temporarily increase the growth rate during the transition, but leave the long-run steady-state growth rate unchanged

- **[C]** Decrease the capital-to-output ratio

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Because steady-state growth θ/(1−α) does not depend on s, a higher saving rate shifts the economy to a higher steady-state level path without changing the long-run slope.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** In the economics of regulation, George Stigler's 'Regulatory Capture Theory' states that:

- **[A]** Government regulators always act purely in the public interest to correct market failures

- **[B]** Over time, regulatory agencies often come to be dominated or influenced by the very industry incumbents they were created to regulate, using regulations to erect barriers to entry against new competitors

- **[C]** Private bargaining eliminates externalities without government intervention

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Regulatory capture occurs when incumbent firms use their concentrated lobbying power and expertise to shape regulatory rules in their own favor (whereas private bargaining without transaction costs is the Coase Theorem).
