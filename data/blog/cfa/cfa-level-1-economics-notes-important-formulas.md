## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Calculating N-Firm Concentration Ratio and Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI)

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
An industry has 5 competitors with market shares of 35%, 25%, 20%, 10%, and 10%. Suppose the two smallest firms (10% and 10%) merge into a single 20% firm.

```formula
MR = MC

MR = P × [1 − (1 / |E_d|)]

HHI = ∑ (Market Share_i)^2
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Pre-Merger: 3-Firm Concentration Ratio (CR_3) = 35% + 25% + 20% = 80.0%. Pre-Merger HHI = 35^2 + 25^2 + 20^2 + 10^2 + 10^2 = 1,225 + 625 + 400 + 100 + 100 = 2,450 (or 0.2450). Post-Merger: CR_3 remains 35% + 25% + 20% = 80.0% (insensitive to #4 and #5 merging!), whereas Post-Merger HHI = 35^2 + 25^2 + 20^2 + 20^2 = 1,225 + 625 + 400 + 400 = 2,650 (+200 point jump!).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Unlike simple N-firm concentration ratios, the HHI detects mergers outside the top N firms and weights large market shares quadratically.

### Worked Example 2: Monopolist Optimal Pricing Using Price Elasticity of Demand

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A pharmaceutical patent holder faces a price elasticity of demand of |E_d| = 2.50 and has a constant marginal production cost of MC = $30.00 per unit.

```formula
GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)

Fiscal Multiplier = 1 / [1 − MPC × (1 − t)]
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Profit maximization requires MR = MC => P × [1 − (1 / 2.50)] = $30.00 => P × (1 − 0.40) = $30.00 => 0.60 × P = $30.00 => Optimal Price P = $50.00 per unit (a 40% Lerner Index markup (P − MC)/P = 1/2.5 = 40%).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> As demand becomes less elastic (smaller |E_d| > 1), the monopolist's optimal markup over marginal cost increases.

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Market Structure | Number of Sellers & Barriers | Pricing Power & Demand Curve | Long-Run Economic Profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perfect Competition | Many firms; zero barriers | Price taker (P = MR = MC; horizontal) | Zero (P = minimum ATC) |
| Monopolistic Competition | Many firms; low barriers; differentiated | Some pricing power (downward D) | Zero (P = ATC above min ATC) |
| Oligopoly | Few dominant firms; high barriers | Strategic interdependence (Cournot/Nash) | Positive possible (especially collusion) |
| Pure Monopoly | Single seller; legal/natural barriers | Price searcher (MR = MC < P) | Positive sustainable economic profit |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** In which market structure do firms face a downward-sloping demand curve, differentiate their products through branding, and earn zero economic profit in long-run equilibrium due to low barriers to entry?

- **[A]** Perfect competition

- **[B]** Monopolistic competition

- **[C]** Oligopoly

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Monopolistic competition combines downward-sloping differentiated product demand (P > MC) with free entry and exit, driving long-run economic profit to zero (P = ATC).

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** In the short run, a perfectly competitive firm will continue operating at an economic loss rather than shutting down production immediately as long as market price (P) is:

- **[A]** Greater than or equal to Average Variable Cost (P ≥ AVC)

- **[B]** Greater than Average Total Cost (P > ATC)

- **[C]** Greater than Average Fixed Cost (P > AFC)

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** When AVC ≤ P < ATC, the firm covers all variable costs and contributes toward fixed costs; if P falls below minimum AVC, the firm shuts down in the short run.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** An industry consists of four firms with market shares of 40%, 30%, 20%, and 10%. Its Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) on a 10,000-point scale is:

- **[A]** 2,500

- **[B]** 3,000

- **[C]** 9,000

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** HHI = 40^2 + 30^2 + 20^2 + 10^2 = 1,600 + 900 + 400 + 100 = 3,000.
