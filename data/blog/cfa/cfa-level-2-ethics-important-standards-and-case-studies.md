### Formula 1: Level II Research-Investment Banking Firewall Rules (Standard I(B) & VI(A))

```formula
1. Research Analysts Must Never Report to Investment Banking (IB)

2. Analyst Compensation Must Not Be Tied to Specific IB Deals (Flat firm-wide profitability bonus OK)

3. Quiet Periods & Restricted Lists Enforce Independence
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** In Level II vignettes, IB may review a draft research report ONLY to verify factual accuracy prior to publication—never to review or approve the rating or price target!.

### Formula 2: Social Media & Departing Employee Rules (Standard IV(A) & V(B))

```formula

```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** If a firm provides a corporate social media account to communicate with clients, the employee must leave that account behind when departing.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Level II Multi-Issue Vignette Analysis: Sell-Side Research & Investment Banking Pressures

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
In a Level II vignette, Equity Analyst Viktor covers CloudCorp. CloudCorp hires Viktor's bank to lead a secondary equity offering. (1) The IB Managing Director emails Viktor a draft section of the report to check whether CloudCorp's patent count is 42 or 45. (2) The IB Managing Director promises Viktor a $50,000 deal bonus if the secondary offering closes oversubscribed. (3) Viktor shares his full research model—including his 'Strong Buy' rating and $90 target price—with CloudCorp's CEO for approval before publishing.

```formula
1. Research Analysts Must Never Report to Investment Banking (IB)

2. Analyst Compensation Must Not Be Tied to Specific IB Deals (Flat firm-wide profitability bonus OK)

3. Quiet Periods & Restricted Lists Enforce Independence
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. Checking factual accuracy (patent count 42 vs 45) through compliance channels: PERMITTED.**

- **Step 2. Tying a research analyst's bonus directly to a specific IB deal ($50k bonus): VIOLATION of Standard I(B) Independence and Objectivity (and VI(A)).**

- **Step 3. Sharing the rating and target price with the subject company prior to publication: VIOLATION of Standard I(B)! Subject companies may only review the strictly factual portions of a draft report (with the recommendation and price target removed) to verify factual accuracy.**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Never allow the subject company or the Investment Banking department to see or approve the recommendation/price target section of a draft report prior to public dissemination!

### Worked Example 2: Level II Multi-Issue Vignette Analysis: Quantitative Model Errors & Client Correction

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Portfolio Manager Lin discovers a coding bug in her firm's quantitative rebalancing algorithm that inadvertently over-allocated 5% to high-beta stocks in 12 client accounts over the past quarter, resulting in a +2.1% gain for those clients during a market rally. Lin silently fixes the code for future quarters without informing the clients or her supervisor.

```formula
Employer-Maintained Social Media Account = Employer Property

Updating Personal LinkedIn Headline AFTER Resignation = Permitted (Provided No Confidential Client List Used & No Non-Compete Violated)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Even though the coding error resulted in a financial GAIN (+2.1%) for the 12 clients and Lin fixed the bug going forward, hiding the error and failing to disclose the IPS/process deviation violates Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care, Standard V(B) Communication with Clients, and Standard I(C)/I(D)!

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> A process error or coding bug is NOT excused just because market luck turned it into a profit for clients—all material model errors must be escalated to compliance and disclosed to affected clients.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** Before publishing a comprehensive initiation report on a pharmaceutical company, an equity analyst wants to confirm that the clinical trial dates and chemical compound names in his draft report are accurate. Under Standard I(B) Independence and Objectivity, the analyst may:

- **[A]** Send the entire draft report, including the 'Buy' recommendation and valuation summary, to the pharmaceutical company's Investor Relations director

- **[B]** Submit only the strictly factual sections of the draft report (omitting the valuation, price target, and investment recommendation) to the company for factual verification, retaining a copy of the pre-review draft for compliance

- **[C]** Ask the Investment Banking department to rewrite the conclusion

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Analysts may share only the factual portions of a draft report with the subject issuer to verify factual accuracy, stripping out the analytical conclusion, price target, and recommendation.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** A wealth management firm offers three disclosed tiers of service: Bronze (online automated rebalancing), Silver (quarterly advisor calls), and Gold (custom tax-loss harvesting and direct access to the research team for accounts over $10 million). All research rating changes are posted simultaneously on the client portal for all three tiers. Does this tiered structure violate Standard III(B) Fair Dealing?

- **[A]** Yes, because all clients must receive identical service

- **[B]** No, because offering different levels of service for different fee tiers is permitted as long as the tiers are disclosed to all clients, rating changes are disseminated simultaneously, and lower tiers are not disadvantaged

- **[C]** Yes, unless Gold clients trade last

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Standard III(B) requires fair dealing, not identical dealing; disclosed premium service tiers are permitted provided core research recommendations and trade allocations do not disadvantage other clients.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** An asset manager hires an external quantitative sub-advisor to manage an emerging-market debt sleeve for its institutional clients. Under Standard V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis, the asset manager must:

- **[A]** Accept the sub-advisor's marketing brochure at face value

- **[B]** Conduct thorough initial and ongoing due diligence on the sub-advisor's investment process, code/model assumptions, risk controls, and compliance adherence

- **[C]** Transfer all fiduciary liability to the sub-advisor

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Hiring an external sub-advisor or third-party research provider requires rigorous due diligence on their assumptions, people, process, and performance verification under Standard V(A).
