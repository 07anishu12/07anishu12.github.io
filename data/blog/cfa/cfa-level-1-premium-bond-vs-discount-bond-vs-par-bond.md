Issuer Effective Interest Expense & Amortization (Interest Expense_t = Carrying Value_{t−1} × Market Yield_issuance ; Amortization = |Interest Expense_t − Cash Coupon|) establishes the primary relationship (Under both IFRS and US GAAP, bond discounts and premiums are amortized over time using the effective interest rate locked in at issuance.), while Constant-Yield Price Trajectory Capital Return (Expected Capital Return = P_t(at initial YTM) − P_{t−1}(at initial YTM) ; Total Return = Coupon + Trajectory Change = P_{t−1} × YTM) governs Along the constant-yield trajectory, the dollar coupon plus the pull-to-par price change always equals the beginning carrying value multiplied by YTM.

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Issuer Effective Interest Expense & Amortization

```formula
Interest Expense_t = Carrying Value_{t−1} × Market Yield_issuance

Amortization = |Interest Expense_t − Cash Coupon|
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Under both IFRS and US GAAP, bond discounts and premiums are amortized over time using the effective interest rate locked in at issuance.

### Formula 2: Constant-Yield Price Trajectory Capital Return

```formula
Expected Capital Return = P_t(at initial YTM) − P_{t−1}(at initial YTM)

Total Return = Coupon + Trajectory Change = P_{t−1} × YTM
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Along the constant-yield trajectory, the dollar coupon plus the pull-to-par price change always equals the beginning carrying value multiplied by YTM.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: 3-Year Effective Interest Amortization Schedule for a Discount vs. Premium Bond

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
On Jan 1, Firm A issues a 3-year, 4.0% annual coupon bond ($1,000 par) when market YTM = 6.0% (Proceeds = $946.54). Firm B issues a 3-year, 8.0% annual coupon bond ($1,000 par) when market YTM = 6.0% (Proceeds = $1,053.46).

```formula
Interest Expense_t = Carrying Value_{t−1} × Market Yield_issuance

Amortization = |Interest Expense_t − Cash Coupon|
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Firm A (Discount Bond): Year 1 Interest Expense = $946.54 × 6.0% = $56.79; Cash Coupon = $40.00; Discount Amortization = $16.79; Ending Year 1 Carrying Value = $946.54 + $16.79 = $963.33. Year 2 Interest Expense = $963.33 × 6.0% = $57.80 (Carrying Value rises to $981.13). Year 3 Interest Expense = $981.13 × 6.0% = $58.87 (Carrying Value hits $1,000.00). Firm B (Premium Bond): Year 1 Interest Expense = $1,053.46 × 6.0% = $63.21; Cash Coupon = $80.00; Premium Amortization = $16.79; Ending Year 1 Carrying Value = $1,053.46 − $16.79 = $1,036.67.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> For a discount bond, carrying value and interest expense increase every year; for a premium bond, carrying value and interest expense decrease every year.

### Worked Example 2: Investor Constant-Yield Trajectory Price Change over 1 Year

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
An investor buys the 3-year, 4.0% discount bond at $946.54 (YTM = 6.0%) and sells it 1 year later when 2-year yields are still 6.0%.

```formula
Expected Capital Return = P_t(at initial YTM) − P_{t−1}(at initial YTM)

Total Return = Coupon + Trajectory Change = P_{t−1} × YTM
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Price at t = 1 (2 years remaining at 6.0%) = $963.33. Investor receives $40.00 coupon + $16.79 capital gain along constant-yield trajectory = $56.79 total return, which is $56.79 / $946.54 = 6.00% realized YTM.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Any market price deviation above or below $963.33 at Year 1 represents a capital gain or loss for tax and performance attribution.

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Accounting / Market Attribute | Discount Bond (C < YTM) | Par Bond (C = YTM) | Premium Bond (C > YTM) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial Carrying Value | Below Par (< $1,000) | Equal to Par ($1,000) | Above Par (> $1,000) |
| Interest Expense vs Cash Coupon | Interest Expense > Cash Coupon | Interest Expense = Cash Coupon | Interest Expense < Cash Coupon |
| Trend in Interest Expense | Increases each period | Constant each period | Decreases each period |
| Issuer Operating Cash Flow (US GAAP) | Higher than Par bond (lower coupon) | Equal to Interest Expense | Lower than Par bond (higher coupon) |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A corporation issues a 5-year fixed-rate bond at a discount to par value. Under the effective interest rate method, over the life of the bond, the issuer's reported annual interest expense will:

- **[A]** Remain constant each year

- **[B]** Increase each year as the bond's carrying value accretes toward par

- **[C]** Decrease each year as the discount is amortized

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Interest expense equals Beginning Carrying Value × Historical Issuance Yield. As the discount amortizes, Carrying Value increases each year, causing Interest Expense to increase each year.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** An investor purchases a 10-year, 7.0% annual coupon bond at a price of $107.36 (YTM = 6.0%). If the bond is sold 1 year later at a price of $106.50, the investor's capital gain or loss relative to the constant-yield price trajectory is closest to:

- **[A]** A capital loss of $0.86

- **[B]** A capital loss of $0.30

- **[C]** A capital gain of $0.20

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** At t = 1 (9 years remaining at the original 6.0% YTM), the constant-yield trajectory carrying value is $106.80. Selling at $106.50 produces a capital loss of $106.50 − $106.80 = −$0.30 relative to the constant-yield price trajectory.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** Under US GAAP, compared to issuing a bond at par for the same proceeds, issuing a zero-coupon bond at a deep discount results in:

- **[A]** Higher operating cash outflows (lower CFO) each year

- **[B]** Zero cash interest paid during the bond's life, overstating Operating Cash Flow (CFO) and understating Financing Cash Flow (CFF) at maturity

- **[C]** Lower reported interest expense on the income statement

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** With a zero-coupon bond, no cash interest leaves CFO during the life of the bond, and the entire par repayment at maturity is classified in CFF under US GAAP.
