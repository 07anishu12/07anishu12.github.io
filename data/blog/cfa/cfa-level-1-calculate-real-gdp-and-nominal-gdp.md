## Introduction

How to Calculate Real GDP and Nominal GDP is a core Level I Economics topic (LM 2: Nominal GDP, Real GDP, GDP Deflator, Per-Capita Real GDP growth, Solow growth accounting (TFP, capital, labor)) focusing on separating physical output growth (Real GDP at constant base-year prices) from price inflation (Nominal GDP and GDP Deflator), plus Solow potential GDP growth accounting. This guide covers the governing equations (Nominal GDP, Real GDP & GDP Deflator and Solow Potential Real GDP Growth Accounting Equation), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, separating physical output growth (Real GDP at constant base-year prices) from price inflation (Nominal GDP and GDP Deflator), plus Solow potential GDP growth accounting links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Economics. Nominal GDP, Real GDP & GDP Deflator (Nominal GDP_t = ∑ (P_{i,t} × Q_{i,t}) ; Real GDP_t = ∑ (P_{i,Base} × Q_{i,t}) ; GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP_t / Real GDP_t) × 100) establishes the primary relationship (Nominal GDP values current output at current prices; Real GDP values current output at constant base-year prices to isolate physical volume growth.), while Solow Potential Real GDP Growth Accounting Equation (Growth in Potential GDP = Growth in TFP + (W_L × Growth in Labor) + (W_C × Growth in Capital)) governs Decomposes long-run real economic growth into Total Factor Productivity (technology) plus factor-share-weighted labor and capital growth (W_L + W_C = 1).

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Nominal GDP, Real GDP & GDP Deflator

```formula
Nominal GDP_t = ∑ (P_{i,t} × Q_{i,t})

Real GDP_t = ∑ (P_{i,Base} × Q_{i,t})

GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP_t / Real GDP_t) × 100
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- Nominal GDP values current output at current prices.

- Real GDP values current output at constant base-year prices to isolate physical volume growth.

### Formula 2: Solow Potential Real GDP Growth Accounting Equation

```formula
Growth in Potential GDP = Growth in TFP + (W_L × Growth in Labor) + (W_C × Growth in Capital)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Decomposes long-run real economic growth into Total Factor Productivity (technology) plus factor-share-weighted labor and capital growth (W_L + W_C = 1).

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Computing Nominal GDP, Real GDP, GDP Deflator, and Real Growth Rate

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
In Base Year 2024, an economy produces 1,000 units of Good A @ $50 ($50,000) and 2,000 units of Good B @ $25 ($50,000), so 2024 Nominal = Real GDP = $100,000. In 2026, it produces 1,100 units of Good A @ $60 and 2,100 units of Good B @ $28.

```formula
Nominal GDP_t = ∑ (P_{i,t} × Q_{i,t})

Real GDP_t = ∑ (P_{i,Base} × Q_{i,t})

GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP_t / Real GDP_t) × 100
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1 (2026 Nominal GDP): (1,100 × $60) + (2,100 × $28) = $66,000 + $58,800 = $124,800 (+24.80% nominal jump). Step 2 (2026 Real GDP at 2024 prices): (1,100 × $50) + (2,100 × $25) = $55,000 + $52,500 = $107,500 (+7.50% real physical growth). Step 3 (2026 GDP Deflator): ($124,800 / $107,500) × 100 = 116.093 (indicating 16.09% cumulative economy-wide price inflation since 2024).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Of the 24.80% nominal expansion, only 7.50% represents real output growth while 16.09% is price inflation (1.075 × 1.16093 = 1.2480).

### Worked Example 2: Solow Growth Accounting for Potential Real GDP and Labor Productivity

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
An economy has a labor share of national income W_L = 0.65 and a capital share W_C = 0.35. Annual Total Factor Productivity (TFP) grows at 1.40%, labor supply grows at 1.00%, and capital stock grows at 3.00%.

```formula
Growth in Potential GDP = Growth in TFP + (W_L × Growth in Labor) + (W_C × Growth in Capital)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Potential Real GDP Growth = 1.40% + (0.65 × 1.00%) + (0.35 × 3.00%) = 1.40% + 0.65% + 1.05% = 3.10% per year. Growth in Labor Productivity (Output per Worker) = Potential GDP Growth − Labor Growth = 3.10% − 1.00% = 2.10% per year.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Because diminishing marginal returns limit pure capital deepening, sustained per-capita growth depends heavily on Total Factor Productivity (1.40%).

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** In 2026, a country's Nominal GDP is $2,310 billion and its GDP Deflator (with base year 2020 = 100) is 110.0. If Real GDP in 2025 was $2,000 billion, the country's real GDP growth rate in 2026 is:

- **[A]** 5.00%

- **[B]** 10.00%

- **[C]** 15.50%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** 2026 Real GDP = ($2,310B / 110.0) × 100 = $2,100 billion. Real GDP growth rate from 2025 to 2026 = ($2,100B − $2,000B) / $2,000B = 5.00%.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** In the Solow neo-classical growth accounting model, if labor's share of national income is 60%, capital's share is 40%, labor force growth is 1.5%, capital stock growth is 2.5%, and Total Factor Productivity (TFP) growth is 1.2%, potential Real GDP growth is:

- **[A]** 3.10%

- **[B]** 3.30%

- **[C]** 5.20%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Potential GDP Growth = 1.2% + 0.60(1.5%) + 0.40(2.5%) = 1.2% + 0.9% + 1.0% = 3.10%.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** How does the GDP Deflator differ from the Consumer Price Index (CPI) as a measure of inflation?

- **[A]** The GDP Deflator covers all domestically produced goods and services (including business investment and exports) but excludes imported consumer goods

- **[B]** The GDP Deflator uses a fixed base-year basket of imported goods

- **[C]** The GDP Deflator only measures wholesale raw materials

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** The GDP Deflator reflects prices of all domestically produced C + I + G + X (excluding imports M), whereas CPI tracks a fixed basket of goods and services purchased by households (including imported consumer goods).

## Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

- **Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Solow Potential Real GDP Growth Accounting Equation.**
