### Formula 1: Beneish M-Score Probabilistic Manipulation Model

```formula
M-Score = −4.84 + 0.920(DSRI) + 0.528(GMI) + 0.404(AQI) + 0.892(SGI) + 0.115(DEPI) − 0.172(SGAI) + 4.679(Accruals) − 0.327(LEVI)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- M-Score is a standard normal Z-score.

- if M-Score > −1.78 (e.g., −1.40 is HIGHER/less negative than −1.78!), the firm is flagged as a likely earnings manipulator.

### Formula 2: Scaled Cash-Flow Accruals Ratio & Persistence Model

```formula
CF Accruals Ratio = (Net Income − CFO − CFI) / [0.5 × (NOI_End + NOI_Beg)]

E(Earnings_{t+1}) = α + β_1(Cash Component_t) + β_2(Accrual Component_t) where β_1 > β_2
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Because accrual earnings mean-revert much faster than cash flow earnings (β_1 > β_2), firms with high positive accruals experience lower future ROA/ROE.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Interpreting Beneish M-Score Variables (DSRI, GMI, AQI, DEPI, Accruals) and the −1.78 Cutoff

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Forensic screening of Company X yields: Days Sales in Receivable Index (DSRI) = 1.35, Gross Margin Index (GMI = Gross Margin_{t−1} / Gross Margin_t) = 1.18, Asset Quality Index (AQI) = 1.25, Depreciation Index (DEPI = Depr Rate_{t−1} / Depr Rate_t) = 1.15, and an overall Beneish M-Score of −1.42.

```formula
M-Score = −4.84 + 0.920(DSRI) + 0.528(GMI) + 0.404(AQI) + 0.892(SGI) + 0.115(DEPI) − 0.172(SGAI) + 4.679(Accruals) − 0.327(LEVI)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. DSRI = 1.35 (> 1.0): Receivables grew 35% faster than revenue (channel stuffing / premature revenue).**

- **Step 2. GMI = 1.18 (> 1.0): Gross margin deteriorated from t−1 to t, creating pressure to manipulate.**

- **Step 3. AQI = 1.25 (> 1.0): Proportion of non-current assets other than PP&E (e.g., capitalized soft intangibles) jumped.**

- **Step 4. DEPI = 1.15 (> 1.0): Depreciation rate slowed down (extending asset lives to boost EBIT).**

- **Step 5. Overall M-Score = −1.42: Because −1.42 > −1.78 (less negative!), Company X exceeds the manipulation threshold.**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Watch the negative sign on the exam: −1.42 is GREATER than −1.78 (closer to zero), which indicates a HIGHER probability of earnings manipulation!

### Worked Example 2: Calculating Net Operating Assets (NOA) and Balance-Sheet vs. Cash-Flow Accruals Ratios

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A company reports Beginning Net Operating Assets (NOA = Operating Assets − Operating Liabilities) = $380M and Ending NOA = $420M (Average NOA = $400M). During the year, Net Income = $68M, Operating Cash Flow (CFO) = $45M, and Investing Cash Flow (CFI) = −$15M.

```formula
CF Accruals Ratio = (Net Income − CFO − CFI) / [0.5 × (NOI_End + NOI_Beg)]

E(Earnings_{t+1}) = α + β_1(Cash Component_t) + β_2(Accrual Component_t) where β_1 > β_2
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. Balance-Sheet Accruals = NOA_End − NOA_Beg = $420M − $380M = +$40M; BS Accruals Ratio = $40M / $400M = 10.0%.**

- **Step 2. Cash-Flow Accruals = NI − CFO − CFI = $68M − $45M − (−$15M) = $68M − $30M = +$38M; CF Accruals Ratio = $38M / $400M = 9.50%.**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> High positive scaled accruals (9.5%–10.0%) warn that reported earnings are heavily reliant on balance sheet accrual expansion rather than free cash flow.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** In the Beneish M-score model, the probability cutoff for flagging a company as a likely earnings manipulator is −1.78. If Company A has an M-score of −2.35 and Company B has an M-score of −1.30, which company is flagged as a likely manipulator?

- **[A]** Company A only

- **[B]** Company B only, because −1.30 is greater (less negative) than the −1.78 cutoff

- **[C]** Both Company A and Company B

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** On the real number line, −1.30 > −1.78 (corresponding to a higher standard normal Z-probability of manipulation), whereas −2.35 < −1.78 is in the safe non-manipulator zone.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Why are the Gross Margin Index (GMI) and Depreciation Index (DEPI) in the Beneish M-score model defined with Period (t − 1) in the numerator and Period (t) in the denominator?

- **[A]** So that a deteriorating gross margin (GM_t < GM_{t−1}) or a slowing depreciation rate (DeprRate_t < DeprRate_{t−1}) produces an index value GREATER than 1.0, consistent with higher manipulation risk

- **[B]** Because t − 1 is always larger than t

- **[C]** To eliminate inflation

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Putting t − 1 in the numerator for GMI and DEPI ensures that margin deterioration and slowing depreciation yield values > 1.0 with positive model coefficients (+0.528 and +0.115).

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** Empirical research on the persistence of corporate earnings (Sloan accrual anomaly) demonstrates that when forecasting next year's Return on Assets (ROA):

- **[A]** The accrual component of earnings is more persistent than the cash flow component

- **[B]** The cash flow component of earnings is significantly more persistent than the accrual component, because high accruals rapidly mean-revert

- **[C]** Cash flows and accruals have identical persistence

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Accrual-driven earnings mean-revert rapidly (lower slope coefficient β_2), whereas cash-backed earnings persist into future periods (higher slope coefficient β_1).
