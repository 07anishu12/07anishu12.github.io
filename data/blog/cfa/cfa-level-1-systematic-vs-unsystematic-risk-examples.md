## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, decomposing total variance into systematic market variance (β_i^2 σ_M^2) and unsystematic idiosyncratic variance (σ_e^2), and explaining why only systematic risk earns a risk premium links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Portfolio Management. Single-Index Total Risk Decomposition (Total Variance σ_i^2 = Systematic Variance + Unsystematic Variance = (β_i^2 × σ_M^2) + σ_e^2) establishes the primary relationship (Splits an asset's total return variance into market-driven variance (β_i^2 σ_M^2) and firm-specific error variance (σ_e^2).), while Proportion of Risk Explained by Market (R-Squared) (R^2 = Systematic Variance / Total Variance = (β_i^2 × σ_M^2) / σ_i^2 = ρ_{i,M}^2) governs Measures the percentage of an asset's total variance that is systematic; (1 − R^2) is the diversifiable unsystematic share.

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Single-Index Total Risk Decomposition

```formula
Total Variance σ_i^2 = Systematic Variance + Unsystematic Variance = (β_i^2 × σ_M^2) + σ_e^2
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Splits an asset's total return variance into market-driven variance (β_i^2 σ_M^2) and firm-specific error variance (σ_e^2).

### Formula 2: Proportion of Risk Explained by Market (R-Squared)

```formula
R^2 = Systematic Variance / Total Variance = (β_i^2 × σ_M^2) / σ_i^2 = ρ_{i,M}^2
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- Measures the percentage of an asset's total variance that is systematic.

- (1 − R^2) is the diversifiable unsystematic share.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Decomposing a Stock's Total Risk into Systematic and Unsystematic Volatility

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Stock T has a total return standard deviation of σ_i = 25.0% (Total Variance = 625.0 %^2) and a CAPM beta of β_i = 1.20. The market portfolio has a standard deviation of σ_M = 15.0% (Market Variance = 225.0 %^2). Compute (1) Systematic Variance, (2) Unsystematic Variance and Standard Deviation (σ_e), and (3) R-squared.

```formula
Total Variance σ_i^2 = Systematic Variance + Unsystematic Variance = (β_i^2 × σ_M^2) + σ_e^2
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1 (Systematic Variance): β_i^2 × σ_M^2 = (1.20^2) × (15.0^2) = 1.44 × 225.0 = 324.0 (%^2) (Systematic Standard Deviation = 1.20 × 15.0% = 18.0%). Step 2 (Unsystematic Variance): σ_e^2 = Total Variance − Systematic Variance = 625.0 − 324.0 = 301.0 (%^2); Unsystematic Standard Deviation σ_e = √301.0 = 17.35%. Step 3 (R-Squared): R^2 = 324.0 / 625.0 = 0.5184 = 51.84% (correlation ρ_{i,M} = √0.5184 = +0.72).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Notice that variances are additive (324 + 301 = 625), whereas standard deviations are NOT additive (18.0% + 17.35% ≠ 25.0%)!

### Worked Example 2: Why Unsystematic Risk Earns Zero Risk Premium in Equilibrium

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Biotech Startup B has high total volatility (σ_B = 60.0%) driven by FDA clinical trial risk, which is completely uncorrelated with the macro economy (ρ_{B,M} = 0.00, so β_B = 0.00). Industrial Stock C has lower total volatility (σ_C = 22.0%) but high correlation with GDP (β_C = 1.30). Risk-free rate R_F = 4.5%, ERP = 5.0%.

```formula
R^2 = Systematic Variance / Total Variance = (β_i^2 × σ_M^2) / σ_i^2 = ρ_{i,M}^2
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Expected Return on Biotech B: E(R_B) = 4.5% + 0.00(5.0%) = 4.50% (= R_F!). Expected Return on Industrial C: E(R_C) = 4.5% + 1.30(5.0%) = 11.00%.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Because investors can eliminate Biotech B's 60% FDA trial risk for free by holding a diversified portfolio, the market pays zero risk premium for unsystematic risk (E(R_B) = R_F = 4.50%).

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Risk Dimension | Systematic (Market / Non-Diversifiable) Risk | Unsystematic (Idiosyncratic / Firm) Risk | Portfolio & Pricing Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Macro vs Micro Drivers | Interest rates, inflation, GDP, war | CEO resignation, FDA failure, factory fire | Systematic hits all risky assets |
| Effect of Diversification | Cannot be diversified away (converges to σ_M^2) | Approaches zero as N grows (≥ 30 stocks) | σ_e^2 / N -> 0 as N -> ∞ |
| Mathematical Measure | Beta (β_i) or Systematic Variance β_i^2 σ_M^2 | Residual error variance σ_e^2 | Total Variance = β_i^2 σ_M^2 + σ_e^2 |
| Equilibrium Return Premium | Priced by market (β_i × ERP) | Zero risk premium in equilibrium | Never pay for diversifiable risk |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** Stock K has a total return standard deviation of 30%, a beta of 1.50, and the market portfolio has a standard deviation of 16%. Stock K's unsystematic variance (σ_e^2) is closest to:

- **[A]** 324 (%^2)

- **[B]** 576 (%^2)

- **[C]** 900 (%^2)

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Total variance = 30^2 = 900 (%^2). Systematic variance = β^2 × σ_M^2 = (1.50^2) × (16^2) = 2.25 × 256 = 576 (%^2). Unsystematic variance = 900 − 576 = 324 (%^2) (so unsystematic standard deviation = √324 = 18.0%).

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** According to capital market theory, why does an asset with very high unsystematic (firm-specific) risk NOT command a higher expected return in market equilibrium?

- **[A]** Because unsystematic risk can be eliminated at virtually zero cost by holding a well-diversified portfolio, so competition among diversified investors bids away any premium for bearing it

- **[B]** Because unsystematic risk is always negative

- **[C]** Because central banks insure against firm-specific risk

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** If an asset offered extra return for diversifiable risk, diversified investors would buy it and diversify the risk away for free, bidding its price up and its expected return down to the CAPM line.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** Which of the following events is the clearest example of systematic risk?

- **[A]** A pharmaceutical company's flagship drug fails Phase III clinical trials

- **[B]** An unexpected 75-basis-point interest rate hike by the Federal Reserve that increases discount rates across the entire economy

- **[C]** A labor strike at a single regional airline

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Central bank interest rate shifts, inflation shocks, and GDP recessions impact the discount rates and cash flows of the entire market and cannot be diversified away within an equity portfolio.
