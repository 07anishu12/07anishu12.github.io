## Introduction

CFA Standard IV Duties to Employers Explained covers Standard IV(A) Loyalty (preparations to leave an employer, non-solicitation, ownership of models/records, independent consulting), IV(B) written consent for additional compensation, and IV(C) supervisory compliance duties in Level I Ethics and Professional Standards (LM 2 & LM 3: Standard IV(A) Loyalty (leaving an employer, independent practice, social media contacts), IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements, IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors), with verified formulas, two worked examples, and three practice MCQs.

## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Standard IV(A) Leaving an Employer & Property Ownership Rule (Before Resigning: May make administrative preparations (incorporate LLC, lease office) ONLY ; All Models, Code, Research & Client Lists Created on the Job = Employer Property (unless public or explicitly permitted)) and Standard IV(B) vs. Standard I(B) Client Gift/Compensation Rule (Future Performance Bonus from Client => Requires ADVANCE WRITTEN CONSENT from All Parties (Standard IV(B)) ; Token Gift for Past Work => Notification to Employer (Standard I(B))) govern Standard IV(A) Loyalty (preparations to leave an employer, non-solicitation, ownership of models/records, independent consulting), IV(B) written consent for additional compensation, and IV(C) supervisory compliance duties.

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Standard IV(A) Leaving an Employer & Property Ownership Rule

```formula
Before Resigning: May make administrative preparations (incorporate LLC, lease office) ONLY

All Models, Code, Research & Client Lists Created on the Job = Employer Property (unless public or explicitly permitted)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Even if an analyst built a Python valuation model at home on weekends for work, it belongs to the employer and cannot be taken on a USB drive without written permission.

### Formula 2: Standard IV(B) vs. Standard I(B) Client Gift/Compensation Rule

```formula
Future Performance Bonus from Client => Requires ADVANCE WRITTEN CONSENT from All Parties (Standard IV(B))

Token Gift for Past Work => Notification to Employer (Standard I(B))
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Any contingent compensation arrangement from a client tied to future multi-period performance requires written consent from the employer (including email).

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Case Study: Departing Portfolio Manager Actions Before vs. After Resignation (Standard IV(A))

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Portfolio Manager Sarah plans to resign on Friday to launch her own RIA. On Wednesday (while still employed), she: (1) registers her new LLC with the state and signs an office lease; (2) copies her client contact list and Excel factor models onto a personal cloud drive; and (3) calls two loyal clients after hours asking them to move their accounts next week. On Monday (after her resignation takes effect and with no non-compete clause), she reconstructs client phone numbers from public directories and calls them.

```formula
Before Resigning: May make administrative preparations (incorporate LLC, lease office) ONLY

All Models, Code, Research & Client Lists Created on the Job = Employer Property (unless public or explicitly permitted)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Action 1 (registering LLC and leasing office before resigning): PERMITTED preparatory step. Action 2 (copying employer client lists and models): VIOLATION of IV(A). Action 3 (soliciting clients while still employed): VIOLATION of IV(A). Action 4 (soliciting former clients AFTER resigning using public directories in the absence of a non-compete agreement): PERMITTED!

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Once employment has officially ended (and assuming no valid non-compete agreement is violated), a former employee may solicit former clients using publicly available information or personal memory.

### Worked Example 2: Case Study: Supervisory Responsibility When Compliance Procedures Are Inadequate (Standard IV(C))

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Maria is promoted to Head of Fixed Income Trading and discovers that her desk has no compliance monitoring system to prevent front-running client bond orders. She informs senior management, who delay installing a compliance system for 6 months due to budget cuts.

```formula
Future Performance Bonus from Client => Requires ADVANCE WRITTEN CONSENT from All Parties (Standard IV(B))

Token Gift for Past Work => Notification to Employer (Standard I(B))
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Under Standard IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors, Maria MUST decline supervisory responsibility in writing until the firm adopts an adequate compliance system! Simply warning senior management while continuing to serve as supervisor without adequate procedures is a violation of Standard IV(C)—even if no employee actually commits a violation!

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> A supervisor can violate Standard IV(C) merely by failing to establish and enforce reasonable compliance procedures, even if zero underlying employee violations occur.

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A wealth manager's client offers her a $25,000 cash bonus if her portfolio outperforms the S&P 500 by at least 200 bps over the next 12 months. To comply with Standard IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements, the wealth manager must:

- **[A]** Refuse the offer because client bonuses are strictly prohibited

- **[B]** Obtain written consent from her employer (and all involved parties) BEFORE accepting the arrangement, disclosing the exact terms and duration

- **[C]** Verbally mention the arrangement to her supervisor at the end of the year if she wins the bonus

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Contingent future compensation from a client creates a conflict with other clients and the employer; Standard IV(B) requires obtaining advance written consent from the employer before accepting.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** While still employed at an asset management firm, an analyst decides to accept a job at a competing firm starting next month. Which of the following actions is PERMITTED prior to the effective date of his resignation?

- **[A]** Downloading the historical trade logs of the portfolios he managed to prove his track record to his new employer

- **[B]** Informing his current clients of his upcoming departure and inviting them to transfer their assets

- **[C]** Renting apartment space and filing incorporation paperwork for a future advisory business on his own time without soliciting clients or taking employer property

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option C.**
>
> **Explanation:** Administrative preparations (incorporating, leasing space) on personal time are permitted prior to resignation, whereas soliciting clients or copying employer records/models while still employed violates Standard IV(A).

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** If a supervisor detects that a subordinate trader has violated firm compliance rules by front-running client orders, what must the supervisor do under Standard IV(C)?

- **[A]** Rely on a verbal promise from the trader never to repeat the offense

- **[B]** Promptly initiate a thorough investigation AND place immediate interim restrictions on the trader's activities (such as suspending trading privileges or increasing monitoring) while the investigation is pending

- **[C]** Wait until the annual performance review to address the issue

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Once a potential violation is detected, a supervisor cannot simply rely on employee assurances; the supervisor must investigate immediately and restrict the employee's activities to prevent further harm.
