## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Dissecting a Classic Level II 'Verbal vs. Written' & 'Before vs. After' Vignette Trap

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Read this vignette excerpt: 'Before accepting a seat on the compensated board of directors of a local bank, Portfolio Manager Kenta obtains verbal approval from his firm's Chief Compliance Officer during a hallway conversation, and sends a formal written disclosure email to his clients one week after joining the board.' Identify the exact status under Standard IV(B) and VI(A).

```formula
2 to 3 Ethics Item Sets (8 to 12 Questions = 10%–15% Weight)

4 Questions per Vignette × 3.0 Minutes/Question = 12.0 Minutes per Item Set
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. Was verbal approval from the CCO sufficient for compensated outside board service if it creates a conflict? Under Standard IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements, written consent from all parties (including the employer) is required BEFORE accepting compensation that could create a conflict! Furthermore, under Standard VI(A), conflicts must be disclosed to clients BEFORE or at the time of providing advice.**

- **Step 2. Watch for single-word traps in Level II vignettes: 'verbal' (when written is required), 'after' (when advance consent/disclosure is required), and 'all' or 'never' in the answer choices.**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Highlighting timing prepositions ('before', 'after', 'upon') and communication format ('verbal', 'written', 'email') catches the majority of Level II Ethics traps.

### Worked Example 2: Handling 'Did X Violate the Standards?' with Three Justification Options

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A common Level II Ethics question format has three options: (A) Yes, because [Reason 1]; (B) No, because [Reason 2]; (C) No, because [Reason 3]. How do you solve when two options share 'No' (or two share 'Yes')?

```formula
Step 1: Read Question Stem 1 FIRST (highlight Character Name, Standard, and MOST/LEAST qualifier) -> Step 2: Read corresponding Paragraph -> Step 3: Check Timing & Written vs Verbal -> Step 4: Eliminate 2 distractors
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Decide independently whether a violation occurred (Yes vs. No) based strictly on the Standards Handbook—do NOT guess 'No' just because two options start with 'No'! Step 2: If the answer is 'No', compare Reason 2 vs. Reason 3 word-by-word: often one reason cites a completely fabricated rule (e.g., 'No, because trades under $10,000 are exempt from insider trading rules'—FALSE!) while the other cites the true Standard (e.g., 'No, because the information was non-material and used under the Mosaic Theory'—TRUE!).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> In 'Yes/No + Because' questions, a correct 'Yes' or 'No' paired with a FALSE rationale is always wrong! Both the conclusion AND the rationale must be 100% accurate.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** When approaching a 4-question CFA Level II Ethics item set (vignette) on exam day, the most time-efficient and accurate reading strategy is to:

- **[A]** Read the entire 6-paragraph vignette twice from start to finish before looking at any of the 4 questions

- **[B]** Read Question Stem 1 first to identify the target character, topic, and 'Most Likely / Least Likely' qualifier, then read the corresponding initial paragraph(s) of the vignette to answer Question 1, and proceed sequentially through Questions 2, 3, and 4

- **[C]** Answer the questions without reading the vignette

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Because Level II vignette questions follow the narrative chronologically, reading each question stem first tells you exactly which character and Standard to scrutinize in that paragraph.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** In a Level II Ethics question, two answer choices begin with 'Yes, because...' while one answer choice begins with 'No, because...'. If a candidate determines that a violation DID occur ('Yes'), how should the candidate decide between the two 'Yes' options?

- **[A]** Pick the longer option

- **[B]** Verify that the 'because...' justification cites an actual requirement of the CFA Standards rather than an invented rule (such as a fabricated dollar threshold or mandatory regulatory whistleblowing)

- **[C]** Switch to 'No'

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Exam writers frequently pair a correct 'Yes' with a plausible-sounding but fabricated rule (e.g., 'Yes, because members must obtain SEC approval before changing price targets'); always verify the rationale against the official Standards.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** Does an email from a supervisor approving an employee's outside consulting arrangement satisfy the 'written consent' requirement of Standard IV(B)?

- **[A]** No, only a notarized paper contract satisfies written consent

- **[B]** Yes, any form of documented electronic communication (such as an email or compliance portal approval) that can be stored and retrieved satisfies 'written consent'

- **[C]** No, consent is not required for consulting

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Under CFA Institute guidance, 'written' includes electronic communications (email, compliance system logs) that create a permanent, retrievable record.
