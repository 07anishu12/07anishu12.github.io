## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Hedge Fund Fee Calculation (Incentive Computed Net of Management Fee) (Mgmt Fee = m × Ending (or Beginning) Gross AUM ; Incentive Fee = p × max[0, Gross Return − Mgmt Fee − max(HWM, Hurdle)]) and Merger Arbitrage Deal Spread Payoff (Deal Spread = Offer Price − Current Target Stock Price (Long Target + Short Acquirer in Stock-for-Stock Deal)) govern calculating hedge fund management and incentive fees with High-Water Marks (HWM) and hurdle rates, comparing hedge fund strategies (Long/Short, Market Neutral, Merger Arb, Macro), and database biases.

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Hedge Fund Fee Calculation (Incentive Computed Net of Management Fee)

```formula
Mgmt Fee = m × Ending (or Beginning) Gross AUM

Incentive Fee = p × max[0, Gross Return − Mgmt Fee − max(HWM, Hurdle)]
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Always check whether the prompt specifies calculating the incentive fee independently of the management fee or net of the management fee, and whether a High-Water Mark (HWM) applies.

### Formula 2: Merger Arbitrage Deal Spread Payoff

```formula
Deal Spread = Offer Price − Current Target Stock Price (Long Target + Short Acquirer in Stock-for-Stock Deal)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- Captures the risk premium for regulatory/antitrust deal collapse.

- exhibits negative skewness (small steady gains when deals close, large loss if a deal breaks).

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Multi-Year Hedge Fund Fee, High-Water Mark (HWM), and Investor Net Return Calculation

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
An investor places $100.0M into a hedge fund with a '2 and 20' fee structure: 2.0% management fee (based on year-end gross AUM) and 20.0% incentive fee calculated NET of the management fee, subject to a High-Water Mark (HWM) and a 4.0% hard hurdle rate. In Year 1, gross fund value drops −10% to $90.0M. In Year 2, gross value rebounds +30% from Year 1's net ending AUM.

```formula
Mgmt Fee = m × Ending (or Beginning) Gross AUM

Incentive Fee = p × max[0, Gross Return − Mgmt Fee − max(HWM, Hurdle)]
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Year 1: Gross AUM = $90.0M. Mgmt Fee = 2.0% × $90.0M = $1.80M. Since AUM < $100.0M initial HWM, Incentive Fee = $0. Year 1 Ending Net AUM = $90.0M − $1.80M = $88.20M (Investor Net Return = −11.80%). HWM remains at $100.0M! Year 2: Gross AUM grows +30% from $88.20M = $88.20M × 1.30 = $114.66M. Year 2 Mgmt Fee = 2.0% × $114.66M = $2.2932M. Net-of-mgmt AUM = $114.66M − $2.2932M = $112.3668M. Hurdle above $100M HWM = $100M × 1.04 = $104.00M. Year 2 Incentive Fee = 20% × ($112.3668M − $104.00M) = 20% × $8.3668M = $1.6734M. Year 2 Ending Net AUM = $112.3668M − $1.6734M = $110.6934M (New HWM for Year 3 = $110.69M).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> The High-Water Mark ($100M) protects the investor from paying a 20% incentive fee in Year 2 on the recovery from $88.20M back up to $100.00M.

### Worked Example 2: Fee Drag Comparison: Single Multi-Strategy Fund vs. Fund-of-Funds

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Two underlying Hedge Funds A and B each start with $50M ($100M total) and charge 20% incentive fees (0% mgmt fee for simplicity). In Year 1, Fund A gains +40% (+$20M) and Fund B loses −20% (−$10M), so combined gross profit is +$10M (+10%).

```formula
Deal Spread = Offer Price − Current Target Stock Price (Long Target + Short Acquirer in Stock-for-Stock Deal)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

In Two Separate Hedge Funds (or a Fund-of-Funds): Fund A charges 20% × $20M = $4.0M incentive fee; Fund B charges $0 -> Total Incentive Fee Paid = $4.0M (40% of the net $10M gain!), leaving only +$6.0M (+6.0% net return) before FoF fees. In a Single Multi-Strategy Fund netting gains and losses internally: Incentive Fee = 20% × ($20M − $10M) = $2.0M, leaving +$8.0M (+8.0% net return).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Investing across separate hedge funds suffers from 'asymmetric fee netting risk': you pay 20% incentive fees on winning managers without offsetting losses from losing managers.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A hedge fund starts the year with $200 million in AUM (which is also its current High-Water Mark). It charges a 2% management fee based on year-end valuation and a 20% incentive fee calculated NET of the management fee with no hurdle rate. If gross AUM at year-end is $250 million, the investor's net percentage return for the year is closest to:

- **[A]** 16.00%

- **[B]** 18.00%

- **[C]** 19.00%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Gross year-end AUM = $250M. Management fee = 2% × $250M = $5.0M. AUM net of management fee = $250M − $5.0M = $245.0M. Profit above $200M HWM = $245.0M − $200.0M = $45.0M. Incentive fee = 20% × $45.0M = $9.0M. Total fees = $5.0M + $9.0M = $14.0M. Ending Net AUM = $250M − $14.0M = $236.0M. Net return = ($236M − $200M) / $200M = 18.00%.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** When successful hedge funds voluntarily begin reporting their multi-year historical track records to commercial hedge fund databases and include their prior un-reported incubating years, this introduces:

- **[A]** Backfill (instant history) bias, which artificially inflates the database's average historical return

- **[B]** Deflationary bias

- **[C]** Zero statistical bias

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Backfill bias occurs when only funds with strong early incubation track records choose to join a public database and retroactively add their past winning returns; survivorship bias occurs when failed funds are dropped from the index.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** A hedge fund notice period (e.g., 30 to 90 days' advance notice required before a quarterly redemption date) combined with a fund 'gate' or 'lock-up period' primarily serves to:

- **[A]** Eliminate management fees

- **[B]** Prevent forced fire-sale liquidation of illiquid portfolio holdings during market stress, protecting remaining investors

- **[C]** Guarantee positive returns

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Lock-ups, notice periods, and redemption gates align investor liquidity terms with the underlying liquidity of the hedge fund's assets.
