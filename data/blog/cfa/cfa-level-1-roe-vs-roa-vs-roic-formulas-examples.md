ROE vs ROA vs ROIC: Formulas, Differences and Numerical Examples is a core Level I Financial Statement Analysis topic (LM 10: Financial Analysis Techniques — Return on Assets (ROA), Return on Invested Capital (ROIC), Return on Equity (ROE), and capital structure neutrality) focusing on aligning profit numerators with capital denominators across ROA, ROIC (NOPAT / Invested Capital), and ROE, plus financial leverage magnification. ## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, aligning profit numerators with capital denominators across ROA, ROIC (NOPAT / Invested Capital), and ROE, plus financial leverage magnification links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Financial Statement Analysis. Return on Equity (ROE) & Return on Assets (ROA) (ROE = Net Income / Average Equity ; ROA_standard = Net Income / Average Assets ; ROA_adjusted = [NI + Int×(1−t)] / Avg Assets) establishes the primary relationship (Matches equity earnings (Net Income) to equity capital, or unlevered earnings to total assets.), while Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) (ROIC = NOPAT / Average Invested Capital = [EBIT × (1 − Tax Rate)] / (Average Total Debt + Average Total Equity − Non-Operating Cash)) governs Measures pre-financing operating return available to both debt and equity holders, independent of capital structure.

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Return on Equity (ROE) & Return on Assets (ROA)

```formula
ROE = Net Income / Average Equity

ROA_standard = Net Income / Average Assets

ROA_adjusted = [NI + Int×(1−t)] / Avg Assets
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Matches equity earnings (Net Income) to equity capital, or unlevered earnings to total assets.

### Formula 2: Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)

```formula
ROIC = NOPAT / Average Invested Capital = [EBIT × (1 − Tax Rate)] / (Average Total Debt + Average Total Equity − Non-Operating Cash)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Measures pre-financing operating return available to both debt and equity holders, independent of capital structure.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Computing ROE, ROA, and ROIC for Two Firms with Identical Operations but Different Leverage

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Firm Unlevered (U) and Firm Levered (L) both have Average Invested Capital = $500M (and Total Assets = $550M including $50M AP) and generate EBIT = $80M at a 25% tax rate. Firm U has $0 Debt and $500M Equity. Firm L has $250M Debt @ 6.0% interest ($15M interest) and $250M Equity.

```formula
ROE = Net Income / Average Equity

ROA_standard = Net Income / Average Assets

ROA_adjusted = [NI + Int×(1−t)] / Avg Assets
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Both firms have NOPAT = $80M × (1 − 0.25) = $60M and ROIC = $60M / $500M = 12.00%. Firm U: Net Income = $60M; ROE_U = $60M / $500M = 12.00%; ROA_U = $60M / $550M = 10.91%. Firm L: EBT = $80M − $15M = $65M; Net Income = $65M × (1 − 0.25) = $48.75M; ROE_L = $48.75M / $250M = 19.50%; ROA_L = $48.75M / $550M = 8.86% (Adjusted ROA_L = [$48.75M + $11.25M]/$550M = 10.91%).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Because ROIC (12.00%) exceeds the after-tax cost of debt (6.0% × 0.75 = 4.50%), 1.0x D/E leverage boosts Firm L's ROE from 12.00% to 19.50% while leaving ROIC unchanged at 12.00%.

### Worked Example 2: Evaluating Economic Value Added (Spread Over WACC) Using ROIC

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Using Firm L above (ROIC = 12.00%, Invested Capital = $500M), if its Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) is 8.50%, calculate the annual Economic Profit (EVA) generated.

```formula
ROIC = NOPAT / Average Invested Capital = [EBIT × (1 − Tax Rate)] / (Average Total Debt + Average Total Equity − Non-Operating Cash)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

ROIC − WACC Spread = 12.00% − 8.50% = +3.50%. Economic Profit = Invested Capital × (ROIC − WACC) = $500M × 3.50% = +$17.50M (or NOPAT $60M − Capital Charge $42.50M = +$17.50M).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> ROIC directly compares against WACC to measure true value creation, whereas ROE must be compared against the levered Cost of Equity (r_e).

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Return Metric | Numerator (Flow) | Denominator (Average Stock) | Sensitivity to Financial Leverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Return on Equity (ROE) | Net Income (or NI − Pref Div) | Average Shareholders' Equity | High (magnified when ROIC > r_d(1−t)) |
| Standard ROA | Net Income | Average Total Assets | Distorted downward by interest expense |
| Interest-Adjusted ROA | Net Income + Interest × (1 − t) | Average Total Assets | Capital-structure neutral |
| Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) | NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − Tax Rate) | Avg Debt + Equity (ex excess cash) | Completely capital-structure neutral |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A company generates EBIT of $120 million, pays $20 million in interest, faces a 25% effective tax rate, and has Average Total Debt of $300 million, Average Shareholders' Equity of $500 million, and no excess cash. Its Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) is closest to:

- **[A]** 9.38%

- **[B]** 11.25%

- **[C]** 15.00%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − t) = $120M × (1 − 0.25) = $90M. Average Invested Capital = Debt ($300M) + Equity ($500M) = $800M. ROIC = $90M / $800M = 11.25%.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Why do equity analysts prefer Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) over Return on Equity (ROE) when comparing operational efficiency across peers with different debt ratios?

- **[A]** ROIC uses pre-interest operating profit after tax (NOPAT) and total debt-plus-equity capital, removing leverage distortion

- **[B]** ROIC excludes depreciation and amortization

- **[C]** ROIC is always higher than ROE

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** ROE can be artificially boosted by replacing equity with cheap debt even when core operating profitability is mediocre; ROIC isolates unlevered operating return.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** If a firm's Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) is 7.0% and its after-tax cost of debt is 8.5%, issuing additional debt to repurchase common shares will cause its Return on Equity (ROE) to:

- **[A]** Increase

- **[B]** Decrease

- **[C]** Remain unchanged

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Leverage only increases ROE when operating return (ROIC) exceeds the after-tax cost of debt. Borrowing at 8.5% after tax to earn 7.0% destroys equity return and reduces ROE.

## Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

- **Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Return on Equity (ROE) & Return on Assets (ROA).**

- **Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Return on Invested Capital (ROIC).**

- **Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.**
