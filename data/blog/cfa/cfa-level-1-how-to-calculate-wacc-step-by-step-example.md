## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, calculating Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) using target or current market-value weights, after-tax marginal cost of debt, preferred stock cost, and CAPM cost of equity links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Corporate Issuers. Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) (WACC = (E / V) × r_e + (P / V) × r_p + (D / V) × r_d × (1 − t)) establishes the primary relationship (V = E + P + D is total market value of capital; only interest on debt D receives the (1 − t) corporate tax shield.), while Correct CFA Treatment of Equity Issuance Flotation Costs (NPV_Adjusted = NPV_Unadjusted (at WACC without flotation markup) − Dollar Flotation Cost_0) governs CFA curriculum requires deducting dollar flotation costs as a t = 0 cash outflow in NPV rather than artificially inflating r_e in WACC.

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)

```formula
WACC = (E / V) × r_e + (P / V) × r_p + (D / V) × r_d × (1 − t)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- V = E + P + D is total market value of capital.

- only interest on debt D receives the (1 − t) corporate tax shield.

### Formula 2: Correct CFA Treatment of Equity Issuance Flotation Costs

```formula
NPV_Adjusted = NPV_Unadjusted (at WACC without flotation markup) − Dollar Flotation Cost_0
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** CFA curriculum requires deducting dollar flotation costs as a t = 0 cash outflow in NPV rather than artificially inflating r_e in WACC.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Full Step-by-Step WACC Calculation Using Market Values vs. Book Values

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A corporation has the following balance sheet and market data: (1) Common Stock: Book Value = $200M; 20M shares trading at $30.00/share (Market Value E = $600M); CAPM Cost of Equity r_e = 11.50%. (2) Preferred Stock: Book Value = $80M; 1M shares trading at $100.00/share paying an $8.00 dividend (Market Value P = $100M; r_p = $8/$100 = 8.00%). (3) Debt: Book Value = $320M; Market Value D = $300M; Pre-Tax Yield to Maturity r_d = 6.00%. Marginal corporate tax rate t = 25.0%.

```formula
WACC = (E / V) × r_e + (P / V) × r_p + (D / V) × r_d × (1 − t)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Total Market Value of Capital V = $600M (E) + $100M (P) + $300M (D) = $1,000M. Market Value Weights: w_e = 60.0%, w_p = 10.0%, w_d = 30.0% (never use Book Value weights!). Step 2: After-Tax Cost of Debt = r_d × (1 − t) = 6.00% × (1 − 0.25) = 4.50%. Step 3: WACC = (0.60 × 11.50%) + (0.10 × 8.00%) + (0.30 × 4.50%) = 6.90% + 0.80% + 1.35% = 9.05%.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Using Book Value weights (33.3% E, 13.3% P, 53.3% D) would have severely understated WACC at 7.30% instead of the true market-value WACC of 9.05%.

### Worked Example 2: Evaluating a Capital Project with Equity Flotation Costs

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
The company above (WACC = 9.05%) evaluates a 5-year expansion project requiring $50.0M initial CapEx that generates $15.0M per year in after-tax operating cash flows. To fund the equity portion ($30.0M), investment bankers charge a 4.0% flotation fee ($1.20M).

```formula
NPV_Adjusted = NPV_Unadjusted (at WACC without flotation markup) − Dollar Flotation Cost_0
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: PV of project cash inflows at 9.05% WACC = $15.0M × [1 − 1.0905^(−5)] / 0.0905 = $58.29M. Step 2: Deduct initial CapEx ($50.0M) AND t = 0 dollar flotation cost ($1.20M): NPV = $58.29M − $50.0M − $1.20M = +$7.09M.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Because flotation costs are a one-time t = 0 cash outlay, deducting $1.20M directly from NPV avoids distorting the ongoing multi-year discount rate.

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| WACC Component | Weight Basis | Cost Input | Tax Adjustment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Common Equity (w_e, r_e) | Target or Market Cap / V | CAPM or DDM (D_1/P_0 + g) | None (dividends not tax-deductible) |
| Preferred Stock (w_p, r_p) | Target or Preferred Mkt Cap / V | D_pref / P_pref | None (preferred div not deductible) |
| Debt (w_d, r_d) | Target or Market Value Debt / V | Pre-tax YTM on existing/new debt | Multiply by (1 − Marginal Tax Rate t) |
| Flotation Costs | t = 0 Cash Outflow in NPV | Dollar fee (% of new equity) | Deduct at t = 0 (do not add to r_e) |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A company has a target capital structure of 40% debt, 10% preferred stock, and 50% common equity. Its pre-tax cost of debt is 6.4%, its cost of preferred stock is 7.5%, its cost of common equity is 12.0%, and its marginal tax rate is 25%. Its WACC is closest to:

- **[A]** 8.67%

- **[B]** 9.31%

- **[C]** 9.47%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** After-tax cost of debt = 6.4% × (1 − 0.25) = 4.80%. WACC = (0.40 × 4.80%) + (0.10 × 7.50%) + (0.50 × 12.00%) = 1.92% + 0.75% + 6.00% = 8.67%.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** When target capital structure weights are not explicitly stated by management, which weights should an analyst use first when computing a company's WACC?

- **[A]** Historical book value weights from the balance sheet

- **[B]** Current market value weights of the company's debt, preferred stock, and common equity (or industry-average target market weights)

- **[C]** Equal 50/50 weights

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Market value weights reflect the true economic proportions of capital at current market prices; book values reflect outdated historical accounting figures.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** How does the CFA curriculum recommend treating investment banking flotation costs incurred when issuing new common equity for a capital project?

- **[A]** Increase the cost of equity r_e by dividing D_1 by P_0(1 − f)

- **[B]** Deduct the dollar amount of the flotation costs as an initial cash outflow at t = 0 when calculating the project's Net Present Value (NPV)

- **[C]** Ignore flotation costs because they are sunk costs

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Flotation costs occur once at issuance (t = 0), not every year forever; adjusting r_e upward incorrectly penalizes all future cash flows.
