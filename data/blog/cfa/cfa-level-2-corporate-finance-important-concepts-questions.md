## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Lintner Target Payout Dividend Adjustment Model

```formula
Expected Dividend Increase (D_1 − D_0) = [(E_1 × Target Payout Ratio) − D_0] × Adjustment Factor

Adj Factor = 1 / Years
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Because managers smooth dividends and hate cutting them, firms only adjust dividends by a fraction (1 / N years) of the gap toward their target payout ratio each year.

### Formula 2: Effective Tax Rate on Dividends: Double Taxation vs. Split-Rate vs. Tax Imputation

```formula
Double Taxation: T_Eff = T_Corp + (1 − T_Corp) × T_Div

Tax Imputation (Full Credit): T_Eff = Investor's Marginal Personal Tax Rate (T_Div)!
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Under a full tax imputation system, corporate taxes paid are credited to the shareholder, so the total effective tax rate on distributed earnings equals the shareholder's personal marginal tax rate.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Forecasting Next Year's Dividend Using Lintner's Target Payout Adjustment Model

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A company currently pays an annual dividend of D_0 = $2.00 per share on EPS of E_0 = $5.00 (40% current payout). Next year, EPS is expected to rise to E_1 = $6.50. The company has a long-term target payout ratio of 50.0% and adjusts its dividend toward the target over a 5-year period (Adjustment Factor = 1 / 5 = 0.20). Calculate expected D_1.

```formula
Expected Dividend Increase (D_1 − D_0) = [(E_1 × Target Payout Ratio) − D_0] × Adjustment Factor

Adj Factor = 1 / Years
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Target Dividend at E_1 = $6.50 × 50.0% = $3.25. Step 2: Gap above current dividend D_0 = $3.25 − $2.00 = +$1.25. Step 3: Expected Dividend Increase = $1.25 × 0.20 = +$0.25. Step 4: Forecasted Next-Year Dividend D_1 = $2.00 + $0.25 = $2.25 per share.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Notice that even though earnings jumped by +30% ($5.00 -> $6.50), Lintner smoothing increases the dividend by only +12.5% ($2.00 -> $2.25) so the actual payout ratio temporarily dips to $2.25 / $6.50 = 34.6%.

### Worked Example 2: Comparing Effective Tax Rates on $100 of Corporate Pre-Tax Profit Across Three Tax Regimes

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A corporation earns $100 of pre-tax income and distributes 100% of after-tax earnings as dividends. Corporate tax rate T_Corp = 30%. Compare the total effective tax rate and net cash received by an investor in the 40% personal dividend tax bracket (T_Div = 40%) under: (1) Double Taxation, (2) Split-Rate System (where distributed profits are taxed at 15% corporate rate), and (3) Full Tax Imputation.

```formula
Double Taxation: T_Eff = T_Corp + (1 − T_Corp) × T_Div

Tax Imputation (Full Credit): T_Eff = Investor's Marginal Personal Tax Rate (T_Div)!
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. Double Taxation: Corp tax = $30; Dividend = $70; Personal tax = 40% × $70 = $28; Total Tax = $30 + $28 = $58 -> Effective Tax Rate = 58.0% (= 30% + 0.70×40%; Net Cash = $42).**

- **Step 2. Split-Rate (15% Corp rate on distributed profit): Corp tax = $15; Dividend = $85; Personal tax = 40% × $85 = $34; Total Tax = $15 + $34 = $49 -> Effective Tax Rate = 49.0% (Net Cash = $51).**

- **Step 3. Tax Imputation: Corp pays $30; Investor owes 40% × $100 = $40 gross personal tax minus $30 imputation credit = $10 additional personal tax; Total Tax = $30 + $10 = $40 -> Effective Tax Rate = 40.0% (= Personal Tax Rate! Net Cash = $60).**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Under a tax imputation system, a tax-exempt pension fund (T_Div = 0%) actually receives a $30 cash tax refund from the government for the corporate taxes paid!

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Dividend Theory / Policy | Core Mechanism | Effect of Higher Payout on Stock Price | Target Investor Group |
|---|---|---|---|

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A company has a current dividend of $1.60 per share, forecasts next-year EPS of $5.00, maintains a target payout ratio of 40%, and uses an adjustment factor of 0.25 (4-year adjustment period) under the Lintner model. Its expected dividend per share next year is:

- **[A]** $1.70

- **[B]** $1.80

- **[C]** $2.00

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Target dividend = $5.00 × 0.40 = $2.00. Expected increase = ($2.00 − $1.60) × 0.25 = $0.40 × 0.25 = $0.10. Expected next-year dividend D_1 = $1.60 + $0.10 = $1.70.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Under a Residual Dividend Policy, a company forecasts Net Income of $120 million, has an optimal capital budget of $150 million, and maintains a target capital structure of 40% debt and 60% common equity. What total dollar dividend will the company pay?

- **[A]** $0

- **[B]** $20 million

- **[C]** $30 million

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option C.**
>
> **Explanation:** Equity needed to fund the $150M capital budget at 60% equity weight = 0.60 × $150M = $90M. Residual Dividend = Net Income ($120M) − Equity Reinvestment ($90M) = $30 million (a 25% payout ratio).

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** In a country operating a 'Double Taxation' system with a 25% corporate tax rate and a 20% personal tax rate on dividends, the combined effective tax rate on $1 of corporate pre-tax earnings distributed as a dividend is:

- **[A]** 40.0%

- **[B]** 45.0%

- **[C]** 50.0%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Effective Tax Rate = T_Corp + (1 − T_Corp) × T_Div = 0.25 + (1 − 0.25) × 0.20 = 0.25 + 0.15 = 0.400 = 40.0%.
