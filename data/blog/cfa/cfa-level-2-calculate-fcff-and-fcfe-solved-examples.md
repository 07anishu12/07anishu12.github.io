## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Four Equivalent Equations for Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF)

```formula
1. From NI: NI + NCC + Int(1−t) − FCInv − WCInv

2. From EBIT: EBIT(1−t) + Dep − FCInv − WCInv

3. From EBITDA: EBITDA(1−t) + (Dep × t) − FCInv − WCInv

4. From CFO: CFO + Int(1−t) − FCInv
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- Every equation yields the exact same dollar FCFF.

- note that in the EBITDA formula, only the depreciation tax shield (Dep × t) is added back!.

### Formula 2: FCFE from NI / FCFF & Target Debt Ratio (DR) Forecasting Formula

```formula
FCFE = FCFF − Int(1−t) + Net Borrowing

Forecast FCFE = NI − (1 − DR) × (FCInv − Dep) − (1 − DR) × WCInv
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** When a firm finances a constant fraction DR of its net new CapEx (FCInv − Dep) and working capital (WCInv) with debt, Net Borrowing = DR × (FCInv − Dep + WCInv).

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Verifying All Four FCFF Formulas and FCFE on a Single Corporate Dataset

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A company reports: Revenue = $1,000M, Cash Operating Expenses = $600M (so EBITDA = $400M), Depreciation (only NCC) = $80M (so EBIT = $320M), Interest Expense = $40M (so EBT = $280M), Tax Rate t = 25% (Taxes = $70M, Net Income = $210M). CapEx (FCInv) = $130M, Working Capital Investment (WCInv = ΔCA_op − ΔCL_op) = $30M, and Net New Debt Issued = $25M.

```formula
1. From NI: NI + NCC + Int(1−t) − FCInv − WCInv

2. From EBIT: EBIT(1−t) + Dep − FCInv − WCInv

3. From EBITDA: EBITDA(1−t) + (Dep × t) − FCInv − WCInv

4. From CFO: CFO + Int(1−t) − FCInv
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. CFO = NI ($210M) + Dep ($80M) − WCInv ($30M) = $260M.**

- **Step 2. FCFF from NI = $210M + $80M + $40M(0.75) − $130M − $30M = $160M.**

- **Step 3. FCFF from EBIT = $320M(0.75) + $80M − $130M − $30M = $240M + $80M − $160M = $160M.**

- **Step 4. FCFF from EBITDA = $400M(0.75) + $80M(0.25) − $130M − $30M = $300M + $20M − $160M = $160M.**

- **Step 5. FCFF from CFO = $260M + $40M(0.75) − $130M = $160M.**

- **Step 6. FCFE = FCFF ($160M) − Int(1−t) ($30M) + Net Borrowing ($25M) = $155M.**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> All four FCFF derivations converge to the exact same $160M figure.

### Worked Example 2: Forecasting Next-Year FCFE per Share Using a Target Debt-to-Assets Ratio (DR)

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
An analyst forecasts for next year: EPS (Net Income per share) = $5.00, Capital Expenditures per share = $3.20, Depreciation per share = $1.40, and Working Capital Increase per share = $0.60. The company funds all net new investments using a target Debt Ratio of DR = 35.0%.

```formula
FCFE = FCFF − Int(1−t) + Net Borrowing

Forecast FCFE = NI − (1 − DR) × (FCInv − Dep) − (1 − DR) × WCInv
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Net New Fixed Capital Investment (FCInv − Dep) = $3.20 − $1.40 = $1.80/share. Step 2: Total Net New Reinvestment = ($3.20 − $1.40) + $0.60 = $2.40/share. Step 3: Equity-funded portion (1 − DR = 65%) = 0.65 × $2.40 = $1.56/share. Step 4: Forecast FCFE = NI − (1 − DR)×(FCInv − Dep + WCInv) = $5.00 − $1.56 = $3.44 per share.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> The Target Debt Ratio formula eliminates the need to forecast balance sheet debt schedules separately when valuing stable-leverage firms.

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Non-Cash / Working Capital Item | Treatment in FCFF from Net Income | Sign / Adjustment | Analytical Trap to Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Depreciation & Amortization | Add back 100% (+Dep) | + NCC | In EBITDA formula, add only +(Dep × t) |
| Amortization of Bond Discount | Add back (+Amort) | + NCC | Non-cash interest expense |
| Deferred Tax Liability Increase | Add back (+ΔDTL if non-reversing) | + NCC | Exclude if reversing in near term |
| Cash & Short-Term Debt in WCInv | EXCLUDE from WCInv! | Ignored in WCInv | WCInv uses only operating CA and CL |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A company reports EBITDA of $500 million, Depreciation Expense of $100 million, Interest Expense of $50 million, a marginal tax rate of 25%, Capital Expenditures (FCInv) of $150 million, and an increase in Non-Cash Working Capital (WCInv) of $30 million. Its Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF) is:

- **[A]** $220 million

- **[B]** $295 million

- **[C]** $320 million

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Using the EBITDA formula: FCFF = EBITDA(1 − t) + (Dep × t) − FCInv − WCInv = $500M(0.75) + $100M(0.25) − $150M − $30M = $375M + $25M − $150M − $30M = $220 million.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** When calculating Working Capital Investment (WCInv = ΔWorking Capital) for FCFF and FCFE, which balance sheet accounts must ALWAYS be excluded from Current Assets and Current Liabilities?

- **[A]** Accounts Receivable and Accounts Payable

- **[B]** Cash and cash equivalents (from Current Assets) and Notes Payable / Short-Term Interest-Bearing Debt (from Current Liabilities)

- **[C]** Inventory and Accrued Expenses

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** WCInv measures only operating working capital: Cash is excluded from Current Assets, and short-term debt/current portion of long-term debt is excluded from Current Liabilities (since debt is captured in financing/Net Borrowing).

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** A firm forecasts next-year Net Income of $120 million, Capital Expenditures of $80 million, Depreciation of $30 million, and a Working Capital increase of $10 million. If the firm finances 40% of its net new investments with debt (DR = 0.40), its forecasted FCFE is:

- **[A]** $60 million

- **[B]** $84 million

- **[C]** $96 million

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Total net new investment = (FCInv − Dep) + WCInv = ($80M − $30M) + $10M = $60M. Equity share = (1 − 0.40) × $60M = $36M. FCFE = NI − $36M = $120M − $36M = $84 million.

## Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

- **Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Four Equivalent Equations for Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF).**

- **Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in FCFE from NI / FCFF & Target Debt Ratio (DR) Forecasting Formula.**

- **Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.**
