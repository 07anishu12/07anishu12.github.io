## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Diagnosing a 58% First Mock Score and Executing a 3-Week Remediation Plan to Reach 73%

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Four weeks before the CFA Level I exam, a candidate scores 58.3% (105 / 180) on Mock Exam #1 (missing 75 questions). A post-mock audit of the 75 missed questions reveals: 22 were Type 1 (Concept Gaps in Deferred Taxes, Duration, and Fra/Swaps); 28 were Type 2 (Forgot exact formula or divided by n instead of n−1); 17 were Type 3 (Misread 'Least Likely' or forgot to multiply semiannual YTM by 2); and 8 were Type 4 (Rushed in final 10 minutes).

```formula
For Every 4.5 Hours Spent Taking a Full Timed Mock Exam => Spend 6 to 9 Hours Debriefing Every Wrong Answer AND Every Flagged Guess!
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step Remediation Action Plan:**

- **Step 1. Fix Type 3 Misreads (17 Qs) immediately on Mock #2 by writing 'T/F' on stems and circling periodicity -> Recovers +13 points (+7.2%).**

- **Step 2. Fix Type 2 Formula Slips (28 Qs) by writing out a 4-page Master Formula Sheet every 3 days -> Recovers +18 points (+10.0%).**

- **Step 3. Fix Type 1 Concept Gaps (22 Qs) by doing five 30-question targeted Q-Bank drills on DTA/DTL, Duration, and Derivatives -> Recovers +12 points (+6.7%). Result on Mock #4 (1 week before exam): 105 + 13 − 15 (net) -> Score climbs from 58.3% to 74.4% (134 / 180)!**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> A 55%–60% score on your very first mock exam 4 weeks out is completely normal; systematic 4-bucket error remediation reliably lifts scores by +12 to +16 percentage points over 4 mocks.

### Worked Example 2: Simulating Exact Prometric Exam-Day Conditions on Mock Exams

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
List the exact simulation rules required when taking Mocks #1 through #5 so exam day feels routine.

```formula
Total Missed Questions = Type 1 (Concept/Knowledge Gap) + Type 2 (Formula/Memory Slip) + Type 3 (Misread Stem / Trap) + Type 4 (Time Pressure Rush)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. Start Session 1 at 8:30 AM sharp (no coffee/notes at desk, no phone, no pausing the clock!).**

- **Step 2. Use ONLY your permitted TI BA II Plus or HP 12C calculator and two blank sheets of scratch paper (or dry-erase board).**

- **Step 3. Take at most a 30-minute break between Session 1 (135m/132m) and Session 2 (135m/132m)—do NOT look up Session 1 answers during the break!**

- **Step 4. Flag any question taking >90 seconds (Level I) or >3 minutes (Level II), enter your best educated guess immediately, and return during your final 20-minute review buffer.**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Never take a mock exam 'open-book' with a formula sheet next to you—glancing at a formula sheet during a mock masks Type 2 memory gaps that will cost you points at the Prometric center.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** If a CFA candidate scores 57% on their first full-length timed mock exam taken 4 weeks before the exam window, what is the most productive next step?

- **[A]** Postpone the exam immediately because a 57% first mock cannot be improved

- **[B]** Perform a complete question-by-question error audit of all missed and guessed questions (classifying errors into concept gaps, formula slips, and stem misreads), drill weak sub-topics in the Q-Bank for 4–5 days, and then take Mock #2 closed-book

- **[C]** Take three more mock exams back-to-back on the next three days without reviewing the answers

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Scoring 55%–60% on Mock #1 four weeks out is common; thorough error-log classification and targeted Q-Bank remediation between mocks is what drives the score up to 70%+ by Mock #4.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Why should candidates NEVER keep a formula sheet open on their desk while taking a practice mock exam?

- **[A]** Because formula sheets are too long

- **[B]** Because peeking at a formula sheet during a mock exam artificially inflates the mock score and hides which specific formulas have not yet been committed to long-term memory for exam day

- **[C]** Because calculators contain all formulas

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Mock exams are diagnostic stress tests of closed-book recall and pacing; using a cheat sheet during a mock defeats its diagnostic purpose.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** During the final 48 hours before sitting for the CFA examination at the Prometric test center, how should a candidate spend their time?

- **[A]** Taking two full-length 4.5-hour mock exams the day before the real exam

- **[B]** Lightly reviewing the Master Formula Sheet, Ethical Standards summary, and personal Error Notebook, verifying calculator settings (P/Y = 1, AOS, 9 decimals) and fresh batteries, and getting a full night's sleep so cognitive stamina is at 100%

- **[C]** Learning brand-new curriculum readings for the first time

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Taking a grueling 4.5-hour mock the day before the exam causes mental fatigue; the final 24–48 hours should focus on light formula/Ethics review, logistics, and rest.
