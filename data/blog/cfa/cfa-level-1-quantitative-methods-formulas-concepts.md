## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, framework of time value of money, descriptive return distributions, probability trees, and statistical inference links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Quantitative Methods. Effective Annual Rate (EAR) (EAR = (1 + Stated Annual Rate / m)^m − 1) establishes the primary relationship (Converts nominal stated APR with m compounding periods per year into an effective annual compound yield.), while Coefficient of Variation & Roy's Safety-First (CV = s / R_bar ; SFRatio = [E(R_P) − R_L] / σ_P) governs CV measures dispersion per unit of mean return; SFRatio measures standard deviations above minimum threshold return R_L.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Comparing Institutional Deposit Compounding Frequencies

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Principal = $10,000,000; Bank A quotes 6.00% APR compounded monthly (m = 12); Bank B quotes 6.05% APR compounded semiannually (m = 2).

```formula
EAR = (1 + Stated Annual Rate / m)^m − 1
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

EAR_A = (1 + 0.06/12)^12 − 1 = 6.1678% ($616,778 interest); EAR_B = (1 + 0.0605/2)^2 − 1 = 6.1415% ($614,151 interest).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Bank A delivers 2.63 bps ($2,627) higher effective annual return due to monthly compounding.

### Worked Example 2: Shortfall Risk Evaluation via CV and Roy's Safety-First Ratio

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Portfolio X: E(R) = 9.5%, σ = 13.0%; Portfolio Y: E(R) = 7.8%, σ = 9.6%; Minimum threshold R_L = 3.0%.

```formula
CV = s / R_bar

SFRatio = [E(R_P) − R_L] / σ_P
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

CV_X = 13.0 / 9.5 = 1.3684; CV_Y = 9.6 / 7.8 = 1.2308; SFRatio_X = (9.5 − 3.0)/13.0 = 0.5000; SFRatio_Y = (7.8 − 3.0)/9.6 = 0.5000.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Portfolio Y offers lower volatility per unit of return (CV = 1.23 vs 1.37), while both portfolios share identical shortfall probability (SFRatio = 0.50).

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** An asset return distribution is unimodal with sample skewness of −0.85 and sample excess kurtosis of +2.40. Which statement is most accurate?

- **[A]** Mean exceeds median and tails are thinner than normal

- **[B]** Mean is less than median and extreme tail outcomes occur more frequently than normal

- **[C]** Total kurtosis equals 2.40 and mode is below mean

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Negative skewness (−0.85 < 0) pulls the mean below the median and mode (Mean < Median < Mode). Positive excess kurtosis (+2.40 > 0, total kurtosis = 5.40) indicates leptokurtic (fat) tails.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** A portfolio has an expected return of 11.2% and standard deviation of 16.0%. Against a minimum acceptable return of 4.0%, Roy's Safety-First Ratio is closest to:

- **[A]** 0.45

- **[B]** 0.70

- **[C]** 1.43

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** SFRatio = (11.2% − 4.0%) / 16.0% = 7.2% / 16.0% = 0.45.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** Across 25 monthly returns, the sum of squared deviations from the sample mean is 144.0 (% squared). The unbiased sample standard deviation is closest to:

- **[A]** 2.40%

- **[B]** 2.45%

- **[C]** 6.00%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Sample variance s^2 = 144.0 / (25 − 1) = 6.00 (% squared). Sample standard deviation s = √6.00 = 2.4495% ≈ 2.45%.
