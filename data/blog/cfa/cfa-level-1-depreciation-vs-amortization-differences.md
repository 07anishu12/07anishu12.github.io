## Introduction

Depreciation vs Amortization: Differences and CFA Questions is a core Level I Financial Statement Analysis topic (LM 6: Long-Lived Assets — Tangible vs intangible assets, finite vs indefinite life intangibles, R&D capitalization, impairment testing) focusing on tangible depreciation vs finite-life intangible amortization, indefinite-life goodwill impairment, and IFRS vs US GAAP R&D rules. This guide covers the governing equations (Finite-Life Intangible Amortization & IFRS Impairment and US GAAP Two-Step Tangible/Intangible Impairment), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, tangible depreciation vs finite-life intangible amortization, indefinite-life goodwill impairment, and IFRS vs US GAAP R&D rules links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Financial Statement Analysis. Finite-Life Intangible Amortization & IFRS Impairment (Annual Amortization = (Acquisition Cost − Residual Value) / Legal or Economic Life ; IFRS Impairment = Carrying Amount − Recoverable Amount) establishes the primary relationship (Recoverable Amount under IFRS (IAS 36) is max(Fair Value less Costs to Sell, Value in Use).), while US GAAP Two-Step Tangible/Intangible Impairment (Step 1 (Recoverability): Carrying Amount > Undiscounted Future Cash Flows ; Step 2 (Loss): Carrying Amount − Fair Value) governs US GAAP tests recoverability using undiscounted cash flows first, then measures the loss against Fair Value (with reversals prohibited).

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Finite-Life Intangible Amortization & IFRS Impairment

```formula
Annual Amortization = (Acquisition Cost − Residual Value) / Legal or Economic Life

IFRS Impairment = Carrying Amount − Recoverable Amount
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Recoverable Amount under IFRS (IAS 36) is max(Fair Value less Costs to Sell, Value in Use).

### Formula 2: US GAAP Two-Step Tangible/Intangible Impairment

```formula
Step 1 (Recoverability): Carrying Amount > Undiscounted Future Cash Flows

Step 2 (Loss): Carrying Amount − Fair Value
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** US GAAP tests recoverability using undiscounted cash flows first, then measures the loss against Fair Value (with reversals prohibited).

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Patent Amortization vs. Software R&D Capitalization under IFRS and US GAAP

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A biotech firm spends $12M on basic lab research and $18M on commercial drug development after meeting technical feasibility criteria on July 1. It also acquires a patent for $24M with a 12-year legal life and 8-year economic life.

```formula
Annual Amortization = (Acquisition Cost − Residual Value) / Legal or Economic Life

IFRS Impairment = Carrying Amount − Recoverable Amount
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Patent Amortization: Use shorter of legal (12 yrs) or economic (8 yrs) life = $24M / 8 = $3.0M/year (both IFRS & US GAAP). For R&D: Under US GAAP, all $30M ($12M + $18M) is expensed immediately. Under IFRS (IAS 38), $12M research is expensed and $18M development is capitalized on the balance sheet.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> IFRS operating income is $18M higher than US GAAP in the development year due to IAS 38 development capitalization.

### Worked Example 2: Impairment Loss Calculation under IFRS (IAS 36) vs. US GAAP (ASC 360)

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A patent/asset group has Carrying Amount = $100M, Undiscounted Future Cash Flows = $105M, Value in Use (PV of cash flows) = $88M, and Fair Value less Costs to Sell = $84M (Fair Value = $86M).

```formula
Step 1 (Recoverability): Carrying Amount > Undiscounted Future Cash Flows

Step 2 (Loss): Carrying Amount − Fair Value
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Under IFRS: Recoverable Amount = max($84M, $88M) = $88M. Since Carrying Amount ($100M) > $88M, IFRS Impairment Loss = $100M − $88M = $12M. Under US GAAP: Undiscounted Cash Flows ($105M) > Carrying Amount ($100M), so the asset passes the Step 1 recoverability test and US GAAP Impairment Loss = $0.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Because US GAAP uses an undiscounted cash flow screen in Step 1, an asset can be impaired by $12M under IFRS while recording zero impairment under US GAAP.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A company acquires a patent for $9.0 million. The patent has a remaining legal protection period of 15 years, but technological obsolescence will limit its economic usefulness to 6 years with zero residual value. Annual straight-line amortization expense is:

- **[A]** $0.60 million

- **[B]** $1.00 million

- **[C]** $1.50 million

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option C.**
>
> **Explanation:** Finite-life intangible assets are amortized over the shorter of their legal life (15 years) or useful economic life (6 years): $9.0M / 6 = $1.50 million per year.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Under US GAAP, which of the following software development costs is capitalized rather than expensed as incurred?

- **[A]** Costs incurred for software to be sold after technological feasibility is established

- **[B]** Research costs incurred prior to technological feasibility

- **[C]** Training costs for employees using newly installed ERP software

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Under US GAAP, software development costs for external sale are expensed until technological feasibility is established and capitalized thereafter (and internal-use software is capitalized during the application development stage).

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** An asset has a carrying amount of $50 million, undiscounted expected future cash flows of $46 million, Value in Use (discounted cash flows) of $41 million, and Fair Value (equal to Fair Value less costs to sell) of $39 million. The impairment loss under IFRS and US GAAP, respectively, is:

- **[A]** IFRS: $9 million; US GAAP: $11 million

- **[B]** IFRS: $11 million; US GAAP: $9 million

- **[C]** IFRS: $9 million; US GAAP: $4 million

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Under IFRS, Recoverable Amount = max(FV less costs to sell $39M, Value in Use $41M) = $41M, so Impairment = $50M − $41M = $9M. Under US GAAP, Carrying ($50M) > Undiscounted CF ($46M), so Impairment = Carrying ($50M) − Fair Value ($39M) = $11M.

## Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

- **Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Finite-Life Intangible Amortization & IFRS Impairment.**

- **Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in US GAAP Two-Step Tangible/Intangible Impairment.**

- **Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.**
