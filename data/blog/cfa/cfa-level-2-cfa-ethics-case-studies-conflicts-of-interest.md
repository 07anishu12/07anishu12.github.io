## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

### Formula 1: Cross-Departmental Information & Conflict Firewall Architecture

```formula
Physical & Electronic Separation between (1) Investment Banking / Corporate Finance and (2) Research & Asset Management

Compliance Officer Controls 'Over-the-Wall' Crossings
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** If a research analyst is brought 'over the wall' to assist IB on a confidential M&A deal, the analyst becomes an temporary insider and is prohibited from publishing research or sharing MNPI with Asset Management.

### Formula 2: Client-to-Client Cross-Trades & Principal Trades

```formula
Cross-Trade (Client A sells to Client B): Permitted ONLY if Suitable for Both, Executed at Independent Market Midpoint Price, and Disclosed

Principal Trade (Firm Inventory to Client): Requires Advance Written Disclosure & Client Consent
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Selling illiquid bonds from one client account to another (or from the firm's proprietary book) to bail out a favored fund violates Standard III(A), III(B), and VI(A).

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Case Study: Bringing an Equity Analyst 'Over the Wall' on a Confidential M&A Transaction

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Senior Biotech Analyst Nora is asked by her firm's Compliance Officer to cross the information barrier ('come over the wall') for 48 hours to advise the firm's Investment Banking team on valuing a confidential acquisition by Biotech Corp (a stock Nora currently rates 'Buy'). While over the wall, Nora learns that Biotech Corp is overpaying massively and that its Q3 sales collapsed. What must Nora do when thefirm's mutual fund manager calls her asking whether to buy more Biotech Corp?

```formula
Physical & Electronic Separation between (1) Investment Banking / Corporate Finance and (2) Research & Asset Management

Compliance Officer Controls 'Over-the-Wall' Crossings
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. While 'over the wall', Nora is in possession of Material Nonpublic Information (MNPI).**

- **Step 2. Biotech Corp must immediately be placed on the firm's Restricted List (suspending Nora's 'Buy' rating or freezing it to purely factual disclosures per compliance policy).**

- **Step 3. When the mutual fund manager calls, Nora MUST NOT reveal the MNPI or drop hints ('I wouldn't buy right now if I were you'); she must simply state what is in her last published public report or refer the manager to the Restricted List notice.**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Winking, nodding, or subtly warning a colleague away from a stock based on MNPI learned 'over the wall' is tipping and violates Standard II(A)!

### Worked Example 2: Case Study: Cross-Trading Illiquid Bonds Between a Hedge Fund and a Retail Mutual Fund

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A portfolio manager manages both a high-fee Hedge Fund (2/20 fee structure) and a retail Fixed-Income Mutual Fund (0.50% flat fee). When the Hedge Fund faces $20M in investor redemptions, the manager sells $20M of illiquid B-rated corporate bonds from the Hedge Fund directly to the retail Mutual Fund at yesterday's stale par mark of $100 (when current dealer bids are $92).

```formula
Cross-Trade (Client A sells to Client B): Permitted ONLY if Suitable for Both, Executed at Independent Market Midpoint Price, and Disclosed

Principal Trade (Firm Inventory to Client): Requires Advance Written Disclosure & Client Consent
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

This cross-trade violates three Standards simultaneously: (1) Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care (harming retail mutual fund shareholders by forcing them to overpay $100 for $92 bonds); (2) Standard III(B) Fair Dealing (favoring the high-fee Hedge Fund over the low-fee Mutual Fund); and (3) Standard VI(A) Conflicts of Interest (managing competing side-by-side accounts with differential incentive fees without independent pricing and compliance oversight).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Managing a 2/20 performance-fee hedge fund alongside a flat-fee mutual fund creates an inherent conflict of interest that must be disclosed under Standard VI(A) and monitored via strict trade allocation and pricing controls.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A portfolio manager oversees both a flat-fee institutional pension account and a performance-fee hedge fund in which the manager also invests his own personal capital. Under Standard VI(A) Avoid or Disclose Conflicts, the manager must:

- **[A]** Close the pension account immediately

- **[B]** Prominently disclose to the pension client the existence of the side-by-side hedge fund management and the incentive fee structure, and implement objective pro-rata trade allocation procedures so neither account is favored

- **[C]** Allocate all IPOs to the hedge fund

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Managing side-by-side accounts with different fee structures (flat fee vs performance fee) creates a material conflict of interest that must be disclosed to clients and controlled via systematic pro-rata trade allocation.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** An analyst serves (with her employer's written permission) on the board of directors of a publicly traded industrial company. During a confidential board meeting, she learns that the company will announce a major product recall next week. One hour later, her firm's growth fund manager asks her opinion on the company. The analyst should:

- **[A]** Tell the fund manager to sell the stock immediately without explaining why

- **[B]** Refrain from sharing the nonpublic recall information or changing her recommendation, and follow firm compliance procedures for restricted securities

- **[C]** Resign from her analyst job

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Communicating MNPI—even via a coded 'sell now' signal without details—violates Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information and her fiduciary duty to the board.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** When is a 'cross-trade' (transferring a security directly from Selling Client A's account to Buying Client B's account without routing through an external broker, saving brokerage commissions for both clients) permissible under the CFA Standards?

- **[A]** Never

- **[B]** Only when the security is suitable for Buying Client B's IPS, the sale is consistent with Selling Client A's IPS, the trade is executed at an independently verified current market midpoint price (favoring neither client), and full compliance/client disclosures are met

- **[C]** Whenever Selling Client A needs urgent cash and the security is illiquid

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Cross-trades that save transaction costs for both clients are permitted only when suitable for both parties, priced objectively at an independent market midpoint, and compliant with regulatory/disclosure rules.
