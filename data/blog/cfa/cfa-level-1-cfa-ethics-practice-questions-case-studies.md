## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A portfolio manager leaves Firm A to join Firm B. At Firm A, she was the sole decision-maker for a mid-cap equity strategy that beat the benchmark by 300 bps annually over 5 years. At Firm B, she wants to include that 5-year track record in her marketing brochure, and reconstructs the performance from publicly available mutual fund filings and Bloomberg data. Has she violated Standard III(D) Performance Presentation?

- **[A]** Yes, because past performance always stays with the previous employer and can never be cited

- **[B]** No, provided she was the sole decision-maker at Firm A, clearly discloses that the performance was achieved at her prior firm, and backs up the record with publicly available data without taking proprietary Firm A files

- **[C]** Yes, unless Firm A gives written consent

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** A manager may present a portable track record from a prior firm if she was the sole (or primary) decision-maker, clearly discloses that the record was earned at the prior firm, and supports it using public records without stealing confidential employer files.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Under the Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS), which of the following statements regarding independent third-party verification is accurate?

- **[A]** Verification is mandatory for any firm claiming GIPS compliance

- **[B]** Verification is voluntary, must be performed on a firm-wide basis (never on a single composite alone), and must be conducted by an independent third party

- **[C]** A firm can self-verify its own GIPS compliance

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** GIPS verification is voluntary (though strongly recommended), must be performed by an independent third party, and applies strictly to the entire firm—there is no such thing as partial or single-composite GIPS verification.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** An analyst visits a retail chain's flagship store as an ordinary shopper, notices that shelves are half-empty and checkout lines are nonexistent, and combines these observations with the retailer's public 10-Q inventory turnover ratios to issue a 'Sell' recommendation. This action:

- **[A]** Violates Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information

- **[B]** Is a proper application of the Mosaic Theory and complies with the Code and Standards

- **[C]** Violates Standard III(E) Confidentiality

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Observing store operations open to the public is non-material nonpublic (or public) information; combining it with public financial filings is the essence of the Mosaic Theory.
