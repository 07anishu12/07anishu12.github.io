## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, inflationary distortion of FIFO vs LIFO ratios, phantom inventory profits, LIFO layer liquidation, and NRV inventory write-downs links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Financial Statement Analysis. Inventory Valuation Lower of Cost or NRV (IFRS & Non-LIFO US GAAP) (Carrying Value = min(Historical Cost, Net Realizable Value) ; NRV = Selling Price − Completion & Selling Costs) establishes the primary relationship (Requires immediate income statement write-down if NRV falls below cost; IFRS permits reversal up to original write-down, US GAAP prohibits reversal.), while LIFO Liquidation Gross Margin Distortion (Phantom Gross Profit = Units Liquidated × (Current Replacement Cost − Historical Layer Cost)) governs Occurs when unit sales exceed current-period purchases under LIFO, dipping into old low-cost inventory layers and artificially inflating net income.

### Formula 1: Inventory Valuation Lower of Cost or NRV (IFRS & Non-LIFO US GAAP)

```formula
Carrying Value = min(Historical Cost, Net Realizable Value)

NRV = Selling Price − Completion & Selling Costs
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- Requires immediate income statement write-down if NRV falls below cost.

- IFRS permits reversal up to original write-down, US GAAP prohibits reversal.

### Formula 2: LIFO Liquidation Gross Margin Distortion

```formula
Phantom Gross Profit = Units Liquidated × (Current Replacement Cost − Historical Layer Cost)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Occurs when unit sales exceed current-period purchases under LIFO, dipping into old low-cost inventory layers and artificially inflating net income.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Comparing FIFO vs. LIFO Financial Ratios During Sustained Inflation

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A firm has Current Assets (excluding inventory) = $300M, Current Liabilities = $250M, Revenue = $1,000M. Under LIFO: Inventory = $150M, COGS = $700M. LIFO Reserve = $50M (so FIFO Inventory = $200M, and ΔLIFO Reserve = +$15M so FIFO COGS = $685M).

```formula
Carrying Value = min(Historical Cost, Net Realizable Value)

NRV = Selling Price − Completion & Selling Costs
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Current Ratio: LIFO = ($300M + $150M)/$250M = 1.80x vs. FIFO = ($300M + $200M)/$250M = 2.00x. Gross Margin: LIFO = ($1,000M − $700M)/$1,000M = 30.0% vs. FIFO = ($1,000M − $685M)/$1,000M = 31.5%. Inventory Turnover: LIFO = $700M / $150M = 4.67x vs. FIFO = $685M / $200M = 3.43x.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> During inflation, FIFO shows a stronger Current Ratio (2.00x vs 1.80x) and higher Gross Margin (31.5% vs 30.0%), but a lower Inventory Turnover (3.43x vs 4.67x) because FIFO Inventory is larger and FIFO COGS is smaller.

### Worked Example 2: Inventory Write-Down Under IFRS (NRV) vs. US GAAP LIFO (Market)

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
An item has Historical Cost = $100, Estimated Selling Price = $108, Selling Costs = $10, Normal Profit Margin = $12, and Replacement Cost = $92.

```formula
Phantom Gross Profit = Units Liquidated × (Current Replacement Cost − Historical Layer Cost)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Under IFRS (and US GAAP FIFO/WAC): NRV = $108 − $10 = $98. Since NRV ($98) < Cost ($100), write down by $2 to $98. Under US GAAP LIFO (Lower of Cost or Market): Ceiling (NRV) = $98; Floor (NRV − Normal Profit) = $98 − $12 = $86. Replacement Cost ($92) lies between Floor ($86) and Ceiling ($98), so Market = $92; write down by $8 to $92.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> US GAAP retains Lower of Cost or Market (LCM) only for LIFO and retail inventory methods, whereas FIFO/WAC and IFRS use Lower of Cost or NRV.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** In an environment of rising inventory unit costs and growing inventory quantities, compared to LIFO, a company using FIFO will report:

- **[A]** Higher inventory turnover and lower current ratio

- **[B]** Lower inventory turnover and higher current ratio

- **[C]** Higher cash flow from operations and lower income taxes

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Under inflation, FIFO yields lower COGS and higher Ending Inventory. Lower COGS divided by higher Inventory produces a lower Inventory Turnover ratio, while higher Inventory produces a higher Current Ratio.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** A LIFO-reporting manufacturer experiences a supply strike that reduces year-end inventory quantities into 10-year-old inventory layers. This LIFO liquidation during an inflationary period will most likely cause:

- **[A]** An unsustainable spike in reported gross profit margin and higher income tax outflows

- **[B]** A temporary reduction in reported net income

- **[C]** An increase in the LIFO reserve

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Liquidating old, low-cost LIFO layers matches outdated low costs against current high selling prices, creating a non-recurring jump in gross profit and higher cash tax payments.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** Under IFRS (IAS 2), if inventory previously written down by $15 million due to a drop in Net Realizable Value subsequently recovers $22 million in value, the company should:

- **[A]** Recognize no recovery because write-down reversals are prohibited

- **[B]** Reverse $15 million of the write-down by reducing COGS in the current period

- **[C]** Recognize a $22 million revaluation gain in Other Comprehensive Income

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** IFRS permits reversing an inventory write-down up to the amount of the original write-down ($15M), recognized as a reduction in COGS.
