## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Expanded CAPM vs. Build-Up Method for Private Cost of Equity (Expanded CAPM: r_e = R_F + (β × ERP) + Small_Stock_Premium + Company_Specific_Risk ; Build-Up: r_e = R_F + ERP + Small_Stock + Industry_Risk + Company_Specific (No Beta!)) and Multiplicative DLOC & DLOM Valuation Discount Formula (DLOC = 1 − [1 / (1 + Control Premium)] ; Total Discount = 1 − [(1 − DLOC) × (1 − DLOM)] ; Minority Non-Marketable Value = Control Value × (1 − DLOC) × (1 − DLOM)) govern normalizing private company earnings, Expanded CAPM vs Build-Up discount rates, the Excess Earnings Method (EEM), and applying multiplicative Discounts for Lack of Control (DLOC) and Lack of Marketability (DLOM).

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Expanded CAPM vs. Build-Up Method for Private Cost of Equity

```formula
Expanded CAPM: r_e = R_F + (β × ERP) + Small_Stock_Premium + Company_Specific_Risk

Build-Up: r_e = R_F + ERP + Small_Stock + Industry_Risk + Company_Specific (No Beta!)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** The Build-Up method omits beta (implicitly setting β = 1.0) when comparable public company betas are unavailable or unreliable.

### Formula 2: Multiplicative DLOC & DLOM Valuation Discount Formula

```formula
DLOC = 1 − [1 / (1 + Control Premium)]

Total Discount = 1 − [(1 − DLOC) × (1 − DLOM)]

Minority Non-Marketable Value = Control Value × (1 − DLOC) × (1 − DLOM)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- DLOC and DLOM are NEVER added linearly.

- they are always applied multiplicatively in sequence!.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Normalizing Private Company EBITDA/EBIT and Applying Multiplicative DLOC and DLOM

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A family-owned private manufacturer reports Pre-Tax Operating Income (EBIT) = $3,200,000. An audit reveals: (1) The founder-CEO paid himself $1,100,000 salary, whereas a market-rate replacement CEO costs $450,000; (2) $150,000 of family luxury auto leases were expensed in SG&A; and (3) the company pays $200,000/yr rent to a family trust for a warehouse whose fair market rent is $300,000/yr. Calculate Normalized EBIT, and if 100% Control Equity Value is $25,000,000, compute the value of a 15% minority non-marketable stake given a Control Premium of 25.0% and a DLOM of 20.0%.

```formula
Expanded CAPM: r_e = R_F + (β × ERP) + Small_Stock_Premium + Company_Specific_Risk

Build-Up: r_e = R_F + ERP + Small_Stock + Industry_Risk + Company_Specific (No Beta!)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1 (Normalized EBIT): $3,200,000 + ($1,100,000 − $450,000 excess CEO pay) + $150,000 (personal auto) − ($300,000 − $200,000 below-market rent adjustment) = $3,200,000 + $650,000 + $150,000 − $100,000 = $3,900,000. Step 2 (Convert 25% Control Premium into DLOC): DLOC = 1 − (1 / 1.25) = 1 − 0.80 = 20.0%. Step 3 (Combined Multiplicative Discount): Total Discount = 1 − [(1 − 0.20) × (1 − 0.20)] = 1 − 0.64 = 36.0% (NOT 20% + 20% = 40%!). Step 4 (Value of 15% Minority Non-Marketable Stake): 15% × $25,000,000 × (1 − 0.36) = $3,750,000 × 0.64 = $2,400,000.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Two favorite Level II private valuation tests in one example: normalizing both above-market owner perks and below-market related-party rent, and multiplying (1 − DLOC)(1 − DLOM).

### Worked Example 2: Valuing a Small Private Firm Using the Excess Earnings Method (EEM)

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A private engineering firm has Working Capital = $400,000 and Fixed Assets = $1,600,000, with Normalized Earnings = $420,000. Required returns are: Working Capital = 5.0%, Fixed Assets = 10.0%, and Intangible Assets = 20.0%, with Residual Income growing at g = 4.0% forever.

```formula
DLOC = 1 − [1 / (1 + Control Premium)]

Total Discount = 1 − [(1 − DLOC) × (1 − DLOM)]

Minority Non-Marketable Value = Control Value × (1 − DLOC) × (1 − DLOM)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Required Dollar Return on Tangible Assets = (5.0% × $400,000) + (10.0% × $1,600,000) = $20,000 + $160,000 = $180,000. Step 2: Excess Earnings attributable to Intangibles = $420,000 − $180,000 = $240,000. Step 3: Value of Intangible Assets (Growing Perpetuity) = [$240,000 × (1 + 0.04)] / (0.20 − 0.04) = $249,600 / 0.16 = $1,560,000. Step 4: Total Firm Value = Working Capital ($400k) + Fixed Assets ($1,600k) + Intangibles ($1,560k) = $3,560,000.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> The Excess Earnings Method (EEM) is specifically designed for small private businesses where tangible working capital and fixed assets are used to isolate intangible goodwill value.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** If comparable M&A transactions indicate an average Control Premium of 25.0%, and an appraiser estimates a Discount for Lack of Marketability (DLOM) of 15.0%, the total combined percentage discount applied to a 100%-control valuation to value a non-marketable minority interest is:

- **[A]** 32.0%

- **[B]** 36.25%

- **[C]** 40.0%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** First compute DLOC from the 25% Control Premium: DLOC = 1 − (1 / 1.25) = 20.0%. Then combine DLOC (20%) and DLOM (15%) multiplicatively: Total Discount = 1 − [(1 − 0.20) × (1 − 0.15)] = 1 − (0.80 × 0.85) = 1 − 0.68 = 0.320 = 32.0%.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** In the Excess Earnings Method (EEM) of private company valuation, how is the dollar amount of 'Excess Earnings' attributable to intangible assets calculated?

- **[A]** Normalized Total Earnings minus the sum of (Working Capital × Required Return on WC) and (Fixed Assets × Required Return on Fixed Assets)

- **[B]** EBITDA minus Capital Expenditures

- **[C]** Net Income minus WACC times Total Assets

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** EEM deducts the required dollar returns on Working Capital and Fixed Assets from Normalized Earnings; the remaining Excess Earnings are capitalized at (r_Intangibles − g) to value intangible assets.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** How does a 'Strategic Buyer' differ from a 'Financial Buyer' (such as a Private Equity LBO sponsor) when forecasting normalized cash flows to value a private acquisition target?

- **[A]** A strategic buyer incorporates buyer-specific operating and revenue synergies into the cash flow projections, whereas a financial buyer values the target on a standalone basis without operational synergies

- **[B]** A financial buyer always pays a higher multiple than a strategic buyer

- **[C]** A strategic buyer uses only the Build-Up method

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Strategic buyers (competitors/industry peers) evaluate investment value including synergistic cost savings and cross-selling, whereas financial buyers evaluate fair market standalone cash flows.
