## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Point & Midpoint (Arc) Price Elasticity of Demand

```formula
E_d_point = (%ΔQ_d) / (%ΔP) = (dQ_d / dP) × (P / Q_d)

E_d_arc = [(Q_1 − Q_0) / Avg Q] ÷ [(P_1 − P_0) / Avg P]
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- Measures percentage quantity response to a 1% price change.

- arc elasticity uses average P and average Q so elasticity is symmetric between two points.

### Formula 2: Income Elasticity (E_I) & Cross-Price Elasticity (E_XY)

```formula
E_I = (%ΔQ_d) / (%ΔIncome)

E_XY = (%ΔQ_X) / (%ΔP_Y)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- E_I < 0 signals an inferior good (E_I > 1 luxury).

- E_XY > 0 signals substitutes, while E_XY < 0 signals complements.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Calculating Arc Price Elasticity and Total Revenue Impact

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
When a streaming service raises its monthly price from P_0 = $18.00 to P_1 = $22.00, subscriber quantity falls from Q_0 = 550,000 to Q_1 = 450,000. Compute the arc price elasticity of demand and change in Total Revenue.

```formula
E_d_point = (%ΔQ_d) / (%ΔP) = (dQ_d / dP) × (P / Q_d)

E_d_arc = [(Q_1 − Q_0) / Avg Q] ÷ [(P_1 − P_0) / Avg P]
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Avg Q = (550,000 + 450,000) / 2 = 500,000; %ΔQ = (450,000 − 550,000) / 500,000 = −20.0%. Step 2: Avg P = ($18 + $22) / 2 = $20.00; %ΔP = ($22 − $18) / $20 = +20.0%. Step 3: Arc Elasticity E_d = −20.0% / +20.0% = −1.00 (unit elastic!). Step 4: Revenue_0 = $18 × 550,000 = $9,900,000; Revenue_1 = $22 × 450,000 = $9,900,000 (ΔTR = $0).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> At unit elasticity (|E_d| = 1.00), total revenue is maximized and remains unchanged ($9.90M) across the price change.

### Worked Example 2: Multi-Variable Demand Function: Point Price, Income, and Cross-Price Elasticities

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Demand for Good X is given by Q_X = 120 − 4P_X + 0.02I + 2P_Y, where P_X = $15, Consumer Income I = $3,000, and Competitor Price P_Y = $20.

```formula
E_I = (%ΔQ_d) / (%ΔIncome)

E_XY = (%ΔQ_X) / (%ΔP_Y)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Quantity Q_X = 120 − 4(15) + 0.02(3,000) + 2(20) = 120 − 60 + 60 + 40 = 160 units. Step 2: Point Own-Price Elasticity = (dQ_X / dP_X) × (P_X / Q_X) = (−4) × (15 / 160) = −0.375 (inelastic since |−0.375| < 1). Step 3: Income Elasticity = (+0.02) × (3,000 / 160) = +0.375 (normal necessity since 0 < E_I < 1). Step 4: Cross-Price Elasticity = (+2) × (20 / 160) = +0.250 (substitutes since E_XY > 0).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Because own-price demand is inelastic (|E_d| = 0.375 < 1), raising P_X above $15 will increase the firm's total revenue.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** The demand function for a product is Q_d = 200 − 5P. At a current price of P = $16, the point price elasticity of demand is closest to:

- **[A]** −0.67

- **[B]** −1.50

- **[C]** −5.00

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** At P = $16, Q_d = 200 − 5(16) = 120 units. Slope dQ_d / dP = −5. Point elasticity = (−5) × (16 / 120) = −80 / 120 = −0.6667 ≈ −0.67.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** If the cross-price elasticity of demand between Good A and Good B is −0.85, then Good A and Good B are:

- **[A]** Substitutes, and a 10% increase in the price of Good B decreases quantity demanded of Good A by 8.5%

- **[B]** Complements, and a 10% increase in the price of Good B decreases quantity demanded of Good A by 8.5%

- **[C]** Inferior goods

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** A negative cross-price elasticity (−0.85 < 0) means that an increase in the price of Good B reduces the quantity demanded of Good A, defining them as complements.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** When a company faces highly elastic demand (|E_d| = 2.4) at its current product price, how can it immediately increase its Total Revenue?

- **[A]** Increase the product price

- **[B]** Decrease the product price, because the percentage increase in quantity sold will exceed the percentage cut in price

- **[C]** Keep the price unchanged because marginal revenue is negative

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** When demand is elastic (|E_d| > 1), Marginal Revenue is positive and lowering price produces a proportionally larger surge in quantity demanded, raising Total Revenue.
