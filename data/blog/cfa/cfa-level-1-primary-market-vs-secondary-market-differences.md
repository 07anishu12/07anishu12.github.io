## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Calculating Net Corporate Proceeds in an Underwritten IPO vs. Best-Efforts Offering

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A technology firm issues 10,000,000 new shares in an Initial Public Offering (IPO) at an offer price of $24.00 per share ($240M gross). The investment bank charges a 5.50% underwriting spread, and legal/SEC filing fees total $2,800,000. On Day 1 of secondary trading on Nasdaq, the stock closes at $30.00.

```formula
Net Issuer Proceeds = Shares Issued × Offer Price × (1 − Underwriting Spread %)

Ex-Rights Price = (N × P_cum + S) / (N + 1)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Gross Primary Proceeds = 10,000,000 × $24.00 = $240,000,000. Underwriting fee = 5.50% × $240M = $13,200,000. Net Cash to Issuing Corporation = $240,000,000 − $13,200,000 − $2,800,000 = $224,000,000. The $60,000,000 Day-1 pop (($30 − $24) × 10M shares) accrues to IPO subscribers selling in the secondary market, NOT to the issuing company.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Cash flows to the issuing corporation ONLY in the primary market transaction ($224M net); subsequent secondary market trading transfers ownership among investors.

### Worked Example 2: Pricing a Primary Market Rights Offering and Value of One Right

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A company with 40M shares trading at $50.00 (cum-rights) raises capital via a 1-for-4 rights offering (N = 4 rights required to buy 1 new share) at a subscription price of S = $40.00.

```formula
Quoted Spread = Ask Price − Bid Price

Midpoint = (Bid + Ask) / 2
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

New shares issued = 40M / 4 = 10M shares; Gross proceeds = 10M × $40 = $400M. Theoretical Ex-Rights Price = [(4 × $50.00) + $40.00] / (4 + 1) = $240.00 / 5 = $48.00 per share. Value of 1 Right = Cum-Rights Price − Ex-Rights Price = $50.00 − $48.00 = $2.00 (verified as [$48.00 − $40.00] / 4 = $2.00).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Existing shareholders suffer zero wealth dilution whether they exercise their rights (4 shares @ $48 + 1 new share @ $48 − $40 cash = $200) or sell their 4 rights for $2.00 each (4 shares @ $48 + $8 cash = $200).

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** In which type of primary market offering does the investment bank guarantee the sale of the entire issue by purchasing the shares from the issuer at a discount and assuming inventory price risk?

- **[A]** Best-efforts offering

- **[B]** Underwritten (firm commitment) offering

- **[C]** Dutch auction registration

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** In an underwritten (firm commitment) offering, the investment bank buys the entire issue from the corporation and resells it to the public, bearing the risk of undersubscription.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** How does a liquid, well-functioning secondary market directly benefit corporations seeking to raise capital in the primary market?

- **[A]** Corporations receive a percentage royalty on every secondary market trade

- **[B]** High secondary market liquidity reduces the liquidity risk premium investors demand, lowering the issuer's cost of capital in future primary offerings

- **[C]** Secondary markets eliminate SEC disclosure requirements

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Investors willingly pay higher prices (demanding lower required returns) in primary offerings when they know they can exit positions cheaply in a liquid secondary market.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** A company's stock trades at $36.00 cum-rights. In a primary rights offering, existing shareholders receive 1 right per share owned, and 3 rights are required to purchase 1 new share at $28.00. The theoretical ex-rights share price is:

- **[A]** $32.00

- **[B]** $33.33

- **[C]** $34.00

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option C.**
>
> **Explanation:** Ex-Rights Price = [(3 × $36.00) + $28.00] / (3 + 1) = ($108.00 + $28.00) / 4 = $136.00 / 4 = $34.00.
