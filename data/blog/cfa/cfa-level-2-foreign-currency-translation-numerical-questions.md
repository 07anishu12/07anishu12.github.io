## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Current Rate Method (Functional Currency = Local Currency) (All Assets & Liabilities @ Current Year-End Spot Rate ; Common Stock @ Historical Rate ; Revenues/Expenses @ Avg Rate ; CTA in Equity (OCI)) and Temporal Method (Functional Currency = Parent Presentation Currency) (Monetary Assets/Liabilities (Cash, AR, AP, Debt) @ Current Rate ; Non-Monetary (Inventory, PP&E, Equity) @ Historical Rate ; COGS/Depr @ Historical Rate ; Remeasurement G/L in Income Statement (P&L)!) govern determining the Functional Currency, executing the Current Rate Method (when Functional = Local) vs the Temporal Method (when Functional = Parent), balance sheet exposure, and ratio distortions.

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Current Rate Method (Functional Currency = Local Currency)

```formula
All Assets & Liabilities @ Current Year-End Spot Rate

Common Stock @ Historical Rate

Revenues/Expenses @ Avg Rate

CTA in Equity (OCI)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Because all Assets and Liabilities are translated at the exact same year-end Current Rate, pure balance sheet ratios (Current Ratio, Debt-to-Assets, Debt-to-Equity) are PRESERVED!.

### Formula 2: Temporal Method (Functional Currency = Parent Presentation Currency)

```formula
Monetary Assets/Liabilities (Cash, AR, AP, Debt) @ Current Rate

Non-Monetary (Inventory, PP&E, Equity) @ Historical Rate

COGS/Depr @ Historical Rate

Remeasurement G/L in Income Statement (P&L)!
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Net Monetary Liability position (Monetary Liabilities > Monetary Assets) gains when the local currency depreciates and loses when the local currency appreciates.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Translating a Foreign Subsidiary Under the Current Rate vs. Temporal Method

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
US Parent (USD presentation currency) establishes Eurozone Sub on Jan 1 with €100M Capital, buying €100M Land/PP&E and borrowing €60M Long-Term Debt to hold €40M Cash + €20M Inventory (purchased when EUR/USD was $1.10/€). Exchange rates (USD per EUR): Historical (Jan 1) = $1.10; Average for year = $1.15; Year-End (Dec 31) = $1.20 (EUR appreciated!). Sub earned €20M Net Income (at avg rate = $23M under Current Rate) and paid €0 dividends.

```formula
All Assets & Liabilities @ Current Year-End Spot Rate

Common Stock @ Historical Rate

Revenues/Expenses @ Avg Rate

CTA in Equity (OCI)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. Current Rate Method: Ending Total Assets (€100M PP&E + €20M Inv + €60M Cash = €180M) × $1.20 Current Rate = $216.0M. Liabilities (€60M) × $1.20 = $72.0M. Common Stock (€100M) × $1.10 Hist = $110.0M; Retained Earnings = $23.0M. Plug Cumulative Translation Adjustment (CTA in OCI) = $216.0M − $72.0M − $110.0M − $23.0M = +$11.0M Positive CTA (because Net Assets > 0 and EUR appreciated!).**

- **Step 2. Temporal Method: Monetary Assets (€60M Cash) − Monetary Liabilities (€60M Debt) = €0 Net Monetary Exposure! Non-monetary PP&E (€100M) and Inventory (€20M) stay at Historical $1.10 ($132.0M).**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Under the Current Rate Method, Net Asset exposure is always Positive (Assets > Liabilities), so an appreciating foreign currency creates a POSITIVE CTA in Equity.

### Worked Example 2: Comparing Financial Ratios Under Current Rate vs. Temporal when Foreign Currency Appreciates

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
When the local foreign currency appreciates against the parent's currency during the year (Year-End Rate > Average Rate > Historical Rate), compare Fixed Asset Turnover and Inventory Turnover under Current Rate vs. Temporal.

```formula
Monetary Assets/Liabilities (Cash, AR, AP, Debt) @ Current Rate

Non-Monetary (Inventory, PP&E, Equity) @ Historical Rate

COGS/Depr @ Historical Rate

Remeasurement G/L in Income Statement (P&L)!
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Under the Temporal Method, Revenue is translated at the higher Average Rate while PP&E, Inventory, COGS, and Depreciation are locked at the lower Historical Rate! Consequently, Temporal Method reports HIGHER Gross Margin (lower historical COGS), HIGHER Fixed Asset Turnover (Avg Rate / Hist Rate), and HIGHER Inventory Turnover than the Current Rate Method.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Under the Current Rate Method, mixed Income Statement / Balance Sheet ratios (like ROA, ROE, Inventory Turnover = Avg Rate / Ending Rate) are LOWER than in local currency when the foreign currency appreciates (Ending Rate > Avg Rate).

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A US multinational has a wholly owned British subsidiary whose functional currency is the British pound (GBP, local currency), so the Current Rate Method applies. During the year, the GBP appreciates strongly against the US dollar (Year-End USD/GBP > Average USD/GBP > Beginning USD/GBP). Which of the following statements is most accurate?

- **[A]** The subsidiary will report a negative Cumulative Translation Adjustment (CTA) in shareholders' equity

- **[B]** The subsidiary's Current Ratio (Current Assets / Current Liabilities) in USD will be identical to its Current Ratio in GBP, while its Return on Ending Assets (Net Income / Ending Assets) will be lower in USD than in GBP

- **[C]** Translation gains will be recognized directly on the consolidated income statement

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Under the Current Rate Method, pure balance sheet ratios (CA/CL, D/E) are unchanged because numerator and denominator multiply by the same year-end rate. Mixed ratios (NI at Avg Rate / Ending Assets at higher Year-End Rate) are lower in USD than in GBP when the GBP appreciates.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** A foreign subsidiary is integrated as a sales distribution arm of its parent company (Functional Currency = Parent Presentation Currency, so the Temporal Method applies). The subsidiary has €50 million in Cash and Receivables and €120 million in Accounts Payable and Long-Term Debt (a Net Monetary Liability position of €70 million). If the Euro appreciates against the parent's currency, the parent's consolidated income statement will record a:

- **[A]** Remeasurement loss in net income, because more parent currency is now required to settle the net €70 million monetary liability

- **[B]** Remeasurement gain in net income

- **[C]** Cumulative Translation Adjustment in OCI

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Under the Temporal Method, holding a Net Monetary Liability position (Monetary Liabilities > Monetary Assets) in an appreciating foreign currency produces a remeasurement LOSS on the income statement.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** How do IFRS (IAS 29) and US GAAP differ when translating the financial statements of a foreign subsidiary operating in a highly inflationary (hyperinflationary) economy?

- **[A]** IFRS requires first restating non-monetary items for local general price-level inflation (GPI) and then translating all items at the current exchange rate; US GAAP requires switching the functional currency to the parent's currency and using the Temporal Method without inflation restatement

- **[B]** Both require the Current Rate Method without adjustment

- **[C]** IFRS uses the Temporal Method and US GAAP uses CPI restatement

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** This is a classic Level II distinction: in hyperinflation, US GAAP forces use of the Temporal Method (no price-index restatement), whereas IFRS restates local statements for CPI inflation first and then translates at the current closing rate.

## Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

- **Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Current Rate Method (Functional Currency = Local Currency).**

- **Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Temporal Method (Functional Currency = Parent Presentation Currency).**

- **Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.**
