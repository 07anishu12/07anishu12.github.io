## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Infrastructure Project Cash Flow & Concession Valuation

```formula
Project Equity Value = ∑_{t=1}^N [FCFE_t / (1 + k_e)^t] (Note: Terminal Value = $0 in BOT concessions that revert to government at Year N)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** In Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT) concessions, ownership reverts to the state at the end of the concession period N, so cash flows must amortize 100% of debt and equity before Year N.

### Formula 2: Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) in Project Finance

```formula
DSCR = Cash Flow Available for Debt Service (CFADS) / (Scheduled Principal + Interest)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Infrastructure lenders require strict minimum DSCR covenants (typically ≥ 1.25x–1.50x) on non-recourse project finance debt.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Comparing Risk, Cash Yield, and Target IRR: Brownfield Availability vs. Greenfield Demand Infrastructure

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Project Alpha is an operating hospital under a 25-year government Public-Private Partnership (PPP) paying inflation-indexed availability payments (Brownfield Social Infrastructure). Project Beta is a newly proposed high-speed toll railway yet to be constructed whose revenue depends on passenger ticket volume (Greenfield Economic Demand-Based Infrastructure).

```formula
Project Equity Value = ∑_{t=1}^N [FCFE_t / (1 + k_e)^t] (Note: Terminal Value = $0 in BOT concessions that revert to government at Year N)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Project Alpha (Brownfield / Availability): Zero construction risk and zero volume demand risk -> High current cash yield (6%–8%) and low target total IRR (7%–9%). Project Beta (Greenfield / Demand-Based): High construction cost-overrun risk, 4-year zero-cash-flow build phase, and GDP-sensitive ridership risk -> 0% initial cash yield and high required target IRR (12%–16%+).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Infrastructure does not have a single risk profile: Brownfield availability assets behave like inflation-linked investment-grade bonds, while Greenfield demand projects resemble private equity growth ventures.

### Worked Example 2: Calculating Project Finance Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) for a Regulated Utility

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A solar power generation project under a 20-year Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) produces Revenue = $42.0M, Operating & Maintenance (O&M) Costs = $8.0M, Cash Taxes = $3.0M, and Maintenance Reserve CapEx = $1.0M. Annual scheduled senior debt service is $12.0M Interest + $8.0M Principal = $20.0M.

```formula
DSCR = Cash Flow Available for Debt Service (CFADS) / (Scheduled Principal + Interest)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Cash Flow Available for Debt Service (CFADS) = $42.0M − $8.0M − $3.0M − $1.0M = $30.0M. Step 2: DSCR = $30.0M / $20.0M = 1.50x. Step 3: Residual FCFE available for equity dividend distribution = $30.0M − $20.0M = $10.0M.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Because the PPA locks in contracted power sales, the 1.50x DSCR comfortably clears the lender's 1.30x distribution lock-up covenant.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** Which type of infrastructure investment carries the LOWEST risk and generates the most stable, bond-like cash flows?

- **[A]** A greenfield merchant power plant selling electricity at spot wholesale prices

- **[B]** A brownfield social infrastructure asset (such as an existing public hospital or school) operating under a long-term government availability-payment contract

- **[C]** A greenfield toll bridge in a developing metropolitan area

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Brownfield social infrastructure eliminates both construction risk (already built) and market demand risk (paid a fixed inflation-linked fee by the government as long as the facility is available).

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** How do 'Greenfield' infrastructure investments differ from 'Brownfield' infrastructure investments?

- **[A]** Greenfield investments involve constructing new, unbuilt infrastructure assets from scratch (bearing construction, permitting, and ramp-up demand risks), whereas Brownfield investments acquire existing operational assets with established cash flow histories

- **[B]** Greenfield investments only invest in agricultural farmland

- **[C]** Brownfield investments have higher construction risk than Greenfield investments

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Greenfield = new development/construction phase (J-curve cash flows, higher risk/return); Brownfield = existing operating infrastructure (immediate cash yield, lower risk).

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** In a Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT) Public-Private Partnership concession, what happens to the physical infrastructure asset at the end of the contracted concession period (e.g., after 30 years)?

- **[A]** The private investor sells it on the stock exchange in perpetuity

- **[B]** Ownership of the asset reverts (transfers back) to the government authority, typically for zero or nominal consideration

- **[C]** The asset is demolished

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Under a BOT structure, the private consortium builds and operates the facility to recover its capital and earn a return over the concession term, after which ownership transfers to the government.
