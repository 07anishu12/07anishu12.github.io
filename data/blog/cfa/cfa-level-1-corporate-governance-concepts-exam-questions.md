### Formula 1: Executive Compensation Alignment & Agency Cost

```formula
Total Agency Cost = Monitoring Costs + Bonding Costs + Residual Loss
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Quantifies the value lost when managers (agents) pursue empire building or excessive perquisites instead of maximizing owner (principal) value.

### Formula 2: Dual-Class Voting Disproportionality Ratio

```formula
Voting Power % / Economic Cash-Flow Ownership %
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** A wide wedge between insider voting control (e.g., 10 votes/share) and economic ownership creates Controlling- vs. Minority-Shareholder agency risk.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Calculating Voting vs. Economic Ownership in a Dual-Class Share Structure

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Founders hold 10M Class B super-voting shares (10 votes per share = 100M votes), while public investors hold 90M Class A shares (1 vote per share = 90M votes). Both share classes receive identical $1.00 dividends.

```formula
Total Agency Cost = Monitoring Costs + Bonding Costs + Residual Loss
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Founders' Economic Cash-Flow Ownership = 10M / (10M + 90M) = 10.0%. Step 2: Founders' Voting Control = 100M / (100M + 90M) = 100M / 190M = 52.63% (> 50% absolute majority!).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> With only 10% of the economic equity at risk, the founders control 52.63% of all shareholder votes, creating a severe Controlling vs. Minority Shareholder conflict.

### Worked Example 2: Evaluating Board Independence and Core Committee Mandates

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Company Alpha's Audit Committee is chaired by the company's former CFO (retired 6 months ago) and includes the CEO. Company Beta's Audit, Remuneration (Compensation), and Nomination Committees are composed 100% of independent non-executive directors, and the roles of CEO and Board Chair are separated.

```formula
Voting Power % / Economic Cash-Flow Ownership %
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Company Alpha exhibits severe governance red flags: having the CEO and recently retired CFO on the Audit Committee compromises external auditor oversight and financial reporting integrity. Company Beta follows global best practice, justifying a lower governance risk premium in its WACC.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> CFA Institute governance standards emphasize that the Audit, Compensation, and Nominating/Governance committees should consist exclusively of independent directors.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** In a two-tier corporate board structure (commonly used in Germany and continental Europe), the 'Supervisory Board' is composed of:

- **[A]** Executive officers who manage daily operations

- **[B]** Non-executive directors (often including employee/labor representatives) who appoint, oversee, and can remove members of the Management Board

- **[C]** External bondholders only

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** A two-tier board strictly separates the Management Board (executives running operations) from the Supervisory Board (non-executives overseeing management).

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Which board committee is directly responsible for appointing, compensating, and overseeing the external independent financial statement auditors?

- **[A]** Compensation (Remuneration) Committee

- **[B]** Audit Committee

- **[C]** Risk Committee

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** The Audit Committee oversees financial reporting integrity, internal controls, and the external independent audit firm, and should consist solely of independent directors with financial expertise.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** A corporate takeover defense that grants existing shareholders (other than the hostile acquirer) the right to buy additional shares at a steep discount if a hostile bidder crosses an ownership threshold is called a:

- **[A]** Golden parachute

- **[B]** Poison pill (shareholder rights plan)

- **[C]** Greenmail repurchase

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** A poison pill (flip-in/flip-over shareholder rights plan) massively dilutes the hostile bidder's stake, forcing the acquirer to negotiate directly with the target's board.
