## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Required vs. Recommended (Best Practice) Distinction in CFA Guidance

```formula
'MUST' / 'SHALL' = Mandatory Requirement of the Standard (Failure is a Violation)

'SHOULD' / 'RECOMMENDED' = Best Practice Procedure for Compliance (Failure Alone Is Not Automatically a Violation!)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Level II Ethics questions frequently ask: 'To avoid violating Standard X, is the member REQUIRED to do Y?' Watch whether Y is a mandatory rule ('must') or merely a recommended best-practice procedure ('should')!.

### Formula 2: Proxy Voting & Mixed-Use Soft-Dollar Rules (Standard III(A))

```formula
Proxy Voting: Proxies Have Economic Value -> Vote in Best Interest of Clients (Cost-benefit analysis allowed

disclose proxy policy)

Mixed-Use Soft Dollars (e.g., 60% Research / 40% Admin): Pay 60% with Soft Dollars and 40% with Firm Cash!
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** When a software package serves both investment research (60%) and back-office accounting/administration (40%), the firm may use client soft dollars ONLY for the 60% research portion and must pay the 40% administrative portion with its own hard dollars.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Case Study: Mixed-Use Soft Dollars and Proxy Voting Under Standard III(A)

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Case 1: An asset manager purchases a $100,000 portfolio analytics software suite. Time-tracking logs show the suite is used 70% by equity analysts for security valuation and 30% by the operations team for client billing and fee invoicing. Case 2: A global index manager holds a tiny 500-share position in a foreign issuer whose proxy ballot requires costly notarized physical translation in person to vote on a routine auditor ratification.

```formula
'MUST' / 'SHALL' = Mandatory Requirement of the Standard (Failure is a Violation)

'SHOULD' / 'RECOMMENDED' = Best Practice Procedure for Compliance (Failure Alone Is Not Automatically a Violation!)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Case 1 (Mixed-Use Soft Dollars): The firm may pay at most 70% ($70,000) using client soft-dollar brokerage commissions and MUST pay the remaining 30% ($30,000 billing/admin share) out of the firm's own hard dollars! Case 2 (Proxy Voting Cost-Benefit): Under Standard III(A), voting proxies is part of fiduciary duty, but managers do NOT have to vote every single proxy regardless of cost! If a cost-benefit analysis shows that voting a routine foreign proxy costs more than the benefit to the client, abstaining in alignment with a disclosed proxy voting policy complies with Standard III(A).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Both mixed-use soft-dollar proration and cost-benefit proxy voting are classic Level II nuances tested under Standard III(A).

### Worked Example 2: Case Study: Distinguishing Mandatory Violations from Recommended Best Practices

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Compare two situations: (1) Analyst A does not adopt a personal trade pre-clearance window of 5 days because his small 3-person boutique has no formal blackout rule, but he always waits until 48 hours after clients have traded before placing personal trades. (2) Analyst B obtains verbal consent (instead of written consent) from her employer for a client performance bonus.

```formula
Proxy Voting: Proxies Have Economic Value -> Vote in Best Interest of Clients (Cost-benefit analysis allowed

disclose proxy policy)

Mixed-Use Soft Dollars (e.g., 60% Research / 40% Admin): Pay 60% with Soft Dollars and 40% with Firm Cash!
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Situation 1: Having a formal 5-day pre-clearance blackout policy is a RECOMMENDED compliance procedure ('should'), not a strict mandatory element if client priority is fully respected -> Analyst A did NOT violate Standard VI(B). Situation 2: Written consent from all parties for additional compensation is an explicit MANDATORY requirement in the text of Standard IV(B) -> Analyst B VIOLATED Standard IV(B)!

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Always check whether a compliance procedure (like a specific number of blackout days or a written firewall manual) is a recommended firm-level best practice vs. a personal mandatory duty under the Standards.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** An investment management firm purchases a $50,000 financial software system that is used 60% for quantitative portfolio research and 40% for back-office trade accounting and client fee billing. Under Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care (and the CFA Institute Soft Dollar Standards), how may the firm pay for this mixed-use software?

- **[A]** Pay 100% ($50,000) using client soft-dollar brokerage commissions because its primary use (>50%) is research

- **[B]** Make a reasonable allocation based on usage—paying up to 60% ($30,000) with client soft-dollar commissions and paying the remaining 40% ($20,000) with the firm's own cash (hard dollars)

- **[C]** Soft dollars can never be used for software

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** For mixed-use goods/services, the firm must prorate the cost between research (eligible for soft dollars) and administration (paid with firm hard dollars).

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Under Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care, which statement regarding proxy voting on behalf of discretionary client accounts is most accurate?

- **[A]** Managers must vote every single proxy ballot on every issue regardless of cost

- **[B]** Proxies have economic value and must be voted responsibly in the best interest of clients, but a manager may refrain from voting routine proxies when a cost-benefit analysis indicates that voting would not benefit the client (provided the firm's proxy voting policy is disclosed to clients)

- **[C]** Managers should always vote in favor of corporate management's recommendations

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** While proxies are client property with economic value, Standard III(A) permits a cost-benefit approach and does not mandate voting every ballot when administrative/translation costs outweigh client benefit.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** A small 4-person investment advisory firm does not maintain a written employee trade pre-clearance form, but all 4 employees strictly execute personal trades only after all client orders have been filled and clients have had 3 business days to act on recommendations. Have the employees violated Standard VI(B) Priority of Transactions?

- **[A]** Yes, because written pre-clearance forms are mandatory for all individuals

- **[B]** No, because the employees placed client interests first and traded only after clients had ample opportunity to act; formal pre-clearance forms are a recommended compliance procedure rather than a mandatory requirement for finding a personal violation

- **[C]** Yes, because employees can never buy stocks held by clients

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Formal pre-clearance paperwork is a recommended compliance procedure; as long as client and employer transactions received strict priority and clients were not disadvantaged, Standard VI(B) was not violated.
