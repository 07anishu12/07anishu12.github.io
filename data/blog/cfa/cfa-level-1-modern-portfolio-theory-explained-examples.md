## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, Markowitz mean-variance dominance criteria, the investment opportunity set, Tobin's Two-Fund Separation Theorem, and assumptions of Modern Portfolio Theory links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Portfolio Management. Mean-Variance Dominance Criterion (Portfolio A dominates B if: [E(R_A) ≥ E(R_B) and σ_A < σ_B] OR [E(R_A) > E(R_B) and σ_A ≤ σ_B]) establishes the primary relationship (Rational risk-averse investors eliminate any dominated portfolio lying below the Global Minimum Variance Portfolio on the minimum-variance frontier.), while Capital Market Line (CML) Equation (E(R_P) = R_F + [(E(R_M) − R_F) / σ_M] × σ_P) governs Describes the linear risk-return trade-off for efficient portfolios combining the risk-free asset with the market portfolio M (only holds for efficient portfolios, not individual stocks).

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Mean-Variance Dominance Criterion

```formula
Portfolio A dominates B if: [E(R_A) ≥ E(R_B) and σ_A < σ_B] OR [E(R_A) > E(R_B) and σ_A ≤ σ_B]
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Rational risk-averse investors eliminate any dominated portfolio lying below the Global Minimum Variance Portfolio on the minimum-variance frontier.

### Formula 2: Capital Market Line (CML) Equation

```formula
E(R_P) = R_F + [(E(R_M) − R_F) / σ_M] × σ_P
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Describes the linear risk-return trade-off for efficient portfolios combining the risk-free asset with the market portfolio M (only holds for efficient portfolios, not individual stocks).

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Applying Mean-Variance Dominance to Screen Four Candidate Portfolios

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
An investment committee reviews 4 portfolios: Portfolio W (E(R) = 9.0%, σ = 14.0%); Portfolio X (E(R) = 9.0%, σ = 11.5%); Portfolio Y (E(R) = 11.2%, σ = 14.0%); Portfolio Z (E(R) = 12.5%, σ = 18.0%). Identify which portfolio is strictly mean-variance dominated.

```formula
Portfolio A dominates B if: [E(R_A) ≥ E(R_B) and σ_A < σ_B] OR [E(R_A) > E(R_B) and σ_A ≤ σ_B]
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Portfolio W (9.0%, 14.0%) is strictly dominated by BOTH Portfolio X (same 9.0% return at lower 11.5% risk) AND Portfolio Y (higher 11.2% return at the exact same 14.0% risk). Portfolios X, Y, and Z do not dominate one another and can all lie on the Markowitz efficient frontier.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Between X, Y, and Z, the investor's specific risk aversion coefficient (A) determines which efficient portfolio maximizes utility U.

### Worked Example 2: Tobin's Two-Fund Separation Theorem in Practice

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Client Conservative (A = 6) and Client Aggressive (A = 2) visit the same wealth manager. The risk-free rate is 4.0%, and the Tangency Market Portfolio M has E(R_M) = 10.0% and σ_M = 15.0%.

```formula
E(R_P) = R_F + [(E(R_M) − R_F) / σ_M] × σ_P
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1 (Investment Decision — Identical for Both Clients): Both clients hold the exact same optimal risky portfolio—the Market Portfolio M (which maximizes the Sharpe ratio at (10−4)/15 = 0.40). Step 2 (Financing Decision — Tailored to Risk Aversion): Client Conservative puts 50% in T-Bills and 50% in Fund M (E(R) = 7.0%, σ = 7.5%). Client Aggressive borrows 20% at R_F to invest 120% in Fund M (E(R) = 11.2%, σ = 18.0%).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> By the Two-Fund Separation Theorem, the composition of the optimal risky portfolio M is completely independent of the investor's risk preferences!

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** According to the Two-Fund Separation Theorem in Modern Portfolio Theory, how do a highly risk-averse investor and a moderately risk-averse investor differ in their optimal portfolio construction (assuming homogeneous expectations)?

- **[A]** The highly risk-averse investor holds a risky portfolio of low-beta utility stocks while the less risk-averse investor holds technology stocks

- **[B]** Both investors hold the exact same optimal risky tangency portfolio (Market Portfolio M), but allocate different weights between the risk-free asset and Portfolio M

- **[C]** Only the aggressive investor lies on the Capital Market Line

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Under Two-Fund Separation, every investor holds the same tangency risky portfolio M (the investment step) and adjusts risk solely by varying the lending/borrowing mix with the risk-free asset (the financing step).

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Which of the following is a core assumption of Markowitz Modern Portfolio Theory and the Capital Asset Pricing Model?

- **[A]** Investors care only about the mean and variance of one-period portfolio returns, and markets are frictionless with no taxes or transaction costs

- **[B]** Investors have heterogeneous expectations regarding asset returns

- **[C]** Investors can borrow unlimited amounts at a rate higher than the risk-free lending rate

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** MPT and CAPM assume single-period mean-variance utility maximizers, homogeneous expectations, frictionless markets, and unlimited borrowing and lending at a single risk-free rate R_F.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** What happens to the slope of the Capital Market Line (CML) above the Market Portfolio M if investors can lend at the risk-free rate R_F (4.0%) but must pay a higher borrowing rate R_B (6.5%) to buy on margin?

- **[A]** The CML remains a single straight line

- **[B]** The efficient frontier becomes kinked at M: it follows the straight lending line from R_F to M, curves along the risky efficient frontier from M to a higher borrowing tangency portfolio M_B, and then follows a flatter straight line above M_B

- **[C]** Leverage becomes impossible

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** When R_B > R_F, the borrowing line has a flatter slope than the lending line, creating a curved segment along the Markowitz risky frontier between the lending tangency portfolio M_L and borrowing tangency portfolio M_B.

## Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

- **Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Mean-Variance Dominance Criterion.**

- **Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Capital Market Line (CML) Equation.**

- **Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.**
