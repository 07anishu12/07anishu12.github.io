## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, solving linear supply-and-demand equilibrium systems, marginal and average cost curve geometry, short-run shutdown vs breakeven thresholds, and long-run minimum efficient scale links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Economics. Market Equilibrium & Excess Supply/Demand (Q_d(P*) = Q_s(P*) ; Excess Supply = Q_s(P_floor) − Q_d(P_floor) for P_floor > P*) establishes the primary relationship (Equates quantity demanded and quantity supplied at market-clearing price P*; binding price floors above P* create surpluses and binding ceilings below P* create shortages.), while Cost Identities & Breakeven / Shutdown Rules (TC = TFC + TVC ; ATC = AFC + AVC ; Breakeven: P = min(ATC) ; Short-Run Shutdown: P < min(AVC)) governs Marginal Cost (MC) intersects both AVC and ATC at their exact minimum points.

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Market Equilibrium & Excess Supply/Demand

```formula
Q_d(P*) = Q_s(P*)

Excess Supply = Q_s(P_floor) − Q_d(P_floor) for P_floor > P*
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- Equates quantity demanded and quantity supplied at market-clearing price P*.

- binding price floors above P* create surpluses and binding ceilings below P* create shortages.

### Formula 2: Cost Identities & Breakeven / Shutdown Rules

```formula
TC = TFC + TVC

ATC = AFC + AVC

Breakeven: P = min(ATC)

Short-Run Shutdown: P < min(AVC)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Marginal Cost (MC) intersects both AVC and ATC at their exact minimum points.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Solving for Market Equilibrium and Binding Price Ceiling Shortage

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Market demand is Q_d = 1,400 − 15P and market supply is Q_s = 200 + 25P. (1) Solve for equilibrium price P* and quantity Q*. (2) If the government imposes a price ceiling of P_c = $24, calculate the resulting market shortage.

```formula
Q_d(P*) = Q_s(P*)

Excess Supply = Q_s(P_floor) − Q_d(P_floor) for P_floor > P*
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Set Q_d = Q_s => 1,400 − 15P* = 200 + 25P* => 1,200 = 40P* => Equilibrium Price P* = $30.00; Equilibrium Quantity Q* = 200 + 25(30) = 950 units. Step 2: At binding ceiling P_c = $24 (< $30): Q_d(24) = 1,400 − 15(24) = 1,040 units; Q_s(24) = 200 + 25(24) = 800 units. Shortage (Excess Demand) = 1,040 − 800 = 240 units.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> A price ceiling set below equilibrium ($24 < $30) is binding and creates an immediate 240-unit shortage.

### Worked Example 2: Analyzing Short-Run Firm Cost Curves and Operating Decisions

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A competitive firm produces Q = 500 units with Total Fixed Cost (TFC) = $4,000 and Total Variable Cost (TVC) = $11,000 (minimum AVC is $20.00). Market price is P = $26.00.

```formula
TC = TFC + TVC

ATC = AFC + AVC

Breakeven: P = min(ATC)

Short-Run Shutdown: P < min(AVC)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

ATC = ($4,000 + $11,000) / 500 = $30.00; AVC = $11,000 / 500 = $22.00; Total Revenue = 500 × $26 = $13,000; Economic Profit = $13,000 − $15,000 = −$2,000 loss. However, because P ($26) > AVC ($22), continuing to operate in the short run generates $2,000 of contribution toward fixed costs (losing −$2,000 instead of −$4,000 TFC if shut down). In the long run, the firm will exit if P stays below $30.00.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Whenever AVC < P < ATC, the firm operates in the short run to minimize losses but exits in the long run.

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Market Price Regime (Perfect Competition) | Price Relationship to Cost Curves | Short-Run Decision | Long-Run Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Above Breakeven | P > ATC | Operate at MR = MC (Profit > 0) | Stay / New firms enter industry |
| At Breakeven Point | P = minimum ATC | Operate at MR = MC (Normal profit = 0) | Stay in long-run equilibrium |
| Between Shutdown & Breakeven | min(AVC) ≤ P < min(ATC) | Operate to cover TVC + part of TFC | Exit industry when leases expire |
| Below Shutdown Point | P < min(AVC) | Shut down immediately (Q = 0; Loss = TFC) | Exit industry permanently |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** Where does a firm's Marginal Cost (MC) curve intersect its Average Variable Cost (AVC) and Average Total Cost (ATC) curves?

- **[A]** At the maximum points of AVC and ATC

- **[B]** At the exact minimum points of both AVC and ATC

- **[C]** Where Average Fixed Cost (AFC) equals zero

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Whenever MC is below AVC (or ATC), the average is pulled downward; whenever MC is above AVC (or ATC), the average is pulled upward. Therefore, MC intersects both AVC and ATC at their minimum points.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** A manufacturing firm has Total Fixed Costs of $50,000 and produces 2,000 units with Total Variable Costs of $90,000. If the market price is $42.00 per unit, in the short run the firm should:

- **[A]** Continue producing because price exceeds average fixed cost ($25.00)

- **[B]** Shut down production immediately because price ($42.00) is below average variable cost ($45.00)

- **[C]** Continue producing to minimize fixed cost losses

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** AVC = $90,000 / 2,000 = $45.00. Because Price ($42.00) < AVC ($45.00), producing loses $50,000 + 2,000($3) = $56,000, whereas shutting down limits the loss to $50,000 TFC.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** As a firm expands its plant size along its Long-Run Average Total Cost (LRATC) curve, the downward-sloping portion of LRATC reflects:

- **[A]** Diminishing marginal product of labor in the short run

- **[B]** Economies of scale (increasing returns to scale) lowering per-unit cost up to Minimum Efficient Scale

- **[C]** Diseconomies of scale from bureaucratic complexity

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** The downward-sloping segment of LRATC is driven by economies of scale (specialization, bulk purchasing, capital efficiency) until reaching Minimum Efficient Scale.
