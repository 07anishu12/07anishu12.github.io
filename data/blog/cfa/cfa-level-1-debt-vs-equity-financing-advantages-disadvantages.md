## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Modigliani-Miller Proposition II (Without Taxes vs. With Corporate Taxes)

```formula
No Taxes: r_e = r_0 + (r_0 − r_d) × (D / E)

With Taxes: r_e = r_0 + (r_0 − r_d) × (1 − t) × (D / E)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- Cost of equity r_e rises linearly with the Debt-to-Equity ratio (D/E).

- with taxes, the slope is dampened by (1 − t) and WACC declines.

### Formula 2: Modigliani-Miller WACC with Corporate Taxes

```formula
WACC_L = r_0 × [1 − t × (D / V)]
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Shows how replacing equity with tax-deductible debt reduces WACC below the unlevered cost of capital r_0.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Applying Modigliani-Miller Proposition II With and Without Corporate Taxes

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
An unlevered firm has an all-equity cost of capital of r_0 = 10.0%. It recapitalizes to a Debt-to-Equity ratio of D/E = 0.50 (meaning D/V = 1/3 and E/V = 2/3) by issuing risk-free debt at r_d = 5.0% and repurchasing shares. Compare its new Cost of Equity (r_e) and WACC under (A) Zero Taxes (t = 0%) vs. (B) 25% Corporate Tax Rate (t = 25%).

```formula
No Taxes: r_e = r_0 + (r_0 − r_d) × (D / E)

With Taxes: r_e = r_0 + (r_0 − r_d) × (1 − t) × (D / E)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Case A (Zero Taxes, t = 0%): r_e = 10.0% + (10.0% − 5.0%) × 0.50 = 12.50%. WACC = (2/3)(12.50%) + (1/3)(5.00%) = 8.333% + 1.667% = 10.00% (= r_0, constant!). Case B (With Taxes, t = 25%): r_e = 10.0% + (10.0% − 5.0%) × (1 − 0.25) × 0.50 = 10.0% + 1.875% = 11.875%. After-tax r_d = 5.0% × 0.75 = 3.75%. WACC = (2/3)(11.875%) + (1/3)(3.75%) = 7.9167% + 1.2500% = 9.1667% ≈ 9.17% (< 10.00%!).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Without taxes, cheap debt is 100% offset by higher r_e so WACC stays at 10.00%; with a 25% tax shield, r_e rises less (to 11.88%) and WACC falls to 9.17%.

### Worked Example 2: Pecking Order Theory and Asymmetric Information Signaling

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Management of a biotech company knows its true intrinsic share value is $80, while the market currently prices the stock at $60. Management needs $100M to fund a high-NPV trial.

```formula
WACC_L = r_0 × [1 − t × (D / V)]
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Under Myers & Majluf's Pecking Order Theory, issuing equity at $60 transfers wealth from existing shareholders to new buyers and signals to the market that management believes the stock might be overvalued (triggering an immediate stock price drop). Therefore, management strongly prefers: (1) Internal Retained Cash Flow first, (2) Debt second, and (3) External Equity only as a last resort.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Because insiders possess superior information (information asymmetry), announcing a secondary equity offering is interpreted as a negative signal by market participants.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** According to Modigliani and Miller Proposition II WITH corporate income taxes (assuming no financial distress costs), an unlevered firm has an all-equity cost of capital (r_0) of 11.0%. If it moves to a Debt-to-Equity ratio of 0.60 by issuing debt at a pre-tax cost of 6.0% under a 25% tax rate, its new levered cost of equity (r_e) is:

- **[A]** 13.25%

- **[B]** 14.00%

- **[C]** 14.60%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** r_e = r_0 + (r_0 − r_d) × (1 − t) × (D / E) = 11.0% + (11.0% − 6.0%) × (1 − 0.25) × 0.60 = 11.0% + (5.0% × 0.75 × 0.60) = 11.0% + 2.25% = 13.25%. (Option B ignores the tax adjustment).

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Under the Pecking Order Theory of capital structure, which financing source do corporate managers prefer FIRST and LAST, respectively?

- **[A]** First: Debt; Last: Internal Cash Flow

- **[B]** First: Internal Financing (Retained Earnings); Last: External Common Equity

- **[C]** First: External Common Equity; Last: Debt

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Pecking Order Theory ranks financing from lowest to highest information asymmetry cost: (1) Internal cash flow, (2) Debt, and (3) External Equity last.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** Which conflict of interest between bondholders and shareholders arises when a financially distressed company with high leverage rejects a safe, positive-NPV project because almost all of the project's cash flows would go to rescuing bondholders?

- **[A]** Debt overhang (underinvestment problem)

- **[B]** Asset substitution (risk-shifting) problem

- **[C]** Empire building

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Debt overhang (underinvestment) occurs when equity holders refuse to fund a safe positive-NPV project because the value gain accrues primarily to existing senior creditors; conversely, risk-shifting (asset substitution) is gambling on high-variance projects.
