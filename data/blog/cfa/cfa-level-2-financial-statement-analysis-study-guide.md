## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Intercorporate Investment Ownership & Influence Thresholds

```formula
< 20% (Passive): Fair Value through P&L (or OCI)

20%–50% (Significant Influence): Equity Method

> 50% (Control): Acquisition Consolidation
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- Under IFRS 9 and US GAAP (ASC 321), equity investments without significant influence are measured at fair value.

- significant influence uses one-line Equity Method.

- control requires 100% line-by-line consolidation.

### Formula 2: Basel III Bank Capital Adequacy Hierarchy (CAMELS)

```formula
CET1 Ratio = Common Equity Tier 1 / Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA) ≥ 4.5%

Total Tier 1 ≥ 6.0%

Total Capital ≥ 8.0%

LCR = HQLA / 30-Day Net Cash Outflows ≥ 100%
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Evaluates financial institutions across Capital adequacy, Asset quality, Management, Earnings, Liquidity (LCR/NSFR), and Sensitivity to market risk.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Evaluating Bank Capital Adequacy, Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR), and Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A commercial bank has Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA) = $200B, Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) = $21B, Additional Tier 1 Capital = $3B, and Tier 2 Subordinated Debt = $6B. It holds High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) = $36B against stressed 30-day net cash outflows of $30B, and Available Stable Funding (ASF) = $165B against Required Stable Funding (RSF) = $150B.

```formula
< 20% (Passive): Fair Value through P&L (or OCI)

20%–50% (Significant Influence): Equity Method

> 50% (Control): Acquisition Consolidation
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. CET1 Ratio = $21B / $200B = 10.50% (well above 4.5% Basel III minimum + 2.5% buffer).**

- **Step 2. Total Tier 1 Ratio = ($21B + $3B) / $200B = 12.00%.**

- **Step 3. Total Capital Ratio = ($24B + $6B) / $200B = 15.00%.**

- **Step 4. Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) = $36B / $30B = 120.0% (≥ 100% minimum, meaning 36 days of survival liquidity).**

- **Step 5. Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) = $165B / $150B = 110.0% (≥ 100% minimum).**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> CAMELS bank analysis at Level II tests CET1/RWA ratios, LCR (HQLA / 30-day cash outflows), and NSFR (Available / Required Stable Funding).

### Worked Example 2: Adjusting Invested Capital and NOPAT for Off-Balance-Sheet Associates

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Conglomerate P reports Total Assets = $1,200M (which includes a $150M Equity-Method Investment in Associate Z) and Net Income = $140M (which includes $25M of Equity Income from Associate Z). How do we isolate P's core operating Return on Invested Capital?

```formula
CET1 Ratio = Common Equity Tier 1 / Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA) ≥ 4.5%

Total Tier 1 ≥ 6.0%

Total Capital ≥ 8.0%

LCR = HQLA / 30-Day Net Cash Outflows ≥ 100%
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Because the $150M carrying value of Associate Z is a one-line net equity asset while P's Revenue and EBIT exclude Associate Z's operations, we deduct the $150M associate carrying value from P's Invested Capital and deduct the $25M associate equity income from P's Net Income before evaluating core parent operating margins and ROIC.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Removing equity-method associates from both numerator and denominator restores consistency when computing parent operating ratios.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** In analyzing a Property and Casualty (P&C) insurance company under the Level II Financial Institutions module, the insurer has a Loss and Loss Adjustment Expense Ratio of 68.0%, an Underwriting Expense Ratio of 29.0%, and a Dividends to Policyholders Ratio of 1.5%. Its Combined Ratio After Dividends is:

- **[A]** 97.0% (indicating an underwriting profit)

- **[B]** 98.5% (indicating a 1.5% underwriting profit before investment income)

- **[C]** 101.5% (indicating an underwriting loss)

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Combined Ratio = Loss Ratio (68.0%) + Expense Ratio (29.0%) = 97.0% (divided by Net Premiums Earned and Net Premiums Written respectively). Adding Dividends to Policyholders (1.5%) gives a Combined Ratio After Dividends of 98.5% (< 100%, indicating profitable underwriting even before investment income).

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Under Basel III banking regulations, the Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) is defined as:

- **[A]** Available Stable Funding divided by Required Stable Funding

- **[B]** High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) divided by expected net cash outflows over a stressed 30-calendar-day period (required to be at least 100%)

- **[C]** Common Equity Tier 1 divided by Risk-Weighted Assets

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** LCR = HQLA / 30-day stressed net cash outflows (≥ 100%); NSFR = Available Stable Funding / Required Stable Funding (≥ 100%).

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** When a company holds a 12% passive equity stake in a publicly traded firm (no significant influence), how are unrealized fair value gains and losses treated under US GAAP (ASC 321) vs. IFRS 9?

- **[A]** Under US GAAP, all equity securities without significant influence must be measured at Fair Value Through Profit or Loss (FVPL); under IFRS 9, FVPL is the default, but the firm may make an irrevocable election at inception to classify non-trading equity at Fair Value Through OCI (FVOCI)

- **[B]** Both require amortized cost

- **[C]** Both require the Equity Method

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Under IFRS 9, non-trading equity investments can be irrevocably designated at FVOCI (where only dividends hit P&L and price changes stay in OCI), whereas US GAAP mandates FVPL for all marketable equity securities.
