## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Venture Capital (VC) Method: Post-Money & Pre-Money Valuation (Post-Money Valuation (V_Post) = Exit Value_T / (1 + Target IRR)^T = Exit Value_T / Target ROI Multiple ; Pre-Money Valuation (V_Pre) = V_Post − Investment Amount (I)) and Multi-Round VC Ownership Dilution (Series A followed by Series B) (Retention Ratio of Series A Investor after Series B (f_B) = f_A × (1 − f_B) => Required Initial Series A Fraction f_A = Final Required Share / (1 − f_B)) govern solving the Venture Capital (VC) Method (Exit Value discounted by target IRR/ROI, Post-Money vs Pre-Money Valuation, Founder Ownership Dilution across Series A and Series B rounds), and LBO vs VC characteristics.

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Venture Capital (VC) Method: Post-Money & Pre-Money Valuation

```formula
Post-Money Valuation (V_Post) = Exit Value_T / (1 + Target IRR)^T = Exit Value_T / Target ROI Multiple

Pre-Money Valuation (V_Pre) = V_Post − Investment Amount (I)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- VC Ownership Share f = I / V_Post = I / (V_Pre + I).

- New Shares Issued to VC = Existing Founder Shares × [f / (1 − f)].

### Formula 2: Multi-Round VC Ownership Dilution (Series A followed by Series B)

```formula
Retention Ratio of Series A Investor after Series B (f_B) = f_A × (1 − f_B) => Required Initial Series A Fraction f_A = Final Required Share / (1 − f_B)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** If a Series A VC knows a future Series B round will take f_B = 20% of the company, the Series A VC must demand f_A = Final_Share / (1 − 0.20) today to offset future dilution!.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Single-Round Venture Capital (VC) Method: Pre-Money, Post-Money, Share Price, and New Shares

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A biotech startup Founded with 2,000,000 existing founder shares seeks a $6,000,000 Series A investment (I = $6.0M). The VC firm projects that the startup will be acquired in T = 5 years for an Exit Value of $90,000,000 and requires a target annual compound return (IRR) of 40.0% per year (where 1.40^5 = 5.37824, or let's use a 5.0x target ROI multiple so math is crystal clear: 1 + r)^5 = 5.0x, i.e., 37.97% IRR).

```formula
Post-Money Valuation (V_Post) = Exit Value_T / (1 + Target IRR)^T = Exit Value_T / Target ROI Multiple

Pre-Money Valuation (V_Pre) = V_Post − Investment Amount (I)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1 (Post-Money Valuation V_Post): V_Post = $90,000,000 / 5.0 = $18,000,000. Step 2 (Pre-Money Valuation V_Pre): V_Pre = V_Post − Investment = $18,000,000 − $6,000,000 = $12,000,000. Step 3 (Required VC Ownership Fraction f): f = $6,000,000 / $18,000,000 = 33.333% (1/3 of the company). Step 4 (New Series A Shares Issued to VC): Shares_VC = 2,000,000 × [(1/3) / (1 − 1/3)] = 2,000,000 × 0.50 = 1,000,000 new shares (Total Post-Money Shares = 3,000,000). Step 5 (Series A Price per Share): $6,000,000 / 1,000,000 shares = $6.00 per share (matching V_Pre / Founder Shares = $12,000,000 / 2,000,000 = $6.00/share!).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Notice the identity: Price per Share = Pre-Money Valuation / Pre-Existing Shares = Post-Money Valuation / Total Post-Money Shares = $6.00!

### Worked Example 2: Multi-Round VC Valuation with Anticipated Series B Dilution

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
In the example above (Exit Value at Year 5 = $90.0M, Series A invests $6.0M at t = 0 requiring 5.0x ROI, so Series A needs $30.0M at exit = 33.33% FINAL ownership at Year 5), suppose the company will also need a $10.0M Series B round at Year 3 (2 years before exit) from a Series B investor requiring a 2.5x ROI multiple ($25.0M at exit). Calculate (1) Series B's ownership share f_B, and (2) the initial ownership share f_A that the Series A investor must demand at t = 0.

```formula
Retention Ratio of Series A Investor after Series B (f_B) = f_A × (1 − f_B) => Required Initial Series A Fraction f_A = Final Required Share / (1 − f_B)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1 (Series B Required Final Ownership f_B at Year 3): Series B requires $10.0M × 2.5 = $25.0M out of the $90.0M exit => f_B = $25.0M / $90.0M = 27.78% (5/18). Step 2 (Series A Initial Ownership f_A at t = 0 before Series B dilution): Because Series A will be diluted by (1 − f_B) = 13/18 when Series B is issued, Series A must start with: f_A = Final Required Share (1/3) / (1 − 5/18) = (6/18) / (13/18) = 6 / 13 = 46.15%! (Check: 46.15% × (1 − 27.78%) = 33.33% at exit!).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Anticipating future Series B dilution forces the Series A investor to demand 46.15% ownership today so that after 27.78% dilution in Series B, Series A still owns 33.33% at exit.

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| VC / PE Metric | Exact Formula | Denominator / Base | Exam Trap to Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Post-Money Valuation (V_Post) | Exit Value_T / (1 + IRR)^T | Includes new cash I invested | Confusing Pre-Money with Post-Money |
| Pre-Money Valuation (V_Pre) | V_Post − Investment I | Value before new cash arrives | V_Pre + I = V_Post always |
| VC Ownership Fraction (f) | I / V_Post = I / (V_Pre + I) | Divided by Post-Money V_Post! | Dividing I by V_Pre instead of V_Post |
| New Shares Issued (N_VC) | N_Founders × [f / (1 − f)] | Scales existing shares by f/(1−f) | Multiplying N_Founders by f directly |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A startup is expected to be sold in 4 years for $80 million. A venture capital firm invests $5 million today and requires a 4.0x cash-on-cash return multiple (equivalent to a 41.4% annual IRR over 4 years). Under the VC method, the startup's Pre-Money Valuation and the VC's required ownership percentage are:

- **[A]** Pre-Money = $15 million; VC Ownership = 25.0%

- **[B]** Pre-Money = $20 million; VC Ownership = 25.0%

- **[C]** Pre-Money = $15 million; VC Ownership = 33.3%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Post-Money Valuation V_Post = $80M / 4.0 = $20 million. Pre-Money Valuation V_Pre = V_Post − Investment = $20M − $5M = $15 million. VC Ownership f = Investment / V_Post = $5M / $20M = 25.0%. (Dividing $5M by $15M V_Pre gives the trap answer 33.3%).

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** In the question above, if the founders hold 3,000,000 shares prior to the Series A financing and the VC receives a 25.0% post-money ownership stake for its $5 million investment, how many new shares must be issued to the VC and what is the Series A price per share?

- **[A]** 750,000 new shares at $6.67 per share

- **[B]** 1,000,000 new shares at $5.00 per share

- **[C]** 1,000,000 new shares at $6.67 per share

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** New shares = 3,000,000 × [0.25 / (1 − 0.25)] = 3,000,000 × (1/3) = 1,000,000 new shares (so VC owns 1M / 4M = 25%). Price per share = $5,000,000 / 1,000,000 shares = $5.00 (matching Pre-Money $15M / 3M founder shares = $5.00).

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** Why do Venture Capital valuations apply an extremely high discount rate (often 35% to 50% target IRR) when discounting management's projected Exit Value?

- **[A]** Because risk-free Treasury rates are 35%

- **[B]** To compensate for both high systematic/illiquidity risk AND the high probability of startup failure coupled with optimistic ('success-case') management financial projections

- **[C]** Because VCs use high debt leverage

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Management business plans represent optimistic 'conditional on full success' scenarios; the 35%–50% VC discount rate embeds both the high probability of failure (survival adjustment) and illiquidity risk.

## Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

- **Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Venture Capital (VC) Method: Post-Money & Pre-Money Valuation.**

- **Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Multi-Round VC Ownership Dilution (Series A followed by Series B).**

- **Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.**
