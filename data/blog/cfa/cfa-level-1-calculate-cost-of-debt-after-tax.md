### Formula 1: After-Tax Cost of Debt

```formula
r_d_after_tax = r_d_pre_tax × (1 − Marginal Tax Rate t)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Uses the current market Yield to Maturity (YTM)—never the historical coupon rate—multiplied by (1 − marginal tax rate).

### Formula 2: After-Tax Cost of Debt with Interest Deductibility Cap

```formula
Effective Tax Shield = min(Interest Expense, Deductible Cap) × t
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** If tax laws cap interest deductibility (e.g., at 30% of EBITDA/EBIT) or the firm has net operating losses, marginal debt above the cap costs the full pre-tax r_d.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Calculating Pre-Tax YTM and After-Tax Cost of Debt vs. Coupon Rate Trap

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A corporation issued 10-year bonds 3 years ago (7 years remaining) with an 8.0% semiannual coupon ($1,000 par). Today, those bonds trade at $1,109.80. The company's effective tax rate is 21.0% and its marginal tax rate on new income/deductions is 25.0%.

```formula
r_d_after_tax = r_d_pre_tax × (1 − Marginal Tax Rate t)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Solve for current Semiannual YTM (N = 14, PV = −1,109.80, PMT = 40.00, FV = 1,000): periodic semiannual yield = 3.00% -> Pre-Tax Cost of Debt r_d = 2 × 3.00% = 6.00% (ignore the historical 8.0% coupon!). Step 2: Apply the Marginal Tax Rate (25.0%, not the 21.0% average effective rate): After-Tax Cost of Debt = 6.00% × (1 − 0.25) = 4.50%.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Two classic CFA exam traps appear in one calculation: always use current market YTM (6.00%) rather than historical coupon rate (8.00%), and always use the marginal tax rate (25.0%) rather than the average effective tax rate (21.0%).

### Worked Example 2: Estimating Pre-Tax and After-Tax Cost of Debt for a Private Firm Using Synthetic Credit Rating

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A private industrial firm has no publicly traded bonds. It reports EBIT = $45M and Interest Expense = $9M. Comparable bond market data show that firms with an Interest Coverage Ratio between 4.5x and 5.5x carry a synthetic rating of A− with a credit spread of 145 bps over the 10-year Treasury yield of 4.35%. Marginal tax rate = 25%.

```formula
Effective Tax Shield = min(Interest Expense, Deductible Cap) × t
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Interest Coverage = $45M / $9M = 5.00x -> Synthetic Rating = A− (145 bps spread). Pre-Tax Cost of Debt r_d = 4.35% + 1.45% = 5.80%. After-Tax Cost of Debt = 5.80% × (1 − 0.25) = 4.35%.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> When a firm's debt does not trade publicly, analysts estimate r_d using matrix pricing or synthetic credit ratings based on coverage ratios.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** A company's 6-year, 5.0% annual coupon bonds ($100 par) currently trade at $95.08, corresponding to a yield to maturity of 6.00%. The company has an average effective tax rate of 18% and a marginal tax rate of 25%. Its after-tax cost of debt for WACC calculations is:

- **[A]** 3.75%

- **[B]** 4.50%

- **[C]** 4.92%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Use the current market YTM (6.00%, not the 5.0% coupon) and the marginal tax rate (25%, not the 18% effective rate): 6.00% × (1 − 0.25) = 4.50%.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** If a corporation operates in a jurisdiction that limits tax-deductible interest expense to 30% of EBITDA and the company's existing debt interest already exceeds that 30% cap, the marginal after-tax cost of issuing additional debt (assuming no carryforward benefit) equals:

- **[A]** r_d × (1 − t)

- **[B]** The full pre-tax cost of debt r_d, because incremental interest provides zero current tax shield

- **[C]** Zero

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Once the statutory interest deductibility cap is reached, incremental interest payments cannot be deducted from taxable income, so the marginal tax shield is zero and the after-tax cost of new debt equals the pre-tax rate r_d.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** When estimating the cost of floating-rate debt or lease obligations in WACC, an analyst should use:

- **[A]** The current market rate required for new debt of equivalent maturity and credit risk (or the lease's incremental borrowing rate)

- **[B]** Zero, because leases are operating expenses

- **[C]** The historical rate from 5 years ago

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** WACC is a forward-looking marginal opportunity cost of capital; all debt and lease obligations must be priced at current market yields/incremental borrowing rates.
