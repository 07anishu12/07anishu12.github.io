## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: The International Parity Triangle Equations (Price/Base Quote S_{P/B})

```formula
1. CIRP: (F − S)/S ≈ r_P − r_B

2. UIRP: %ΔE(S) ≈ r_P − r_B

3. Ex-Ante PPP: %ΔE(S) ≈ E(π_P) − E(π_B)

4. Int'l Fisher Effect (UIRP + PPP): r_P − r_B = E(π_P) − E(π_B)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** When both UIRP and Ex-Ante PPP hold simultaneously, nominal interest rate differentials equal expected inflation differentials (so Real Interest Rates are equal globally: r_real_P = r_real_B!).

### Formula 2: FX Carry Trade Unhedged Return

```formula
Carry Return (Borrow Low-Rate Currency B, Invest in High-Rate Currency H) = (1 + r_H) × (S_{1, B/H} / S_{0, B/H}) − (1 + r_B) ≈ (r_H − r_B) − %Depreciation_of_H
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Earns positive excess return as long as the high-yield investment currency does not depreciate by more than the interest rate spread (r_H − r_B).

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Verifying the International Fisher Effect and Real Interest Rate Parity

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
In the United States (USD), the 1-year nominal risk-free rate is r_USD = 5.20% and expected inflation is E(π_USD) = 3.00%. In Switzerland (CHF), expected inflation is E(π_CHF) = 1.10%. If Real Interest Rate Parity (the International Fisher Effect) holds, find (1) the 1-year Swiss nominal interest rate r_CHF, and (2) the expected 1-year change in the CHF/USD spot rate under UIRP and Ex-Ante PPP.

```formula
1. CIRP: (F − S)/S ≈ r_P − r_B

2. UIRP: %ΔE(S) ≈ r_P − r_B

3. Ex-Ante PPP: %ΔE(S) ≈ E(π_P) − E(π_B)

4. Int'l Fisher Effect (UIRP + PPP): r_P − r_B = E(π_P) − E(π_B)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1 (US Real Interest Rate): r_real_USD = 5.20% − 3.00% = 2.20%. Step 2 (By Real Interest Rate Parity, r_real_CHF = r_real_USD = 2.20%): Nominal r_CHF = 2.20% + 1.10% = 3.30%. Step 3 (Expected %ΔS_{CHF/USD} under both UIRP and Ex-Ante PPP): r_CHF − r_USD = 3.30% − 5.20% = −1.90% (identical to E(π_CHF) − E(π_USD) = 1.10% − 3.00% = −1.90%!). Thus USD is expected to depreciate by 1.90% against CHF.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> When all international parity conditions hold, the Forward Rate F is an unbiased predictor of the Future Spot Rate E(S_1) (Forward Rate Unbiasedness).

### Worked Example 2: Calculating Realized Return on a Leveraged FX Carry Trade

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A macro hedge fund borrows 100,000,000 JPY (low-yield funding currency at r_JPY = 0.50%) and converts it at the spot rate S_0 = 20.00 JPY/MXN into 5,000,000 MXN to invest in 1-year Mexican government bonds yielding r_MXN = 10.50%. After 1 year, the spot rate has moved to S_1 = 19.40 JPY/MXN (MXN depreciated by 3.00% against JPY). Calculate the realized net profit in JPY and percentage spread.

```formula
Carry Return (Borrow Low-Rate Currency B, Invest in High-Rate Currency H) = (1 + r_H) × (S_{1, B/H} / S_{0, B/H}) − (1 + r_B) ≈ (r_H − r_B) − %Depreciation_of_H
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1 (Ending MXN Principal + Interest): 5,000,000 MXN × (1 + 0.1050) = 5,525,000 MXN. Step 2 (Convert back to JPY at S_1 = 19.40 JPY/MXN): 5,525,000 × 19.40 = 107,185,000 JPY. Step 3 (Repay JPY Loan + 0.50% Interest): 100,000,000 × 1.0050 = 100,500,000 JPY. Step 4 (Net Carry Profit): 107,185,000 − 100,500,000 = +6,685,000 JPY (+6.685% net return on notional, ≈ 10.0% interest differential − 3.0% MXN depreciation).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Even though the high-yielding MXN depreciated by 3.00%, it depreciated by LESS than the 10.00% interest rate differential predicted by UIRP, making the carry trade highly profitable (+6.69%).

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Parity Condition | Mathematical Link | Enforcement Mechanism | Empirical Horizon Validity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Covered IRP (CIRP) | (F − S)/S = r_P − r_B | Riskless arbitrage (Forward locked) | Holds instantaneously at all times |
| Uncovered IRP (UIRP) | %ΔE(S) = r_P − r_B | Risk-neutral unhedged speculation | Fails short/medium term; holds very long run |
| Absolute PPP | S_{P/B} = Price_Level_P / Price_Level_B | Law of One Price across identical baskets | Fails due to tariffs, shipping, non-tradables |
| Relative / Ex-Ante PPP | %ΔE(S) = E(π_P) − E(π_B) | International goods trade over long run | Holds over multi-year / high-inflation horizons |
| International Fisher Effect | r_P − r_B = E(π_P) − E(π_B) | Combines UIRP + Ex-Ante PPP | Implies equal global real interest rates |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** Which two international parity conditions, when combined, imply that real interest rates are equal across countries ( the International Fisher Effect: r_Price − r_Base = E(π_Price) − E(π_Base))?

- **[A]** Covered Interest Rate Parity and Absolute PPP

- **[B]** Uncovered Interest Rate Parity (UIRP) and Ex-Ante Relative Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)

- **[C]** Covered Interest Rate Parity and the Marshall-Lerner condition

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** UIRP states %ΔE(S) = r_P − r_B, and Ex-Ante PPP states %ΔE(S) = E(π_P) − E(π_B). Equating the two yields r_P − r_B = E(π_P) − E(π_B), or r_P − E(π_P) = r_B − E(π_B) (equal real interest rates).

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Compared to a normal distribution, the empirical return distribution of an unhedged FX carry trade (borrowing in low-interest-rate safe-haven currencies like JPY/CHF to invest in high-interest-rate currencies) exhibits:

- **[A]** Positive skewness and negative excess kurtosis

- **[B]** Negative skewness (crash risk) and positive excess kurtosis (fat tails), because funding currencies spike sharply when global risk aversion surges and leveraged traders unwind positions simultaneously

- **[C]** Zero skewness and zero volatility

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** FX carry trades earn steady small positive carry in calm markets ('walking up the escalator') but suffer sudden violent losses when risk panics trigger simultaneous flight-to-safety unwinds ('falling down the elevator shaft'), producing negative skewness and fat tails.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** Why does Absolute Purchasing Power Parity (where the nominal exchange rate equals the ratio of national price levels) rarely hold in practice even when trade is free?

- **[A]** Because consumption baskets have different weights across countries and include non-tradable goods and services (such as housing, healthcare, and local labor)

- **[B]** Because covered interest rate parity prevents it

- **[C]** Because central banks ban gold exports

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Different national CPI basket weights, transportation costs, and large non-tradable services sectors (the Balassa-Samuelson effect) prevent Absolute PPP from holding.
