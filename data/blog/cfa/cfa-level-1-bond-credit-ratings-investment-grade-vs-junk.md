## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Expected Loss & Credit Spread Approximation

```formula
Expected Loss (EL) = PD × LGD = PD × (1 − Recovery Rate)

Credit Spread ≈ PD × (1 − RR) + Liquidity Premium
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Links default probability (PD) and loss severity (LGD = 1 − RR) to the yield spread demanded over risk-free benchmarks.

### Formula 2: Corporate Credit Metrics (Leverage & FFO Coverage)

```formula
Debt / EBITDA = Total Debt / EBITDA

FFO / Total Debt = (Net Income + D&A + Deferred Taxes) / Total Debt
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Primary quantitative ratios used by S&P, Moody's, and Fitch to assign corporate credit ratings.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Calculating Expected Credit Loss, Fair Credit Spread, and Downgrade Price Impact

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A 7-year Baa2/BBB corporate bond has an annual Probability of Default (PD) = 0.80%, Recovery Rate (RR) = 40.0%, and Modified Duration = 5.60. If a rating downgrade to Ba1/BB+ (fallen angel) causes its credit spread to widen by 135 bps (+0.0135), calculate (1) initial Expected Loss %, and (2) the immediate % price drop from the spread widening.

```formula
Expected Loss (EL) = PD × LGD = PD × (1 − Recovery Rate)

Credit Spread ≈ PD × (1 − RR) + Liquidity Premium
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Loss Given Default (LGD) = 1 − 0.40 = 60.0%. Annual Expected Loss = PD × LGD = 0.80% × 60.0% = 0.48% (48 bps per year). Step 2: Immediate price impact of +135 bps spread widening: %ΔPrice ≈ −ModDur × ΔSpread = −5.60 × (+0.0135) = −7.56%.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Crossing the BBB−/Baa3 threshold into high-yield ('fallen angel') triggers forced selling by investment-grade-only mandates, widening spreads by 135 bps and dropping bond value by 7.56%.

### Worked Example 2: Seniority Priority and Pari Passu Bankruptcy Recovery Waterfall

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A defaulted issuer has $180M of liquidation value. Its liabilities are: Senior Secured Debt = $100M (backed by $120M collateral), Senior Unsecured Bonds = $120M, and Subordinated Debentures = $80M.

```formula
Debt / EBITDA = Total Debt / EBITDA

FFO / Total Debt = (Net Income + D&A + Deferred Taxes) / Total Debt
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Senior Secured creditors recover 100% ($100M) from their $120M collateral, leaving $80M of remaining enterprise value. Step 2: Under the absolute priority rule, Senior Unsecured creditors ($120M claim) receive the entire remaining $80M (Recovery Rate = $80M / $120M = 66.67%), while Subordinated Debentures receive $0 (0% recovery).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Within the same seniority class, bonds rank pari passu (equal recovery percentage regardless of remaining maturity).

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** What is the lowest credit rating still classified as Investment Grade by S&P/Fitch and Moody's, respectively?

- **[A]** BB+ and Ba1

- **[B]** BBB− and Baa3

- **[C]** BBB and Baa2

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** BBB− (S&P/Fitch) and Baa3 (Moody's) mark the lowest rung of Investment Grade; one notch lower (BB+ / Ba1) enters High-Yield (junk) territory.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Why do credit rating agencies often notch the unsecured debt of a parent holding company lower than the unsecured debt of its operating subsidiary?

- **[A]** Structural subordination, because the operating subsidiary's creditors have first claim on operating assets and cash flows before upstream dividends can reach the parent

- **[B]** Because parent holding companies cannot issue covenants

- **[C]** Because holding company bonds have longer duration

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Structural subordination arises because operating cash flows and assets reside at the subsidiary level and must satisfy subsidiary creditors first before any equity distributions reach the parent.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** A corporate bond has an estimated annual probability of default of 1.50% and an expected recovery rate of 36.0% of par value. Its annual expected credit loss is closest to:

- **[A]** 0.54%

- **[B]** 0.96%

- **[C]** 1.50%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Loss Given Default (LGD) = 1 − Recovery Rate = 1 − 0.36 = 0.64 (64%). Expected Loss = PD × LGD = 1.50% × 0.64 = 0.96% (96 bps).
