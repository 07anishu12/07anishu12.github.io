## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Pyramid Ownership Control vs. Cash-Flow Rights Wedge

```formula
Control Rights in Bottom Company = Control_Level1 (if > 50% at each chain link)

Cash-Flow Ownership = Ownership_Level1 × Ownership_Level2 × ...
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** In a corporate pyramid, a family holding 51% of Holding Co A, which holds 51% of Operating Co B, exercises 51% voting control over Co B while owning only 0.51 × 0.51 = 26.01% of Co B's economic cash flows!.

### Formula 2: Integrating ESG Risk into Equity DCF & Bond Credit Valuation

```formula
Equity Value = ∑ [Adjusted_FCFF_t / (WACC + ESG_Premium)^t] (Warning: Adjust Cash Flows OR WACC—Never Double Count Both!)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Analysts adjust projected revenues/costs/CapEx/fines OR add a governance/ESG risk premium to r_e/r_d, taking care not to double-count the exact same risk in both numerator and denominator.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Calculating the Voting Control vs. Cash-Flow Ownership Wedge in a Pyramid Structure

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A controlling family owns 60% of Holding Company Alpha. Holding Company Alpha owns 55% of Intermediate Company Beta, and Intermediate Company Beta owns 52% of Operating Company Gamma.

```formula
Control Rights in Bottom Company = Control_Level1 (if > 50% at each chain link)

Cash-Flow Ownership = Ownership_Level1 × Ownership_Level2 × ...
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1 (Effective Voting Control over Operating Co Gamma): Because each link exceeds 50% (> 50% majority control at every tier), the controlling family exercises 100% effective majority voting control (52% direct vote) over Operating Co Gamma! Step 2 (Economic Cash-Flow Ownership in Operating Co Gamma): 0.60 × 0.55 × 0.52 = 0.1716 = 17.16%.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> With only 17.16% of the cash flows, the controlling family has a strong incentive to 'tunnel' resources out of Operating Co Gamma (where they lose 82.84% of every dollar) up to Holding Co Alpha (where they keep 60% of every dollar)!

### Worked Example 2: Integrating an Environmental Carbon Tax & Governance Flaw into Equity DCF Valuation

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
An analyst values a chemical producer (current unadjusted FCFF = $100M/yr, WACC = 9.0%, g = 2.0% -> Base Firm Value = $102M / 0.07 = $1,457.1M). New environmental rules will require $8.0M/yr in permanent after-tax carbon abatement expenses (reducing FCFF_1 from $102M to $94M). Additionally, a board independence deficiency warrants a +50 bps (+0.50%) governance premium on WACC (raising WACC from 9.0% to 9.5%).

```formula
Equity Value = ∑ [Adjusted_FCFF_t / (WACC + ESG_Premium)^t] (Warning: Adjust Cash Flows OR WACC—Never Double Count Both!)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1 (Value after Environmental Cash Flow Adjustment at 9.0% WACC): $94.0M / (0.090 − 0.020) = $1,342.9M (−$114.2M environmental impact). Step 2 (Value after adding +50 bps Governance WACC Premium): $94.0M / (0.095 − 0.020) = $94.0M / 0.075 = $1,253.3M (−$89.6M governance impact; Total ESG Value Adjustment = −$203.8M).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Quantifiable operational ESG costs (like carbon taxes or remediation CapEx) are best modeled directly in FCFF cash flows, while broad governance risks are often modeled as a WACC/Cost-of-Equity basis-point adjustment.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** In a corporate pyramid structure, Corporation X owns 55% of the voting stock of Corporation Y, and Corporation Y owns 51% of the voting stock of Corporation Z. What is Corporation X's economic cash-flow ownership in Corporation Z, and does Corporation X control Corporation Z?

- **[A]** Cash-flow ownership = 28.05%; Corporation X exercises majority voting control over Corporation Z

- **[B]** Cash-flow ownership = 51.00%; Corporation X has no control

- **[C]** Cash-flow ownership = 106.00%; Corporation X has minority control

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Economic cash-flow rights multiply across the chain: 0.55 × 0.51 = 0.2805 = 28.05%, while majority (>50%) voting control at each link gives Corporation X effective voting control over Corporation Z.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** Why are ESG factors often considered even more asymmetric and critical for Fixed-Income credit analysts than for Equity analysts?

- **[A]** Because bondholders receive all the upside when a company invents a green technology

- **[B]** Because fixed-income bondholders have capped upside (par + coupon) and severe downside exposure to tail-risk credit events, regulatory fines, stranded assets, and litigation defaults

- **[C]** Because bonds have no maturity date

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Bond payoffs are negatively skewed (capped upside, 100% downside loss in default), so tail-risk ESG liabilities (like environmental disasters or accounting fraud) directly threaten credit spreads and solvency.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** What is a 'Sustainability-Linked Bond' (SLB), and how does it differ from a traditional 'Green Bond'?

- **[A]** A Green Bond restricts the use of proceeds to specific environmental projects, whereas a Sustainability-Linked Bond (SLB) can be used for general corporate purposes but features a coupon rate that steps up (increases) if the issuer fails to meet predefined company-wide sustainability Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

- **[B]** An SLB has zero coupon

- **[C]** A Green Bond pays equity dividends

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Green Bonds are 'use-of-proceeds' bonds tied to green projects, whereas Sustainability-Linked Bonds (SLBs) are general-purpose bonds whose coupon rate is contractually linked to achieving ESG targets.
