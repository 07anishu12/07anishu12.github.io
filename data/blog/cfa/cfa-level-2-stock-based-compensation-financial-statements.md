## Introduction

Stock-Based Compensation: How Does It Affect Financial Statements? covers accounting for Restricted Stock Units (RSUs) and Employee Stock Options over the vesting service period, Diluted EPS Treasury Stock Method (including unrecognized compensation cost), and tax windfall/shortfall impacts in Level II Financial Statement Analysis (LM 2: Employee Compensation: Post-Employment and Share-Based — Restricted Stock Units (RSUs), Employee Stock Options, grant-date fair value amortization over vesting period, Diluted EPS treasury stock method with unrecognized compensation expense, cash flow statement & tax windfall effects), with verified formulas, two worked examples, and three practice MCQs.

## Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Annual Share-Based Compensation Expense & Paid-In Capital (Annual Compensation Expense = (Grant-Date Fair Value × Expected Vested Awards) / Service Vesting Period (Years)) and Diluted EPS Treasury Stock Method for Unvested RSUs / Options (Assumed Proceeds = Cash Strike Proceeds + Average Unrecognized Share-Based Compensation Expense ; Net Dilutive Shares = Granted Shares − (Assumed Proceeds / Avg Market Price)) govern accounting for Restricted Stock Units (RSUs) and Employee Stock Options over the vesting service period, Diluted EPS Treasury Stock Method (including unrecognized compensation cost), and tax windfall/shortfall impacts.

## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: Annual Share-Based Compensation Expense & Paid-In Capital

```formula
Annual Compensation Expense = (Grant-Date Fair Value × Expected Vested Awards) / Service Vesting Period (Years)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Recognized as a non-cash operating expense on the income statement (reducing EBIT and NI) with an offsetting credit to Share-Based Compensation Reserve / APIC inside Shareholders' Equity (zero net effect on Total Equity!).

### Formula 2: Diluted EPS Treasury Stock Method for Unvested RSUs / Options

```formula
Assumed Proceeds = Cash Strike Proceeds + Average Unrecognized Share-Based Compensation Expense

Net Dilutive Shares = Granted Shares − (Assumed Proceeds / Avg Market Price)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** In Level II, assumed proceeds in the Treasury Stock Method include BOTH the option exercise price AND the average unrecognized compensation cost remaining to be expensed!.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Accounting for Restricted Stock Units (RSUs) Over a 3-Year Cliff Vesting Period

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
On Jan 1, 2026, a software firm grants 600,000 RSUs to engineers when the stock's grant-date fair value is $50.00 per share ($30,000,000 total grant value). The RSUs vest at the end of 3 years, and management estimates 0% forfeitures. During 2026, the stock price rises to $68.00.

```formula
Annual Compensation Expense = (Grant-Date Fair Value × Expected Vested Awards) / Service Vesting Period (Years)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Grant-date fair value is locked at $50.00 (ignore the subsequent rise to $68.00 for equity-settled awards!). Step 2: 2026 Compensation Expense = $30,000,000 / 3 years = $10,000,000 (reduces pre-tax operating income by $10.0M). Step 3: Balance Sheet Impact: Retained Earnings decreases by $10.0M (pre-tax) while Additional Paid-In Capital (APIC) increases by +$10.0M -> Net impact on Total Shareholders' Equity = $0! Step 4: Cash Flow Impact: +$10.0M non-cash add-back in Operating Cash Flow (CFO).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Equity-settled RSUs and stock options are never remeasured for stock price changes after the grant date; only cash-settled Stock Appreciation Rights (SARs, recorded as liabilities) are remeasured to fair value each quarter.

### Worked Example 2: Calculating Diluted EPS Treasury Stock Shares Including Unrecognized Compensation Cost

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Using the 600,000 RSUs above (Exercise Price = $0; Total Grant Value = $30M; Year 1 Ending Unrecognized Compensation Cost = $20M, so Average Year 1 Unrecognized Cost = ($30M + $20M)/2 = $25.0M), if the average market stock price during 2026 was $62.50, calculate the net dilutive shares added to Diluted EPS.

```formula
Assumed Proceeds = Cash Strike Proceeds + Average Unrecognized Share-Based Compensation Expense

Net Dilutive Shares = Granted Shares − (Assumed Proceeds / Avg Market Price)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Step 1: Assumed Proceeds under the Treasury Stock Method = Cash Exercise Price ($0) + Average Unrecognized Compensation Expense ($25,000,000) = $25,000,000. Step 2: Shares assumed repurchased at $62.50 average stock price = $25,000,000 / $62.50 = 400,000 shares. Step 3: Net Dilutive Shares added to WACS denominator = 600,000 − 400,000 = 200,000 shares.

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Including average unrecognized compensation cost ($25M) as assumed proceeds in the Treasury Stock Method reduces dilution from 600,000 shares down to 200,000 shares.

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** On January 1, a company grants 300,000 employee stock options with a grant-date fair value of $12.00 per option that vest over a 4-year service period. Assuming no forfeitures, how does the recognition of Year 1 stock-based compensation expense affect the company's Operating Income (EBIT) and Total Shareholders' Equity (ignoring taxes)?

- **[A]** EBIT decreases by $900,000; Total Shareholders' Equity remains unchanged (Retained Earnings decreases by $900,000 and Paid-In Capital increases by $900,000)

- **[B]** EBIT decreases by $900,000; Total Shareholders' Equity decreases by $900,000

- **[C]** Neither EBIT nor Equity is affected until options are exercised

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Total fair value = 300,000 × $12 = $3,600,000; annual expense = $3,600,000 / 4 = $900,000. Expensing $900,000 reduces Retained Earnings by $900,000 while crediting APIC (Paid-In Capital) by +$900,000, leaving Total Equity unchanged.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** When applying the Treasury Stock Method to calculate the dilutive impact of unvested Restricted Stock Units (RSUs) for Diluted EPS, the 'assumed proceeds' used to repurchase shares at the average market price equal:

- **[A]** Zero, because RSUs have no exercise price

- **[B]** The average unrecognized share-based compensation expense remaining to be amortized over the future vesting period

- **[C]** The par value of the common stock

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Under both IFRS and US GAAP, unvested share-based awards include the average unrecognized compensation cost as assumed proceeds in the Treasury Stock Method.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** How does the accounting for cash-settled Stock Appreciation Rights (SARs) differ from equity-settled employee stock options?

- **[A]** Cash-settled SARs are classified as a liability on the balance sheet and must be remeasured to fair value at the end of every reporting period through the income statement

- **[B]** Cash-settled SARs are never expensed on the income statement

- **[C]** Equity-settled options are remeasured to fair value every quarter

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Because cash-settled SARs require a future cash outflow, they are recorded as a balance sheet liability and remeasured to fair value at each reporting date until settlement.

## Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid
