## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: GIPS Composite Inclusion Rule & Terminated Accounts

```formula
Composite = All Actual, Fee-Paying, Discretionary Portfolios Managed to the Same Strategy

Terminated Accounts MUST Remain in Historical Composite Returns Through Their Last Full Measurement Period!
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- Simulated/model portfolios and non-discretionary accounts MUST NEVER be included in a composite.

- terminated accounts cannot be deleted retroactively (which prevents survivorship bias!).

### Formula 2: GIPS Historical Track Record & External Cash Flow Valuation Rules

```formula
Initial Claim: Minimum 5 Years of GIPS-Compliant History (or Since Inception if < 5 yrs), Building Annually to a Minimum of 10 Years
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:** Total Firm Assets = Fair Value of ALL Discretionary AND Non-Discretionary Assets (both fee-paying and non-fee-paying) managed by the Firm!.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Auditing a Firm's Composite Construction and Total Firm Assets Under GIPS

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Apex Asset Management claims GIPS compliance and manages 5 portfolios: (1) Account A ($100M, discretionary, fee-paying Large-Cap Growth); (2) Account B ($80M, discretionary, fee-paying Large-Cap Growth that closed on Oct 31 after underperforming through Sept 30); (3) Account C ($50M, non-discretionary Large-Cap Growth where the client must approve every trade); (4) Account D ($30M, discretionary non-fee-paying charity account); and (5) a $20M backtested paper model portfolio. Determine which accounts belong in the Large-Cap Growth Composite and what Total Firm Assets equal.

```formula
Composite = All Actual, Fee-Paying, Discretionary Portfolios Managed to the Same Strategy

Terminated Accounts MUST Remain in Historical Composite Returns Through Their Last Full Measurement Period!
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. Large-Cap Growth Composite MUST include: Account A ($100M) AND Account B's historical performance through Sept 30 (its last full measurement period before termination!).**

- **Step 2. Account C ($50M non-discretionary) and the $20M paper model portfolio MUST BE EXCLUDED from all composites!**

- **Step 3. Account D ($30M discretionary non-fee-paying) may be included ONLY if the firm's policy includes non-fee-paying discretionary accounts (and discloses the % of composite assets that are non-fee-paying).**

- **Step 4. Total Firm Assets = $100M (A) + $50M (C, non-discretionary IS included in Total Firm Assets!) + $30M (D) = $180M (excluding terminated B and paper model).**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Notice the crucial distinction: Non-discretionary accounts (Account C) are EXCLUDED from composites, but INCLUDED in Total Firm Assets!

### Worked Example 2: Evaluating GIPS Compliance Claims and Local Law Conflicts

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
Audit two statements made by Investment Firm Z (founded 3 years ago): Statement 1: 'Firm Z has been verified as GIPS compliant for its Flagship Small-Cap Composite.' Statement 2: 'Because Firm Z is only 3 years old, it cannot claim GIPS compliance until it has 5 years of history.'

```formula
Initial Claim: Minimum 5 Years of GIPS-Compliant History (or Since Inception if < 5 yrs), Building Annually to a Minimum of 10 Years
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Statement 1 is a VIOLATION: GIPS compliance and GIPS verification apply strictly on a FIRM-WIDE basis—a firm can never claim GIPS compliance or verification for a single composite! Statement 2 is FALSE: If a firm has been in existence for less than 5 years (e.g., 3 years), it CAN claim GIPS compliance immediately by presenting its GIPS-compliant performance since inception (3 years), and then adding 1 year each year until reaching 10 years!

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> Firms < 5 years old present GIPS-compliant performance since inception and build toward 10 years.

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Account / Portfolio Type | Include in Composite? | Include in Total Firm Assets? | GIPS Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actual, Fee-Paying, Discretionary | MANDATORY (Must include in ≥1) | Yes | Prevents cherry-picking winners |
| Terminated Discretionary Account | Include through last full period | No (after exit) | Eliminates survivorship bias |
| Non-Discretionary Client Account | PROHIBITED (Never include!) | Yes (Included in Firm AUM!) | Client constraints prevent strategy execution |
| Simulated / Backtested Model | PROHIBITED (Never in composite) | No (Not actual assets) | Prevents hypothetical return mixing |
| Non-Fee-Paying Discretionary | Optional (Disclose % if included) | Yes | Transparency on pro-bono/seed accounts |

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** When a discretionary, fee-paying client terminates their investment management contract with a GIPS-compliant firm on November 15, how must that client's historical performance be treated in the composite?

- **[A]** The client's entire historical performance must be removed from the composite retroactively

- **[B]** The client's performance must remain in the composite's historical returns for all periods up through the last full measurement period prior to termination (e.g., through October 31)

- **[C]** The client's performance is moved to a non-discretionary composite

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** To prevent survivorship bias, GIPS mandates that terminated portfolios remain in the historical composite record through their last full measurement period before termination.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** An investment firm was founded 3 years ago and has managed all of its portfolios in strict accordance with GIPS since Day 1. Can the firm claim GIPS compliance today?

- **[A]** No, a firm must wait until it has at least 5 full years of history before claiming GIPS compliance

- **[B]** Yes, a firm in existence for less than 5 years may claim GIPS compliance by presenting its GIPS-compliant performance since inception (3 years) and then adding an additional year of performance each year until it builds a 10-year track record

- **[C]** Yes, if it backtests 2 years of simulated returns to reach 5 years

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Firms in existence for less than 5 years present GIPS-compliant performance since inception; linking simulated backtested returns to actual composite returns is strictly prohibited.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** If a local country's securities regulation conflicts with a specific requirement of the Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS), what must a GIPS-compliant firm operating in that country do?

- **[A]** Violate local law in order to follow GIPS

- **[B]** Comply with the local law or regulation AND make full disclosure of the conflict in the GIPS report

- **[C]** Drop its claim of GIPS compliance

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Under GIPS (and Standard I(A)), when local law conflicts with GIPS, the firm must follow local law and disclose the nature of the conflict in the GIPS report.
