## Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

> [!NOTE]
> The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

### Formula 1: GP/LP Fee & Waterfall Net Return Structure

```formula
Management Fee = % × Committed Capital (PE) or % × NAV (Hedge Funds)

Carried Interest = Incentive % × (Profits above Hurdle Rate)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- Private equity charges management fees on Total Committed Capital during the investment period.

- hedge funds charge management fees on Net Asset Value (NAV).

### Formula 2: European (Whole-of-Fund) vs. American (Deal-by-Deal) Waterfall

```formula
European Waterfall: LPs receive 100% of contributed capital + hurdle across ALL deals before GP receives carry

American: Deal-by-deal carry (requires Clawback)
```

**Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:**

- European waterfalls are more favorable to Limited Partners (LPs).

- American waterfalls pay GP carry earlier on winning deals but require a clawback clause if later deals lose money.

## Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 1: Comparing European (Whole-of-Fund) vs. American (Deal-by-Deal) Carried Interest Waterfalls

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A PE fund raises $100M committed capital (20% GP carry, 0% hurdle for simplicity across 2 deals of $50M cost each). In Year 3, Deal 1 exits for $80M (+$30M profit). In Year 5, Deal 2 exits for $40M (−$10M loss). Total fund profit across both deals = +$20M.

```formula
Management Fee = % × Committed Capital (PE) or % × NAV (Hedge Funds)

Carried Interest = Incentive % × (Profits above Hurdle Rate)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

- **Step 1. American (Deal-by-Deal) Waterfall: In Year 3, GP takes 20% × $30M = $6.0M carry on Deal**

- **Step 1. In Year 5, Deal 2 loses −$10M, so cumulative fund profit is only $20M (true total carry owed = 20% × $20M = $4.0M). Under a Clawback provision, the GP must return $2.0M ($6.0M − $4.0M) to LPs!**

- **Step 2. European (Whole-of-Fund) Waterfall: In Year 3, all $80M from Deal 1 goes to LPs toward returning their $100M total capital ($0 to GP in Year 3). In Year 5, out of Deal 2's $40M, the first $20M completes the $100M LP return of capital, and the remaining $20M profit is split $16M to LPs and $4.0M to GP.**

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> European waterfalls protect LPs by returning 100% of total fund paid-in capital before paying a single dollar of carried interest to the GP.

### Worked Example 2: Soft Hurdle (With Catch-Up) vs. Hard Hurdle Waterfall Distribution

**Problem & Given Scenario:**
A $100M PE fund exits for $130M after 1 year (+30.0% gross return = $30M profit). The fund has an 8.0% ($8M) hurdle rate and 20% GP performance fee. Compare GP performance fee under (A) Hard Hurdle vs. (B) Soft Hurdle with 100% Catch-Up.

```formula
European Waterfall: LPs receive 100% of contributed capital + hurdle across ALL deals before GP receives carry

American: Deal-by-deal carry (requires Clawback)
```

**Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:**

Case A (Hard Hurdle — GP gets 20% ONLY on profits above 8%): GP Carry = 20% × ($30M − $8M) = 20% × $22M = $4.40M (LP gets $125.60M). Case B (Soft Hurdle with Catch-Up — once 8% is cleared, GP catches up to 20% of ALL $30M profits): First $8M goes to LP; next $2M goes 100% to GP (so GP has $2M / $10M = 20%); remaining $20M split 80/20 ($4M to GP) -> Total GP Carry = $2.0M + $4.0M = $6.00M (= 20% × $30M!).

> [!TIP]
> **Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway:**
> A hard hurdle reduces GP carry ($4.40M vs $6.00M) because the first 8% return is permanently exempt from performance fees.

## Comparison & Reference Analysis

| Access Method | LP Control & Customization | Fee Layering | Typical Institutional Investor |
|---|---|---|---|

## CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Practice Question 1

**Question:** In a private equity limited partnership, which distribution waterfall structure is most favorable to the Limited Partners (LPs) because the General Partner (GP) receives zero carried interest until 100% of total LP paid-in capital across the entire fund has been returned?

- **[A]** American (deal-by-deal) waterfall without a clawback

- **[B]** European (whole-of-fund) waterfall

- **[C]** American waterfall with a soft hurdle

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Under a European (whole-of-fund) waterfall, all cash inflows from early exits go to repaying total LP contributed capital (and preferred return) before the GP collects any carried interest.

### Practice Question 2

**Question:** A private equity fund generates a 16.0% annual return before fees. It charges a 20% performance fee subject to an 8.0% HARD hurdle rate (no catch-up clause). The performance fee earned by the General Partner is:

- **[A]** 1.60% (20% of the excess return above 8.0%)

- **[B]** 3.20% (20% of the entire 16.0% return)

- **[C]** 2.40%

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option A.**
>
> **Explanation:** Under a hard hurdle rate, the GP earns 20% only on the return in excess of the 8.0% hurdle: 20% × (16.0% − 8.0%) = 1.60%.

### Practice Question 3

**Question:** Because private real estate and private equity funds value illiquid holdings using periodic quarterly appraisals rather than continuous market prices, their reported historical return series artificially exhibit:

- **[A]** Overstated standard deviation and overstated correlation with public equities

- **[B]** Smoothed returns with understated standard deviation, positive serial correlation, and artificially overstated Sharpe ratios

- **[C]** Zero survivorship bias

> [!TIP]
> **Correct Answer: Option B.**
>
> **Explanation:** Appraisal smoothing dampens apparent quarter-to-quarter volatility (understating σ and beta) and inflates reported Sharpe ratios.

## Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

- **Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.**
