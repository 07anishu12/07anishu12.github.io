Equity value belongs to ordinary shareholders; enterprise value measures the operating business across financing claims, using a defined bridge. A common expression is equity value plus debt, preferred claims and non-controlling interests, less cash and specified non-operating assets. Check definitions carefully: the bridge must match the assets and earnings included in the valuation.

## Know which claim you are valuing

For a listed company, market capitalisation is share price multiplied by the relevant shares outstanding. That is an equity measure. Buying ordinary shares gives a residual claim after obligations to creditors and other senior capital providers. The market capitalisation is not automatically the price required to acquire the entire company.

Enterprise value aims to separate operating value from financing choices. [CFA Institute's market-multiples reading](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/market-based-valuation-price-enterprise-value-multiples?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) distinguishes price multiples from enterprise multiples. The numerator and earnings denominator should refer to the same stakeholders and business perimeter.

This is why EV is paired with operating measures such as EBITDA, while equity value is paired with earnings attributable to ordinary shareholders. A numerator that includes lenders' claims should not casually be divided by profit after interest belonging only to shareholders.

## Build a numerical bridge

Consider a hypothetical company with equity market value ₹800 crore, debt ₹300 crore, preferred capital ₹20 crore, non-controlling interests ₹40 crore and cash ₹100 crore. Under a simple consistent definition, EV is `800+300+20+40-100`, or ₹1,060 crore.

Reverse the calculation to estimate equity from an operating valuation: `Equity = EV-Debt-Preferred-NCI+Cash`, before any additional required adjustments. If operating EV is instead estimated at ₹1,200 crore using the same balance-sheet items, equity value is ₹940 crore.

With ten crore relevant diluted shares, the simplified value per share is ₹94. Review the treatment of options, convertibles and other dilutive claims rather than dividing by a convenient historic share count. A security treated as debt in the bridge should not also be counted as converted equity without a consistent adjustment.

## Understand cash and minority interests

Cash is commonly subtracted because it is not the operating business being valued in an unlevered operating model. However, not every cash balance is freely distributable. Restricted balances, regulatory requirements, trapped cash or cash essential for operations can require a more careful treatment.

Non-controlling interests matter when consolidated earnings include 100% of a subsidiary but the parent owns less than 100%. Adding the other owners' claim to the numerator helps align EV with that consolidated denominator. Alternatively, a valuation can separately model the parent's proportionate interests, but the method must remain consistent.

Non-operating investments and associates can create similar perimeter issues. If their earnings are excluded from operating EBITDA, their value may need to be handled separately. Read the financial statement notes rather than assuming a data-provider EV contains exactly the adjustments your analysis requires.

## Apply the bridge to the actual decision

A quoted EV is not necessarily identical to transaction cash paid at closing. Acquisitions can involve debt refinancing, working-capital adjustments, fees, contingent consideration and retained cash. A deal described as cash-free and debt-free needs precise contractual definitions, not merely a classroom formula.

Lease liabilities require consistent treatment across EBITDA, enterprise value and peer comparisons. Accounting changes can affect both the numerator and denominator. Pension obligations, provisions and other debt-like items may also be material, but there is no universal rule that every liability is debt-like.

[Damodaran's valuation resources](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/valuation/val.htm) treat cash and other claims as distinct valuation questions. Use the bridge as a transparent reconciliation table with a source, date and rationale for each adjustment. Avoid hiding judgement inside one unexplained net-debt line.

Before presenting a conclusion, reconcile both directions and test whether the implied per-share value uses the correct share count. Clearly separate market-observed equity value from model-estimated equity value. The arithmetic can be verified; the classification and valuation of each claim still require company-specific evidence.

## Quick questions, clear answers

### Can enterprise value be negative?

It can be under a particular definition when cash and relevant non-operating assets exceed equity and added claims. That does not automatically mean an investor receives free operating assets: access to cash, liabilities, business losses and data quality still need investigation.

### Is all debt recorded at book value?

Not necessarily. Market-value debt is conceptually relevant, although book debt may be used as an approximation where appropriate. For distressed or long-dated debt, the difference can be material. State the convention and check whether it fits the valuation purpose.

### Why do two websites show different EV figures?

They may use different dates, share counts, cash definitions, lease treatment or minority-interest adjustments. Rebuild the bridge from primary filings before treating either number as authoritative. A label alone does not establish that two data fields describe the same measure.
