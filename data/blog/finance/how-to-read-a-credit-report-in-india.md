Read a credit report as a record of accounts, repayment history and lender enquiries, rather than judging it only by the score. Check whether every entry belongs to you, matches your documents and has a clear reporting date. Dispute inaccurate data through the bureau and reporting lender; a genuine adverse history is different from a correctable error.

## Verify identity and account ownership

Start with the report date, name, birth date, identification details and contact information. Older addresses may reflect past applications rather than fraud, but unfamiliar identification or accounts require investigation. Avoid sharing a full report publicly because it contains sensitive personal and financial information.

[CIBIL's report guide](https://www.cibil.com/faq/understand-your-credit-score-and-report) identifies personal, employment, account and enquiry sections. For each account, review the lender, facility type, opening date, ownership role, reported limit or loan amount, current balance and payment history.

A joint or guaranteed exposure can appear even if it is not the loan you personally spend. Understand the obligation before treating it as a mistaken account. If you cannot recognise a lender name, check whether it differs from the consumer-facing brand, while avoiding assumptions that an unfamiliar entry must be legitimate.

## Read payment history and status carefully

DPD means days past due in the relevant monthly record. Numeric values indicate reported delay, while other codes require the report's official glossary. Do not interpret a missing-data symbol as proof that the account was paid on time.

[CIBIL's explanatory brochure](https://www.cibil.com/content/dam/cibil/consumer/TU-FAQ-Broucher-Online_21-11-17.pdf) explains DPD and status terminology. It is an older glossary, so confirm current display codes in the report being reviewed rather than assuming every screen uses the same layout.

Distinguish paid in full, closed, settled and written-off descriptions. A settlement can involve less than the full contractual amount, and a zero current balance does not necessarily erase the account's past status. Obtain the lender's closure or no-dues documents and compare them with the reported fields.

Consider a hypothetical loan repaid in full on 10 September while the report still shows an overdue balance as of an earlier date. First compare the reporting date and payment confirmation. If the current reported position remains wrong, request correction with evidence. Do not silently assume a processing delay explains every discrepancy.

## Review enquiries and organise evidence

An enquiry records access associated with a credit application under the relevant reporting process. Check the institution, date and purpose against applications you made. An unrecognised enquiry may require clarification or a dispute, particularly if accompanied by an unfamiliar account.

Prepare an issues table with account identifier, field, reported value, expected value, supporting document and action requested. For example: “Account repaid in full on the attached receipt date; current balance should reflect the lender-confirmed closure.” This is more actionable than requesting an unexplained score increase.

Preserve payment receipts, statements, lender correspondence and report versions. Mask identifiers when sharing material with an adviser unless the complete details are necessary through a secure channel. Never give a third party banking passwords or one-time codes to obtain a promised credit repair.

## Use the official dispute and escalation process

[CIBIL's dispute FAQ](https://www.cibil.com/contact-us-faq) describes raising an item through the report's dispute centre, selecting the relevant field and tracking the dispute ID. It also states that CIBIL needs confirmation from the reporting credit institution before modifying supplied records.

Contact the lender as well and keep written references. Track the submission date and response, then inspect the corrected report rather than assuming a closure message means every field changed. A correction does not guarantee a particular score movement or loan approval.

Applicable reporting frequency, complaint deadlines, escalation rights and compensation rules can change. Check the current RBI framework and the bureau's official grievance information for the specific complaint. This guide does not promise a fixed resolution time or compensation amount and does not rely on old website statements about monthly reporting as current law.

The practical goal is a complete, accurate record and responsible ongoing repayment. Address errors specifically, retain evidence and escalate unresolved issues through the authorised channels. No personal report has been reviewed in preparing this guide.

## Quick questions, clear answers

### Can a bureau delete an accurate default on request?

A dispute process addresses inaccurate or incomplete information, not an unrestricted right to erase genuine history. If a lender agrees that a field is wrong, correction follows the applicable process. Be sceptical of paid services guaranteeing removal of accurate adverse records.

### Why does my closed loan still appear?

An account's historical presence is different from whether its current status and balance are correct. Compare the closure date, reported status and payment history with the lender documents. A closed account need not disappear simply because the current obligation has ended.

### Will resolving an error guarantee approval?

No. Correct data improves the reliability of assessment, but lenders also consider income, obligations, collateral and internal policy. Ask the lender about the specific application outcome. Do not interpret a corrected field as a promise about a particular score or borrowing limit.
