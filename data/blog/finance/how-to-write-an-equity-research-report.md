An equity research report should explain what the business is worth under stated assumptions, why the market price may differ, and what could invalidate the view. Begin with evidence and a testable thesis. A target price is an estimate, not a promise, and publishing research or recommendations may involve Indian regulatory obligations that must be assessed separately.

## Define the question and evidence base

Identify the company, security, valuation date, current price source and reporting currency. Explain the business model, customers, competitive position and principal revenue drivers. Separate audited financials, management guidance, third-party estimates and your own assumptions.

Collect annual reports, exchange filings, results announcements and relevant operating disclosures. Maintain a source ledger with dates and page references. A claim repeated by several websites may still originate from one unverified statement, so trace important facts to the original evidence.

[CFA Institute's diligence standard](https://www.cfainstitute.org/standards/professionals/code-ethics-standards/standards-of-practice-v-a) calls for an adequate research basis. Treat missing information as a limitation to investigate, not permission to invent customer metrics, market shares or management comments.

## Write a thesis that can be challenged

A thesis should connect a business development to financial outcomes and valuation. “The company is a market leader” is descriptive and needs evidence. “Improved collection terms could reduce receivables and release cash” is a mechanism that can be modelled, tested and challenged.

Use a few specific propositions, with supporting and contrary evidence. Distinguish a good company from a good investment at the current price. Strong growth may already be reflected in valuation; weak recent results may already be expected.

For a synthetic example, assume a company's receivable days fall from 90 to 75 with annual revenue ₹1,000 crore. A simple 365-day model implies approximately `1000/365*15`, or ₹41.1 crore less receivables, all else equal. That is a working-capital scenario, not automatically recurring annual profit or verified company guidance.

## Build forecasts, valuation and catalysts

Forecast revenue, margins, taxes and reinvestment using defined drivers. Reconcile profit to cash and explain financing needs. Compare base, downside and upside cases, keeping accounting definitions and units consistent across them.

Use a valuation method suited to the business. A DCF should disclose cash-flow assumptions, discount rate and terminal treatment. Trading multiples should use defensible peers and comparable periods. Show the bridge from enterprise value to equity and the relevant diluted share count before presenting a per-share estimate.

In a hypothetical case, EV ₹600 crore less net debt ₹100 crore gives equity ₹500 crore. With ten crore shares and no other claims, estimated value is ₹50 per share. If an illustrative current price is ₹40, implied price upside is 25%. Neither figure describes a real security or establishes the probability of that outcome.

A catalyst is an event that might change expectations or reveal information: a result, contract milestone, capacity ramp or regulatory decision. State uncertainty and timing assumptions. A calendar date alone is not a catalyst unless it can change the investment case.

## Present risks, disclosures and an update rule

Link risks to the model: customer loss can affect revenue, input costs can affect margins, and delayed collections can affect cash. Include valuation risk, balance-sheet exposure, governance and liquidity where material. A generic sentence that markets are risky does not explain the specific downside.

[CFA Institute's communication standard](https://www.cfainstitute.org/standards/professionals/code-ethics-standards/standards-of-practice-v-b) requires members and candidates to distinguish fact from opinion and disclose significant limitations. Clearly label forecasts and target values as judgements, and explain what evidence would cause a revision.

Disclose relevant interests, conflicts, methodology and the report's scope. For Indian public dissemination, verify applicable SEBI research-analyst requirements and any exemptions with qualified guidance; an educational disclaimer does not automatically settle legal status. This guide does not determine anyone's registration obligation.

Before release, reconcile every table to the model, verify source dates and remove unsupported claims. Set a review trigger for material filings, thesis changes or new risks. A useful report remains accountable to evidence after publication rather than preserving an old recommendation because it was confidently written.

## Quick questions, clear answers

### Must every report include a buy or sell rating?

No. A report can present analytical scenarios without a directional rating. If a rating is used, define its meaning, horizon and comparison basis. Avoid implying certainty, and assess any regulatory requirements applicable to the activity and intended audience.

### Is management guidance a verified forecast?

It is a statement by management, not proof of the future outcome. Attribute it accurately, note the date and test the assumptions against operating evidence. Your own forecast can differ, but explain why rather than presenting either view as an established future fact.

### How often should the report be updated?

Use material information and the thesis to define triggers. Results, acquisitions, financing changes or evidence contradicting a central assumption may justify an update. A fixed calendar can help administration, but it should not delay communicating a change that materially affects the analysis.
