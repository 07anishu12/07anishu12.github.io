A private equity deal moves from finding an opportunity to testing an investment thesis, agreeing terms, completing diligence, funding the acquisition and managing the holding towards exit. The process is iterative: a problem found late can change price, structure or the decision to invest. A completed transaction is the start of ownership, not evidence that the investment will succeed.

## Source and screen the opportunity

Opportunities may arise through advisers, industry relationships, company approaches or the fund's own research. Initial screening checks mandate fit: sector, geography, ownership structure, company maturity, capital required and the fund's remaining investment capacity.

Write a concise thesis explaining why the company might become more valuable and what could prevent it. Separate operational improvements from favourable exit pricing. “Buy at a low multiple” is incomplete unless earnings quality and the reason for that price are understood.

[CFA Institute's private-equity reading](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/private-equity) distinguishes venture, growth and buyout strategies. Not every private-equity transaction buys control or uses heavy debt. The detailed workflow depends on the strategy and negotiated rights.

## Develop the offer and test the thesis

After appropriate confidentiality arrangements, the investor reviews financials, commercial information and management plans. A preliminary valuation and financing model help determine a possible price range. Indicative proposals may remain subject to diligence, approvals and financing.

Commercial diligence examines customers, competition, pricing and market demand. Financial diligence investigates earnings quality, working capital and debt-like items. Legal, tax, operational, technology and other specialist work address different risks. One stream should not be treated as a substitute for another.

Consider a hypothetical distributor reporting EBITDA ₹50 crore. Diligence identifies ₹5 crore of recurring costs omitted from the seller's adjusted figure. If the buyer's selected multiple is 8×, applying it to ₹45 crore rather than ₹50 crore changes enterprise value from ₹400 crore to ₹360 crore before other adjustments.

The example demonstrates why a diligence finding must flow into valuation. It does not establish the right multiple or prove that the transaction should proceed. A buyer might negotiate, change protections, revise financing or walk away depending on the evidence.

## Obtain approval and complete the transaction

An investment committee typically reviews the thesis, valuation, diligence findings, financing, downside cases and proposed protections. The precise authority and process are firm-specific. A credible investment memo includes disconfirming evidence and unresolved issues, rather than presenting only the strongest version of the case.

Definitive documents set out price mechanics, conditions, governance and allocation of identified risks. Depending on the transaction, approvals, funding commitments and completion steps may be required. Do not assume an announced agreement and a completed acquisition are the same event.

At closing, reconcile sources and uses, debt refinancing, fees, working-capital adjustments and ownership records. Material findings should have an assigned resolution: price adjustment, covenant, indemnity, insurance where appropriate, operating action or explicit acceptance. A risk does not disappear because it was included in a presentation.

## Manage ownership and prepare an exit

An ownership plan should translate the thesis into accountable actions and measurable milestones. Possible initiatives include pricing discipline, working-capital improvement, capacity expansion, management development or acquisitions. Distinguish management's operating responsibilities from the investor's governance role; arrangements vary by rights and structure.

Monitor cash, leverage, covenants, customer concentration and progress against the investment case. Additional funding needs can dilute or reduce investor returns. Report setbacks honestly and reassess whether the original plan remains credible.

[Investor.gov's private-equity overview](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/private-investment-funds/private-equity) highlights long horizons and illiquidity. Its US legal eligibility rules are not Indian fund rules, but the liquidity warning is relevant to understanding private ownership.

Exit possibilities include a strategic sale, another financial investor, a public listing or a partial secondary transaction, subject to feasibility and market conditions. Prepare reliable reporting and resolve issues early. Model proceeds after debt, costs and other claims. The investment succeeds only if realised outcomes justify the capital and risks taken; there is no guaranteed holding period or exit route.

## Quick questions, clear answers

### Does every sourced deal reach completion?

No. Screening, diligence, price disagreements, financing or approvals can stop a process. The purpose of diligence includes identifying reasons not to invest. Track the quality of decisions and evidence rather than treating a higher number of completed transactions as automatically better investing.

### What does an investment committee actually approve?

Its remit depends on the organisation, but commonly includes the investment, capital commitment, valuation and major risks within delegated limits. Approval may include conditions. Check the actual authority documents rather than assuming that one meeting resolves legal, financing or regulatory requirements.

### Is operational improvement the only return driver?

No. Returns can reflect business growth, cash generation, debt reduction, changes in valuation multiples and financial structuring. Separate these drivers in the model. A strong reported return caused mainly by favourable market pricing should not be described solely as operating value creation.
