Trading comparables use current public-market prices; precedent transactions use prices paid in earlier acquisitions. They describe different situations, dates and rights. Transactions can reflect control, buyer-specific synergies and deal competition, but they are not automatically more valuable or more reliable. Build consistent datasets and explain why each observation is relevant to the company being valued.

## Ask what each method measures

A trading-comps table relates listed-company equity or enterprise values to financial metrics. It offers a current market reference, subject to changing sentiment and peer differences. The ordinary trading price reflects the rights attached to the traded security, rather than a negotiated acquisition of all operations.

A precedent table reconstructs acquisition value from announced terms and the target's relevant financials. A [Delaware court opinion examining valuation evidence](https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=361310) illustrates why transaction-premium analysis must distinguish control from buyer synergies. This example informs the analytical distinction, not Indian legal requirements.

A transaction may involve a full business, a controlling stake, a minority investment or only selected assets. Record exactly what changed hands. A percentage stake price multiplied mechanically to 100% can misrepresent value when rights or preferences differ across securities.

## Collect the information behind each number

For trading peers, align share prices to one valuation date and use consistent historical or forecast periods. Rebuild EV from equity value, debt, cash and other relevant claims. Include growth, margins and business characteristics to explain the observed multiples.

For precedents, record announcement and completion dates, buyer, target, stake acquired, consideration type, equity value, debt treatment, enterprise value and financial period. Check contingent consideration, assumed obligations, asset disposals and whether announced figures are provisional.

Primary acquisition announcements, offer documents, exchange filings and audited reports are stronger starting points than a database label alone. If EBITDA is undisclosed, do not manufacture it from an assumed margin. Mark the multiple unavailable and explain the limitation rather than turning an incomplete deal into a misleading observation.

## Calculate a hypothetical comparison

Suppose a target produces comparable EBITDA of ₹40 crore. Synthetic trading peers show a selected range of 7×–9× EV/EBITDA, implying EV ₹280–₹360 crore. A synthetic precedent set shows 8×–11×, implying ₹320–₹440 crore.

If target net debt is ₹60 crore with no other bridge adjustments, those ranges imply equity ₹220–₹300 crore and ₹260–₹380 crore respectively. The higher precedent range in this example is an assumption, not evidence that acquisition multiples always exceed trading multiples.

For a separately observed public-target offer, hypothetical offer price ₹130 versus an unaffected reference price ₹100 implies a 30% equity premium: `130/100-1`. State the reference date. The EV uplift need not also be 30%, because debt and cash adjustments do not necessarily rise with the equity offer.

## Explain control, synergies and timing

Control can allow changes to operations, strategy or financing. A buyer may also expect synergies unavailable to an ordinary stand-alone investor. The price paid depends on how those benefits are shared between buyer and seller, alongside bargaining, financing and execution risk.

Do not add a generic control premium on top of precedent multiples that already reflect acquisition prices. Equally, do not assume every transaction contains the same rights or synergy potential. A distressed sale can have very different economics from a competitive strategic auction.

Old transactions may reflect different interest rates, market conditions, regulations and industry prospects. Current trading comps can be timely but imperfectly comparable; precedents can be economically relevant but stale or thinly disclosed. Evaluate the evidence rather than choosing the method producing the preferred answer.

Conclude with separate ranges, a reconciliation of their differences and a stated purpose: minority investment, sale discussion or strategic acquisition. [CFA Institute's multiples framework](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/market-based-valuation-price-enterprise-value-multiples?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) reinforces that benchmark comparisons require consistent definitions. No real deal sample or current premium estimate is established in this guide; every numerical case is hypothetical.

## Quick questions, clear answers

### Are precedent transactions always the highest valuation?

No. Acquisition prices depend on target condition, timing, rights, synergies and negotiation. Distress or limited competition can produce low prices. The common expectation of a control premium is a reason to investigate differences, not a rule allowing an analyst to force the outcome.

### Can I combine both methods into one median?

Usually keep them separate because they measure different contexts. A combined statistic can hide timing, control and data-quality differences. Present each range, explain the evidence and use judgement alongside other methods rather than averaging incompatible observations mechanically.

### What if only two comparable deals are disclosed?

State that the dataset is thin, examine each deal closely and test how either observation changes the range. Avoid precise percentiles that imply a robust market sample. Supplement with trading evidence or a DCF where useful, without inventing missing financial details.
