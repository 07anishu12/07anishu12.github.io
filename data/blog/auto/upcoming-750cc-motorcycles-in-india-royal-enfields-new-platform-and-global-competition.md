## 1. Introduction

When Royal Enfield introduced the 650 Twin platform (Interceptor and Continental GT 650) in late 2018, it revolutionized India's middleweight motorcycling landscape by democratizing twin-cylinder performance below Rs 3 lakh. Over the past eight years, the 650 platform established Royal Enfield as an uncontested global leader in middleweight modern-classics. However, at EICMA 2026 in Milan, Royal Enfield signaled the dawn of its next great chapter with the world premiere of its all-new liquid-cooled 750cc parallel-twin platform, powering the Himalayan 750 and Continental GT 750. Representing a major technological leap into liquid cooling, higher compression, and sophisticated IMU electronics, this new 750cc platform is poised to dominate India's sub-Rs 5 lakh motorcycle market in 2027 while triggering fierce competition from global middleweight rivals.

## 2. Launch and Availability

Royal Enfield officially confirmed at EICMA that the 750cc twin platform will enter commercial production at its high-precision manufacturing facility in Vallam Vadagal, Tamil Nadu, in mid-2027. The Himalayan 750 will spearhead the domestic rollout in late summer 2027, followed by the Continental GT 750 café racer during the high-spending 2027 festive season. Both motorcycles will be distributed through Royal Enfield's extensive domestic network of over 2,000 retail touchpoints across India.

## 3. Engine and Performance

The mechanical centerpiece of Royal Enfield's new platform is a clean-sheet 750cc, liquid-cooled, four-stroke parallel-twin engine featuring a 270-degree crankshaft, double overhead camshafts (DOHC), and four valves per cylinder. Moving from air/oil-cooling to liquid-cooling allows tighter internal tolerances, sustained highrpm highway cruising in 45°C Indian summers, and full BS6 Phase 2 (OBD-2) compliance. The engine is tuned across two distinct states: 58 bhp at 7,500 rpm and 65 Nm at 5,500 rpm in the Himalayan 750, and 62 bhp at 7,800 rpm and 68 Nm at 5,600 rpm in the Continental GT 750. Power is managed via a six-speed transmission equipped with an assist-and-slipper clutch and an optional bi-directional quickshifter.

## 4. Design and hardware

Chassis engineering on the 750 platform represents a massive upgrade over the outgoing 650 twins. Developed with Harris Performance, the chassis utilizes high-rigidity steel frames engineered to handle the 32 percent increase in horsepower. The Himalayan 750 features 43mm Showa inverted forks with 200mm of travel, a Showa linkage monoshock, a 20-litre fuel tank, and 21/17-inch tubeless cross-spoked wheels. The Continental GT 750 adopts an athletic café racer crouch with clip-on bars, rearset pegs, twin rear piggyback shocks, and dual 300mm front disc brakes clamped by radial calipers, ending previous single-disc braking limitations.

## 5. Features and technology

Electronics on the 750 twins bring Royal Enfield into the modern technological era. The cockpit features a 5.0-inch circular TFT TripperDash display powered by Google Maps full-screen navigation, smartphone pairing, and music controls. An advanced six-axis IMU coordinates lean-sensitive Cornering ABS and multilevel Traction Control. Multiple riding modes (Road, Rain, and Off-Road on the Himalayan) allow tailored throttle response and switchable rear ABS for dirt sliding.

## 6. Expected price and market positioning

Royal Enfield's decisive commercial weapon is domestic manufacturing economics. While imported middleweights from Kawasaki and Yamaha retail between Rs 6.80 lakh and Rs 10.50 lakh, Royal Enfield's 100 percent localized sourcing in Tamil Nadu enables an astonishingly competitive price structure. The Continental GT 750 is projected to launch between Rs 3.80 lakh and Rs 4.20 lakh, while the Himalayan 750 will sit between Rs 4.20 lakh and Rs 4.60 lakh ex-showroom. This pricing undercuts global competitors by over Rs 3 lakh, creating an unassailable value moat.

## 9. Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Will Royal Enfield discontinue the 650 twins when the 750 launches?

No, Royal Enfield will sell the air/oil-cooled 650 twins alongside the new liquid-cooled 750 twins, serving as an accessible entry tier.

### Q: How much more power does the 750cc engine produce compared to the 650cc?

The 750cc liquid-cooled engine produces 58 to 62 bhp, representing a substantial 23% to 32% power increase over the 47 bhp 648cc engine.

### Q: Does the Himalayan 750 have tubeless spoked wheels?

Yes, the Himalayan 750 comes equipped with 21-inch front and 17-inch rear cross-spoked tubeless wheels as standard equipment.

### Q: When will the 750 twins be available for test rides in India?

Royal Enfield is scheduled to open customer test rides and pre-bookings in India in late summer 2027 ahead of the festive season.
