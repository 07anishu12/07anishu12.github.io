In India, a new car's true mandatory on-road price equals the Ex-Showroom Price (which already includes factory cost, GST, and dealer margin) plus State RTO Road Tax and Registration Fees, Compulsory Motor Insurance (3-year third-party + 1-year own-damage), a ₹500–₹600 FASTag, Hypothecation Fee (₹1,500 if financed), and 1% Tax Collected at Source (TCS) if the ex-showroom price exceeds ₹10,00,000. Accessories, ceramic coatings, and logistics/handling fees are not legally mandatory.


## The 5 Mandatory Building Blocks of an Indian On-Road Price


When a salesperson hands you a price quotation (proforma invoice), separate the numbers into statutory requirements versus dealership profit centres:

1. **Ex-Showroom Price:** Set by the automaker for your city. It already includes the ex-factory price, Goods and Services Tax (GST plus Compensation Cess ranging from 29% on small petrol cars to 50% on large SUVs), freight to the dealership, and the dealer's base margin.
1. **State RTO Road Tax & Registration:** Collected by the state transport department. It varies dramatically by state—ranging from roughly 4%–8% on petrol cars in Delhi/Himachal to 11%–14% in Maharashtra and 14%–20% in Karnataka and Telangana. Also includes a ₹600 smart-card RC fee, ₹400–₹600 High Security Registration Plate (HSRP) charge, and ₹1,500 hypothecation endorsement if you take a car loan.
1. **1% TCS (Tax Collected at Source):** Under Section 206C(1F) of the Income Tax Act, if a car's ex-showroom invoice exceeds ₹10,00,000 (even at ₹10,01,000), the dealer must collect 1% TCS (₹10,010+). **Crucial fact:** This is NOT an extra tax; it appears in your Form 26AS and can be claimed back or adjusted against your annual income tax liability when filing your ITR.
1. **Motor Insurance (1-Year OD + 3-Year TP):** By Supreme Court mandate, every new private car must carry 3 years of Third-Party (TP) liability cover, typically bundled with 1 year of Own-Damage (OD) plus Zero-Depreciation, Return-to-Invoice (RTI), and Engine Protector add-ons.
1. **FASTag:** Mandatory toll tag costing ₹400–₹600 (including security deposit and initial balance).

## 3 Charges You Should Always Question or Negotiate

- **Inflated Showroom Insurance Quotes:** Dealerships often mark up the Own-Damage premium by 35% to 50% over direct insurer rates. Get an online quote from two tier-1 insurers (ensuring Zero-Dep, Engine Protect, Consumables, and RTI are ticked) and ask the dealer to match it—most dealers will immediately cut ₹15,000–₹25,000 off their insurance quote.
- **'Logistics', 'Depot', or 'Handling' Charges:** Courts and state transport departments have repeatedly ruled that charging buyers separate handling or unloading fees over the ex-showroom price is unlawful. Ask for it to be waived.
- **Pre-Bundled 'Essential Accessory Kits' & Teflon/Ceramic Packages:** Floor mats, mud flaps, and idol/perfume kits billed at ₹12,000–₹18,000 are completely optional. Buy only the specific individual part numbers you actually want.

## Smart Buyer Action Plan Before Paying the Booking Amount

- **Ask for an itemised proforma invoice** before paying your booking token so you can compare the bottom-line on-road total across two different dealership groups in your city.
- **Check the ₹9.99 Lakh Ex-Showroom Threshold:** In many states, road tax jumps by 2%–3% AND 1% TCS kicks in the moment ex-showroom crosses ₹10.00 lakh. Choosing a variant at ₹9.95 lakh instead of ₹10.05 lakh can save ₹35,000–₹45,000 on-road.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Can a car dealership legally refuse delivery if I buy insurance from outside?

No. Under IRDAI and Competition Commission principles, a dealer cannot force you to buy insurance through their in-house broker. However, asking the dealer to match an external quote within ₹2,000–₹3,000 often gives you both the price saving and convenient cashless claim coordination at their workshop.

### Q2. Why did my car's on-road price jump by ₹45,000 when the ex-showroom price increased by only ₹10,000 across ₹10 lakh?

Cross-stepping the ₹10,00,000 ex-showroom mark triggers two simultaneous jumps in most Indian states: the state road tax slab typically increases by 2%–3% on the entire vehicle value, and the 1% TCS (₹10,000+) becomes payable upfront.

### Q3. What is BH-Series registration and how does it reduce upfront on-road price?

If you are a central/state government employee or work for a private company with offices in four or more states/UTs, Bharat (BH) Series registration lets you pay road tax in 2-year instalments instead of a 15-year lump sum, reducing your day-one on-road cash outlay by ₹60,000 to ₹1.50 lakh.

### Q4. Is Extended Warranty worth keeping on the invoice?

Yes. Unlike paint coatings or chrome kits, an official manufacturer Extended Warranty (covering years 4 and 5) protects expensive AC compressors, steering racks, and infotainment modules and is fully transferable if you sell the car.

### Q5. Should I get the Parivahan tax receipt from the dealer?

Always collect the official Parivahan e-receipt for road tax and registration at delivery. Verify that the exact tax paid to the government matches what the dealership billed you on the proforma invoice.
