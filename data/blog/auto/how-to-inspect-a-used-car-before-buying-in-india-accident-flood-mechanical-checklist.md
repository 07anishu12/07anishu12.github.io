To inspect a used car safely in India, follow two non-negotiable rules before you even touch the car: inspect it only in bright outdoor daylight (never in a dim basement or at night) and insist that the engine is stone-cold before you arrive. Then execute a 5-stage audit: (1) check structural pillars and aprons for factory spot-welds, (2) check under-carpet wiring and seatbelt anchors for flood silt, (3) check cold radiator coolant and engine blow-by, (4) verify every dashboard warning light and glass date stamp, and (5) complete a 20-minute road test followed by a hoist check at an authorised workshop.


## Stage 1: Spotting Structural Accidents vs Harmless Bumper Repaints


In Indian city traffic, a repainted plastic bumper or minor door scratch repair is normal and harmless. What you must **reject immediately** is a car whose structural skeleton (A/B/C pillars, roof rails, front apron beams, firewall, or rear boot floor) was bent and hammered back:

1. **The Rubber Beading Spot-Weld Test (Takes 30 Seconds):** Gently pull down 12 inches of the black rubber weatherstripping seal around each door frame and tailgate opening.
1. **Check All 6 Glass Date Codes:** Every window and windshield has a manufacturer logo with a year number and month dots (e.g., `..22`). If a 2022 car has a left rear door glass and quarter glass stamped `24`, that side suffered an impact.
1. **Front Apron & Radiator Core Support:** Open the bonnet and inspect the two structural side aprons holding the suspension strut towers and the metal bolts on the bonnet hinges.

## Stage 2: The 4-Point Indian Monsoon Flood-Damage Test


After urban flash floods in Mumbai, Chennai, Delhi NCR, or Bengaluru, thousands of waterlogged cars are dried out and sold across states. Spot them immediately:

- **Pull All 4 Seatbelts to the Very End:** Nobody washes the bottom 6 inches of seatbelt webbing coiled inside the B-pillar reel. Brown mud stains, water rings, or a damp mildew smell at the end of the belt prove water entered the cabin.
- **Look Under the Front Seat Rails & Steering Column:** Shine your phone flashlight at the bare unpainted metal brackets under the driver's seat and high up behind the brake pedal. Surface rust on unpainted interior metal means high humidity/flooding.
- **Check the Fuse Box & Spare-Wheel Well:** Look for dried beige silt in the corners of the engine-bay fuse box and under the boot carpet.

| Inspection Stage | Exact Physical Check to Perform | Healthy Pass Sign | Immediate Walk-Away Red Flag |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Structural Skeleton | Pull door rubber seals on A/B/C pillars & boot | Uniform circular factory robotic spot-welds | Putty filler, missing dimples, or uneven weld beads |
| 2. Flood / Waterlog | Pull seatbelts fully out; inspect under-seat brackets | Clean dry webbing; bright silver under-dash metal | Mud silt rings on belt end; rusted seat/steering brackets |
| 3. Cold Engine & Bay | Open cold radiator cap; check oil cap & exhaust smoke | Clear pink/green coolant; honey-brown oil; no smoke | Milky coffee oil on cap (head gasket!) or blue smoke |
| 4. Cluster & Electronics | Switch ignition ON; test AC, all windows & cameras | Airbag, ABS & Check-Engine lights glow then turn OFF | Airbag/ABS light never turns ON at key-on (bulb taped!) |
| 5. Road Test & Hoist | Full-lock U-turn; rough-road drive; 4th-gear pull | Silent suspension; crisp clutch; straight braking | Clicking CV joints, slipping clutch, or oil-wet underbody |

## Stage 3 & 4: Cold Engine Bay & Dashboard Warning-Light Audit

- **Turn Ignition ON (Without Starting):** Confirm that the **Check Engine, ABS, SRS Airbag, Oil Pressure, and EPS lights all illuminate** and then **turn OFF one by one** after starting. Unscrupulous sellers sometimes tape over or disconnect a glowing Airbag or ABS LED behind the speedometer!
- **Cold Start & Blow-By:** Listen for metallic timing-chain rattle during the first 3 seconds of a cold start, verify green/pink coolant in the radiator, and check that the exhaust emits zero blue smoke.

## Stage 5: The 20-Minute Road Test & Authorised Workshop Hoist Check


Drive over broken roads (listening for suspension clunks), execute full left and right U-turns (listening for 'tak-tak-tak' driveshaft CV-joint clicks), test 4th-gear uphill acceleration (for clutch slip), and test hands-lightly-off alignment at 50 km/h on a safe straight road.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Why should I insist the seller NOT warm up the car before I arrive?

Many engine and gearbox faults—such as worn piston rings smoking at startup, timing-chain tensioner rattle, weak batteries, and hard cold shifting—disappear once the engine reaches 90°C operating temperature. Touch the bonnet carefully when you arrive to confirm the engine is cold.

### Q2. Should I reject a used car if the front bumper and one door have been repainted?

No. In crowded Indian cities, 80% of 4-year-old cars have had a bumper or door panel repainted to fix biker scratches. As long as the door opens and closes smoothly and the structural A/B/C pillars, sills, and aprons underneath are untouched factory steel, cosmetic paintwork is only a ₹5,000–₹10,000 price-negotiation point.

### Q3. What does milky, mayonnaise-like paste under the engine oil filler cap mean?

Thick frothy beige/white paste on the underside of the oil cap or dipstick indicates that engine coolant is leaking into the engine oil through a blown cylinder head gasket or cracked oil cooler—walk away immediately.

### Q4. Can I hire a professional technician to inspect the car at the seller's home?

Yes, third-party inspection services and experienced local mechanics offer doorstep checks. However, taking the car to the brand's authorised service centre for a paid Pre-Purchase Inspection gives you two advantages doorstep mechanics cannot match: access to the brand's national service history database and a hydraulic two-post lift to inspect the underbody.

### Q5. How do I check if the tyres are wearing unevenly due to a bent suspension or chassis?

Turn the front wheels fully to one side and run your palm flat across the inner, middle, and outer tread blocks of both front and rear tyres. If the inner edge is worn bald while the outer edge has deep tread, the suspension camber/toe alignment or control arm is out of spec.
