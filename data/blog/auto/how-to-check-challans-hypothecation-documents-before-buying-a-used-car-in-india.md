Before paying any advance for a used car in India, run five mandatory checks using the car's Registration Number and full 17-character Chassis (VIN) Number: (1) check pending traffic fines on BOTH `echallan.parivahan.gov.in` AND your state traffic police / `vcourts.gov.in` Virtual Court portal (an RTO will block RC transfer until all challans are ₹0); (2) check the 'Financer / Hypothecated To' field on the official mParivahan app; (3) verify 'Blacklist / NOC Status' on Vahan; (4) physically match the stamped metal Chassis and Engine numbers on the car body against the Original RC; and (5) check the NCB percentage on the insurance policy to confirm whether an accident claim was filed last year.


## 1. The 3-Portal Traffic Challan Check (Why One App Isn't Enough)


Under updated Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) rules, **an RTO will NOT process a Transfer of Ownership or Hypothecation Termination if even a single unpaid traffic challan is pending against the vehicle**.


Never rely on a third-party app alone. Check all three official portals using the Registration No. + Last 5 digits of Chassis/Engine No.:

1. **Central Parivahan eChallan Portal (`echallan.parivahan.gov.in`):** Shows camera and handheld device challans across NHAI and state highways.
1. **Virtual Courts Portal (`vcourts.gov.in`):** When an eChallan remains unpaid for 60–90 days, the traffic police automatically forward it to the online **Virtual Traffic Court**—where it disappears from the basic 'Pending' tab on some apps and sits waiting for judicial fine settlement!
1. **Local State/City Traffic Police Portal (e.g., Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad Traffic Police):** Catches local municipal camera notices before they sync to central Vahan.

## 2. Auditing Bank Hypothecation (HP) & The 90-Day Bank NOC Trap


Look at the physical RC card AND the **mParivahan app** under *'Financer Name / Hypothecated To'*. Three scenarios can appear:

- **Scenario A — 'NA / None' on Both RC and mParivahan:** The car is 100% debt-free. You are good to go.
- **Scenario B — Seller Says 'Loan Is Paid Off, Here Are the Bank NOC Papers':** **Check the Date on the Bank NOC!** A Bank Loan Closure No Objection Certificate (NOC) and signed **Form 35** are **legally valid at the.
- **Scenario C — Loan Is Still Active:** Never hand loan-closure money to the seller's personal account.

## 3. How the Insurance Policy Reveals Hidden Accident Claims in 10 Seconds


Want to know if the seller made an accident claim last year? Look at the **'No Claim Bonus (NCB) %'** line printed on the seller's current Own-Damage insurance schedule:

- If a 3-year-old single-owner car has had **zero claims**, its NCB will be **35% or 45%**.
- If the NCB printed on the policy is **0%**, either the seller made an accident claim in the previous policy year (resetting NCB to zero!) or let the policy lapse for >90 days.

## 4. Physical Metal VIN & Engine Number Match


Locate the physical **17-character Chassis Number (VIN)** stamped into the metal body (usually under a plastic flap below the front seat, on the firewall in the engine bay, or on the B-pillar) and inspect the metal surrounding the stamped letters.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Can I submit Hypothecation Termination (Form 35) and Ownership Transfer (Form 29/30) together at the RTO?

Yes! If the seller has a valid, unexpired Bank NOC and two copies of Form 35 (signed by both the Bank Manager with seal and the Seller), you can file Hypothecation Termination + Transfer of Ownership simultaneously in a single Parivahan application, saving a separate 3-week RTO cycle.

### Q2. What does 'Blacklist Status: Flagged' mean on the Parivahan Vahan portal?

An RTO or police authority can blacklist a vehicle on Vahan for several reasons: multiple unpaid camera challans, involvement in a hit-and-run or police FIR, unpaid commercial/state tax, or a bank recovery dispute. A blacklisted vehicle cannot be legally sold or transferred until the issuing authority clears the flag.

### Q3. Why should I check that the physical RC says 'Original' and not 'DRC (Duplicate RC)'?

Sometimes an unscrupulous owner pledges the Original RC with an informal private financier and obtains a 'Duplicate RC (DRC)' claiming the original was lost. If the RC has 'DRC' printed on it, exercise extra caution, verify the police lost-report, and double-check mParivahan.

### Q4. How can I decode the manufacturing month and year from the 17-character VIN / Chassis Number?

Every Indian automaker encodes the Manufacturing Month and Year into specific characters of the 17-digit VIN (typically the 10th and 11th or 10th and 12th characters, or on a 2-character code stamped after the 17 digits on Maruti/Hyundai cars). Matching the VIN decode against the year printed on the RC confirms the car was not a 2-year-old showroom leftover.

### Q5. Should I keep a copy of the seller's PAN Card and Aadhaar Card in my permanent file?

Yes. Keep self-attested copies of the seller's Aadhaar and PAN card, the signed Sale Agreement, the time-stamped Delivery Note, and the Parivahan fee receipt in your home folder permanently—they prove clean title acquisition whenever you eventually sell the car years later.
