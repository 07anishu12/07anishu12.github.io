Choose the Hyundai Grand i10 Nios if you want a quieter four-cylinder engine, superior dashboard quality, rear AC vents, and a ₹45,000–₹65,000 lower on-road price for equivalent mid-trim features. Choose the Maruti Suzuki Swift if you drive more kilometres per month and prioritise 3 to 4 km/l higher real-world fuel efficiency, sharper steering feedback, a wider cabin footprint, and slightly stronger 5-year resale value.


## The Engine Reversal: 3-Cylinder Swift vs 4-Cylinder Grand i10 Nios


For years, buyers picked the Maruti Swift specifically for its silky four-cylinder K-Series engine. With the latest generation, Maruti switched to a new 1.2-litre three-cylinder Z12E engine (82 PS, 112 Nm) tuned specifically for low-RPM city torque and high thermal efficiency. Meanwhile, the Hyundai Grand i10 Nios continues with a proven 1.2-litre four-cylinder Kappa petrol engine (83 PS, 114 Nm).


In everyday traffic, this creates a distinct personality split: the Grand i10 Nios is noticeably quieter and more vibration-free at idle with the AC compressor running, while the Swift has a gentle three-cylinder thrum at idle but pulls cleanly from 1,500 RPM and returns significantly better mileage.


## Mid-Variant Spec & Ownership Comparison: Swift VXi (O) vs Nios Sportz


Most private buyers shop the mid-level VXi (O) and Sportz trims. Both cars now include 6 airbags, ABS with EBD, Electronic Stability Control (ESC/ESP), and Hill-Hold Assist as standard across all variants.

## City Driving, Cabin Practicality & AMT Behaviour

- **Cabin Feel & Rear Seat:** The Grand i10 Nios feels more upmarket inside, with lighter dual-tone plastics that make the cabin feel airy, higher rear window sills (as opposed to the Swift's dark, smaller rear glass area), and dedicated rear AC vents on the Sportz trim. The Swift is 55 mm wider externally, giving slightly better front shoulder separation, though its all-black cabin feels cosier in the back.
- **AMT Gearbox in Heavy Traffic:** Both use 5-speed AMTs. Because Hyundai pairs an electric AMT actuator with a smooth four-cylinder engine, the Nios AMT shifts slightly more seamlessly in 10–25 km/h bumper-to-bumper crawls than the Swift AGS.

## 5-Year Fuel & Ownership Math (At 1,000 km/Month)


Over 60,000 km (5 years at 1,000 km/month) with petrol at ₹102/litre:

- **Maruti Swift (17.5 km/l city avg):** Consumes ~3,428 litres = **₹3,49,700 in fuel**.
- **Grand i10 Nios (14.5 km/l city avg):** Consumes ~4,138 litres = **₹4,22,070 in fuel**.

The Swift saves roughly **₹72,000 in petrol over 5 years**, which completely offsets its higher initial purchase price while also returning ₹25,000–₹35,000 more at resale. Routine annual service costs are nearly identical (₹4,800–₹6,200/year for both).


## Final Verdict: Which Hatchback Should You Bring Home?

- **Buy the Hyundai Grand i10 Nios Sportz if:** Your monthly driving is under 800 km, you want a smoother four-cylinder engine and smoother AMT, and you value rear AC vents, an airier back seat, and saving ₹50,000+ upfront.
- **Buy the Maruti Suzuki Swift VXi (O) / ZXi if:** You drive 1,000+ km per month, want 17–19 km/l city fuel efficiency, enjoy livelier steering dynamics, and want maximum resale liquidity whenever you upgrade.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Why does the Hyundai Grand i10 Nios Sportz offer better value on paper than the Swift VXi (O)?

Despite costing roughly ₹50,000 less on-road, the Grand i10 Nios Sportz includes rear AC vents, cruise control, a rear parking camera, automatic headlamps, and a tyre-pressure monitoring system (TPMS)—features that require stepping up to the pricier Swift ZXi or ZXi+.

### Q2. Does the new 3-cylinder Maruti Swift vibrate a lot compared to the old 4-cylinder Swift?

At cold start and idle, you can feel a faint three-cylinder pulse through the floor and gear lever that was absent in the older four-cylinder K12 engine. Once moving above 20 km/h, engine noise settles down well.

### Q3. Which car has better build quality and crash safety?

Both cars now come standard with 6 airbags, electronic stability control, and three-point seatbelts for all passengers. However, neither the current Indian-spec Nios nor the Indian-spec fourth-gen Swift holds a 5-star Bharat NCAP rating; buyers prioritising verified crash-test scores in this price band often cross-shop the Tata Tiago, Tata Punch, or Maruti Dzire.

### Q4. Are both cars available with factory CNG?

Yes. The Grand i10 Nios offers Hyundai's Hy-CNG Duo twin-cylinder system (preserving ~200 litres of boot space), while the Swift S-CNG uses a single cylinder that delivers higher km/kg efficiency (~27–28 km/kg) but occupies most of the boot.

### Q5. Which car is better for senior citizens in the family?

The Grand i10 Nios is generally preferred by older passengers because its rear window glass is larger and brighter, its suspension is slightly more supple at low city speeds, and it includes rear AC vents.
