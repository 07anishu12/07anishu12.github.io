At current urban fuel prices (approximately ₹102/litre for petrol and ₹87/kg for CNG), a factory-fitted CNG car under ₹10 lakh saves roughly ₹2.35 per kilometre after accounting for cold-start petrol use. Because the on-road price premium for a CNG variant is ₹1,00,000 to ₹1,12,000 (plus extra insurance and spark-plug upkeep), your true break-even distance is approximately 44,000 to 48,000 kilometres—which makes CNG financially worthwhile only if you drive at least 1,000 to 1,200 km per month.


## Step 1: Calculating the True Upfront Premium (Not Just Ex-Showroom)


Across popular sub-₹10 lakh models like the Maruti WagonR, Maruti Dzire, Tata Punch, and Hyundai Exter, the factory CNG variant carries an ex-showroom premium of ₹90,000 to ₹96,000 over the identical petrol trim.


Once you add state road tax (7%–10%) and the bi-fuel insurance endorsement on that higher invoice, the actual **on-road price difference is roughly ₹1,05,000**. If you finance that ₹1,05,000 over 5 years at 9.2% interest, your total upfront + interest premium rises to roughly **₹1,31,000**.


## Step 2: Real-World Running Cost Per Kilometre (Declared Assumptions)


Let us compare an identical compact hatchback/micro-SUV in city traffic using October 2026 Delhi reference prices:

- **Petrol Price:** ₹102.12/litre | **Real City Mileage:** 15.5 km/l → **Petrol Running Cost = ₹6.59 per km**.
- **CNG Price:** ₹86.98/kg | **Real City Mileage:** 22.5 km/kg → Raw CNG cost = ₹3.87 per km. Adding ~₹0.35/km for cold-start petrol usage and periodic tank warm-ups gives a **Blended CNG Running Cost = ₹4.22 per km**.
- **Gross Fuel Saving Per Kilometre:** ₹6.59 − ₹4.22 = **₹2.37 saved per km**.

## Why CNG Prices Relative to Petrol Matter So Much


Five years ago, when CNG cost ₹45–₹50/kg and petrol was ₹95/L, buyers recovered the CNG premium in just 25,000 km. Today, with CNG priced around ₹83–₹90/kg across major Indian cities (around 83%–86% of the per-litre price of petrol), your per-kilometre saving comes primarily from CNG's higher energy density by mass (giving ~45% more km per kg than petrol gives per litre). If your daily commute is short, the savings accumulate slowly.


## Clear Decision Rule: Petrol or CNG?

- **Buy the Petrol Variant if:** You drive less than 900 km per month (under 30 km/day), plan to sell the car within 3–4 years, or value full boot space, stronger highway overtaking power, and zero time spent waiting at CNG dispensing stations.
- **Buy the Factory CNG Variant if:** Your guaranteed daily commute exceeds 40 km/day (1,200+ km/month), you have a low-queue CNG pump along your regular route, and you intend to keep the car for at least 5 years (60,000+ km).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. How does taking a car loan change the Petrol vs CNG break-even distance?

If you finance the ₹1,05,000 on-road price difference over 5 years at ~9.2% interest, you pay roughly ₹26,000 in additional interest. That pushes your real break-even distance from ~47,000 km up to ~58,000 km.

### Q2. Do CNG cars fetch higher resale value when selling after 5 years?

A factory-fitted CNG hatchback or compact sedan typically fetches ₹35,000 to ₹50,000 more in the used-car market than its petrol equivalent after 5 years, which helps recover about 40% of your initial premium at resale.

### Q3. How much petrol does a CNG car still consume every month?

Most owners spend ₹500 to ₹800 per month on petrol for initial morning cold starts, occasional switches when a CNG tank runs dry before reaching a pump, and keeping fresh fuel circulating through the injectors.

### Q4. What is mandatory hydro-testing for a CNG cylinder and what does it cost?

Every 3 years from the cylinder's manufacturing date, Indian safety regulations require the CNG tank to undergo pressure hydro-testing at a PESO-approved testing station. It takes 2–3 hours and costs roughly ₹1,500 to ₹2,200.

### Q5. Should I consider a small EV instead of a CNG car if I drive 1,500 km a month?

If you have dedicated home charging and your budget stretches to ₹8.5–₹11 lakh (for a Tata Tiago EV or Punch EV), home electricity cuts running costs further to ~₹1.10–₹1.30/km with zero pump queues.
