Most motorcycle owners in India can garage and insure only one machine. That single bike must slip through weekday rush-hour gridlock without roasting the rider's legs, and then cruise securely at 95–105 km/h on weekend expressway getaways without feeling breathless. Achieving this dual mandate requires a distinct engineering compromise: sufficient displacement (160cc to 250cc) for high-speed passing reserves, dual-channel ABS for highway braking confidence, and compliant ergonomics that prevent lower back strain after three hours in the saddle. This review assesses the five most capable dual-purpose commuters available in 2026.

## Quick Answer & At-a-Glance Buying Verdict

For Indian riders needing one versatile machine for both city commuting and weekend highway touring, the top recommendation is the **Bajaj Pulsar N250** (ex-showroom Delhi: ₹1.51 lakh; on-road: ~₹1.74 lakh). Its torquey 249cc oil-cooled engine cruises comfortably at 100 km/h with low vibration, while delivering 35–38 km/l in city traffic. Riders favoring lighter weight and sharper handling should look at the **TVS Apache RTR 160 4V**, while those wanting relaxed retro touring should select the **Royal Enfield Hunter 350**.

## Detailed Model-by-Model Evaluation & Official Links

### 1. Bajaj Pulsar N250 / Pulsar NS200

Built by Bajaj Auto, the Pulsar N250 produces 24.5 PS and 21.5 Nm of torque. Its standout attribute for highway trips is the wide torque curve: you can hold 90–100 km/h in top gear without stressing the motor, while assist-and-slipper clutch tech keeps lever pull light in bumper-to-bumper city commuting. Official Link: [Bajaj Pulsar N250 / Pulsar NS200 Official Page](https://www.bajajauto.com/bikes/pulsar)

### 2. TVS Apache RTR 160 4V (USD & Dual-Channel ABS)

TVS Motor Company's 159.7 cc oil-cooled motor generates 17.55 PS in Sport mode. Weighing 146 kg with Showa-tuned suspension and ride modes, it is easily maneuverable in weekday traffic and remains stable during weekend highway runs up to 100 km/h. Official Link: [TVS Apache RTR 160 4V Official Page](https://www.tvsmotor.com/tvs-apache/apache-rtr-160-4v)

### 3. Royal Enfield Hunter 350

The Hunter 350 uses Royal Enfield's J-series 349 cc engine (20.2 bhp / 27 Nm). With 17-inch alloy wheels and a nimble 790 mm seat height, it is the most agile Royal Enfield in town, while the relaxed low-end torque allows stress-free cruising on rural highways. Official Link: [Royal Enfield Hunter 350 Official Page](https://www.royalenfield.com/in/en/motorcycles/hunter-350/prices-in-delhi/)

### 4. Yamaha MT-15 Version 2.0

Yamaha's MT-15 delivers high-revving performance from its 155 cc liquid-cooled VVA engine (18.4 PS). Its ultra-light 141 kg curb weight makes it brilliant for slicing through urban gridlock, and the Deltabox chassis provides highway stability, although windblast is noticeable above 100 km/h. Official Link: [Yamaha MT-15 Version 2.0 Official Page](https://www.yamaha-motor-india.com/yamaha-mt-15-v2.html)

### 5. Suzuki V-Strom SX 250 / Royal Enfield Himalayan 450

For riders who spend serious time on broken highways, the Suzuki V-Strom SX 250 (26.5 PS oil-cooled) and Himalayan 450 (40 PS liquid-cooled) provide long-travel suspension, wind protection screens, and upright seating ergonomics that eliminate fatigue on 500 km touring days. Official Link: [Suzuki V-Strom SX Official Page](https://www.suzukimotorcycle.co.in/product-details/v-strom-sx)

## Buyer-Specific Recommendations & Use-Case Verdict

1. **Balanced 50/50 City & Highway Commuters:** Pick the **Bajaj Pulsar N250** for effortless 100 km/h cruising, tractable city torque, and 35 km/l real-world mileage.
2. **Budget-Conscious Dual-Use Commuters:** Pick the **TVS Apache RTR 160 4V** for the lowest purchase cost and 42+ km/l efficiency.
3. **Relaxed Weekend Tourers:** Choose the **Royal Enfield Hunter 350** for classic exhaust thump, comfortable ergonomics, and strong highway presence.
4. **Adventure Tourers:** Choose the **Suzuki V-Strom SX 250** for highway wind protection and high ground clearance over rough roads.

## Ownership Cost, City On-Road Variations & Financing

State RTO levies range from 7%–10% in North India to 15%–18% in Maharashtra and Karnataka. For a rider covering 1,500 km per month (1,000 km city + 500 km highway), fuel expenses for a 250cc bike returning 35 km/l total roughly ₹4,070/month at ₹95/L.
