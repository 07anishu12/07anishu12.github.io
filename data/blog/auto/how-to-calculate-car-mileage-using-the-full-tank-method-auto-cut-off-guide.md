To calculate your car's true real-world mileage accurately, use the Full-Tank Auto-Cut-Off Method: (1) fill your tank until the pump nozzle clicks off automatically for the first time, (2) reset Trip Meter A to 0.0 km, (3) drive normally for at least 250 to 400 km, (4) refill at a fuel station until the nozzle clicks off at the first auto-cut-off again, and (5) divide the kilometres on Trip Meter A by the exact litres printed on the second fuel receipt. Never use the 'reserve-to-reserve' method or top up to the filler neck brim.


## Why 'Reserve-to-Reserve' and Dashboard MID Readings Mislead You

- **Why Reserve-to-Reserve Fails:** The low-fuel warning light is triggered by a floating thermistor/arm inside the fuel tank. If you park on a slight incline, accelerate hard, or go around a corner, the float dips and triggers the warning light with anywhere between 5 litres and 8.5 litres left—creating a massive 30%–40% calculation error.
- **Why Dashboard MID Reads 6%–10% High:** Your car's Multi-Information Display estimates fuel flow by counting injector open-time milliseconds rather than measuring physical liquid volume, and automakers calibrate it slightly optimistically.

## The 5-Step Full-Tank Auto-Cut-Off Protocol (Do This Exactly)

1. **Fill #1 (Setting the Baseline):** Visit a trusted fuel station (ideally in the morning when underground tanks are cool). Tell the attendant: *'Full tank, auto-cut-off only—do not round off the rupee amount.'* The moment the nozzle's vacuum sensor clicks off, stop. (You do not need the litre figure from Fill #1).
1. **Zero the Trip Meter:** Before moving the car, press and hold your instrument cluster button to reset **Trip A to 0.0 km** (or take a photo of the main odometer reading).
1. **Drive 250 to 400 km:** Drive through your normal weekly routine. Driving at least 250+ km ensures that any tiny 0.2-litre difference in nozzle shut-off foam affects the final result by less than 0.1 km/l.
1. **Fill #2 (Measuring the Exact Fuel Used):** Return to a level fuel bay (ideally the same station and dispenser pump). Again, instruct the attendant to fill strictly until the **first auto-cut-off click** without rounding up to the nearest ₹50 or ₹100. Collect the printed receipt showing the **Exact Litres Dispensed**.
1. **Run the Formula:** **True Mileage (km/l) = Trip A Distance (km) ÷ Litres Dispensed on Fill #2**.

| Step / Metric | Recorded Value in Test | How to Interpret It |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Odometer at Fill #1 (Auto-Cut-Off) | 14,210 km (Trip A reset to 0.0) | Starting baseline established (Tank 100% full to sensor level) |
| Odometer at Fill #2 (Auto-Cut-Off) | 14,564 km (Trip A = 354.0 km) | Total distance driven = 354.0 km |
| Fuel Dispensed at Fill #2 Receipt | 23.14 Litres (@ ₹102.12/L = ₹2,363) | Exact volume burned to cover those 354.0 km |
| Calculated Real-World Mileage | 354.0 ÷ 23.14 = 15.30 km/l | Your true, verified tank-to-tank fuel efficiency |
| Dashboard MID Display Reading | 16.6 km/l | MID Optimism Error = +1.30 km/l (+8.5% high) |
| Real Running Cost per Kilometre | ₹2,363 ÷ 354.0 km = ₹6.68 / km | Exact rupee figure for your monthly budget |

## Why You Must Never 'Top Up to the Brim' Past Auto-Cut-Off


Many Indian fuel attendants pull the nozzle halfway out after the auto-cut-off click and keep squeezing until liquid petrol is visible in the metal filler neck so the bill reaches a round number. **Never allow this for two reasons:**

1. It destroys measurement consistency because air pockets in the filler pipe burp unpredictably.
1. Modern BS6 cars have an **Evaporative Emission Control (EVAP) charcoal canister** connected to the top of the tank to capture fuel vapours. Overfilling forces raw liquid petrol into the charcoal canister, saturating it, triggering a Check Engine Light, and causing a ₹4,500–₹8,000 replacement bill.

## How to Adapt This Method for Factory CNG Cars


Because CNG is a compressible gas, the weight of gas pushed into your cylinder depends heavily on the station's compressor pressure (180 bar vs 210 bar) and ambient temperature. To get an accurate CNG km/kg figure, average your total kilometres and total kilograms across **three consecutive refills** rather than relying on a single fill.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Why shouldn't I calculate mileage after driving just 50 km?

Because auto-cut-off nozzles can vary by ±0.25 litres depending on fuel foaming and pump flow rate. Over a 350 km drive (22 litres), a 0.25-litre variance changes your answer by only 0.15 km/l; over a tiny 50 km drive (3.2 litres), a 0.25-litre variance skews your result by more than 1.2 km/l!

### Q2. Does filling petrol early in the morning actually give more fuel?

Underground storage tanks at petrol pumps are heavily insulated several feet beneath concrete, so temperature shifts between 7 AM and 2 PM are minimal (<1% density difference). However, morning visits have shorter queues, making it easier to supervise the attendant for zero-setting and first auto-cut-off.

### Q3. What if I forgot to reset Trip A at my first full tank?

If you took a photo of your main odometer at Fill #1 (or wrote the odometer mileage on your previous fuel receipt), simply subtract Odometer #1 from Odometer #2 to get your exact distance driven.

### Q4. Why did my tank-to-tank mileage suddenly drop by 3 km/l at a new petrol pump?

If your driving route, traffic, and tyre pressures did not change, a sudden 2.5–3 km/l drop at an unfamiliar station often points to short-dispensing or adulterated fuel. Switch back to a Company-Owned, Company-Operated (COCO) outlet and re-test.

### Q5. Can I track tank-to-tank mileage automatically on my phone?

Yes. Entering just two numbers—your odometer reading and litres filled at auto-cut-off—into a simple notes sheet or fuel-log app at every refill automatically builds a lifelong rolling average and alerts you the moment a clogged air filter or dragging brake reduces efficiency.
