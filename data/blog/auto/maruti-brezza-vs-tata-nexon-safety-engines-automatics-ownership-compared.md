Choose the Maruti Suzuki Brezza if you drive primarily in city and suburban conditions and want a super-smooth 1.5-litre four-cylinder naturally aspirated petrol engine, a bulletproof 6-speed torque converter automatic, higher city fuel efficiency (13–15 km/l), and hassle-free service. Choose the Tata Nexon if you want a verified 5-star Bharat NCAP crash rating, stronger highway overtaking torque (120 PS turbo-petrol or 115 PS diesel), 208 mm ground clearance, or a twin-cylinder turbo-CNG option.


## Mechanical Philosophy: 1.5L 4-Cylinder NA vs 1.2L 3-Cylinder Turbo & 1.5L Diesel


Beneath the sheet metal, these two SUVs take opposite engineering paths:

- **Maruti Brezza (1.5L K15C 4-Cylinder NA Petrol, 103 PS / 137 Nm):** Because its engine exceeds the 1,200 cc small-car GST limit, Maruti pays higher factory tax to give you a larger four-cylinder naturally aspirated motor. The pay-off in city traffic is zero turbo lag, linear throttle response from 1,200 RPM, whisper-quiet idle, and superior city fuel efficiency (13.5–15.5 km/l).
- **Tata Nexon (1.2L 3-Cylinder Turbo-Petrol, 120 PS / 170 Nm & 1.5L 4-Cylinder Diesel, 115 PS / 260 Nm):** The Nexon's three-cylinder turbo-petrol has slight low-RPM lag below 1,750 RPM and returns 10.5–12.5 km/l in dense city traffic, but once the turbo spools on highways, its 170 Nm torque pulls past trucks far more effortlessly than the Brezza. Furthermore, the Nexon offers a 1.5L diesel for 1,500+ km/month highway runners—something Maruti no longer sells.

## Automatic Transmission Comparison: Torque Converter vs AMT / Wet-Clutch DCA

- **Maruti Brezza VXi/ZXi/ZXi+ AT:** Uses a conventional **6-speed Torque Converter (TC)** automatic with paddle shifters. Torque converters use a fluid coupling rather than friction clutches for launch, making them the most durable, creep-friendly, and trouble-free automatic technology for congested Indian cities.
- **Tata Nexon AMT & 7-Speed DCA:** On lower-mid trims, the Nexon uses a 6-speed AMT (functional but noticeable shift pauses). On Creative and Fearless trims, it uses a 7-speed wet-clutch Dual-Clutch Automatic (DCA) that shifts rapidly on highways but can feel slightly hesitant during creeping stop-go transitions and is more complex over a 7-to-10-year ownership horizon.

## Safety, Cabin Ergonomics & CNG Comparison

- **Crash Safety:** The Tata Nexon holds a **5-star Bharat NCAP rating** (29.41/32 Adult, 43.83/49 Child). The Brezza is built on Suzuki's Global C platform (whose previous iteration scored 4 stars in Global NCAP) and comes standard with 6 airbags, ESP, and Hill-Hold.
- **Cabin Ergonomics & Visibility:** The Brezza has a boxy, upright greenhouse with a flat bonnet view, physical climate buttons, and easy-to-use controls. The Nexon has a futuristic cabin with twin 10.25-inch screens, ventilated front seats (Fearless+), and a JBL sound system, though its touch-capacitive climate/hazard panel is more distracting to use on the move.
- **CNG:** The Brezza offers factory S-CNG across LXi, VXi, and ZXi, but its single cylinder eats up most of the boot. The Nexon iCNG is India's first **turbo-petrol twin-cylinder CNG SUV**, retaining 321 litres of boot space and a panoramic sunroof option on higher trims.

## Final Verdict: Which SUV Fits Your Life?

- **Buy the Maruti Brezza (VXi AT / ZXi MT or AT) if:** You want peace-of-mind 7-year ownership, a refined 4-cylinder engine, the most reliable 6-speed torque converter automatic in the segment, physical dashboard controls, and higher petrol city mileage.
- **Buy the Tata Nexon (Pure+ / Creative+ / Diesel / iCNG) if:** You want a verified 5-star Bharat NCAP safety rating, strong turbo highway punch, a diesel engine for long highway runs, or a twin-cylinder CNG SUV with a panoramic sunroof and usable boot.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Why is the Maruti Brezza smoother in slow city traffic than the Tata Nexon?

The Brezza uses a four-cylinder 1.5-litre naturally aspirated engine with no turbocharger lag and a torque converter automatic. The Nexon petrol uses a three-cylinder 1.2-litre turbocharged engine that builds strong torque only above 1,750 RPM.

### Q2. Which CNG SUV is better: Maruti Brezza S-CNG or Tata Nexon iCNG?

The Tata Nexon iCNG is more practical for families because its twin-cylinder layout preserves 321 litres of boot space and its turbocharged engine maintains 100 PS and 170 Nm even in CNG mode. The Brezza S-CNG gives 2–3 km/kg higher mileage (~24 km/kg) but loses most of its luggage boot to a single tank.

### Q3. Does the Tata Nexon offer a panoramic sunroof now?

Yes. Tata introduced a panoramic sunroof on the higher Fearless+ PS and selected iCNG trims of the Nexon, whereas the Maruti Brezza offers a single-pane electric sunroof on its ZXi and ZXi+ variants.

### Q4. How do 5-year maintenance costs compare between the Brezza and Nexon?

Over 5 years / 50,000 km, a petrol Brezza typically costs ₹32,000–₹38,000 in scheduled servicing. A petrol Nexon averages ₹38,000–₹46,000 (and ₹48,000–₹55,000 for the diesel or DCA variants), with Maruti maintaining a wider and more consistent workshop network.

### Q5. Which SUV has a more comfortable rear seat for three adults?

Both have virtually identical cabin widths (~1,790–1,804 mm), comfortably seating two adults and a child. The Brezza offers a flatter window line and slightly better headroom for tall passengers, while the Nexon offers slightly deeper rear seat cushioning.
