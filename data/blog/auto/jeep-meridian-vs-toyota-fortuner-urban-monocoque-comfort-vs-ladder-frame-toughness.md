## Daily Driving Manners: Why Self-Drivers Prefer the Meridian on Tarmac

- **Ride, Handling & Braking:** The Jeep Meridian is built on a high-tensile monocoque shell with independent MacPherson strut front and rear suspension featuring **Frequency Selective Damping (FSD)**. At 110 km/h over highway undulations and ghat curves, the Meridian stays planted like a European sedan with zero ladder-frame shudder. The Fortuner sits 130 mm taller on a rigid truck frame—it commands respect from oncoming buses, but pitches and rolls noticeably more.
- **Engine & Gearbox:** Here the Fortuner hits back hard. Its 2.8L engine (204 PS / 500 Nm) and responsive 6-speed torque converter surge ahead effortlessly with a tap of the throttle. The Meridian's 2.0L Multijet II diesel (170 PS / 350 Nm) is refined at highway cruise, but its 9-speed automatic gearbox is tuned conservatively and pauses for a second before kicking down for sudden overtakes.

## Cabin Width, Third Row & Ownership Reality

- **Cabin Space:** Despite being 24 mm narrower, the Fortuner has a wider, more accommodating second-row bench for three adults. The Meridian has richer suede/leather upholstery, a panoramic sunroof, and 10.1-inch Uconnect screens, but its second row is best for two adults + a child, and its **third row is strictly for small children** (whereas the Fortuner's third row fits short adults for 1-hour trips).
- **Service & Resale:** Toyota wins decisively here—400+ cities vs Jeep's ~65–70 dealer locations, and ~70% 5-year resale retention vs ~50% on the Meridian.

## Clear Buying Verdict

- **Buy the Jeep Meridian (Limited (O) AT or Overland 4x4 AT) if:** You live in a tier-1/2 city with a Jeep dealer, drive yourself, want plush highway suspension comfort, panoramic sunroof, and ADAS, and only need 5 seats + a huge 481-litre boot.
- **Buy the Toyota Fortuner 2.8L Diesel AT if:** You need unshakeable mechanical reliability across remote India, effortless 500 Nm torque, a wider 3-person middle bench, and top resale value.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Can I buy the Jeep Meridian as a pure 5-seater now?

Yes. Jeep offers the base Longitude variant of the Meridian as a dedicated 5-seater (~₹23.33 lakh ex-showroom) with a massive 670-litre boot, while Limited (O) and Overland variants are 7-seaters whose third row folds completely flat into the floor to create a 481-litre boot.

### Q2. Why does the Jeep Meridian's 9-speed automatic sometimes feel slow in city traffic?

To maximise fuel economy and emissions compliance, the ZF 9-speed automatic launches in 2nd gear under normal throttle and shifts up early. Engaging 'Sand/Mud' mode or using manual tiptronic control keeps the engine in its meaty 1,800–2,800 RPM torque band during hill climbs or quick overtakes.

### Q3. Is the Jeep Meridian 4x4 capable of real off-roading like the Fortuner 4x4?

The Meridian Overland 4x4 uses Jeep's Active Drive AWD system with a 4WD Low mode, Hill Descent Control, and 214 mm ground clearance—handling snow, slush, sand, and rocky estate trails effortlessly. However, for extreme boulder crawling or deep ruts, the Fortuner's ladder frame and locking rear differential remain tougher.

### Q4. Which SUV has better fuel economy on highways: Meridian or Fortuner?

Because the Jeep Meridian is ~200 kg lighter and more aerodynamic with a tall 9th overdrive gear, it returns 14 to 16 km/l on open highways (versus 12 to 13.5 km/l in the 2.8L Fortuner AT).

### Q5. Are Jeep spare parts and maintenance much more expensive than Toyota's?

Routine annual oil/filter services on the Jeep Meridian cost roughly ₹16,000–₹22,000 (versus ₹12,000–₹16,000 on the Fortuner), while wear-and-tear parts like brake pads, suspension struts, and clutch/flywheel assemblies cost roughly 25%–35% more at Jeep workshops.
