The Nexon EV suits buyers seeking the larger documented body and a larger listed battery option; Punch EV deserves attention when a shorter car and a choice of smaller battery configurations fit the commute. Compare current battery variants and range methods carefully. Neither a badge nor a certified range figure guarantees your usable highway distance.

## Compare the current families, not old specifications

Tata's [support table](https://ev.tata.cars/support.html) distinguishes multiple Nexon.ev generations and Punch.ev battery families. That matters because older articles may describe different battery sizes from the cars now offered. The official [Nexon specification search extract](https://prod-ev.tatamotors.com/nexon/ev/specifications.html) describes a 45 kWh battery, while the [Punch specification extract](https://ev.tata.cars/punch/ev/specifications.html) lists 30 and 40 model families.

These manufacturer extracts were visible in search, but direct page retrieval failed during this research. Confirm the current sale brochure before publishing an exhaustive variant list. Do not treat an older Nexon MR or an earlier Punch battery as the same configuration as the current order.

Nominal capacity is not identical to usable energy. Battery management reserves, operating conditions and displayed state of charge affect the practical energy available. A larger pack generally provides more stored energy, but consumption and usable limits still determine distance.

## Use dimensions to focus the showroom visit

The official extracts list Nexon.ev at 3,995 mm long with a 2,498 mm wheelbase and Punch.ev at 3,880 mm with a 2,445 mm wheelbase. The documented length difference is 115 mm and the wheelbase difference 53 mm. These numbers guide parking and seating checks; they do not prove a particular comfort advantage.

Punch's extract describes 366 litres of boot space without its parcel shelf. Do not compare that unqualified with another car's capacity measured under a different convention. Bring bags and evaluate both with the same seats, shelf arrangement and loading assumptions.

A shorter body makes Punch a logical first candidate for a tight parking bay. The Nexon's larger package warrants examination for families needing more space, but inspect the rear seat and loading opening before paying for that assumption.

## Keep certified and manufacturer-estimated range separate

Tata's support material presents MIDC figures and C75 estimates separately. They answer different questions and must not be merged into a single unlabelled range claim. Neither is a controlled independent test performed for this guide.

For a hypothetical battery with 36 kWh usable energy, consumption of 0.15 kWh/km implies 240 km before any reserve. Reserving 20% leaves 28.8 kWh for the planned leg, giving 192 km under that assumption. At 0.18 kWh/km, the same planned energy supports 160 km.

Those figures are illustrative and do not estimate either Tata model. They show why speed, weather, air conditioning, load and reserve policy matter. Plan intercity legs from conservative usable energy and working chargers, not the longest advertised certification distance.

## Check charging at home before choosing the battery

Ask an electrician to assess parking access, earthing, protection and the electrical circuit required by the supplied charger. Get society or landlord permission where needed. Include installation and any cable-routing work in the purchase comparison.

Charging time depends on the vehicle's accepted power, charger output, starting charge, battery temperature and charging curve. A large advertised DC peak is not the average rate for the entire session. Compare times over the same stated state-of-charge window.

For a hypothetical daily use of 6 kWh, a charger averaging 2 kW requires approximately three hours of energy transfer before losses and other limits. This does not establish charging time for either vehicle; it helps test whether your overnight window is sufficient.

## Choose according to the whole week

Prioritise Punch.ev if its verified battery version comfortably covers daily use and its shorter body resolves parking needs. Prioritise Nexon.ev when its documented larger pack/body combination provides a useful margin for your family's travel and the premium is affordable. Do not buy the larger pack solely to avoid planning the occasional long trip.

Compare battery warranty conditions, transferability, insurance cover and authorised high-voltage support. Add public-charging tariffs for the share of travel that will actually use them. Obtain dated city quotes including the charger, installation and any optional packages.

## Quick questions, clear answers

### Can I use the full certified range on a highway?

Do not assume so. Certification follows a stated cycle, while highway speed and operating conditions can change consumption. Keep a reserve and plan with conservative evidence that matches the chosen variant. This guide contains no independently measured highway-range result.

### Is a bigger battery always the cheaper option?

No. It can add purchase cost and may be unnecessary for your usage. Compare the premium with the benefit of fewer charging stops or additional reserve. Use actual quotations and avoid treating unused capacity as guaranteed future resale value.

### Can both cars use any public charger?

Check connector compatibility, the car's supported charging type and the charger's actual availability. Network access, payment and working status also matter. Obtain the current manuals and plan a backup site rather than relying on a map pin alone.
