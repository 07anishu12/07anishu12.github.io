Whether you should buy a car in cash or on a loan in India depends on two things: where your cash is currently parked and whether you are salaried or a business owner. If you are a salaried buyer (who gets zero income-tax deduction on car-loan interest) and your cash is sitting in a bank Fixed Deposit or Debt Fund yielding 7% pre-tax (~4.9% after 30% tax), paying Cash is mathematically superior—because avoiding a 9.1% car loan gives you a guaranteed, risk-free 9.1% post-tax return (provided your 6-month emergency fund remains untouched). Conversely, if you are a profitable business owner who can deduct loan interest against 25%–30% corporate tax (lowering effective loan cost to ~6.4%) or use capital at 18%+ in your business, taking a loan is smarter.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Why do car dealerships try so hard to convince cash buyers to take at least a small loan?

Because banks and NBFCs pay dealerships a 1.5% to 3.5% finance payout commission on the loan amount. Never let a finance executive talk you into an unnecessary loan with processing and hypothecation fees unless they give you a written upfront cash discount on the car's invoice that exceeds the loan's processing fee + first-year interest + foreclosure fee!

### Q2. Does taking a car loan improve my CIBIL score if I already have a credit card or home loan?

If you already have an active Home Loan or 1–2 credit cards paid on time, your CIBIL credit mix and score (780+) are already strong—taking a 9.1% car loan and paying ₹2.2 lakh in interest just for 'CIBIL score building' is a complete waste of money.

### Q3. Why should I never redeem existing long-term Equity Mutual Funds during a market dip to buy a car in cash?

If the stock market is down 15%–20% when you buy your car, redeeming depressed equity units locks in losses and triggers 12.5% Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax on past gains. In that situation, taking a floating-rate PSU bank car loan (with zero prepayment penalty) and paying it off over 12–24 months from fresh monthly cashflows is smarter.

### Q4. What extra RTO paperwork do I avoid when buying a car in 100% Cash?

When you buy in cash, your Registration Certificate (RC) has zero bank hypothecation endorsement—saving you the ₹1,500 hypothecation addition fee on Day 1 and saving you from chasing the bank for a Form 35 + NOC and paying ₹1,500–₹2,500 at the RTO when closing the loan.

### Q5. What about buying a Used Car—should I ever take a 14% Used-Car Loan if I have cash?

Never! No risk-free investment in India earns 14% post-tax (which requires earning 20% pre-tax every year!). If you have the cash to buy a used car while keeping your emergency fund intact, paying 100% cash for a used car is an unbeatable 14% guaranteed return.
