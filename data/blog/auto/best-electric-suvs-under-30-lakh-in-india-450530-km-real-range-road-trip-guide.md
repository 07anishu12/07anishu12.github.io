## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Why do the Mahindra XEV 9e and BE 6 use Rear-Wheel Drive (RWD) instead of Front-Wheel Drive?

When an electric motor produces 286 PS and 380 Nm of instant torque from 0 RPM, sending that power through the front steering wheels causes violent wheelspin and torque steer. Placing the motor on the rear axle (RWD) pushes the rear tyres into the tarmac under acceleration, giving luxury-car balance and a tight 5.0-metre turning circle!

### Q2. Do 79 kWh EVs require an expensive 11 kW home charger?

If you drive 50–80 km daily, a standard 7.2 kW single/three-phase AC wallbox charger (adding ~7 kWh or ~48 km of range per hour) is completely sufficient—topping up your daily usage in just 1.5 hours overnight. An 11 kW three-phase charger is only needed if you frequently drain the 79 kWh pack from 10% to 100% overnight.

### Q3. Why does the Hyundai Creta Electric use an NMC battery while rivals use LFP?

Hyundai uses a 51.4 kWh Nickel Manganese Cobalt (NMC) pouch battery because NMC has higher energy density per kilogram—allowing Hyundai to fit 51.4 kWh inside the existing Creta's wheelbase while keeping the kerb weight light (~1,610 kg) for brisk 7.9-second 0–100 km/h acceleration.

### Q4. What is the ground clearance under the battery pack of the Creta Electric vs XEV 9e / Harrier EV?

Because the Creta Electric adapts an ICE platform with an underfloor battery, its lowest battery-guard clearance is 200 mm (with a slightly raised cabin floor inside). Dedicated Born-EVs like the BE 6 and XEV 9e integrate the battery inside the skateboard chassis frame with 207 mm ground clearance and a 218 mm battery clearance height.

### Q5. How do I plan highway charging stops on a 600 km road trip in India?

Use aggregator apps like Statiq, PlugShare, ChargeZone, Jio-bp pulse, or Tata Power EZ Charge before setting off. Look for stations with dual-gun 60 kW or 120 kW CCS2 chargers that show a recent successful check-in from another EV owner within the past 48 hours, and trigger your charging stop when your battery is at 20%–25% (never wait until 5%).
