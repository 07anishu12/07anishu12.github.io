Choose the Toyota Fortuner if you want maximum mechanical durability over rough roads, effortless low-RPM torque from its larger 2.8-litre diesel engine, service support in every corner of India, and 70%+ resale value retention after 5 years. Choose the MG Gloster (or its facelifted MG Majestor sibling) if your full-size SUV is primarily a chauffeur-driven or family highway cruiser where plush suspension damping, whisper-quiet cabin insulation, second-row captain chairs, a genuinely usable adult third row, and a panoramic sunroof matter more to you than resale value.


## Size, Space & Ride Quality: Why the Gloster Feels a Half-Size Larger


Parked side-by-side, the MG Gloster is **190 mm longer (4,985 mm vs 4,795 mm)** and has a **205 mm longer wheelbase (2,950 mm vs 2,745 mm)** than the Toyota Fortuner. Inside the cabin, that extra length and softer spring calibration transform family comfort:

- **Ride & Acoustic Comfort:** The Fortuner has a firm, taut suspension that jiggles over low-speed urban patches and lets noticeable 2.8L diesel clatter into the cabin under acceleration. The MG Gloster uses softer coil-spring tuning and heavy acoustic sound deadening that glides over expansion joints with far less vertical toss.
- **Second & Third Row:** The Gloster offers optional second-row captain chairs and a wide, flat-folding third row where two adults can sit comfortably on long trips—whereas the Fortuner has a 60:40 bench second row and a tight, knees-up third row that hangs to the sides when folded.

## Engine & 4x4 Hardware: Big-Displacement Simplicity vs Twin-Turbo Tech

- **Toyota Fortuner (2.8L Single-Turbo Diesel — 204 PS / 500 Nm, 6-Speed AT):** Displacement has no substitute under heavy load. The Fortuner's 2,755 cc engine delivers immediate low-end surge from 1,600 RPM without working hard, and its part-time 4x4 system with a mechanical shift-on-the-fly dial, low-range transfer case (4L), and locking rear differential is far more rugged in deep mud or rocks.
- **MG Gloster (2.0L Twin-Turbo Diesel — 216 PS / 478 Nm, 8-Speed ZF AT):** Squeezes 216 PS out of a 1,996 cc engine using two turbochargers paired with a slick 8-speed automatic and an on-demand BorgWarner 4WD system with terrain modes. It is smoother and quieter on expressways, though slightly more hesitant off the line below 1,700 RPM.

## The 5-Year Depreciation & Resale Reality


Suppose both SUVs cost ₹49.00 lakh on-road today. After 5 years and 75,000 km:

- A well-kept **Toyota Fortuner** routinely commands **₹33.0 to ₹36.0 lakh** in the Indian used-car market.
- A 5-year-old **MG Gloster** typically trades around **₹22.0 to ₹25.5 lakh** (unless MG assures a buyback value upfront). You trade roughly ₹9–₹10 lakh in higher 5-year depreciation in exchange for enjoying a vastly more luxurious, feature-rich cabin every single day of those 5 years.

## Clear Buying Verdict

- **Buy the Toyota Fortuner 2.8L Diesel AT if:** You travel extensively into tier-2/3 towns and bad roads, want the toughest 4x4 hardware, and treat your SUV as a high-liquidity asset that holds 70% of its value.
- **Buy the MG Gloster Savvy (or MG Majestor) if:** You travel with 6–7 family members on paved highways, want a 12-way massaging seat, panoramic sunroof, 360-degree camera, and plush ride comfort, and plan to keep the vehicle for 7+ years.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Why does the Toyota Fortuner still lack a sunroof and 360-degree camera on most variants?

Toyota prioritises mechanical ruggedness, roof-rack load capacity, and simplicity on the IMV platform—and because the Fortuner dominates sales with minimal competition, Toyota has held back major cabin overhauls until the next generational cycle.

### Q2. What is the difference between the MG Gloster and the new MG Majestor?

The MG Majestor (~₹43.49 lakh ex-showroom) is a heavily restyled, more premium evolution of the Gloster platform featuring an upright squared-off front grille, vertical LED headlamps, a redesigned freestanding dual-screen dashboard, and updated suspension tuning.

### Q3. Which SUV is easier to drive and park in Indian city traffic: Fortuner or Gloster?

Neither is compact, but the MG Gloster is easier to manoeuvre at parking speeds because it uses light Electric Power Steering (EPS), a crisp 360-degree camera, and automatic park assist—whereas the Fortuner uses heavier hydraulic power steering and a basic rear camera.

### Q4. Can I buy the 2WD single-turbo MG Gloster if I don't need 4x4?

Yes. The 2WD MG Gloster Sharp uses a single-turbo version of the 2.0-litre diesel (161 PS / 375 Nm) that costs ₹3–₹4 lakh less, is mechanically simpler, and returns roughly 1 km/l better city mileage.

### Q5. Does the 10-year Delhi NCR diesel rule affect the Fortuner's resale value?

Surprisingly little. Even when a Delhi-registered Fortuner approaches year 8 or 9, buyers from Punjab, Haryana (non-NCR districts), Rajasthan, UP, and Southern states pay strong prices using an RTO No Objection Certificate (NOC) because the 2.8L engine easily runs past 2,50,000 km.
