An electric scooter reaches financial break-even when its net operating savings recover the extra acquisition and financing cost. There is no universal number of months because distance, charging arrangements and the chosen scooters differ. Use wall electricity rather than ignoring charging losses, and keep uncertain battery repairs or resale as scenarios.

## Compare scooters that serve the same job

Match rider/pillion fit, storage, suitable legal-speed performance and daily distance. Comparing the cheapest petrol scooter with a premium electric flagship can describe two budgets, but it does not isolate the powertrain effect.

Current electric families vary substantially. The [TVS iQube table](https://www.tvsmotor.com/electric-scooters/tvs-iqube) lists several battery configurations, while [Ather's Rizta specifications](https://www.atherenergy.com/rizta/specifications) distinguish S/Z and charging packages. Choose an exact version before entering cost or range.

Obtain complete local quotations including charger, installation, insurance and required packs. Verify incentive eligibility on the actual purchase date; a manufacturer's displayed subsidy line does not prove you qualify. Keep optional accessories separate and identify any battery subscription or unusual ownership structure.

## Calculate the energy cost per kilometre

For petrol, divide local price per litre by expected kilometres per litre. For electric, multiply measured or conservatively assumed wall kWh per kilometre by the electricity tariff. Battery-display consumption can omit charging losses and should not automatically be used as bill consumption.

Assume, hypothetically, petrol costs ₹100/litre and the scooter returns 45 km/l. Fuel cost is ₹2.2222/km. Assume the electric scooter uses 0.035 kWh/km from the wall at ₹10/kWh. Electricity costs ₹0.35/km, giving an unrounded energy saving of approximately ₹1.8722/km.

These values are teaching inputs, not current tariffs or measured scooter performance. Public charging, session charges and parking fees can change the electric case. Use the actual home/work/public charging mix rather than pricing every unit at the cheapest available rate.

## Convert the premium into distance and time

Suppose the electric option costs ₹30,000 more after all verified acquisition costs. Simple energy-only break-even is ₹30,000 ÷ ₹1.8722, or approximately 16,024 km using the unrounded calculation.

At 800 km monthly, recovery takes about 20 months. At 300 km monthly, it takes about 53 months. These are scenario results, not advice that every buyer above a particular monthly distance should choose electric.

If the scooters cost the same initially, there is no positive acquisition premium to recover in this simple model. Compare running expenses and other differences directly. If electric costs less, do not force the result into a misleading negative-payback story; show its starting advantage and remaining risks.

## Add service, finance and required recurring fees

Ask both workshops for the correct maintenance schedules and expected consumables. An EV does not need combustion-engine oil changes, but tyres, brakes, bearings and other components still require attention. No blanket zero-maintenance claim is justified.

Include any mandatory software, connectivity or battery arrangement you intend to retain. For example, a hypothetical ₹1,200 annual required charge reduces annual energy savings of ₹17,973 at 800 km monthly to around ₹16,773 before other costs. Use unrounded arithmetic behind displayed rounded amounts.

Compare total loan interest and fees, not only EMI. A ₹30,000 purchase premium financed over time can cost more than ₹30,000. Conversely, an actual promotional offer may reduce the difference, but only while its verified conditions apply.

## Treat the battery and resale as uncertainty

Read the exact battery warranty, thresholds, exclusions and transfer conditions. A warranty length is not a mandatory replacement date. Do not automatically insert a full battery replacement after five years or assume no battery-related risk can occur.

Show a base case and a separate adverse repair/resale scenario supported by clearly stated assumptions. Future resale cannot be guaranteed from today's asking prices. Include any transfer limits of a subscription or warranty if they affect the sale.

Choose electric when dependable charging, conservative savings and suitable performance align. Choose petrol when low usage, a lower acquisition cost or charging inconvenience makes that package more practical. Test rider fit and maintain a financial buffer whichever powertrain wins the spreadsheet.

## Quick questions, clear answers

### Can I use battery capacity divided by range as consumption?

It is at best an approximate starting estimate under the stated range conditions. Installed capacity may differ from usable energy, and wall charging includes losses. Prefer appropriate measured wall consumption or show a conservative assumption with its limits.

### Do electric scooters have no servicing cost?

No. They avoid some engine-related tasks but still have wear items, checks and potential repairs. Use the actual manufacturer's schedule and local workshop prices. A general lower-maintenance claim cannot replace a model-specific cost estimate.

### Does battery warranty guarantee a new battery?

The remedy depends on the contract, including eligibility, thresholds and exclusions. It may permit repair or another response. Read the actual wording and ownership-transfer terms rather than interpreting the headline duration as a promise of a new pack at any time.
