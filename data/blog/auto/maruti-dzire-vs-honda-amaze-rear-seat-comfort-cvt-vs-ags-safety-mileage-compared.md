Choose the Maruti Suzuki Dzire if you want a verified 5-star Global NCAP crash-test rating, 18–20 km/l real city fuel efficiency, an electric sunroof, a 360-degree camera, or a factory S-CNG option, and you are buying a manual or don't mind an AMT. Choose the Honda Amaze if you are buying an automatic for heavy traffic—where its four-cylinder i-VTEC engine and stepless CVT with paddle shifters outclass the Dzire's three-cylinder AMT—or need the larger 416-litre boot.


## How Recent Generational Updates Changed This Rivalry


Both the Maruti Dzire and Honda Amaze shed their old weaknesses in their latest generations:

- **The New Maruti Dzire** broke away from the Swift's styling with a mature sedan grille, earned a landmark **5-star Global NCAP adult safety rating** (with 6 airbags, ESP, and Hill-Hold standard across all trims), added an electric sunroof and 360-degree camera on top variants, and adopted the ultra-efficient 1.2-litre 3-cylinder Z12E engine.
- **The New Honda Amaze** fixed its older model's soft rear suspension and low ground clearance (now raised to 172 mm), added 6 airbags and rear AC vents across trims, retained its buttery **1.2-litre 4-cylinder i-VTEC engine and true CVT automatic**, and introduced camera-based **Honda SENSING ADAS** on the top ZX trim.

## Rear-Seat Chauffeur Comfort & Luggage Boot


Both sedans offer plush rear benches with fold-down centre armrests, dedicated rear AC vents, and generous knee room for 6-foot passengers:

- ** Maruti Dzire:** Offers slightly better under-thigh support and a marginally airier feeling on ZXi+ trims thanks to the sunroof and beige/black cabin palette. Its 382-litre boot easily fits two large suitcases plus duffle bags.
- **Honda Amaze:** Offers a flatter floor hump for a middle child passenger, larger door pockets, and a segment-leading **416-litre boot** (34 litres larger than the Dzire) with a wide loading lip.

## Engine & Automatic Showdown: 3-Cyl AGS vs 4-Cyl CVT


This is the decisive factor for most buyers:

- **Refinement & Automatic Smoothness:** The Amaze wins clearly. Its four-cylinder 90 PS i-VTEC engine is smoother at idle and rev-happy on highways, and its stepless CVT automatic eliminates shift jerks in city traffic. The Dzire's 82 PS three-cylinder engine has a mild thrum at idle and uses a 5-speed AMT (AGS) that exhibits a noticeable pause between 1st and 2nd gear.
- **Fuel Economy & Running Cost:** The Dzire wins by a wide margin. In city traffic, the Dzire returns 17.5–19.5 km/l versus 13–14.5 km/l in the Amaze. At 1,200 km/month, the Dzire saves roughly ₹28,000–₹34,000 per year in petrol—and also offers a factory S-CNG option (VXi/ZXi).

## Clear Buying Verdict

- **Buy the Maruti Dzire (VXi / ZXi / ZXi+) if:** You are buying a manual or factory CNG car, drive over 1,000 km/month, want a verified 5-star Global NCAP crash test score, and prefer features like a sunroof and 360-degree camera.
- **Buy the Honda Amaze (VX CVT / ZX CVT) if:** You are buying an automatic for dense city traffic, demand four-cylinder smoothness and a jerk-free CVT gearbox, carry maximum airport luggage (416L boot), or want Honda's 10-year anytime warranty peace of mind.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Did the new Maruti Dzire really score 5 stars in Global NCAP crash testing?

Yes. The fourth-generation Indian-made Maruti Dzire was crash-tested by Global NCAP and achieved a 5-star Adult Occupant Protection rating (31.24 out of 34 points) and a 4-star Child Occupant Protection rating, with a stable body shell.

### Q2. Is the Honda SENSING ADAS on the Honda Amaze ZX useful on Indian roads?

On multi-lane marked highways and expressways, the Amaze ZX's Adaptive Cruise Control and Lane Keep Assist markedly reduce highway fatigue. Inside chaotic city traffic with two-wheelers cutting closely, most drivers turn off Autonomous Emergency Braking (CMBS) to avoid sudden braking.

### Q3. Why is the Honda Amaze's starting price higher than the Maruti Dzire's?

Honda starts the Amaze lineup directly at the well-equipped V trim (~₹7.65 lakh ex-showroom, which already includes an 8-inch touchscreen, wireless CarPlay, LED projector headlamps, and alloy-style covers), whereas Maruti offers a stripped entry LXi trim at ₹6.31 lakh.

### Q4. Does the new Honda Amaze scrape over tall speed breakers with four passengers?

No. Unlike the second-generation Amaze which had soft rear springs, the latest third-generation Amaze has firmer rear damping and 172 mm of unladen ground clearance (9 mm higher than the Dzire's 163 mm).

### Q5. Which sedan has lower maintenance costs over 5 years?

Routine service costs are very close—roughly ₹5,500–₹7,000 per year for the Dzire (10,000 km/1-year interval) and ₹6,500–₹8,500 per year for the Amaze. Honda also offers a 3-year unlimited-kilometre standard warranty extendable up to 10 years.
