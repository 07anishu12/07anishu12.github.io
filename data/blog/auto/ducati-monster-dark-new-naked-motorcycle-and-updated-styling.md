## 1. Introduction

Ducati has revived one of its most legendary and beloved design themes at EICMA 2026 with the global unveiling of the 2027 Ducati Monster Dark. First popularized in the late 1990s on the iconic air-cooled Monster 600, the 'Dark' designation has always represented the purest, most stripped-back expression of the Bologna naked roadster: a sinister matte-black aesthetic that strips away decorative pretense to celebrate essential mechanical form and urban attitude. Built around the modern, ultra-lightweight Front Frame chassis and the characterful 937cc Testastretta 11° engine, the new Monster Dark pairs stealthy aggression with contemporary electronic sophistication, offering a purposeful, understated machine for purist street riders.

## 2. Launch and Availability

The 2027 Ducati Monster Dark made its official public debut on November 3, 2026, at the Ducati stand in Hall 15 at EICMA in Milan. Ducati confirmed that the Monster Dark will arrive in European dealerships in February 2027, offered in both standard 111 hp trim and an A2-compliant 35 kW restricted format for young riders. For the Indian market, Ducati India has officially confirmed that the Monster Dark will be launched in the third quarter of 2027 as an accessible entry point into the premium Italian naked lineup, available across its nationwide dealership network.

## 3. Engine and Performance

Powering the Monster Dark is Ducati's revered 937cc, liquid-cooled, desmodromic four-valve Testastretta 11° L-twin engine. Fully compliant with Euro 5+ emissions standards, the engine produces a vigorous 111 hp (82 kW) at 9,250 rpm and 93 Nm of peak torque at 6,500 rpm. The torque curve is broad and punchy, with over 80 percent of maximum torque available from just 4,000 rpm, delivering instantaneous throttle response for darting through congested urban corridors or carving up winding mountain ascents. Power is routed through a six-speed gearbox equipped with an anti-hopping assist-and-slipper clutch and the Ducati Quick Shift (DQS) up/down system as standard equipment.

## 4. Design and hardware

Aesthetically, the Monster Dark is drenched in Ducati's menacing 'Dark Stealth' matte black livery. The fuel tank, front mudguard, and tail section are finished in satin matte black, complemented by blacked-out engine casings, exhaust headers, and footpeg brackets. The motorcycle utilizes an aluminum Front Frame inspired by the Panigale, weighing a minuscule 3 kg, which bolts directly to the engine crankcases. This minimalist architecture results in an extraordinary dry weight of just 166 kg (188 kg road-ready). The chassis is paired with 43mm inverted front forks and an adjustable rear monoshock, while braking is commanded by Brembo M4.32 monobloc calipers clamping dual 320mm front discs.

## 5. Features and technology

The cockpit of the Monster Dark is dominated by a crisp 4.3-inch full-colour TFT instrument display with graphics inspired by the Panigale V4. Electronic rider aids, governed by a six-axis Bosch IMU, include Cornering ABS, Ducati Traction Control (DTC) with 8 intervention levels, Ducati Wheelie Control (DWC), and Launch Control. Riders can choose from three tailored riding modes: Sport, Touring, and Urban (which limits power to 75 hp for slippery wet conditions). Lighting is 100 percent LED, featuring the iconic round headlight with a continuous daytime running light ring and integrated 'sweeping' dynamic LED turn indicators.

## 6. Expected price and market positioning

In European showrooms, the 2027 Ducati Monster Dark is priced attractively at approximately €12,400, positioned as the most accessible gateway into the high-performance Ducati L-twin lineup. In India, where it is imported via the CBU route from Thailand, the anticipated ex-showroom price is estimated between Rs 12.80 lakh and Rs 13.50 lakh. Ducati positions the Monster Dark as a stylish, no-nonsense naked roadster for riders who demand authentic Italian performance and heritage without flamboyant graphics.

## 8. Rivals and alternatives

The Ducati Monster Dark competes in the premium middleweight naked segment against the Triumph Street Triple 765 R, the Yamaha MT-09, the Kawasaki Z900, and the KTM 990 Duke. Compared to the inline-four Kawasaki Z900, the Monster Dark is significantly lighter (over 24 kg lighter), features superior desmodromic L-twin torque character, and offers a more comprehensive IMU-backed safety package.
