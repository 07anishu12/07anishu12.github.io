## 1. Introduction

At EICMA 2026, Royal Enfield unveiled the all-new Continental GT 750, expanding its iconic café racer lineage onto a newly engineered 750cc parallel-twin platform. The Continental GT 650 has long stood as one of the world's most popular modern-classic motorcycles, beloved for its authentic 1960s British silhouette, communicative chassis dynamics, and accessible character. However, as global highway speeds and enthusiast demands matured, riders sought greater mid-range punch, sharper braking performance, and modern electronic safety suites. The new Continental GT 750 answers that call decisively, introducing a highoutput liquid-cooled twin, dual front disc brakes, inverted front forks, and comprehensive rider aids while honoring the pure café racer aesthetic that defines the Ton-Up boy heritage.

## 2. Launch and Availability

The Royal Enfield Continental GT 750 celebrated its global premiere on November 3, 2026, at EICMA in Milan. Displayed alongside its adventure sibling, the Himalayan 750, the café racer captured intense interest from European dealers and international media. Royal Enfield confirmed that the motorcycle is slated for commercial market launch across Europe and the UK in summer 2027. Production will take place at Royal Enfield's manufacturing plants in Tamil Nadu, India. Indian domestic showroom dispatches are scheduled to commence in the third quarter of 2027, making it a centerpiece of the brand's premium domestic portfolio.

## 3. Engine and Performance

Powering the Continental GT 750 is Royal Enfield's newly developed 750cc, liquid-cooled, parallel-twin engine. Featuring a 270-degree crankshaft and four valves per cylinder driven by dual overhead camshafts, the engine is tuned for sharp throttle response and robust top-end roll-on acceleration. It produces 62 bhp of maximum power at 7,800 rpm and 68 Nm of peak torque at 5,600 rpm, representing a substantial 32 percent power increase over the outgoing air/oil-cooled 648cc unit. Power is transmitted through a six-speed transmission equipped with an assist-and-slipper clutch and an optional bi-directional quickshifter, allowing clutchless up- and downshifts during spirited canyon carving.

## 4. Design and hardware

The Continental GT 750 retains the classic, muscular silhouette of a quintessential British café racer. It features a sculpted, long-range 14.5-litre fuel tank with deep knee recesses, clip-on handlebars mounted below the top triple clamp, and rearset footpegs for a sporty, crouched riding posture. A removable rear seat cowl quickly converts the machine between a purposeful single-seater and a dual-occupant roadster. The chassis is a revised tubular steel double-cradle frame tuned by Harris Performance to handle the heightened horsepower. Premium 41mm inverted front forks and twin piggyback rear shock absorbers deliver taut, composed handling. Braking is dramatically improved with twin 300mm floating discs up front clamped by twin-piston radial calipers, ending previous enthusiast complaints regarding single-disc limitations.

## 5. Features and technology

While honoring heritage styling, the Continental GT 750 integrates contemporary electronics. It features a circular 4.0-inch TFT digital gauge housed in an authentic retro aluminum casing. The display supports smartphone Bluetooth pairing, full turn-by-turn navigation, and music telemetry. Electronics include ride-bywire throttle control with three selectable ride modes—Sport, Urban, and Rain—which modulate power delivery and traction control sensitivity. Dual-channel ABS with cornering functionality is standard. Lighting is entirely LED, including an iconic circular LED projector headlamp with an integrated daytime running light (DRL) ring.

## 6. Expected price and market positioning

In international markets, Royal Enfield plans to retail the Continental GT 750 at approximately £6,999 in the UK and €7,800 across continental Europe, positioning it as an accessible alternative to high-priced British and European roadsters. In India, local manufacturing guarantees highly competitive pricing. The expected exshowroom price is projected to range between Rs 3.80 lakh and Rs 4.20 lakh. This pricing keeps the Continental GT 750 comfortably below the Triumph Bonneville range, capturing riders who desire genuine modern-classic character with contemporary performance.

## 8. Rivals and alternatives

Primary rivals to the Continental GT 750 include the Triumph Speed 400 and Thruxton-inspired custom models, the Kawasaki Z650RS, and the Moto Guzzi V7. In the domestic Indian market, its closest competitors will be its smaller sibling, the Continental GT 650, as well as the Triumph Scrambler 400X and BSA Gold Star 650. The GT 750 outshines competitors by providing twin-cylinder smoothness, authentic clip-on ergonomics, twin front brake discs, and unbeatable price-to-performance metrics.
