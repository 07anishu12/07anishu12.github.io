A Bharat NCAP rating summarises performance under a defined vehicle-safety assessment. Read adult and child protection results separately, match the report to the car you are buying and check the protocol date. Stars are useful comparative evidence, but they do not guarantee protection in every real-world crash or describe every safety feature.

## Understand what the programme publishes

The [Bharat NCAP website](https://www.bncap.in/) describes assessments covering Adult Occupant Protection, Child Occupant Protection and Safety Assist Technologies. The [government's launch announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=1950593&lang=1&reg=3) explains the publication of adult and child occupant star ratings. Use the programme's actual report rather than a dealer's cropped badge graphic.

Adult and child results answer related but distinct questions. A child assessment involves the specified restraints, installation and test conditions; it is not a promise that an unrestrained child is protected. Safety-assist assessment also does not mean every advertised driver-assistance function has been tested in every possible situation.

This guide does not reproduce numerical star thresholds or claim a complete audit of the protocol current in October 2026. If adding those details, obtain the final applicable protocol and amendments, not a draft document or an article discussing a future version.

## Match the exact model and variant coverage

Start with the report's vehicle name, tested configuration, publication date and applicability notes. A name can remain similar after a substantial redesign, and related electric or combustion versions should not automatically share a result. Check the covered production range and safety specification.

For example, a hypothetical report might apply to specified variants produced after an identified manufacturing date. That does not establish the result for an earlier vehicle with different equipment. This is an illustrative reading problem, not a claim about any named car's report.

Ask the dealer to identify the source report for the exact allotted vehicle. If the report does not clearly answer applicability, seek clarification from the manufacturer or programme before publishing a score. Do not fill a missing rating with an overseas result without explaining differences in market specification and assessment method.

## Look beyond the headline score

Read the available breakdowns and explanatory notes. Different injury-related findings, restraint behaviour or installation observations can explain why two cars with the same stars are not identical in every assessed area. Avoid inventing a new percentage safety score by dividing points by the maximum and treating it as real-world survival probability.

Check what equipment was present and what is fitted to your chosen variant. Airbag count, stability control, restraints and child-seat provisions matter, but a feature list alone does not establish structural or crash performance. Conversely, crash stars do not tell you whether a driver can comfortably see out of the car.

If a report discusses a limitation, retain that qualification in any summary. 

## Compare protocols, not just graphics

Bharat NCAP, Global NCAP and other regional programmes can use different procedures and versions. Even within a programme, protocols may evolve. A star from one year is not automatically equivalent to the same star under a later or different framework.

For a comparison table, include programme, protocol/report year, adult result, child result and exact applicability. If comparability is uncertain, say so instead of ranking cars by the colour or number of stars alone. An untested car has an unknown result, not an automatically poor or good one.

Do not interpret a controlled crash-test speed as the maximum safe road speed or the boundary below which injury cannot occur. Real crashes differ in angle, object, occupant size, restraint use and many other conditions.

## Use the rating as part of a purchase decision

Shortlist cars with relevant documented safety evidence, then inspect the equipment you will actually receive. Try your child restraint according to both manufacturers' instructions and check access to the belts and anchors. A high-rated model with an incorrectly fitted restraint does not deliver the same conditions as a laboratory assessment.

Test driving position, visibility and control accessibility. Maintain tyres, brakes and required systems after purchase. A rating concerns assessed performance; it is not a maintenance certificate for every example on the road.

When publishing, link the exact report where available and date the check. If only a programme search extract was accessible, disclose that limitation. The programme homepage timed out during retrieval; final report claims require rechecking.

## Quick questions, clear answers

### Does five stars mean no one can be injured?

No. It describes performance under a particular assessment framework and conditions. Real crashes vary substantially. Correct restraints, appropriate speed, vehicle condition and attentive driving remain essential; a rating cannot eliminate all injury risk.

### Can an EV inherit the petrol version's rating?

Only if the programme's applicability explicitly supports that conclusion. Battery placement, structure and equipment can differ. Check the exact named report and covered variants rather than transferring a score because the vehicles share a model-family name.

### Is no published rating the same as zero stars?

No. An unavailable result means this evidence has not been established for that car in the source checked. Zero stars is a reported outcome under a defined assessment. Keep unknown, untested and tested results distinct in a buying table.
