The 390 Duke-versus-Speed 400 comparison requires a fresh model-year check before quoting engine capacity or output. Current KTM India material inspected here describes a 349.32 cc 390 Duke, while older articles discuss different specifications; Triumph's India range also signals an updated 350-class family. Badges are not reliable displacement measurements.

## Resolve the generation before comparing performance

The [KTM India 390 Duke page](https://www.ktmindia.com/ktm-bikes/naked-bike/ktm-390-duke) lists 349.32 cc, 41.5 PS and 33.5 Nm in the current inspected presentation. It also contains inconsistent power-unit wording in its FAQ, so use the current signed-off brochure before publishing a detailed technical table.

The [Triumph India modern-classic range](https://www.triumphmotorcycles.in/bikes/classic) lists Speed 400 alongside an updated family description. Its dedicated model-table retrieval was blocked during this research. A global or 2025 Speed 400 specification should not silently be treated as the current Indian purchase.

Write manufacturing year, generation, full variant and exact engine on each quote. If comparing a used older Duke with a current Speed, say so explicitly. Combining the most attractive figures from different years produces a misleading bike that cannot actually be ordered.

## Understand the documented product emphasis

KTM's current page advertises ride modes, traction control, cornering ABS, a TFT interface, cruise control and adjustable suspension in its described package. Confirm trim and activation conditions before assuming every listed item is included in your delivery.

Triumph positions Speed 400 within its modern-classic roadster range. That documented design positioning gives a useful shortlist direction for buyers who prefer its style, but it does not prove superior comfort, gentler response or lower maintenance. Those claims need version-matched evidence.

If adjustable suspension and the listed electronic aids are essential, start by examining the actual Duke package. If the Speed's modern-classic layout is the attraction, test that particular model rather than expecting it to behave like a different Triumph derivative. Neither styling category establishes your personal fit.

## Test control before chasing output

Wear suitable gear and assess seat reach, wrist loading, knee angle and the ability to manoeuvre backwards. A motorcycle's manageable moving feel does not guarantee easy parking on a sloped driveway. Check where engine heat and controls sit relative to your legs.

On a supervised legal ride, examine throttle response, clutch control and normal overtaking needs without using public roads for timed performance runs. No acceleration, braking or handling test was conducted for this guide. Advertised performance is not an independent finding.

Understand the ABS modes and leave road-appropriate assistance active under the manual's instructions. A mode intended for a specific surface or specialist use should not be selected merely because its name sounds sportier. More electronic settings also create more functions the rider must learn correctly.

## Compare service and consumables realistically

Ask the appropriate workshops for scheduled work, approved oil, filters, tyres, brake consumables and chain-and-sprocket estimates over the same distance. Check which costs a service package excludes. Do not infer cheaper maintenance solely from purchase price or brand origin.

For commuting, inspect luggage and rainwear arrangements and the effect of any accessory on steering or visibility. For touring, confirm permitted load, passenger comfort and service access on the routes you actually take. A naked-roadster badge does not prevent touring, but wind exposure and luggage need practical solutions.

No comparative reliability dataset was established here. Individual owners' experiences can suggest inspection questions, but they do not determine failure rates. Check warranty terms, roadside support and parts-ordering procedures directly.

## Put the preferred package into a budget

Suppose, hypothetically, one bike costs ₹50,000 more and adds ₹5,000 annually in insurance and consumables. Over five years, the difference becomes ₹75,000 before financing and resale, or ₹1,250 monthly on average. These are illustrations, not Duke or Speed quotations.

If you value the verified electronics or ergonomics by that amount and can afford the total, the premium may be reasonable. Do not attempt to prove it financially with an unsupported resale forecast or mileage claim. Price suitable protective equipment and training alongside the motorcycle.

Prioritise the Duke when its confirmed current electronics/adjustability solve clear needs. Prioritise Speed 400 when the exact Indian version's tested fit and modern-classic package better suit your routine. Until the current Triumph specification and KTM unit inconsistency are reconciled, no quantitative performance winner is established.

## Quick questions, clear answers

### Does 390 or 400 identify exact displacement?

No. The current inspected India pages demonstrate why badge names must not replace the specification sheet. Confirm the production-year engine in the brochure and documents, particularly when search results mix previous generations, export versions and current Indian models.

### Should a new rider choose the less powerful bike automatically?

Power is one consideration, but predictable controls, fit, braking equipment and training also matter. Avoid selecting an unsuitable machine because a single figure looks smaller. Arrange a safe supervised assessment and keep the purchase within your skill and financial comfort.

### Can global Speed 400 specifications settle the comparison?

Only if they match the current Indian model and year. Market configurations can differ. The dedicated India model table was inaccessible during this research, so a global figure should remain qualified rather than being copied as a verified local specification.
