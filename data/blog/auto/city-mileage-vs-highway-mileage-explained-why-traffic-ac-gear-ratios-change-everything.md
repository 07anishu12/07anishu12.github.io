In a standard petrol or diesel car, highway mileage is typically 35% to 55% higher than city mileage (for example, 18.5 km/l on the highway vs 12 km/l in the city) due to four mechanical realities: in city traffic, your engine turns 2.5 times more revolutions per kilometre in 1st and 2nd gear, burns 0.7 to 1.1 litres per hour at a standstill with the AC on, wastes kinetic energy as brake heat at every signal, and runs on a rich fuel mixture during the first 3 km of a cold trip. Only Strong Hybrids and EVs reverse this pattern via regenerative braking.


## 1. Gear Ratios: Your Engine Fires 2.5x More Times Per Kilometre in City Traffic


Think of your gearbox as a bicycle derailleur. When crawling in 1st or 2nd gear at 20 km/h and 2,000 RPM, the engine crankshaft turns roughly **3,500 to 4,500 revolutions to move the car just 1 kilometre**.


On the highway in 5th or 6th overdrive gear at 80 km/h and 2,000 RPM, the engine turns only **1,500 revolutions to cover that exact same 1 kilometre**! Every revolution involves fuel injection pulses—so crawling in low gears physically demands far more fuel per kilometre.


## 2. Idling with AC On: The '0 km/l' Traffic-Signal Drain


Whenever your car sits stationary at a red light or gridlocked flyover with the engine and air conditioner running, your instantaneous mileage is literally **0.0 km/l**. A typical 1.2-litre to 1.5-litre petrol engine burns **0.7 to 1.1 litres of petrol per hour** at idle with the AC compressor engaged.


If your 10 km office commute takes 55 minutes—of which 20 minutes are spent stationary in traffic—you burn ~0.30 litres idling before moving an inch, dragging a 16 km/l car down to 11.5 km/l.

## 3. The 'Short Cold Trip' Penalty & The 110+ km/h Expressway Drop

- **Why 3 km School/Grocery Runs Give 9 km/l:** For the first 2 to 3 kilometres after a morning cold start, the engine coolant and catalytic converter are cold. The ECU intentionally injects a **rich fuel mixture** to warm the catalyst to 400°C+. If your entire trip is only 3 km, you spend the whole drive in the rich cold-start zone!
- **Why 120 km/h Burns 25% More Fuel Than 85 km/h:** On the highway, peak mileage occurs between **75 km/h and 90 km/h in top gear**. Above 95 km/h, aerodynamic wind resistance grows exponentially—pushing a boxy SUV at 120 km/h requires nearly double the horsepower of cruising at 85 km/h, pulling highway mileage back down from 19 km/l to 14.5 km/l.

## Practical Habits to Close the City-to-Highway Gap

- **Use Idle Start-Stop (or Switch Off at Signals >30 Seconds):** Saves 8%–12% fuel in signal-heavy corridors.
- **Lift Off Early & Coast in Gear:** When you see a red light 150 metres ahead, take your foot off the accelerator while staying in gear—modern fuel injectors cut fuel flow to **zero (Deceleration Fuel Cut-Off)** while the wheels turn the engine.
- **Cruise at 85–95 km/h on Highways:** Hits the aerodynamic and thermal sweet spot on Indian expressways.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Why does my AC reduce mileage much more in city traffic than on the highway?

An AC compressor draws roughly 2.5 to 4 kilowatts of power regardless of speed. When crawling in city traffic at 15 km/h where the engine is producing only 8–10 kW, the AC consumes ~30% of the engine's work. At 85 km/h on the highway where the engine produces 25+ kW and fast airflow cools the condenser naturally, the AC's percentage load is much smaller.

### Q2. Does driving with windows rolled down save more fuel than using the AC?

Below 45 km/h inside the city on a pleasant evening, rolling the windows down and switching off the AC compressor saves 10%–12% fuel. Above 70 km/h on the highway, open windows create parachute-like aerodynamic drag inside the cabin that wastes nearly as much fuel as running the AC with windows shut.

### Q3. Why do Strong Hybrids and EVs get better mileage in the city than on the highway?

Unlike regular petrol cars that turn momentum into wasted brake-pad heat at every traffic light, Strong Hybrids and EVs use regenerative braking to convert stop-and-go slowdowns back into stored battery electricity, and they suffer far lower aerodynamic drag at 25 km/h than at 100 km/h.

### Q4. Does my car's mileage improve after the first 2,000–3,000 km?

Yes, slightly (by about 1 to 1.5 km/l). As piston rings, gearbox bearings, and tyres bed in—and as you learn the engine's torque band and shift points—real-world efficiency settles upwards after the first 2,500 km.

### Q5. How much does carrying 4 adults + luggage reduce highway mileage compared to driving solo?

Every extra 100 kg of passenger and luggage weight reduces city mileage by roughly 3%–5% (because you must accelerate that mass after every stop) and reduces flat-highway cruising mileage by roughly 1.5%–2.5%.
