Mathematical Rigor in Modern Finance

Quantitative finance bridges economic theory, econometric forecasting, and software engineering. In volatile markets, financial decision-makers cannot rely on retrospective vanity metrics or unverified assumptions. Whether forecasting mutual fund liquidity demands or determining intrinsic corporate equity value, models must enforce strict mathematical invariants: zero temporal information leakage, bounded loss ceilings, and robust sensitivity under extreme tail-risk scenarios.

Core methodologies practiced across my quantitative work:

  • Walk-Forward Cross-Validation: Evaluating predictive models strictly on expanding historical folds. Scalers, imputation baselines, and estimators are fit strictly inside training windows, preventing forward-looking bias from corrupting test predictions.
  • Deterministic Valuation Modeling: Constructing Python-based Discounted Cash Flow (DCF), Dividend Discount (DDM), and Residual Income models where WACC calculations, terminal growth assumptions, and sensitivity matrices are executed with zero spreadsheet circularity.
  • Factor Exposure & Risk Attribution: Decomposing active portfolio returns across systematic risk factors (Size, Value, Momentum, Quality, Volatility) and isolating true managerial skill using Jensen's Alpha and Brinson-Fachler attribution models.
  • Liquidity & Redemption Stress Labs: Modeling severe redemption cascades across mutual fund schemes, capping maximum asset-at-risk outflows at 100% of reference AUM and computing secondary liquidity buffers under market illiquidity shocks.
