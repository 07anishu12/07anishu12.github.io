Graph Neural Networks (GNNs).

Modeling non-Euclidean relationships, inductive neighborhood aggregation, attention over topological graphs, and scientific applications at the Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology.

What are Graph Neural Networks?

Standard deep learning architectures—such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for 2D pixel grids or Transformers and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) for 1D sequential text—rely on strict geometric grids. However, a significant fraction of high-value real-world phenomena exists in non-Euclidean space:

  • Biological & Ecological Systems: Predator-prey interactions, food webs, and molecular structures.
  • Knowledge & Semantic Graphs: Entity-relation networks, corporate cross-holdings, and academic citations.
  • Financial & Transactional Networks: Counterparty credit exposure, payment flows, and anti-money laundering graphs.

Graph Neural Networks process graphs $G = (V, E)$ directly, preserving permutation invariance and structural relational dependencies through recursive message passing between connected vertices.

Core GNN Architectures: GraphSAGE vs. GAT

During my research at the Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology (OIST), our work investigated how to represent dense, heterogeneous interaction graphs without succumbing to memory bottlenecks or oversmoothing:

GraphSAGE (Hamilton et al.)

Mechanism: Uniform local neighborhood sampling followed by symmetric feature aggregation (Mean, Pooling, or LSTM aggregator).

Core Advantage: Inductive capability. The model learns aggregator functions rather than individual node embeddings, allowing generalization to previously unseen subgraphs.

Graph Attention Networks (GAT)

Mechanism: Multi-head self-attention applied over edge connections $\alpha_{ij} = \text{Softmax}_j(e_{ij})$.

Core Advantage: Differential weighting. Enables the neural network to prioritize critical topological links (such as keystone predator dependencies in a food web) over peripheral interactions.

Mathematical Foundation: The Message-Passing Framework

Most modern spatial GNNs can be formalized through the Gilmer et al. Message Passing Neural Network (MPNN) paradigm. For a node $v$ with neighborhood $\mathcal{N}(v)$ at layer $k$:

h_v^{(k)} = \text{UPDATE}^{(k)} \left( h_v^{(k-1)}, \text{AGGREGATE}^{(k)} \left( \left\{ \text{MESSAGE}^{(k)} \left( h_v^{(k-1)}, h_u^{(k-1)}, e_{uv} \right) : u \in \mathcal{N}(v) \right\} \right) \right)

By stacking $K$ message-passing layers, each node incorporates information from its $K$-hop neighborhood.

Key Technologies & Libraries

  • PyTorch Geometric (PyG): Geometric deep learning extension library for PyTorch providing scalable message-passing implementations and CUDA sparse kernels.
  • NetworkX: Complex network creation, topological metric extraction (betweenness centrality, clustering coefficients), and graph visualization.
  • DGL (Deep Graph Library): Framework for efficient message passing on large heterogeneous graphs.
