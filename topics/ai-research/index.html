Non-Euclidean Deep Learning & Relational Graphs

Conventional deep learning architectures (Convolutional Neural Networks and Transformers) excel on structured Euclidean data grids (images, text sequences). However, vast domains of science, commerce, and nature are governed by complex non-Euclidean topologies: molecular interactions, biological food webs, financial transaction graphs, and citation structures. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) generalize deep learning to irregular graph topologies by parameterizing neural message-passing along edge connections.

Key research directions and principles investigated in my academic and applied research:

  • Message Passing Neural Networks (MPNNs): Formulating node state updates through neighborhood aggregation, message transformation, and permutation-invariant pooling functions.
  • Inductive Representation Learning (GraphSAGE vs GAT): Evaluating neighborhood sampling and multi-head self-attention mechanisms over graph edges, enabling trained models to generalize inductively to previously unseen graphs and nodes.
  • Ecological Network Dynamics at OIST: Modeling marine trophic interactions at the Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology Graduate University (OIST), predicting energy transfer efficiencies and food-web vulnerability under biodiversity loss.
  • Empirical Scientific LLM Evaluation: Developing evaluation protocols to measure whether language models possess genuine relational reasoning over non-linear graphs or merely recite memorized token correlations.
