Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Trailing P/E, Forward P/E & Justified Forward P/E

Formula Expression
Trailing P/E = P_0 / EPS_0

Forward P/E = P_0 / E_1

Justified P_0 / E_1 = (1 − b) / (r − g) = Payout Ratio / (r − g)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Links the fundamental P/E multiple directly to dividend payout ratio (1 − b), required return on equity (r), and sustainable growth rate (g).

Formula 2: Price-to-Book (P/B), Price-to-Sales (P/S) & EV/EBITDA

Formula Expression
P/B = P_0 / BVPS = Market Cap / Common Equity

P/S = P_0 / SPS

EV = Market Cap + Preferred + Total Debt − Cash

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Provides balance-sheet and top-line valuation multiples when earnings are negative or cyclical.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Calculating Actual Forward P/E vs. Justified Forward P/E to Screen Valuation

Problem & Given Scenario: A stock trades at P_0 = $72.00. Trailing EPS (E_0) = $3.75, Expected Next-Year EPS (E_1) = $4.00, Expected Dividend (D_1) = $1.60, Return on Equity (ROE) = 15.0%, and CAPM required return (r) = 11.0%.

Formula Expression
Trailing P/E = P_0 / EPS_0

Forward P/E = P_0 / E_1

Justified P_0 / E_1 = (1 − b) / (r − g) = Payout Ratio / (r − g)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: Actual Trailing P/E = $72.00 / $3.75 = 19.20x; Actual Forward P/E = $72.00 / $4.00 = 18.00x. Step 2: Dividend Payout Ratio (1 − b) = $1.60 / $4.00 = 40.0% (so retention b = 60.0%). Step 3: Sustainable Growth Rate g = ROE × b = 15.0% × 0.60 = 9.00%. Step 4: Justified Forward P/E = (1 − b) / (r − g) = 0.40 / (0.11 − 0.09) = 0.40 / 0.02 = 20.00x (Justified Price = 20.00 × $4.00 = $80.00).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Because the stock's actual Forward P/E (18.00x) is below its fundamental Justified Forward P/E (20.00x), the stock is undervalued by $8.00 per share ($72 vs $80).

Worked Example 2: Computing P/B, P/S, and EV/EBITDA from Corporate Financial Data

Problem & Given Scenario: A firm has 50M shares trading at $30.00. Total Assets = $1,800M, Total Liabilities = $1,050M (consisting of $450M interest-bearing Debt and $600M AP/Accruals), Preferred Stock = $50M, Cash = $100M, Revenue = $2,500M, and EBITDA = $250M.

Formula Expression
P/B = P_0 / BVPS = Market Cap / Common Equity

P/S = P_0 / SPS

EV = Market Cap + Preferred + Total Debt − Cash

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Market Cap = 50M × $30 = $1,500M. Common Book Equity = Total Assets ($1,800M) − Total Liabilities ($1,050M) − Preferred Stock ($50M) = $700M (BVPS = $14.00). P/B = $1,500M / $700M = 2.14x. P/S = $1,500M / $2,500M = 0.60x. Enterprise Value (EV) = Market Cap ($1,500M) + Debt ($450M) + Preferred ($50M) − Cash ($100M) = $1,900M. EV / EBITDA = $1,900M / $250M = 7.60x.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Always deduct Preferred Stock from Total Equity when computing Common Book Value for P/B, and include Preferred Stock and Debt (minus Cash) in Enterprise Value.

Comparison & Reference Analysis

Valuation Multiple Primary Fundamental Driver Major Advantage Key Limitation / Distortion
Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Payout (1−b), r, and EPS growth g Direct link to equity earnings power Meaningless when EPS ≤ 0; accrual noise
Price-to-Book (P/B) ROE − r spread (P/B = (ROE−g)/(r−g)) Stable for banks, insurers, asset-heavy Ignores off-balance-sheet intangibles
Price-to-Sales (P/S) Net profit margin & sales growth Always positive even in net losses Ignores cost structure & debt leverage
EV / EBITDA ROIC, WACC, D&A intensity Capital-structure & tax neutral Overstates cash flow if CapEx is high

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A company maintains a constant dividend payout ratio of 45%, a sustainable growth rate of 6.5%, and a required return on equity of 11.0%. Its justified forward P/E ratio (P_0 / E_1) is closest to:

  • [A] 10.0x

  • [B] 10.6x

  • [C] 12.2x

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Justified Forward P/E = Payout Ratio / (r − g) = 0.45 / (0.110 − 0.065) = 0.45 / 0.045 = 10.0x.

Practice Question 2

Question: Holding all else equal in the justified P/E formula, an increase in the investor's required rate of return (r) will cause the justified P/E multiple to:

  • [A] Increase

  • [B] Decrease

  • [C] Remain unchanged

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Required return r is in the denominator of (1 − b) / (r − g); as r increases, the denominator (r − g) widens and the justified P/E contracts.

Practice Question 3

Question: A company has 20 million common shares trading at $25.00, Total Debt of $180 million, Preferred Stock of $20 million, Cash and Marketable Securities of $40 million, and EBITDA of $110 million. Its EV/EBITDA multiple is closest to:

  • [A] 4.55x

  • [B] 6.00x

  • [C] 6.73x

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Market Cap = 20M × $25 = $500M. Enterprise Value (EV) = $500M + $180M + $20M − $40M = $660M. EV / EBITDA = $660M / $110M = 6.00x.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Trailing P/E, Forward P/E & Justified Forward P/E.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Price-to-Book (P/B), Price-to-Sales (P/S) & EV/EBITDA.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
