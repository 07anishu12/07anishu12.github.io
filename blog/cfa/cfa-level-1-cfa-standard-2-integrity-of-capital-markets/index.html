Introduction

CFA Standard II Integrity of Capital Markets Explained is a core Level I Ethics and Professional Standards topic (LM 2 & LM 3: Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information (MNPI) & Mosaic Theory, Standard II(B) Market Manipulation (Transaction-Based & Information-Based)) focusing on Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information (definition of materiality and nonpublic, Mosaic Theory, information firewalls) and Standard II(B) Market Manipulation (wash trades, spoofing, pump-and-dump). This guide covers the governing equations (Materiality & Nonpublic Tests (Standard II(A)) and Mosaic Theory Safe Harbor), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information (definition of materiality and nonpublic, Mosaic Theory, information firewalls) and Standard II(B) Market Manipulation (wash trades, spoofing, pump-and-dump) links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Ethics and Professional Standards. Materiality & Nonpublic Tests (Standard II(A)) (Material = Reasonable investor would want to know before trading OR would significantly affect price ; Nonpublic = Not yet disseminated to the general marketplace) establishes the primary relationship (Information is nonpublic until it has been broadly distributed to investors (e.g., via regulatory filing/press wire)—selective disclosure to a small group of analysts is STILL nonpublic!), while Mosaic Theory Safe Harbor (Public Information + Non-Material Nonpublic Information + Analyst Skill = Valid Investment Conclusion (No Violation!)) governs Analysts are encouraged to piece together public data and non-material nonpublic observations (e.g., store foot-traffic counts or supplier checks) under the Mosaic Theory.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Distinguishing Illegal MNPI from Valid Mosaic Theory Research

Problem & Given Scenario: Case 1: During a private factory tour, a furniture company's plant manager casually remarks to an analyst that 'we switched sawdust waste contractors last month to save $15,000 a year.' Combining this with public lumber data and retailer surveys, the analyst upgrades the stock. Case 2: At a closed-door dinner for 15 top institutional analysts, the CFO reveals that the company will announce a 40% dividend cut tomorrow morning.

Formula Expression
Material = Reasonable investor would want to know before trading OR would significantly affect price

Nonpublic = Not yet disseminated to the general marketplace

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Case 1 is a textbook application of the Mosaic Theory: the sawdust comment is nonpublic, but completely NON-MATERIAL ($15,000 is trivial to a public company). Combining public info with non-material nonpublic info is 100% legal and encouraged! Case 2 is Material AND Nonpublic (selective disclosure to 15 analysts is NOT public dissemination!). Any analyst who trades or tips before the public press release violates Standard II(A).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Selective disclosure to a private room of Wall Street analysts never makes material information public.

Worked Example 2: Distinguishing Legitimate Trading from Market Manipulation under Standard II(B)

Problem & Given Scenario: Compare three market activities: (1) A trader places simultaneous buy and sell orders for the same stock across two accounts he controls ('wash trading') to make a thinly traded micro-cap appear highly liquid and trigger momentum algorithms. (2) A hedge fund spreads false rumors on an online forum that a bank is insolvent after shorting the bank's stock. (3) An institutional portfolio manager sells a bond in December and repurchases a similar bond to harvest a tax loss.

Formula Expression
Public Information + Non-Material Nonpublic Information + Analyst Skill = Valid Investment Conclusion (No Violation!)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Activity 1 is illegal Transaction-Based Manipulation (artificial volume/price distortion). Activity 2 is illegal Information-Based Manipulation (spreading false rumors). Activity 3 (tax-loss harvesting or exploiting genuine arbitrage/liquidity differences) has a legitimate economic/tax purpose and NO intent to deceive or manipulate market participants -> No violation of Standard II(B).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Standard II(B) hinges on INTENT to deceive or artificially distort prices/volume; legitimate tax-loss selling or high-frequency liquidity provision without deceptive intent is permitted.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: An airline equity analyst hires postgraduate students to sit at major airport gates and count the number of passengers boarding flights of a covered airline over a 30-day period. Combining these passenger counts with publicly disclosed fuel prices and ticket fares, the analyst forecasts a major earnings beat and buys the stock for clients. The analyst has:

  • [A] Violated Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information

  • [B] Not violated the Standards because combining public information with non-material nonpublic observations is permitted under the Mosaic Theory

  • [C] Violated Standard II(B) Market Manipulation

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Counting passengers in a public airport is non-material nonpublic information; synthesizing it with public data to reach an investment conclusion is protected under the Mosaic Theory.

Practice Question 2

Question: A corporate CFO accidentally leaves a confidential merger term sheet on a restaurant table. A CFA candidate sitting at the next table reads the document, realizes a takeover at a 50% premium will be announced on Monday, and immediately buys call options on the target company in her personal account. Has the candidate violated Standard II(A)?

  • [A] No, because the candidate did not steal the document or owe a fiduciary duty to the company

  • [B] Yes, because Standard II(A) prohibits acting or causing others to act on Material Nonpublic Information regardless of how it was obtained (even if overheard by accident)

  • [C] No, because options are derivatives rather than common stock

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Unlike certain narrow legal interpretations of misappropriation, CFA Standard II(A) strictly prohibits trading or causing others to trade on any information that is both Material and Nonpublic, even if overheard accidentally.

Practice Question 3

Question: Which of the following actions constitutes a violation of Standard II(B) Market Manipulation?

  • [A] Selling a losing equity position at year-end to realize a capital loss for tax purposes

  • [B] Entering large limit buy orders that the trader intends to cancel before execution ('spoofing' or 'layering') solely to create a false impression of buying demand and push up the execution price of a sell order on the opposite side of the book

  • [C] Executing a cross-market arbitrage trade between a stock and its futures contract

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Spoofing/layering is transaction-based market manipulation designed to deceive other market participants about true supply and demand.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Materiality & Nonpublic Tests (Standard II(A)).

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Mosaic Theory Safe Harbor.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
