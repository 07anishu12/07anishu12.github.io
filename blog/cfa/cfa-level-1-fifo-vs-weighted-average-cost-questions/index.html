Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, cost flow assumptions (FIFO, Weighted Average, LIFO), periodic vs perpetual inventory systems, and LIFO-to-FIFO financial statement adjustments links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Financial Statement Analysis. Inventory Cost Flow & COGS Identity (COGS = Beginning Inventory + Purchases − Ending Inventory) establishes the primary relationship (Allocates total goods available for sale (GAS) between income statement COGS and balance sheet Ending Inventory.), while LIFO-to-FIFO Balance Sheet & COGS Conversion (Inventory_FIFO = Inventory_LIFO + LIFO Reserve ; COGS_FIFO = COGS_LIFO − Δ(LIFO Reserve)) governs Converts US GAAP LIFO statements to FIFO comparability; Equity increases by LIFO Reserve × (1 − Tax Rate).

Formula 1: Inventory Cost Flow & COGS Identity

Formula Expression
COGS = Beginning Inventory + Purchases − Ending Inventory

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Allocates total goods available for sale (GAS) between income statement COGS and balance sheet Ending Inventory.

Formula Expression

COGS_FIFO = COGS_LIFO − Δ(LIFO Reserve)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Periodic FIFO vs. Weighted Average Cost Calculation

Problem & Given Scenario: Purchases during the quarter: Jan 1 Beginning Inventory = 200 units @ $10 ($2,000); Feb 10 Purchase = 300 units @ $12 ($3,600); Mar 20 Purchase = 500 units @ $14 ($7,000). Total Available = 1,000 units costing $12,600. Units sold = 650 units; Ending Inventory = 350 units.

Formula Expression
COGS = Beginning Inventory + Purchases − Ending Inventory

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Under FIFO: Ending 350 units come from the latest Mar 20 batch @ $14: Ending Inventory_FIFO = 350 × $14 = $4,900; COGS_FIFO = $12,600 − $4,900 = $7,700 (or 200×$10 + 300×$12 + 150×$14 = $7,700). Under Weighted Average Cost (WAC): Average unit cost = $12,600 / 1,000 = $12.60/unit; Ending Inventory_WAC = 350 × $12.60 = $4,410; COGS_WAC = 650 × $12.60 = $8,190.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: In a rising price environment ($10 -> $12 -> $14), FIFO reports $490 higher Ending Inventory ($4,900 vs $4,410) and $490 lower COGS ($7,700 vs $8,190) than Weighted Average Cost.

Worked Example 2: Converting a US GAAP LIFO Company to FIFO for Peer Comparison

Problem & Given Scenario: A US industrial firm reports LIFO Inventory = $420M, COGS_LIFO = $1,100M, Net Income_LIFO = $95M, Beg LIFO Reserve = $60M, End LIFO Reserve = $80M, and Tax Rate = 25%.

Formula Expression
Inventory_FIFO = Inventory_LIFO + LIFO Reserve

COGS_FIFO = COGS_LIFO − Δ(LIFO Reserve)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Inventory_FIFO = $420M + $80M = $500M. COGS_FIFO = $1,100M − ($80M − $60M) = $1,080M. Net Income_FIFO = $95M + $20M × (1 − 0.25) = $95M + $15M = $110M. Cumulative Retained Earnings adjustment = $80M × (1 − 0.25) = +$60M.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Because the LIFO reserve rose by $20M during inflation, FIFO COGS is $20M lower and FIFO Net Income is $15M higher.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A company has Beginning Inventory of 100 units at $20, buys 200 units at $23, and buys 100 units at $26. If it sells 280 units during the period, its Cost of Goods Sold under periodic FIFO is closest to:

  • [A] $6,140

  • [B] $6,180

  • [C] $6,300

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Total available = 400 units ($9,200). Under FIFO, the 280 units sold consist of 100 units @ $20 ($2,000) + 180 units @ $23 ($4,140) = $6,140 (Ending Inventory = $3,060).

Practice Question 2

Question: Under which inventory cost flow method do the periodic and perpetual inventory systems always produce the exact same Cost of Goods Sold and Ending Inventory figures?

  • [A] Weighted Average Cost

  • [B] First-In, First-Out (FIFO)

  • [C] Last-In, First-Out (LIFO)

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Under FIFO (and Specific Identification), the oldest units are always sold first regardless of whether COGS is computed at each sale (perpetual) or at period-end (periodic).

Practice Question 3

Question: A US firm using LIFO reports COGS of $540 million. Its LIFO reserve decreased from $45 million at the start of the year to $37 million at year-end. Its FIFO COGS for the year is:

  • [A] $532 million

  • [B] $540 million

  • [C] $548 million

Tip: Correct Answer: Option C.

Explanation: COGS_FIFO = COGS_LIFO − Δ(LIFO Reserve) = $540M − ($37M − $45M) = $540M − (−$8M) = $548 million.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Inventory Cost Flow & COGS Identity.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in LIFO-to-FIFO Balance Sheet & COGS Conversion.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
