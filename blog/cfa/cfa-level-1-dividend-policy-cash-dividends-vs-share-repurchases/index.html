Introduction

Dividend Policy Explained: Cash Dividends vs Share Repurchases covers wealth equivalence of cash dividends and share repurchases, EPS effect (Earnings Yield vs After-Tax Cost of Funds), Book Value per Share (BVPS) effect (P > BVPS vs P < BVPS), and dividend dates in Level I Corporate Issuers (LM 6 & Equity Investments: Cash dividends vs share repurchases, EPS and BVPS accretion/dilution rules, Miller & Modigliani dividend irrelevance, ex-dividend price drop), with verified formulas, two worked examples, and three practice MCQs.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Share Repurchase EPS Rule (Earnings Yield vs. Financing Cost) (If Earnings Yield (E / P) > After-Tax Cost of Debt/Cash => EPS Increases ; If (E / P) < After-Tax Cost => EPS Decreases) and Share Repurchase Book Value per Share (BVPS) Rule (If Market Price P > Initial BVPS => BVPS Decreases ; If Market Price P < Initial BVPS => BVPS Increases) govern wealth equivalence of cash dividends and share repurchases, EPS effect (Earnings Yield vs After-Tax Cost of Funds), Book Value per Share (BVPS) effect (P > BVPS vs P < BVPS), and dividend dates.

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Share Repurchase EPS Rule (Earnings Yield vs. Financing Cost)

Formula Expression
If Earnings Yield (E / P) > After-Tax Cost of Debt/Cash => EPS Increases

If (E / P) < After-Tax Cost => EPS Decreases

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Buying back shares with borrowed funds or cash increases EPS if and only if the stock's earnings yield (1 / P/E) exceeds the after-tax interest rate.

Formula 2: Share Repurchase Book Value per Share (BVPS) Rule

Formula Expression
If Market Price P > Initial BVPS => BVPS Decreases

If Market Price P < Initial BVPS => BVPS Increases

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Repurchasing shares at a price above Book Value per Share (P/B > 1.0x) reduces remaining BVPS, whereas buying back below BVPS (P/B < 1.0x) increases BVPS.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Comparing EPS and Shareholder Wealth: Cash Dividend vs. Open-Market Share Repurchase

Problem & Given Scenario: A firm has 10M shares trading at $50.00 (Market Cap = $500M, including $50M excess cash earning 0%) and expects Net Income of $45M. Compare (A) paying a $5.00/share cash dividend ($50M total) vs. (B) repurchasing 1M shares at $50.00 ($50M total).

Formula Expression
If Earnings Yield (E / P) > After-Tax Cost of Debt/Cash => EPS Increases

If (E / P) < After-Tax Cost => EPS Decreases

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Option A (Cash Dividend): Ex-dividend share price drops by $5.00 to $45.00 (10M shares = $450M Market Cap). Shareholder wealth = $45.00 share + $5.00 cash = $50.00. EPS = $45M / 10M = $4.50. Option B (Share Repurchase of 1M shares @ $50): Remaining shares = 9M; Market Cap = $450M -> Share price stays $450M / 9M = $50.00! Shareholder wealth = $50.00. New EPS = $45M / 9M = $5.00 (because E/P = 9% > 0% cash yield!).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Absent differential taxes and transaction costs, a $50M cash dividend and a $50M share buyback leave total shareholder wealth identical at $50.00 per share.

Worked Example 2: Testing EPS and BVPS Impact When Borrowing Debt to Repurchase Shares

Problem & Given Scenario: A firm has 20M shares trading at P = $40.00, Net Income = $80M (EPS = $4.00, so P/E = 10.0x and Earnings Yield E/P = 10.0%), and Total Book Equity = $500M (BVPS = $25.00). It borrows $80M at a pre-tax interest rate of 8.0% (tax rate = 25%, so after-tax cost of debt = 6.0%) to repurchase 2M shares at $40.00.

Formula Expression
If Market Price P > Initial BVPS => BVPS Decreases

If Market Price P < Initial BVPS => BVPS Increases

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1 (EPS Impact): After-tax interest cost = $80M × 6.0% = $4.80M; New Net Income = $80M − $4.80M = $75.20M; New Shares = 18M; New EPS = $75.20M / 18M = $4.1778 ≈ $4.18 (increased from $4.00 because E/P [10.0%] > after-tax debt cost [6.0%]!). Step 2 (BVPS Impact): New Book Equity = $500M − $80M = $420M; New BVPS = $420M / 18M = $23.33 (decreased from $25.00 because Repurchase Price [$40] > Initial BVPS [$25]!).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: This single numerical test verifies both CFA Level I & II rules: E/P (10%) > r_d(1−t) (6%) makes the buyback EPS-accretive, while P ($40) > BVPS ($25) makes it BVPS-dilutive.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A company's stock trades at a P/E ratio of 20.0x (meaning its Earnings Yield E/P is 5.0%). If the company borrows funds at an after-tax interest rate of 6.5% to repurchase its own common shares at the current market price, its Earnings Per Share (EPS) will:

  • [A] Increase

  • [B] Decrease because the after-tax borrowing cost (6.5%) exceeds the stock's earnings yield (5.0%)

  • [C] Remain unchanged

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: When the after-tax cost of debt (6.5%) is greater than the earnings yield E/P (1/20 = 5.0%), the interest lost exceeds the earnings reduction per share retired, decreasing EPS.

Practice Question 2

Question: A corporation has a Book Value per Share (BVPS) of $32.00 and its stock currently trades in the market at $24.00 per share (P/B = 0.75x). If the company uses excess cash to repurchase 10% of its shares at $24.00, the Book Value per Share of the remaining shares will:

  • [A] Increase above $32.00

  • [B] Decrease below $32.00

  • [C] Equal $24.00

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Whenever a company repurchases shares at a market price below its current Book Value per Share ($24.00 < $32.00), the remaining shareholders' Book Value per Share increases.

Practice Question 3

Question: In the chronological sequence of corporate cash dividend dates, on which date does the stock first begin trading WITHOUT the right to receive the upcoming dividend?

  • [A] Declaration date

  • [B] Ex-dividend date (typically one business day before the Holder-of-Record date under T+1/T+2 rules)

  • [C] Payment date

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Investors who buy the stock on or after the Ex-Dividend Date do not receive the declared dividend; consequently, the share price drops on the Ex-Dividend Date.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Share Repurchase EPS Rule (Earnings Yield vs. Financing Cost).

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Share Repurchase Book Value per Share (BVPS) Rule.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
