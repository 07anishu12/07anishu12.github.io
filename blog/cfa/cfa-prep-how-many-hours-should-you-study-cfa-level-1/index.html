Worked Example 2: Comparing Weekly Study Schedules for Working Professionals (24 Weeks vs. 16 Weeks)

Problem & Given Scenario: Compare the daily weekday and weekend commitment required to log 320 study hours over a 6-month (24-week) plan vs. a 4-month (16-week) plan, assuming 2 buffer weeks for illness/travel/work emergencies.

Formula Expression
Topic Study Hours = Total Hours × Exam Midpoint Weight % × Personal Difficulty Multiplier

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Plan A (6 Months = 22 net study weeks): 320 hrs / 22 weeks = 14.5 hours/week -> 1.5 hours per weekday morning (Mon–Fri = 7.5 hrs) + 3.5 hours on Saturday and Sunday (7.0 hrs). Plan B (4 Months = 14 net study weeks): 320 hrs / 14 weeks = 22.9 hours/week -> 2.5 hours per weekday (12.5 hrs) + 5.2 hours on Saturday and Sunday (10.4 hrs).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Building 2 explicit buffer weeks into your calendar prevents a single busy work week or illness from derailing your final 30-day mock exam phase.

Practice Question 2

Question: In an effective 320-hour CFA Level I study plan, how should a candidate divide study time between passive reading/videos and active practice (solving Learning Ecosystem Q-Bank questions and full-length timed mock exams)?

  • [A] 90% reading the curriculum twice from cover to cover and 10% practice questions

  • [B] Approximately 40% concept acquisition (reading/summaries) and 60% active retrieval practice (solving 2,000+ Q-Bank questions, End-of-Chapter problems, and 4–6 full timed mock exams)

  • [C] 100% memorizing formula flashcards without mock exams

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Cognitive retention and exam-day speed depend heavily on active problem-solving; at least 50%–60% of total hours should be spent solving practice questions and reviewing mock exam errors.

Practice Question 3

Question: How many weeks before exam day should a CFA candidate aim to finish reading the initial curriculum so that the final phase can be dedicated exclusively to full-length mock exams, weak-area remediation, and Ethics/formula revision?

  • [A] 2 days before the exam

  • [B] At least 4 weeks (28 to 30 days) before the exam window

  • [C] 12 weeks before the exam

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Finishing initial topic coverage 4 weeks before exam day guarantees 28–30 days to complete 4 to 6 full-length mock exams, analyze error logs, and master Ethics and high-yield formulas.
