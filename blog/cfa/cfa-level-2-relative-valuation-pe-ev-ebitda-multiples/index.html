Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Normalized Cyclical EPS: Method 1 (Historical Avg EPS) vs. Method 2 (Avg ROE × Current BVPS)

Formula Expression
EPS_Norm_Method1 = (1/n) ∑ EPS_t

EPS_Norm_Method2 = (Avg Full-Cycle ROE) × Current Book Value per Share (BVPS_0)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Method 2 (Average ROE × Current BVPS) is superior to Method 1 because Method 1 ignores changes in the firm's asset size and equity scale over the business cycle!.

Formula 2: PEG Ratio & Justified Price-to-Sales (P/S) Ratio

Formula Expression
PEG = (P / E) / (100 × g)

Justified P / S = [Net Profit Margin × (1 − b) × (1 + g)] / (r − g)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Shows that the Price-to-Sales (P/S) multiple is directly driven by Net Profit Margin (E_0 / S_0), payout ratio (1 − b), required return r, and growth g.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Normalizing Cyclical Earnings Using Method 1 vs. Method 2 (Level II Favorite)

Problem & Given Scenario: A cyclical steelmaker has grown its Book Value per Share from $20.00 five years ago to $45.00 today (Current Stock Price = $67.50). Over the full 5-year business cycle, its average annual EPS was $3.00 and its average annual ROE was 12.0%. Calculate Normalized EPS and Normalized P/E under Method 1 and Method 2, and explain which is preferred.

Formula Expression
EPS_Norm_Method1 = (1/n) ∑ EPS_t

EPS_Norm_Method2 = (Avg Full-Cycle ROE) × Current Book Value per Share (BVPS_0)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Method 1 (Historical Average EPS): Normalized EPS = $3.00 -> Normalized P/E = $67.50 / $3.00 = 22.50x. Method 2 (Average Cycle ROE × Current BVPS): Normalized EPS = 12.0% × $45.00 = $5.40 -> Normalized P/E = $67.50 / $5.40 = 12.50x.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Because the company more than doubled its equity base ($20 -> $45 BVPS), Method 1 ($3.00 avg EPS) severely understates current mid-cycle earning power and overstates P/E (22.5x vs 12.5x). Method 2 (Avg ROE × Current BVPS) is preferred by the CFA curriculum.

Worked Example 2: Cross-Sectional Regression of Predicted P/E and PEG Ratio Evaluation

Problem & Given Scenario: An analyst estimates a cross-sectional regression across 100 retail stocks: Predicted P/E = 6.50 + 35.0(Dividend Payout) + 80.0(EPS Growth g) − 2.50(Beta). Retailer K has Payout = 0.40, Growth g = 0.12 (12%), Beta = 1.20, and currently trades at an actual P/E of 21.0x.

Formula Expression
PEG = (P / E) / (100 × g)

Justified P / S = [Net Profit Margin × (1 − b) × (1 + g)] / (r − g)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1 (Predicted Fundamental P/E): 6.50 + 35.0(0.40) + 80.0(0.12) − 2.50(1.20) = 6.50 + 14.00 + 9.60 − 3.00 = 27.10x. Step 2 (Valuation Signal): Since Actual P/E (21.0x) < Predicted P/E (27.10x), Retailer K is undervalued relative to peers. Step 3 (PEG Ratio): PEG = 21.0 / 12.0 = 1.75x.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Cross-sectional regressions capture multiple fundamental drivers simultaneously, though they suffer from multicollinearity and time-varying slope instability.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: According to the 'Molodovsky effect' in cyclical company valuation, why do cyclical stocks typically trade at a HIGH trailing P/E multiple at the bottom of a recession and a LOW trailing P/E multiple at the peak of an economic boom?

  • [A] Because investors expect earnings to mean-revert—recovering strongly from depressed trough earnings and declining from unsustainable peak earnings

  • [B] Because interest rates are highest at the bottom of a recession

  • [C] Because cyclical companies never pay dividends

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Market prices anticipate mid-cycle normalized earnings: dividing a forward-looking price by temporarily depressed trough EPS produces a high trailing P/E at the bottom of the cycle (the Molodovsky effect).

Practice Question 2

Question: When ranking a group of companies that includes several firms with negative or zero earnings per share, the CFA curriculum recommends ranking them by:

  • [A] Trailing P/E from lowest to highest

  • [B] Earnings Yield (E/P), because E/P is continuous through zero and correctly ranks companies with larger losses as having lower (more negative) yields

  • [C] PEG ratio

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: P/E is discontinuous at EPS = 0 and inverts the ranking for negative EPS (a company losing $1 has a more negative P/E than one losing $2 at the same price); Earnings Yield (E/P) preserves the correct continuous order.

Practice Question 3

Question: Which of the following is a primary limitation of the PEG ratio [(P/E) / g] in relative equity valuation?

  • [A] It cannot be computed for companies with positive earnings growth

  • [B] It assumes a strictly linear relationship between P/E and growth g, does not account for differences in risk (beta / required return r), and ignores the duration of the high-growth period

  • [C] It requires book value of equity

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: The PEG ratio ignores risk (beta/r), dividend payout differences, and the fact that P/E is a non-linear function of g.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Normalized Cyclical EPS: Method 1 (Historical Avg EPS) vs. Method 2 (Avg ROE × Current BVPS).

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in PEG Ratio & Justified Price-to-Sales (P/S) Ratio.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
