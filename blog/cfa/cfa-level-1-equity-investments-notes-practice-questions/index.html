Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Margin Purchase Leverage Return and Maintenance Margin Call Price

Problem & Given Scenario: An investor buys 1,000 shares of a stock at P_0 = $80.00 ($80,000 total position) using an initial margin requirement of 50% ($40,000 equity, $40,000 margin loan at 6.0% annual interest). The maintenance margin requirement is 25%. (1) Find the exact stock price P_Call that triggers a margin call. (2) If the stock rises to $96.00 (+20%) after 1 year and pays a $2.00/share dividend, calculate the investor's net leveraged return.

Formula Expression
Margin Return = [(Sale Proceeds − Loan − Margin Interest − Commissions) / Initial Equity] − 1

P_Call = P_0 × (1 − Initial Margin %) / (1 − Maintenance Margin %)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1 (Margin Call Price): P_Call = $80.00 × (1 − 0.50) / (1 − 0.25) = $40.00 / 0.75 = $53.33 per share. Step 2 (Leveraged Return at P_1 = $96.00): Ending stock value = $96,000; Dividends = $2,000; Loan interest = 6.0% × $40,000 = $2,400. Net Ending Equity = $96,000 − $40,000 + $2,000 − $2,400 = $55,600. Leveraged Return = ($55,600 − $40,000) / $40,000 = $15,600 / $40,000 = +39.00% (vs +22.50% unleveraged).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Buying on 50% margin doubles the price return sensitivity (2.0x leverage multiplier) minus the after-dividend financing drag.

Worked Example 2: Comparing Cumulative vs. Non-Cumulative and Participating Preferred Shares

Problem & Given Scenario: A firm has 100,000 shares of $100 par, 6.0% Cumulative Preferred Stock. It suspended dividends for the prior 2 years and now wishes to resume common stock dividends.

Formula Expression
P / B = Market Value of Equity / Book Value of Equity

Intrinsic Value V_0 = ∑ [CF_t / (1 + k_e)^t]

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Annual preferred dividend = 100,000 × $100 × 6.0% = $600,000/year. Because the shares are cumulative, the firm must pay 2 years of dividends in arrears ($1,200,000) plus the current year's preferred dividend ($600,000) = $1,800,000 total to preferred shareholders before a single dollar can be paid to common shareholders.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Cumulative preferred stock protects dividend priority across suspended years, whereas non-cumulative preferred dividends are permanently forfeited if not declared.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: An investor purchases 500 shares of stock at $60.00 per share using a 60% initial margin requirement. If the broker enforces a 30% maintenance margin requirement, a margin call will first be triggered when the stock price falls below:

  • [A] $34.29

  • [B] $40.00

  • [C] $42.00

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: P_Call = P_0 × (1 − Initial Margin) / (1 − Maintenance Margin) = $60.00 × (1 − 0.60) / (1 − 0.30) = $24.00 / 0.70 = $34.2857 ≈ $34.29.

Practice Question 2

Question: Which type of American Depositary Receipt (ADR) allows a foreign issuer both to list its shares on a major US exchange (such as the NYSE) and to raise new equity capital through a public offering in the United States?

  • [A] Unsponsored ADR

  • [B] Level II Sponsored ADR

  • [C] Level III Sponsored ADR

Tip: Correct Answer: Option C.

Explanation: Only a Level III Sponsored ADR permits the foreign issuer to raise new capital in US public markets via a full SEC registration (Form F-1).

Practice Question 3

Question: Compared to statutory voting, cumulative voting for a corporate board of directors:

  • [A] Favors majority shareholders by applying votes one seat at a time

  • [B] Benefits minority shareholders by allowing them to concentrate all their votes (Shares × Board Seats) on a single candidate

  • [C] Prohibits proxy voting

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Cumulative voting lets a minority shareholder multiply their shares by the number of seats up for election and cast that entire block for one director.
