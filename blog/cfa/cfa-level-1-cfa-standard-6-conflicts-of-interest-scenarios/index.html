Introduction

CFA Standard VI Conflicts of Interest: Common Exam Scenarios covers Standard VI(A) full plain-language disclosure of conflicts (stock ownership, IB ties, board service), Standard VI(B) trade priority hierarchy and fee-paying family accounts, and Standard VI(C) referral fee disclosure in Level I Ethics and Professional Standards (LM 2 & LM 3: Standard VI(A) Avoid or Disclose Conflicts, Standard VI(B) Priority of Transactions (Clients > Employer > Personal; Family Accounts), Standard VI(C) Referral Fees), with verified formulas, two worked examples, and three practice MCQs.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Standard VI(B) Strict Priority of Transactions Hierarchy (1st: Clients -> 2nd: Employer -> 3rd (Last): Personal & Beneficial-Interest Accounts) and Fee-Paying Family Account Rule vs. Beneficial-Interest Account Rule (Fee-Paying Family Account (with no manager ownership/benefit) = Treated Equally with All Other Client Accounts (Eligible for pro-rata trades, NOT pushed to back of line!)) govern Standard VI(A) full plain-language disclosure of conflicts (stock ownership, IB ties, board service), Standard VI(B) trade priority hierarchy and fee-paying family accounts, and Standard VI(C) referral fee disclosure.

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Standard VI(B) Strict Priority of Transactions Hierarchy

Formula Expression
1st: Clients -> 2nd: Employer -> 3rd (Last): Personal & Beneficial-Interest Accounts

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Personal and beneficial-interest accounts (including spouse/children accounts where the manager has a pecuniary interest) must NEVER front-run client or employer trades.

Formula 2: Fee-Paying Family Account Rule vs. Beneficial-Interest Account Rule

Formula Expression
Fee-Paying Family Account (with no manager ownership/benefit) = Treated Equally with All Other Client Accounts (Eligible for pro-rata trades, NOT pushed to back of line!)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Crucial distinction: If a parent or sibling pays standard management fees and the manager has no beneficial ownership in the account, pushing the parent's account behind other clients violates Standard III(B) Fair Dealing!.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Case Study: Priority of Transactions for Fee-Paying Family Accounts vs. Personal Accounts (Standard VI(B))

Problem & Given Scenario: Portfolio Manager David manages 50 client accounts, including: (1) Account M, owned by David's mother, who pays the firm's standard 1.0% annual management fee (David has zero beneficial ownership or inheritance control); and (2) Account W, a joint brokerage account owned by David and his wife. David initiates a block purchase of an undervalued mid-cap stock.

Formula Expression
1st: Clients -> 2nd: Employer -> 3rd (Last): Personal & Beneficial-Interest Accounts

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Under Standard VI(B) and III(B): Account M (fee-paying mother's account with no beneficial interest) MUST be included in the initial client block trade on a pro-rata basis alongside the other 49 regular clients! (Holding his mother's fee-paying account back until after other clients would violate Standard III(B) Fair Dealing!). Conversely, Account W (David & wife's beneficial-interest personal account) MUST wait until all client and employer trades have been executed and clients have had adequate time to act.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: This exact distinction between a fee-paying family client account (trade WITH clients) and a beneficial-interest personal account (trade AFTER clients) is tested on virtually every CFA Ethics exam.

Worked Example 2: Case Study: Referral Fees under Standard VI(C)

Problem & Given Scenario: Wealth advisor Priya refers tax-planning clients to an external CPA firm. For every client who signs up, the CPA firm pays Priya a $500 cash finder's fee AND refers estate clients back to Priya.

Formula Expression
Fee-Paying Family Account (with no manager ownership/benefit) = Treated Equally with All Other Client Accounts (Eligible for pro-rata trades, NOT pushed to back of line!)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Under Standard VI(C) Referral Fees, Priya MUST disclose to the client or prospective client BEFORE entering into any formal agreement for services: (1) the existence of the referral arrangement, and (2) the nature and estimated dollar value of BOTH the $500 cash fee AND the reciprocal client referrals!

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Referral fee disclosure must occur BEFORE the client signs the engagement contract so the client can evaluate both the true cost of the service and any bias in the recommendation.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A portfolio manager manages a normal fee-paying discretionary account for her brother-in-law, in which the portfolio manager has no beneficial ownership or financial interest. When allocating shares of a new equity block trade suitable for all growth accounts, the portfolio manager should:

  • [A] Wait until 24 hours after all other client accounts are filled before buying shares for her brother-in-law's account

  • [B] Allocate shares to her brother-in-law's account on an equal, pro-rata basis alongside all other regular client accounts

  • [C] Exclude her brother-in-law from all equity trades

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: A family member's account that pays normal firm fees and in which the manager has no beneficial interest must be treated just like any other regular client account; subordinating it violates Standard III(B) Fair Dealing.

Practice Question 2

Question: Under Standard VI(C) Referral Fees, when must a member or candidate disclose to a prospective client the existence, nature, and estimated dollar value of a referral fee arrangement?

  • [A] Within 30 days after the client signs the investment management contract

  • [B] Before entering into any formal agreement for investment services with the prospective client

  • [C] Only if the referral fee exceeds $1,000 in cash

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Referral fee disclosures must be made BEFORE the client enters into a formal service agreement, and must cover both cash fees and non-cash/reciprocal referral benefits.

Practice Question 3

Question: An equity analyst holds 500 shares of a large-cap stock in his personal retirement account, inherited from his grandfather. He is assigned by his research director to initiate coverage on that company. Under Standard VI(A) Avoid or Disclose Conflicts, the analyst must:

  • [A] Immediately sell the shares at a loss

  • [B] Disclose the personal share ownership to his employer/compliance officer and prominently disclose the holding in the published research report

  • [C] Keep the holding secret because 500 shares cannot move the stock's price

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Unless firm compliance policy requires divestiture or reassignment, Standard VI(A) requires full, prominent disclosure of the personal ownership conflict to both the employer and report readers.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Standard VI(B) Strict Priority of Transactions Hierarchy.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Fee-Paying Family Account Rule vs. Beneficial-Interest Account Rule.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
