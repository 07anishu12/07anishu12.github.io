Introduction

How to Calculate Goodwill in a Business Combination covers calculating Full Goodwill and Partial Goodwill when acquiring < 100% control, measuring Non-Controlling Interest (NCI), bargain purchase gains, and Goodwill Impairment under IFRS (IAS 36) vs US GAAP (ASC 350) in Level II Financial Statement Analysis (LM 1: Intercorporate Investments — Full Goodwill (US GAAP & IFRS option) vs Partial Goodwill (IFRS option), Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) measurement, bargain purchase gain, goodwill impairment under IFRS vs US GAAP), with verified formulas, two worked examples, and three practice MCQs.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Full Goodwill & Fair-Value Non-Controlling Interest (US GAAP & IFRS Option) (Full Goodwill = Acquisition Fair Value of 100% of Target − Fair Value of Target's Identifiable Net Assets ; NCI_Full = NCI % × Fair Value of 100% of Target) and Partial Goodwill & Proportionate Non-Controlling Interest (IFRS Only Option) (Partial Goodwill = Purchase Price Paid (for P%) − [P% × Fair Value of Target's Identifiable Net Assets] ; NCI_Partial = NCI % × FV of Identifiable Net Assets) govern calculating Full Goodwill and Partial Goodwill when acquiring < 100% control, measuring Non-Controlling Interest (NCI), bargain purchase gains, and Goodwill Impairment under IFRS (IAS 36) vs US GAAP (ASC 350).

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Full Goodwill & Fair-Value Non-Controlling Interest (US GAAP & IFRS Option)

Formula Expression
Full Goodwill = Acquisition Fair Value of 100% of Target − Fair Value of Target's Identifiable Net Assets

NCI_Full = NCI % × Fair Value of 100% of Target

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Allocates goodwill to both the controlling parent and the non-controlling interest based on the full implied fair value of the subsidiary.

Formula 2: Partial Goodwill & Proportionate Non-Controlling Interest (IFRS Only Option)

Formula Expression
Partial Goodwill = Purchase Price Paid (for P%) − [P% × Fair Value of Target's Identifiable Net Assets]

NCI_Partial = NCI % × FV of Identifiable Net Assets

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:

  • Permitted ONLY under IFRS (prohibited under US GAAP).

  • excludes NCI's share of goodwill, resulting in lower Goodwill, lower Total Assets, and lower Total Equity (NCI) than Full Goodwill.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Calculating Full Goodwill vs. Partial Goodwill and NCI on an 80% Acquisition

Problem & Given Scenario: Parent P acquires 80% of Target T's voting shares for $600M cash (implying 100% Fair Value of Target T = $600M / 0.80 = $750M). On the acquisition date, Target T's Book Value of Net Assets is $450M, and fair value appraisals identify $150M of undervalued PP&E and patents (so Fair Value of Identifiable Net Assets = $450M + $150M = $600M). Calculate Goodwill and Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) under (1) Full Goodwill (US GAAP / IFRS) and (2) Partial Goodwill (IFRS only).

Formula Expression
Full Goodwill = Acquisition Fair Value of 100% of Target − Fair Value of Target's Identifiable Net Assets

NCI_Full = NCI % × Fair Value of 100% of Target

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Method 1 (Full Goodwill — Mandatory under US GAAP, Optional under IFRS): Total Implied Fair Value (100%) = $750M; Less FV of Identifiable Net Assets ($600M) => Full Goodwill = $750M − $600M = $150M. Fair-Value NCI (20%) = 20% × $750M = $150M. Method 2 (Partial Goodwill — IFRS Only): Purchase Price Paid (80%) = $600M; Less 80% of FV of Identifiable Net Assets (80% × $600M = $480M) => Partial Goodwill = $600M − $480M = $120M. Proportionate NCI (20%) = 20% × $600M = $120M.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Compared to Full Goodwill ($150M Goodwill, $150M NCI), IFRS Partial Goodwill reports $30M less Goodwill ($120M) and $30M less NCI Equity ($120M), resulting in a higher Debt-to-Equity ratio and higher ROA!

Worked Example 2: Goodwill Impairment Testing Under IFRS (Cash-Generating Unit) vs. US GAAP (Reporting Unit)

Problem & Given Scenario: A Reporting Unit / Cash-Generating Unit (CGU) has a Total Carrying Value of $800M (which includes $120M of Goodwill) and a Fair Value (and Recoverable Amount) of $710M. Carrying value of identifiable net assets is $680M.

Formula Expression
Partial Goodwill = Purchase Price Paid (for P%) − [P% × Fair Value of Target's Identifiable Net Assets]

NCI_Partial = NCI % × FV of Identifiable Net Assets

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Under both modern US GAAP (one-step ASC 350 test) and IFRS (IAS 36): Total Impairment Loss = Carrying Value of Unit − Fair Value (Recoverable Amount) = $800M − $710M = $90M. All $90M is applied first to reduce Goodwill from $120M down to $30M (capped at the $120M Goodwill balance under US GAAP; under IFRS, any excess above $120M would then reduce other CGU assets pro-rata). Goodwill impairment reversals are STRICTLY PROHIBITED under both IFRS and US GAAP!

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Modern US GAAP and IFRS both compare the unit's Total Carrying Value against its Fair Value/Recoverable Amount in a single step, writing down Goodwill first.

Comparison & Reference Analysis

Accounting Feature Full Goodwill Method (GAAP & IFRS) Partial Goodwill Method (IFRS Only) Balance Sheet & Ratio Impact
Goodwill Recognized 100% (Parent + NCI share) Parent's % share only Full Goodwill has higher Total Assets
Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) % × Full Fair Value of Subsidiary % × FV of Identifiable Net Assets Full Goodwill has higher Total Equity
Debt-to-Equity Ratio Lower (larger NCI Equity denominator) Higher (smaller NCI Equity) Partial Goodwill looks more leveraged
Bargain Purchase (Price < FV Net Assets) Gain in P&L immediately (no negative GW) Gain in P&L immediately Identical under both IFRS & US GAAP

Real-World Financial Applications

Analysts apply Full Goodwill & Fair-Value Non-Controlling Interest (US GAAP & IFRS Option) and Partial Goodwill & Proportionate Non-Controlling Interest (IFRS Only Option) across Financial Statement Analysis valuation and risk management.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: Acquirer Corp purchases 75% of Target Inc. for $450 million (implying a 100% fair value of $600 million). Target Inc.'s identifiable net assets have a book value of $380 million and a fair value of $480 million. The Goodwill recognized under the Full Goodwill method (US GAAP) and Partial Goodwill method (IFRS option), respectively, is:

  • [A] Full Goodwill: $120 million; Partial Goodwill: $90 million

  • [B] Full Goodwill: $220 million; Partial Goodwill: $165 million

  • [C] Full Goodwill: $90 million; Partial Goodwill: $120 million

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Full Goodwill = 100% Implied Fair Value ($600M) − FV of Identifiable Net Assets ($480M) = $120 million. Partial Goodwill = Purchase Price ($450M) − 75% × FV of Identifiable Net Assets (0.75 × $480M = $360M) = $90 million.

Practice Question 2

Question: In a business combination where the fair value of the target's identifiable net assets ($520 million) EXCEEDS the total acquisition purchase price ($490 million)—a 'bargain purchase'—how is the $30 million difference accounted for under both IFRS and US GAAP?

  • [A] Recorded as negative goodwill on the balance sheet and amortized over 10 years

  • [B] Recognized immediately as a $30 million bargain purchase gain in profit or loss (income statement) on the acquisition date

  • [C] Credited directly to Retained Earnings without passing through the income statement

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Both IFRS 3 and US GAAP ASC 805 prohibit recognizing negative goodwill on the balance sheet; any bargain purchase excess is recognized immediately as a gain on the income statement.

Practice Question 3

Question: Once a Goodwill impairment loss is recognized on the income statement, under which accounting standard may that Goodwill impairment loss be reversed if the reporting unit's fair value recovers in a later year?

  • [A] Under IFRS only

  • [B] Under US GAAP only

  • [C] Under NEITHER IFRS nor US GAAP—goodwill impairment reversals are strictly prohibited under both standards

Tip: Correct Answer: Option C.

Explanation: While IFRS permits reversing impairments on tangible PP&E and finite-life intangibles (IAS 36), BOTH IFRS and US GAAP strictly prohibit reversing Goodwill impairments.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Full Goodwill & Fair-Value Non-Controlling Interest (US GAAP & IFRS Option).

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Partial Goodwill & Proportionate Non-Controlling Interest (IFRS Only Option).

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
