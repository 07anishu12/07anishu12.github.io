Introduction

How to Calculate Basic EPS and Diluted EPS With Examples is a core Level I Financial Statement Analysis topic (LM 2: Analyzing Income Statements — Basic EPS, weighted average shares, stock splits/dividends, Diluted EPS (if-converted and treasury stock methods), antidilutive securities) focusing on weighted average common shares with retroactive stock split adjustments, Basic EPS, Diluted EPS via if-converted and treasury stock methods, and antidilution screening. This guide covers the governing equations (Basic Earnings Per Share (Basic EPS) and Diluted EPS & Treasury Stock Method), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, weighted average common shares with retroactive stock split adjustments, Basic EPS, Diluted EPS via if-converted and treasury stock methods, and antidilution screening links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Financial Statement Analysis. Basic Earnings Per Share (Basic EPS) (Basic EPS = (Net Income − Preferred Dividends) / Weighted Average Common Shares (WACS)) establishes the primary relationship (Deducts declared cumulative or non-cumulative preferred dividends from Net Income and divides by time-weighted common shares.), while Diluted EPS & Treasury Stock Method (Diluted EPS = [NI − Pref Div + Conv Pref Div + Conv Debt Int × (1 − t)] / (WACS + New Conv Shares + Net Option Shares)) governs Net Option Shares = Options × [(Average Market Price − Exercise Price) / Average Market Price] when Average Price > Exercise Price.

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Basic Earnings Per Share (Basic EPS)

Formula Expression
Basic EPS = (Net Income − Preferred Dividends) / Weighted Average Common Shares (WACS)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Deducts declared cumulative or non-cumulative preferred dividends from Net Income and divides by time-weighted common shares.

Formula 2: Diluted EPS & Treasury Stock Method

Formula Expression
Diluted EPS = [NI − Pref Div + Conv Pref Div + Conv Debt Int × (1 − t)] / (WACS + New Conv Shares + Net Option Shares)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Net Option Shares = Options × [(Average Market Price − Exercise Price) / Average Market Price] when Average Price > Exercise Price.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Calculating WACS and Basic EPS with a Mid-Year Share Issuance and 2-for-1 Stock Split

Problem & Given Scenario: Net Income = $33,000,000; Preferred Dividends = $3,000,000. On Jan 1, 8,000,000 shares were outstanding. On April 1 (9 months remaining), 2,000,000 new shares were issued for cash. On October 1, the firm executed a 2-for-1 stock split.

Formula Expression
Basic EPS = (Net Income − Preferred Dividends) / Weighted Average Common Shares (WACS)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: Time-weight pre-split shares: Jan 1 shares = 8,000,000 × (12/12) = 8,000,000; Apr 1 issuance = 2,000,000 × (9/12) = 1,500,000; Pre-split WACS = 9,500,000. Step 2: Apply 2-for-1 split retroactively to all prior periods in the year: Adjusted WACS = 9,500,000 × 2 = 19,000,000 shares. Step 3: Basic EPS = ($33,000,000 − $3,000,000) / 19,000,000 = $30,000,000 / 19,000,000 = $1.5789 ≈ $1.58 per share.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Stock dividends and stock splits are always applied retroactively to the beginning of the year (or issuance date if later) when computing WACS.

Worked Example 2: Comprehensive Diluted EPS with Options, Convertible Bonds, and Antidilutive Screening

Problem & Given Scenario: A firm has Basic EPS = $2.50 ($25,000,000 Net Income / 10,000,000 WACS, no preferred stock) and tax rate = 20%. It has: (1) 500,000 employee options with strike $30 (average stock price = $50); and (2) $10,000,000 par of 6.0% convertible bonds convertible into 180,000 common shares.

Formula Expression
Diluted EPS = [NI − Pref Div + Conv Pref Div + Conv Debt Int × (1 − t)] / (WACS + New Conv Shares + Net Option Shares)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1 (Options — Treasury Stock Method): Proceeds = 500,000 × $30 = $15,000,000; Shares repurchased @ $50 = 300,000; Net new option shares = 500,000 − 300,000 = 200,000 (dilutive since 0/200,000 < $2.50). EPS with options = $25,000,000 / 10,200,000 = $2.4510 ≈ $2.45. Step 2 (Convertible Bonds — If-Converted Test): After-tax interest saved = $10,000,000 × 6.0% × (1 − 0.20) = $480,000. Per-share impact = $480,000 / 180,000 = $2.67 per share. Since $2.67 > $2.45 (and > $2.50 Basic EPS), the convertible bonds are antidilutive and excluded! Final Diluted EPS = $2.45.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Always test convertible securities individually against Basic EPS (and ranked dilutive EPS); excluding the antidilutive convertible bond prevents overstating Diluted EPS.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A company has 4,000,000 common shares outstanding throughout the year, Net Income of $12,000,000, and no preferred stock (Basic EPS = $3.00). It also has 600,000 warrants outstanding with an exercise price of $28.00. If the average market price of the common stock during the year was $40.00 (and year-end price was $45.00), Diluted EPS is closest to:

  • [A] $2.87

  • [B] $2.84

  • [C] $3.00

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Under the treasury stock method, always use the average market price ($40.00): Net new shares = 600,000 × [($40 − $28) / $40] = 600,000 × 0.30 = 180,000 shares. Diluted EPS = $12,000,000 / (4,000,000 + 180,000) = $12,000,000 / 4,180,000 = $2.8708 ≈ $2.87.

Practice Question 2

Question: A firm with Basic EPS of $2.20 has 100,000 shares of 5% convertible preferred stock ($100 par value, $5.00 annual dividend per share), each convertible into 2 common shares. The convertible preferred stock is:

  • [A] Dilutive because $5.00 / 2 = $2.50 > $2.20

  • [B] Antidilutive because $5.00 / 2 = $2.50 > $2.20, and must be excluded from Diluted EPS

  • [C] Dilutive because preferred dividends are not tax-deductible

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: The incremental EPS of the convertible preferred is $5.00 / 2 = $2.50 per share, which exceeds Basic EPS ($2.20). Including it would increase EPS, so it is antidilutive and excluded.

Practice Question 3

Question: On January 1, a company had 1,000,000 shares outstanding. On July 1, it issued 200,000 new shares for cash. On November 1, it declared a 10% stock dividend. The weighted average number of common shares for the year is:

  • [A] 1,116,667

  • [B] 1,210,000

  • [C] 1,320,000

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Pre-dividend time-weighted shares = 1,000,000 × (12/12) + 200,000 × (6/12) = 1,100,000. Applying the 10% stock dividend retroactively multiplies by 1.10: 1,100,000 × 1.10 = 1,210,000 shares.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Basic Earnings Per Share (Basic EPS).

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Diluted EPS & Treasury Stock Method.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
