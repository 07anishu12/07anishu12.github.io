Introduction

CFA Economics Level 1: Complete Notes and Important Formulas is a core Level I Economics topic (LM 1–8: The Firm and Market Structures, Business Cycles, Fiscal Policy, Monetary Policy, Geopolitics, International Trade, Capital Flows and FX) focusing on microeconomic market structures (perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, monopoly), HHI concentration, AD-AS business cycles, and FX parity. Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, microeconomic market structures (perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, monopoly), HHI concentration, AD-AS business cycles, and FX parity links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Economics. Profit Maximization Rule & Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) (MR = MC ; MR = P × [1 − (1 / |E_d|)] ; HHI = ∑ (Market Share_i)^2) establishes the primary relationship (All firms maximize profit where Marginal Revenue equals Marginal Cost; HHI sums squared market shares to measure industry concentration and antitrust risk.), while GDP Expenditure Identity & Fiscal Multiplier (GDP = C + I + G + (X − M) ; Fiscal Multiplier = 1 / [1 − MPC × (1 − t)]) governs Decomposes aggregate output into consumption, investment, government spending, and net exports, and quantifies fiscal stimulus amplification.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Calculating N-Firm Concentration Ratio and Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI)

Problem & Given Scenario: An industry has 5 competitors with market shares of 35%, 25%, 20%, 10%, and 10%. Suppose the two smallest firms (10% and 10%) merge into a single 20% firm.

Formula Expression
MR = MC

MR = P × [1 − (1 / |E_d|)]

HHI = ∑ (Market Share_i)^2

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Pre-Merger: 3-Firm Concentration Ratio (CR_3) = 35% + 25% + 20% = 80.0%. Pre-Merger HHI = 35^2 + 25^2 + 20^2 + 10^2 + 10^2 = 1,225 + 625 + 400 + 100 + 100 = 2,450 (or 0.2450). Post-Merger: CR_3 remains 35% + 25% + 20% = 80.0% (insensitive to #4 and #5 merging!), whereas Post-Merger HHI = 35^2 + 25^2 + 20^2 + 20^2 = 1,225 + 625 + 400 + 400 = 2,650 (+200 point jump!).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Unlike simple N-firm concentration ratios, the HHI detects mergers outside the top N firms and weights large market shares quadratically.

Worked Example 2: Monopolist Optimal Pricing Using Price Elasticity of Demand

Problem & Given Scenario: A pharmaceutical patent holder faces a price elasticity of demand of |E_d| = 2.50 and has a constant marginal production cost of MC = $30.00 per unit.

Formula Expression
GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)

Fiscal Multiplier = 1 / [1 − MPC × (1 − t)]

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Profit maximization requires MR = MC => P × [1 − (1 / 2.50)] = $30.00 => P × (1 − 0.40) = $30.00 => 0.60 × P = $30.00 => Optimal Price P = $50.00 per unit (a 40% Lerner Index markup (P − MC)/P = 1/2.5 = 40%).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: As demand becomes less elastic (smaller |E_d| > 1), the monopolist's optimal markup over marginal cost increases.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: In which market structure do firms face a downward-sloping demand curve, differentiate their products through branding, and earn zero economic profit in long-run equilibrium due to low barriers to entry?

  • [A] Perfect competition

  • [B] Monopolistic competition

  • [C] Oligopoly

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Monopolistic competition combines downward-sloping differentiated product demand (P > MC) with free entry and exit, driving long-run economic profit to zero (P = ATC).

Practice Question 2

Question: In the short run, a perfectly competitive firm will continue operating at an economic loss rather than shutting down production immediately as long as market price (P) is:

  • [A] Greater than or equal to Average Variable Cost (P ≥ AVC)

  • [B] Greater than Average Total Cost (P > ATC)

  • [C] Greater than Average Fixed Cost (P > AFC)

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: When AVC ≤ P < ATC, the firm covers all variable costs and contributes toward fixed costs; if P falls below minimum AVC, the firm shuts down in the short run.

Practice Question 3

Question: An industry consists of four firms with market shares of 40%, 30%, 20%, and 10%. Its Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) on a 10,000-point scale is:

  • [A] 2,500

  • [B] 3,000

  • [C] 9,000

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: HHI = 40^2 + 30^2 + 20^2 + 10^2 = 1,600 + 900 + 400 + 100 = 3,000.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Profit Maximization Rule & Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI).

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in GDP Expenditure Identity & Fiscal Multiplier.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
