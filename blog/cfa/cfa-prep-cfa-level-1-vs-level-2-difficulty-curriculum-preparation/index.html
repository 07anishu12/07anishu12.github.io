Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

• Subjects that GAIN potential upside weight or analytical depth at Level II: Derivatives (5%–8% at L1 -> 5%–10% at L2, jumping from basic payoffs to BSM, Binomial trees, Swaps, and FRAs), Corporate Issuers / M&A, Equity Valuation (11%–14% -> 10%–15% with full FCFF/RI/Private valuation), Fixed Income (11%–14% -> 10%–15% with Binomial trees, CVA, CDS), and Portfolio Management (8%–12% -> 10%–15%). • Subjects that narrow into deep specialized modules at Level II: FSA (moves from basic ratios/LIFO to Intercorporate Consolidation, Pensions, Multinational FX, and Banks) and Quantitative Methods (moves from TVM/basic stats to Multiple Regression, Time Series, and Machine Learning).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: At Level II, every single topic converges on one central theme: Asset Valuation and Risk Analysis.

Level I Question (90 seconds): 'A bond trades at $100. If yields fall 25 bps its price is $101.50; if yields rise 25 bps its price is $98.60. What is its approximate modified duration?' (1-step plug-in: [101.50 − 98.60] / [2 × 0.0025 × 100] = 5.80). Level II Vignette Question (3 minutes): Exhibit 1 shows a 3-year binomial interest rate tree calibrated to the spot curve and three bonds (Straight, Callable at $100 in Year 1, Putable at $100 in Year 1). Question 1 asks you to compute the value of the Callable Bond via backward induction capping the down-node; Question 2 asks how an increase in rate volatility affects the Callable Bond's OAS; Question 3 asks which bond has the lowest Down-Duration; and Question 4 asks you to hedge the bond using a Pay-Fixed Interest Rate Swap!

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Level II requires understanding how multiple concepts interact inside a real-world portfolio case.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: According to the CFA Institute's learning taxonomy across the three levels of the CFA Program, how does the primary cognitive focus progress from Level I to Level II to Level III?

  • [A] Level I: Portfolio Management; Level II: Definitions; Level III: Accounting

  • [B] Level I: Knowledge and Comprehension (investment tools and concepts); Level II: Application and Analysis (asset valuation and vignette case analysis); Level III: Synthesis and Evaluation (portfolio management and wealth planning with constructed-response essay questions)

  • [C] All three levels test identical multiple-choice recall

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: The CFA Program follows Bloom's taxonomy: Level I focuses on Knowledge/Comprehension, Level II on Application/Analysis (Valuation), and Level III on Synthesis/Evaluation (Portfolio Management).

Practice Question 2

Question: Why is the historical average pass rate for CFA Level II (~45%–46%) slightly higher than the historical average pass rate for CFA Level I (~40%–43%) even though candidates almost universally rate the Level II curriculum as significantly more difficult?

  • [A] Because Level II questions only have two options (A and B)

  • [B] Because of self-selection/survivorship bias: 100% of the candidates sitting for CFA Level II have already proven their capability by passing CFA Level I, whereas the Level I pool includes many underprepared first-time test takers

  • [C] Because Level II has no Ethics section

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: The Level II candidate pool is pre-screened by Level I, removing the bottom ~58% of initial entrants; competing against a pool of proven Level I passers makes Level II intensely competitive.

Practice Question 3

Question: What study habit that often works for passing CFA Level I is most likely to FAIL at CFA Level II?

  • [A] Solving official End-of-Chapter questions

  • [B] Relying purely on rote memorization of standalone one-line formulas and skimming summary flashcards without practicing multi-step accounting adjustments and vignette exhibit extraction

  • [C] Studying for over 300 hours

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: At Level II, vignettes test multi-step connections across exhibits (e.g., pension adjustments feeding into valuation multiples); superficial flashcard memorization collapses on vignette item sets.
