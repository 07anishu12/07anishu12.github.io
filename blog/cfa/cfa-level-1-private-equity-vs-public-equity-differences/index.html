Introduction

Private Equity vs Public Equity: Key Differences is a core Level I Alternative Investments topic (LM 1 & LM 3: Private Capital vs Public Equity — Governance control, illiquidity premium, capital commitment drawdowns, valuation methods, and public-to-private LBOs) focusing on comparing Private Equity and Public Equity across governance alignment, liquidity, leverage, fee structures, capital call mechanics, and performance measurement (IRR/MOIC vs TWRR). This guide covers the governing equations (Unfunded Capital Commitment & Capital Call Mechanics and Illiquidity Risk Premium & Levered Return Decomposition), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, comparing Private Equity and Public Equity across governance alignment, liquidity, leverage, fee structures, capital call mechanics, and performance measurement (IRR/MOIC vs TWRR) links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Alternative Investments. Unfunded Capital Commitment & Capital Call Mechanics (Unfunded Commitment = Total Committed Capital − Cumulative Paid-In Capital (Called to Date)) establishes the primary relationship (LPs do not wire 100% of capital on Day 1; GPs issue capital calls over a 3–5 year investment period as deals close.), while Illiquidity Risk Premium & Levered Return Decomposition (E(R_Private_Equity) = E(R_Public_Equity) + Illiquidity Premium + Leverage & Operational Control Alpha − GP Fee Drag) governs Compensates LPs for 7–10 year capital lock-ups and unfunded commitment liquidity risk.

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Unfunded Capital Commitment & Capital Call Mechanics

Formula Expression
Unfunded Commitment = Total Committed Capital − Cumulative Paid-In Capital (Called to Date)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:

  • LPs do not wire 100% of capital on Day 1.

  • GPs issue capital calls over a 3–5 year investment period as deals close.

Formula 2: Illiquidity Risk Premium & Levered Return Decomposition

Formula Expression
E(R_Private_Equity) = E(R_Public_Equity) + Illiquidity Premium + Leverage & Operational Control Alpha − GP Fee Drag

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Compensates LPs for 7–10 year capital lock-ups and unfunded commitment liquidity risk.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Tracking LP Capital Calls, Management Fees, and Net Cash Flows Over the J-Curve

Problem & Given Scenario: An endowment commits $50.0M to a Private Equity fund with a 2.0% annual management fee charged on Total Committed Capital during the 4-year investment period. In Year 1, the GP calls 25% of committed capital ($12.5M) for acquisitions plus the Year 1 management fee ($1.0M), and portfolio NAV at year-end is $12.2M.

Formula Expression
Unfunded Commitment = Total Committed Capital − Cumulative Paid-In Capital (Called to Date)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: Total Year 1 LP Cash Outflow (Paid-In Capital) = $12.5M + $1.0M = $13.5M. Step 2: Remaining Unfunded Commitment = $50.0M − $13.5M = $36.5M. Step 3: Year 1 Net Return = ($12.2M − $13.5M) / $13.5M = −9.63% (the initial dip of the 'J-Curve' caused by charging 2% fees on the full $50M commitment while only $12.5M is deployed!).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Charging management fees on Total Committed Capital ($50M) while only a fraction ($12.5M) is invested creates negative net returns in Years 1–3, known as the Private Equity J-Curve.

Worked Example 2: Governance and Strategic Time Horizon: Private Equity GP vs. Public Corporation Board

Problem & Given Scenario: Compare how a Private Equity GP owner manages a turnaround vs. a dispersed public company board.

Formula Expression
E(R_Private_Equity) = E(R_Public_Equity) + Illiquidity Premium + Leverage & Operational Control Alpha − GP Fee Drag

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

In Private Equity: The GP holds 80%–100% voting control, sits directly on the board, ties management equity compensation (10%–15% sweet equity option pool) directly to the 5-year exit MOIC/IRR, and can execute multi-year restructuring away from quarterly earnings guidance pressure. In Public Equity: Dispersed shareholders face free-rider monitoring problems, quarterly EPS scrutiny, and strict SEC/exchange disclosure costs.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Active hands-on governance, operational intervention, and aligned management equity ownership are primary sources of private equity value creation.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: Why is the Money-Weighted Rate of Return (Internal Rate of Return, IRR) used as the primary performance metric for Private Equity funds, whereas the Time-Weighted Rate of Return (TWRR) is used for public equity mutual funds?

  • [A] Because IRR is easier to compute than TWRR

  • [B] Because the Private Equity General Partner (GP)—not the client—controls the exact timing of capital calls (inflows) and exit distributions (outflows)

  • [C] Because GIPS prohibits TWRR for all asset classes

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: In private equity, deciding when to call capital from LPs and when to distribute exit proceeds is an integral part of the GP's investment skill, making IRR the appropriate metric.

Practice Question 2

Question: What causes the 'J-Curve' effect observed in the early years (Years 1–3) of a newly launched Private Equity fund?

  • [A] Immediate realization of large capital gains in Year 1

  • [B] Management fees and organizational/deal expenses charged on total committed capital while investments are still held near cost and have not yet matured into exits

  • [C] Declining interest rates

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: In Years 1–3, management fees (charged on full committed capital) and transaction costs drag net returns below zero before operational improvements and exits drive steep positive returns in Years 4–8.

Practice Question 3

Question: Compared to public equity shareholders, Private Equity General Partners reduce principal-agent conflicts with portfolio company executives primarily by:

  • [A] Eliminating executive performance bonuses

  • [B] Holding concentrated majority board control, requiring executives to co-invest personal wealth alongside the fund, and tying management payouts directly to exit value creation

  • [C] Publishing quarterly earnings guidance

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Concentrated ownership, direct board control, and high-powered equity incentives align portfolio company management directly with LP/GP exit value.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Unfunded Capital Commitment & Capital Call Mechanics.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Illiquidity Risk Premium & Levered Return Decomposition.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
