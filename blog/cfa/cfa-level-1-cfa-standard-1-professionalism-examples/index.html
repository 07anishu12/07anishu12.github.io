Introduction

CFA Standard I Professionalism: Examples and Practice Questions covers Standard I(A) dissociation steps, Standard I(B) gifts (client vs issuer/vendor) and issuer-paid research, Standard I(C) plagiarism and performance guarantees, and Standard I(D) professional misconduct in Level I Ethics and Professional Standards (LM 2 & LM 3: Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law, Standard I(B) Independence and Objectivity, Standard I(C) Misrepresentation, Standard I(D) Misconduct), with verified formulas, two worked examples, and three practice MCQs.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Standard I(A) Dissociation Sequence from Illegal/Unethical Activity (Step 1: Report to Supervisor/Compliance -> Step 2: If unaddressed, Dissociate (remove name, step off account) -> Step 3: In extreme cases, Resign (Whistleblowing is permitted, NOT required unless law mandates)) and Standard I(B) Gift & Entertainment Distinction: Clients vs. Third-Party Issuers/Brokers (Gifts from Issuers/Vendors: Modest tokens only (never lavish travel/gifts that compromise objectivity) ; Gifts from Clients: Permitted with Supervisor Notification/Consent (written consent if future performance bonus)) govern Standard I(A) dissociation steps, Standard I(B) gifts (client vs issuer/vendor) and issuer-paid research, Standard I(C) plagiarism and performance guarantees, and Standard I(D) professional misconduct.

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Standard I(A) Dissociation Sequence from Illegal/Unethical Activity

Formula Expression
Step 1: Report to Supervisor/Compliance -> Step 2: If unaddressed, Dissociate (remove name, step off account) -> Step 3: In extreme cases, Resign (Whistleblowing is permitted, NOT required unless law mandates)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Inaction while remaining associated with a known violation is construed as participation in the violation.

Formula 2: Standard I(B) Gift & Entertainment Distinction: Clients vs. Third-Party Issuers/Brokers

Formula Expression
Gifts from Issuers/Vendors: Modest tokens only (never lavish travel/gifts that compromise objectivity)

Gifts from Clients: Permitted with Supervisor Notification/Consent (written consent if future performance bonus)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Clients have already hired the manager, so client gifts are less likely to compromise objectivity toward third parties (provided disclosed to employer to ensure Fair Dealing).

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Evaluating Travel, Lodging, andIssuer-Paid Research Under Standard I(B)

Problem & Given Scenario: Scenario A: A mining company in a remote arctic site with no commercial flights offers to fly visiting equity analysts on its corporate jet and pay for a 5-star resort stay in a major city afterward. Scenario B: A micro-cap biotech firm with no analyst coverage offers to pay an independent research boutique a flat $25,000 fee (or 50,000 stock warrants) to publish an objective research report.

Formula Expression
Step 1: Report to Supervisor/Compliance -> Step 2: If unaddressed, Dissociate (remove name, step off account) -> Step 3: In extreme cases, Resign (Whistleblowing is permitted, NOT required unless law mandates)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Scenario A: The analyst MAY accept the charter flight to the remote mine site ONLY because commercial transport is unavailable, but MUST pay for commercial flights to the nearest hub and MUST refuse the luxury resort stay (firm/analyst pays own hotel). Scenario B: Issuer-paid research is permitted ONLY if compensation is a flat fixed fee predetermined before writing (NEVER stock, warrants, or performance-linked fees!) and the fee arrangement is prominently disclosed.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Standard I(B) strictly prohibits accepting stock or warrants for issuer-paid research, and requires analysts to pay their own commercial travel and lodging whenever available.

Worked Example 2: Applying Standard I(C) Misrepresentation to Plagiarism, Quantitative Models, and Social Media

Problem & Given Scenario: An analyst summarizes a statistical table from the Federal Reserve's public website without citing the Fed, vs. copying a proprietary commentary paragraph from a sell-side broker report without naming the author.

Formula Expression
Gifts from Issuers/Vendors: Modest tokens only (never lavish travel/gifts that compromise objectivity)

Gifts from Clients: Permitted with Supervisor Notification/Consent (written consent if future performance bonus)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Factual statistics published by recognized statistical/government agencies (such as the Federal Reserve, IMF, or Bureau of Labor Statistics) may be used without formal citation (though citing is good practice). However, copying proprietary analysis, projections, or prose from another analyst (outside one's own firm) without explicit attribution is plagiarism and violates Standard I(C).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Internal firm research produced by a team may be used without individual bylines, provided the departed colleague's work is not misrepresented as solely a new analyst's personal creation.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: An equity analyst is invited by a covered luxury hotel chain to attend a two-day investor conference in Paris, with the issuer offering to pay for business-class airfare and a four-night stay at its flagship suite. To comply with Standard I(B) Independence and Objectivity, the analyst should:

  • [A] Accept the trip and disclose it in the footnote of the next research report

  • [B] Attend the conference if valuable for research, but require her employer (or herself) to pay for commercial airfare and standard hotel accommodations

  • [C] Accept the hotel stay but pay for the airfare

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Because commercial airfare and lodging are readily available in Paris, corporate issuers must not pay for the analyst's travel or hotel expenses; the analyst's firm must pay to preserve independence and objectivity.

Practice Question 2

Question: Which of the following actions is a violation of Standard I(D) Misconduct?

  • [A] A portfolio manager is arrested for participating in a non-violent weekend environmental protest

  • [B] A candidate declares personal bankruptcy due to catastrophic family medical bills

  • [C] A wealth manager frequently consumes excessive alcohol during lunch hours, impairing his judgment when executing afternoon client trades, and submits falsified personal expense receipts to his employer

Tip: Correct Answer: Option C.

Explanation: Falsifying expense reports involves dishonesty/fraud, and substance abuse during work hours impairs professional competence—both violating Standard I(D). Honest personal bankruptcy or peaceful civic protest not involving dishonesty does not violate I(D).

Practice Question 3

Question: An investment advisor discovers that her supervisor is intentionally overstating the fair value of illiquid mortgage bonds in monthly client statements. After confronting the supervisor and reporting the matter to the firm's chief compliance officer, the firm refuses to correct the statements. Under Standard I(A), what is the advisor's mandatory obligation?

  • [A] Immediately report the firm to the SEC and media

  • [B] Dissociate from the activity by removing her name from the accounts, refusing to distribute the false statements, and—if complete dissociation is impossible—resigning from the firm

  • [C] Continue distributing the statements while keeping a private memo on file

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Standard I(A) requires dissociation from any known legal or ethical violation; if the employer refuses to remedy the violation and dissociation cannot be achieved internally, the member must resign. (Whistleblowing to regulators is permitted to protect clients/markets, not universally mandatory unless local law requires it).

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Standard I(A) Dissociation Sequence from Illegal/Unethical Activity.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Standard I(B) Gift & Entertainment Distinction: Clients vs. Third-Party Issuers/Brokers.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
