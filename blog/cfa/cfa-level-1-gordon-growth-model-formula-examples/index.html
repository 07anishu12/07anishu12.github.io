Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, Gordon Growth Model (GGM) equations, D_0 vs D_1 distinction, sustainable growth rate (g = b × ROE), implied required return, and Present Value of Growth Opportunities (PVGO) links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Equity Investments. Gordon Growth Model (GGM) & Implied Required Return (V_0 = D_1 / (r − g) = [D_0 × (1 + g)] / (r − g) ; r = (D_1 / P_0) + g) establishes the primary relationship (Requires r > g strictly; rearranges to decompose required return into forward dividend yield (D_1/P_0) plus capital appreciation rate (g).), while Sustainable Growth Rate & Present Value of Growth Opportunities (PVGO) (g = b × ROE = (1 − Payout Ratio) × ROE ; V_0 = (E_1 / r) + PVGO) governs Decomposes stock price into no-growth perpetuity value (E_1 / r) plus the net present value of future reinvested growth projects (PVGO).

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Gordon Growth Model (GGM) & Implied Required Return

Formula Expression
V_0 = D_1 / (r − g) = [D_0 × (1 + g)] / (r − g)

r = (D_1 / P_0) + g

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:

  • Requires r > g strictly.

  • rearranges to decompose required return into forward dividend yield (D_1/P_0) plus capital appreciation rate (g).

Formula 2: Sustainable Growth Rate & Present Value of Growth Opportunities (PVGO)

Formula Expression
g = b × ROE = (1 − Payout Ratio) × ROE

V_0 = (E_1 / r) + PVGO

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Decomposes stock price into no-growth perpetuity value (E_1 / r) plus the net present value of future reinvested growth projects (PVGO).

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Gordon Growth Valuation and Present Value of Growth Opportunities (PVGO)

Problem & Given Scenario: A mature utility expects next-year earnings of E_1 = $5.00 per share, maintains a return on equity of ROE = 14.0%, retains b = 40.0% of earnings (paying out 60.0%), and has a required return of r = 10.6%.

Formula Expression
V_0 = D_1 / (r − g) = [D_0 × (1 + g)] / (r − g)

r = (D_1 / P_0) + g

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: Expected Dividend D_1 = $5.00 × (1 − 0.40) = $3.00. Step 2: Sustainable growth rate g = b × ROE = 0.40 × 14.0% = 5.60%. Step 3: Intrinsic Value V_0 = D_1 / (r − g) = $3.00 / (0.106 − 0.056) = $3.00 / 0.050 = $60.00. Step 4: No-growth value per share = E_1 / r = $5.00 / 0.106 = $47.17. Step 5: PVGO = V_0 − (E_1 / r) = $60.00 − $47.17 = +$12.83 (21.38% of share value).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Because ROE (14.0%) > required return r (10.6%), retaining 40% of earnings creates positive PVGO (+$12.83/share).

Worked Example 2: Demonstrating Negative PVGO When ROE < Required Return r

Problem & Given Scenario: Suppose a firm has E_1 = $4.00, r = 10.0%, and retains b = 50% of earnings (D_1 = $2.00), but its ROE on reinvested capital is only 6.0% (< 10.0% r).

Formula Expression
g = b × ROE = (1 − Payout Ratio) × ROE

V_0 = (E_1 / r) + PVGO

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Growth rate g = 0.50 × 6.0% = 3.0%. Gordon Growth Price V_0 = $2.00 / (0.10 − 0.03) = $28.57. No-Growth Value = E_1 / r = $4.00 / 0.10 = $40.00. PVGO = $28.57 − $40.00 = −$11.43!

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Even though earnings and dividends grow at +3.0% per year, reinvesting capital at 6% when shareholders require 10% destroys $11.43 per share in value (negative PVGO).

Comparison & Reference Analysis

Value-Neutral Growth ROE = r PVGO = 0 (V_0 = E_1 / r) Dividend policy is irrelevant to V_0

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A company just paid an annual dividend of $2.50 per share (D_0 = $2.50). If its return on equity is 15.0%, its dividend payout ratio is 60.0%, and its required return on equity is 11.0%, its intrinsic value per share under the Gordon Growth Model is closest to:

  • [A] $50.00

  • [B] $53.00

  • [C] $55.25

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Retention ratio b = 1 − 0.60 = 0.40. Sustainable growth rate g = 0.40 × 15.0% = 6.0%. Next year's dividend D_1 = D_0 × (1 + g) = $2.50 × 1.06 = $2.65. Intrinsic value V_0 = $2.65 / (0.11 − 0.06) = $2.65 / 0.05 = $53.00. (Option A forgets to grow D_0 into D_1).

Practice Question 2

Question: A stock trades at $50.00, expects next-year earnings of E_1 = $4.00, and has a required return on equity of 10.0%. The proportion of the stock's price attributable to the Present Value of Growth Opportunities (PVGO / P_0) is:

  • [A] 10.0%

  • [B] 20.0%

  • [C] 25.0%

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: No-growth value = E_1 / r = $4.00 / 0.10 = $40.00. PVGO = P_0 − (E_1 / r) = $50.00 − $40.00 = $10.00. Ratio PVGO / P_0 = $10.00 / $50.00 = 20.0%.

Practice Question 3

Question: Why does the Gordon Growth Model produce a meaningless negative or infinite result when the dividend growth rate g equals or exceeds the required return r?

  • [A] Because taxes are ignored

  • [B] Because the infinite geometric series of discounted dividends diverges to infinity when (1 + g) / (1 + r) ≥ 1, and no company can grow faster than the economy and its discount rate forever

  • [C] Because beta becomes negative

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: The sum of an infinite geometric series converges only when g < r; any firm with g ≥ r is in a temporary high-growth phase and must be valued with a multi-stage model.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Gordon Growth Model (GGM) & Implied Required Return.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Sustainable Growth Rate & Present Value of Growth Opportunities (PVGO).

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
