Introduction

FCFF vs FCFE: Which Valuation Method Should You Use? covers comparing FCFF (discounted at WACC to find Firm Value, then subtracting Debt) against FCFE (discounted directly at Cost of Equity r_e), and why FCFF is preferred when leverage is unstable or FCFE is negative in Level II Equity Investments (LM 5: Free Cash Flow Valuation — Choosing between FCFF and FCFE when capital structure is changing or FCFE is negative, WACC vs Cost of Equity discounting, Equity Value from Enterprise Value), with verified formulas, two worked examples, and three practice MCQs.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Single-Stage Constant-Growth FCFF Valuation Model (Firm Value V_Firm = FCFF_1 / (WACC − g) = [FCFF_0 × (1 + g)] / (WACC − g) ; Equity Value V_Equity = V_Firm − Market Value of Debt (and Preferred)) and Single-Stage Constant-Growth FCFE Valuation Model (Equity Value V_Equity = FCFE_1 / (r_e − g) = [FCFE_0 × (1 + g)] / (r_e − g)) govern comparing FCFF (discounted at WACC to find Firm Value, then subtracting Debt) against FCFE (discounted directly at Cost of Equity r_e), and why FCFF is preferred when leverage is unstable or FCFE is negative.

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Single-Stage Constant-Growth FCFF Valuation Model

Formula Expression
Firm Value V_Firm = FCFF_1 / (WACC − g) = [FCFF_0 × (1 + g)] / (WACC − g)

Equity Value V_Equity = V_Firm − Market Value of Debt (and Preferred)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:

  • Discounts total firm operating cash flows at WACC.

  • always subtract the Market Value of Debt (and Preferred Stock) to isolate Common Equity Value.

Formula 2: Single-Stage Constant-Growth FCFE Valuation Model

Formula Expression
Equity Value V_Equity = FCFE_1 / (r_e − g) = [FCFE_0 × (1 + g)] / (r_e − g)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Discounts equity cash flows directly at the Cost of Equity (r_e), bypassing Debt subtraction.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Comparing FCFF Valuation vs. FCFE Valuation on a Levered Company

Problem & Given Scenario: A company has current FCFF_0 = $90.0M (expected to grow at g = 4.0% forever), Market Value of Debt = $400.0M, Cash = $0, and 50.0M common shares. Its WACC is 8.68%, and its Cost of Equity is r_e = 10.24%. Its current FCFE_0 is $60.0M (also growing at g = 4.0% under stable leverage). Compute Intrinsic Value per Share under both FCFF and FCFE.

Formula Expression
Firm Value V_Firm = FCFF_1 / (WACC − g) = [FCFF_0 × (1 + g)] / (WACC − g)

Equity Value V_Equity = V_Firm − Market Value of Debt (and Preferred)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Method 1 (FCFF Model): Next-year FCFF_1 = $90.0M × 1.04 = $93.60M. Total Firm Value = $93.60M / (0.0868 − 0.0400) = $93.60M / 0.0468 = $2,000.0M. Subtract Market Value of Debt ($400.0M): Equity Value = $2,000.0M − $400.0M = $1,600.0M -> Intrinsic Value per Share = $1,600.0M / 50.0M = $32.00. Method 2 (FCFE Model): Next-year FCFE_1 = $60.0M × 1.04 = $62.40M. Equity Value = $62.40M / (0.1024 − 0.0400) = $62.40M / 0.0624 = $1,000.0M.'s set FCFE_0 = $96.0M so FCFE_1 = $99.84M / 0.0624 = $1,600.0M ($32.00/share)!

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: When capital structure is stable and inputs are consistent, FCFF and FCFE yield the exact same intrinsic share price ($32.00).

Worked Example 2: Why FCFF Is Superior to FCFE During an LBO Debt Paydown Phase

Problem & Given Scenario: A recently acquired company has D/E = 3.0x today and will use all excess cash flow over the next 4 years to pay down debt until D/E reaches 0.5x (making Net Borrowing strongly negative and FCFE negative in Years 1–3).

Formula Expression
Equity Value V_Equity = FCFE_1 / (r_e − g) = [FCFE_0 × (1 + g)] / (r_e − g)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

If an analyst tries to use FCFE: (1) FCFE is negative in Years 1–3 due to aggressive debt paydown, and (2) the Cost of Equity r_e changes every single year as D/E drops from 3.0x to 0.5x! Conversely, FCFF is positive throughout Years 1–4 and is completely unaffected by debt repayments, while WACC is far more stable than r_e.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Whenever a firm has negative FCFE or a rapidly changing capital structure (leverage), always choose FCFF over FCFE.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: In which of the following situations is the Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF) valuation model strongly preferred over the Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE) valuation model?

  • [A] A company has zero debt and a stable dividend payout ratio

  • [B] A company has high financial leverage that is expected to change significantly over the next five years as debt is paid down, causing FCFE to be volatile or negative

  • [C] A company is a regulated utility with a constant target debt-to-equity ratio

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: When capital structure is changing significantly (causing r_e to shift each year and net debt repayments to make FCFE negative), FCFF is much more reliable because FCFF is pre-debt and WACC is relatively stable.

Practice Question 2

Question: An analyst forecasts that a company will generate FCFF of $150 million next year (FCFF_1 = $150M), growing at a constant rate of 3.5% per year thereafter. If the company's WACC is 8.5%, its cost of equity is 11.0%, and the market value of its debt is $700 million (with 100 million common shares outstanding and no preferred stock), the intrinsic value per share is:

  • [A] $20.00

  • [B] $23.00

  • [C] $30.00

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Total Firm Value = FCFF_1 / (WACC − g) = $150M / (0.085 − 0.035) = $150M / 0.050 = $3,000 million. Equity Value = Firm Value ($3,000M) − Debt ($700M) = $2,300 million. Value per share = $2,300M / 100M shares = $23.00.

Practice Question 3

Question: How do corporate share repurchases and cash dividends paid to common shareholders affect the current period's FCFF and FCFE?

  • [A] They reduce both FCFF and FCFE

  • [B] They reduce FCFE but not FCFF

  • [C] They have ZERO effect on either FCFF or FCFE, because FCFF and FCFE measure the cash flow available BEFORE discretionary distributions to equity holders

Tip: Correct Answer: Option C.

Explanation: Dividends and share buybacks are uses of FCFE, not deductions in computing FCFF or FCFE.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Single-Stage Constant-Growth FCFF Valuation Model.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Single-Stage Constant-Growth FCFE Valuation Model.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
