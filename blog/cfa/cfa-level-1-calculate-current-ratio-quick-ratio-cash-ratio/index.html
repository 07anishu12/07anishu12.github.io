Introduction

How to Calculate Current Ratio, Quick Ratio and Cash Ratio is a core Level I Financial Statement Analysis topic (LM 10: Financial Analysis Techniques — Liquidity ratios (current, quick, cash, defensive interval) and working capital management) focusing on tiered short-term liquidity ratios (current, quick/acid-test, cash, defensive interval) and balance sheet transaction sensitivity. This guide covers the governing equations (Current, Quick (Acid-Test), and Cash Ratios and Defensive Interval Ratio), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, tiered short-term liquidity ratios (current, quick/acid-test, cash, defensive interval) and balance sheet transaction sensitivity links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Financial Statement Analysis. Current, Quick (Acid-Test), and Cash Ratios (Current = CA / CL ; Quick = (Cash + Marketable Sec + AR) / CL ; Cash = (Cash + Marketable Sec) / CL) establishes the primary relationship (Progressively strips illiquid inventory and prepaid expenses from the numerator to test immediate debt-service capacity.), while Defensive Interval Ratio (Defensive Interval = (Cash + Marketable Securities + Accounts Receivable) / Daily Cash Operating Expenditures) governs Measures how many days the firm can fund daily cash operating expenses purely from existing quick liquid assets.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Tiered Liquidity Ratio Analysis of a Consumer Goods Company

Problem & Given Scenario: Balance sheet items ($ millions): Cash = $45, Marketable Securities = $35, Accounts Receivable = $100, Inventory = $140, Prepaid Expenses = $20, Accounts Payable = $90, Short-Term Debt = $70, Accrued Liabilities = $40. Daily cash operating expenses = $4.0M/day.

Formula Expression
Current = CA / CL

Quick = (Cash + Marketable Sec + AR) / CL

Cash = (Cash + Marketable Sec) / CL

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Total Current Assets (CA) = 45 + 35 + 100 + 140 + 20 = $340M. Total Current Liabilities (CL) = 90 + 70 + 40 = $200M. Current Ratio = $340M / $200M = 1.70x. Quick Assets = 45 + 35 + 100 = $180M -> Quick Ratio = $180M / $200M = 0.90x. Cash Ratio = (45 + 35) / $200M = $80M / $200M = 0.40x. Defensive Interval = $180M / $4.0M = 45.0 days.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Although the Current Ratio looks comfortable at 1.70x, stripping out $140M of inventory and $20M of prepaids drops the Quick Ratio below 1.0x (to 0.90x).

Worked Example 2: Effect of Paying Down Accounts Payable with Cash on Liquidity Ratios

Problem & Given Scenario: Suppose the company uses $40M of Cash to pay off $40M of Accounts Payable. Recompute the Current Ratio and Quick Ratio.

Formula Expression
Defensive Interval = (Cash + Marketable Securities + Accounts Receivable) / Daily Cash Operating Expenditures

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

New CA = $340M − $40M = $300M; New Quick Assets = $180M − $40M = $140M; New CL = $200M − $40M = $160M. New Current Ratio = $300M / $160M = 1.875x ≈ 1.88x (increased from 1.70x!). New Quick Ratio = $140M / $160M = 0.875x ≈ 0.88x (decreased from 0.90x!).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Paying off current liabilities with cash increases any ratio that started above 1.0x (Current Ratio: 1.70x -> 1.88x) but decreases any ratio that started below 1.0x (Quick Ratio: 0.90x -> 0.88x).

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A company has Cash of $30M, Marketable Securities of $20M, Accounts Receivable of $60M, Inventory of $90M, Prepaid Expenses of $10M, and Total Current Liabilities of $100M. Its Quick Ratio is:

  • [A] 0.50x

  • [B] 1.10x

  • [C] 1.20x

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Quick Ratio includes only Cash ($30M) + Marketable Securities ($20M) + Accounts Receivable ($60M) = $110M, divided by Current Liabilities ($100M) = 1.10x. Inventory and Prepaids are excluded.

Practice Question 2

Question: If a company has a Current Ratio of 1.60x and a Quick Ratio of 0.85x, purchasing $15 million of inventory on 60-day trade credit (Accounts Payable) will:

  • [A] Decrease the Current Ratio and decrease the Quick Ratio

  • [B] Keep the Current Ratio unchanged and decrease the Quick Ratio

  • [C] Increase the Current Ratio and decrease the Quick Ratio

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Adding equal amounts (+$15M) to both CA and CL pushes a Current Ratio above 1.0x downward toward 1.0x. For the Quick Ratio, Quick Assets are unchanged while CL increases by $15M, decreasing the Quick Ratio as well.

Practice Question 3

Question: A firm holds $15M in Cash, $10M in Marketable Securities, and $50M in Accounts Receivable. Its annual COGS is $210M, SG&A is $85M (which includes $20M of non-cash depreciation), and uses a 365-day year. Its defensive interval ratio is closest to:

  • [A] 99.5 days

  • [B] 105.4 days

  • [C] 112.0 days

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Annual cash operating expenditures = COGS ($210M) + SG&A ($85M) − Depreciation ($20M) = $275M. Daily cash expenditures = $275M / 365 = $0.753425M/day. Defensive Interval = ($15M + $10M + $50M) / $0.753425M = $75M / $0.753425M = 99.55 days.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Current, Quick (Acid-Test), and Cash Ratios.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Defensive Interval Ratio.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
