Introduction

How to Calculate Market Capitalization and Free Float is a core Level I Equity Investments topic (LM 2 & LM 4: Security Market Indexes & Overview of Equity Securities — Total market capitalization, free-float factor, float-adjusted market cap, treasury shares) focusing on issued vs outstanding vs treasury shares, strategic locked-in holdings, Free-Float Factor, Float-Adjusted Market Capitalization, and index investability. This guide covers the governing equations (Issued, Outstanding, and Free-Float Shares and Total Market Cap vs. Float-Adjusted Market Capitalization), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, issued vs outstanding vs treasury shares, strategic locked-in holdings, Free-Float Factor, Float-Adjusted Market Capitalization, and index investability links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Equity Investments. Issued, Outstanding, and Free-Float Shares (Outstanding Shares = Issued Shares − Treasury Shares ; Free-Float Shares = Outstanding Shares − Restricted/Strategic Holdings) establishes the primary relationship (Excludes corporate treasury stock, founder/family control blocks, government holdings, and corporate cross-holdings unavailable for public trading.), while Total Market Cap vs. Float-Adjusted Market Capitalization (Total Market Cap = Outstanding Shares × Market Price ; Float-Adjusted Market Cap = Free-Float Shares × Market Price) governs Major index providers (S&P, MSCI, FTSE) weight constituents by Float-Adjusted Market Cap to reflect true tradable supply.

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Issued, Outstanding, and Free-Float Shares

Formula Expression
Outstanding Shares = Issued Shares − Treasury Shares

Free-Float Shares = Outstanding Shares − Restricted/Strategic Holdings

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Excludes corporate treasury stock, founder/family control blocks, government holdings, and corporate cross-holdings unavailable for public trading.

Formula 2: Total Market Cap vs. Float-Adjusted Market Capitalization

Formula Expression
Total Market Cap = Outstanding Shares × Market Price

Float-Adjusted Market Cap = Free-Float Shares × Market Price

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Major index providers (S&P, MSCI, FTSE) weight constituents by Float-Adjusted Market Cap to reflect true tradable supply.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Step-by-Step Calculation of Outstanding Shares, Free-Float Factor, and Float-Adjusted Market Cap

Problem & Given Scenario: A corporation has 500M authorized shares and 320M issued shares, of which 20M are held in corporate treasury. Of the remaining shares, the founding family holds 75M shares, the national government holds 30M shares, a strategic corporate partner holds 15M locked-in shares, and public institutional/retail investors hold the rest. The stock trades at $40.00.

Formula Expression
Outstanding Shares = Issued Shares − Treasury Shares

Free-Float Shares = Outstanding Shares − Restricted/Strategic Holdings

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: Outstanding Shares = 320M − 20M = 300M shares (Total Market Cap = 300M × $40 = $12,000M). Step 2: Strategic/Restricted Holdings = 75M + 30M + 15M = 120M shares. Step 3: Free-Float Shares = 300M − 120M = 180M shares. Step 4: Free-Float Factor = 180M / 300M = 60.0%. Step 5: Float-Adjusted Market Cap = 180M × $40.00 = $7,200M (or 60% × $12,000M).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Although Total Market Cap is $12.0 billion, index funds tracking a float-adjusted benchmark weight the company at $7.2 billion (60% free float).

Worked Example 2: Impact of Open-Market Share Repurchases on Market Cap and Free Float

Problem & Given Scenario: Suppose the company uses $400M of excess cash to repurchase 10M shares in the open market at $40.00 and holds them as Treasury Stock (assuming share price stays $40.00).

Formula Expression
Total Market Cap = Outstanding Shares × Market Price

Float-Adjusted Market Cap = Free-Float Shares × Market Price

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

New Outstanding Shares = 300M − 10M = 290M (Total Market Cap = $11,600M). New Free-Float Shares = 180M − 10M = 170M (Float-Adjusted Market Cap = $6,800M). Free-Float Factor = 170M / 290M = 58.62%.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Repurchasing public shares into treasury reduces both Outstanding Shares and Free-Float Shares one-for-one.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A company has 200 million shares issued, of which 15 million are held as treasury stock. Insiders and the government hold 55 million shares, and the remaining shares are held by the investing public. If the share price is $25.00, the company's float-adjusted market capitalization is:

  • [A] $3,250 million

  • [B] $3,625 million

  • [C] $4,625 million

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Outstanding shares = 200M − 15M = 185M. Free-float shares = 185M − 55M = 130M. Float-adjusted market cap = 130M × $25.00 = $3,250 million. (Option C is total market cap 185M × $25 = $4,625M).

Practice Question 2

Question: Why do global equity index providers (such as MSCI and S&P Dow Jones) use float-adjusted market capitalization rather than total market capitalization to weight index constituents?

  • [A] To increase the weight of state-owned enterprises

  • [B] To prevent index funds from being forced to buy illiquid shares locked up by controlling insiders, governments, or cross-holdings

  • [C] To match price-weighted indexes

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Weighting by free float ensures that passive index trackers only allocate capital in proportion to shares that are actually available for trading in the public market.

Practice Question 3

Question: Which of the following rights is retained by shares held in a corporation's own Treasury Stock account?

  • [A] Voting rights at the annual general meeting

  • [B] Right to receive cash dividends

  • [C] Neither voting rights nor dividend rights

Tip: Correct Answer: Option C.

Explanation: Treasury shares have been repurchased by the issuer and carry no voting rights, no dividend rights, and no liquidation claim.
