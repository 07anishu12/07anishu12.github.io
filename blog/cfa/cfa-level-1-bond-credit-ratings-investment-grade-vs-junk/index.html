Introduction

Bond Credit Ratings Explained: Investment Grade vs Junk Bonds is a core Level I Fixed Income topic (LM 14 & LM 15: Credit Risk, Credit Analysis for Government and Corporate Issuers — Expected Loss (PD × LGD), rating scales (Moody's/S&P/Fitch), seniority and structural subordination, 4 Cs of credit) focusing on credit rating cutoffs (BBB−/Baa3), Probability of Default (PD), Loss Given Default (LGD), Expected Loss, seniority waterfalls, and structural subordination. This guide covers the governing equations (Expected Loss & Credit Spread Approximation and Corporate Credit Metrics (Leverage & FFO Coverage)), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, credit rating cutoffs (BBB−/Baa3), Probability of Default (PD), Loss Given Default (LGD), Expected Loss, seniority waterfalls, and structural subordination links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Fixed Income. Expected Loss & Credit Spread Approximation (Expected Loss (EL) = PD × LGD = PD × (1 − Recovery Rate) ; Credit Spread ≈ PD × (1 − RR) + Liquidity Premium) establishes the primary relationship (Links default probability (PD) and loss severity (LGD = 1 − RR) to the yield spread demanded over risk-free benchmarks.), while Corporate Credit Metrics (Leverage & FFO Coverage) (Debt / EBITDA = Total Debt / EBITDA ; FFO / Total Debt = (Net Income + D&A + Deferred Taxes) / Total Debt) governs Primary quantitative ratios used by S&P, Moody's, and Fitch to assign corporate credit ratings.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Calculating Expected Credit Loss, Fair Credit Spread, and Downgrade Price Impact

Problem & Given Scenario: A 7-year Baa2/BBB corporate bond has an annual Probability of Default (PD) = 0.80%, Recovery Rate (RR) = 40.0%, and Modified Duration = 5.60. If a rating downgrade to Ba1/BB+ (fallen angel) causes its credit spread to widen by 135 bps (+0.0135), calculate (1) initial Expected Loss %, and (2) the immediate % price drop from the spread widening.

Formula Expression
Expected Loss (EL) = PD × LGD = PD × (1 − Recovery Rate)

Credit Spread ≈ PD × (1 − RR) + Liquidity Premium

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: Loss Given Default (LGD) = 1 − 0.40 = 60.0%. Annual Expected Loss = PD × LGD = 0.80% × 60.0% = 0.48% (48 bps per year). Step 2: Immediate price impact of +135 bps spread widening: %ΔPrice ≈ −ModDur × ΔSpread = −5.60 × (+0.0135) = −7.56%.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Crossing the BBB−/Baa3 threshold into high-yield ('fallen angel') triggers forced selling by investment-grade-only mandates, widening spreads by 135 bps and dropping bond value by 7.56%.

Worked Example 2: Seniority Priority and Pari Passu Bankruptcy Recovery Waterfall

Problem & Given Scenario: A defaulted issuer has $180M of liquidation value. Its liabilities are: Senior Secured Debt = $100M (backed by $120M collateral), Senior Unsecured Bonds = $120M, and Subordinated Debentures = $80M.

Formula Expression
Debt / EBITDA = Total Debt / EBITDA

FFO / Total Debt = (Net Income + D&A + Deferred Taxes) / Total Debt

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: Senior Secured creditors recover 100% ($100M) from their $120M collateral, leaving $80M of remaining enterprise value. Step 2: Under the absolute priority rule, Senior Unsecured creditors ($120M claim) receive the entire remaining $80M (Recovery Rate = $80M / $120M = 66.67%), while Subordinated Debentures receive $0 (0% recovery).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Within the same seniority class, bonds rank pari passu (equal recovery percentage regardless of remaining maturity).

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: What is the lowest credit rating still classified as Investment Grade by S&P/Fitch and Moody's, respectively?

  • [A] BB+ and Ba1

  • [B] BBB− and Baa3

  • [C] BBB and Baa2

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: BBB− (S&P/Fitch) and Baa3 (Moody's) mark the lowest rung of Investment Grade; one notch lower (BB+ / Ba1) enters High-Yield (junk) territory.

Practice Question 2

Question: Why do credit rating agencies often notch the unsecured debt of a parent holding company lower than the unsecured debt of its operating subsidiary?

  • [A] Structural subordination, because the operating subsidiary's creditors have first claim on operating assets and cash flows before upstream dividends can reach the parent

  • [B] Because parent holding companies cannot issue covenants

  • [C] Because holding company bonds have longer duration

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Structural subordination arises because operating cash flows and assets reside at the subsidiary level and must satisfy subsidiary creditors first before any equity distributions reach the parent.

Practice Question 3

Question: A corporate bond has an estimated annual probability of default of 1.50% and an expected recovery rate of 36.0% of par value. Its annual expected credit loss is closest to:

  • [A] 0.54%

  • [B] 0.96%

  • [C] 1.50%

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Loss Given Default (LGD) = 1 − Recovery Rate = 1 − 0.36 = 0.64 (64%). Expected Loss = PD × LGD = 1.50% × 0.64 = 0.96% (96 bps).

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Expected Loss & Credit Spread Approximation.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Corporate Credit Metrics (Leverage & FFO Coverage).

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
