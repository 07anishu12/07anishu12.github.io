Introduction

This guide covers the governing equations (Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF) from NI and CFO and Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE)), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, direct vs indirect Operating Cash Flow (CFO), Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF), Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE), and IFRS interest adjustment links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Financial Statement Analysis. Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF) from NI and CFO (FCFF = NI + NCC + Int × (1 − t) − FCInv − WCInv = CFO + Int × (1 − t) − FCInv) establishes the primary relationship (Measures discretionary cash flow available to all capital providers (bondholders and shareholders) after CapEx and working capital reinvestment.), while Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE) (FCFE = CFO − FCInv + Net Borrowing = FCFF − Int × (1 − t) + Net Borrowing) governs Measures discretionary cash flow available for common dividends and share repurchases after servicing debt.

Formula 1: Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF) from NI and CFO

Formula Expression
FCFF = NI + NCC + Int × (1 − t) − FCInv − WCInv = CFO + Int × (1 − t) − FCInv

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Measures discretionary cash flow available to all capital providers (bondholders and shareholders) after CapEx and working capital reinvestment.

Formula 2: Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE)

Formula Expression
FCFE = CFO − FCInv + Net Borrowing = FCFF − Int × (1 − t) + Net Borrowing

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Measures discretionary cash flow available for common dividends and share repurchases after servicing debt.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Calculating CFO, FCFF, and FCFE from Financial Statements (US GAAP)

Problem & Given Scenario: Net Income = $140M, Depreciation (NCC) = $45M, Interest Expense = $20M, Tax Rate = 25%. Working capital changes: ΔAR = +$12M, ΔInventory = +$8M, ΔAP = +$5M. Capital Expenditures (FCInv) = $65M (no asset sales). New Debt Issued = $30M, Debt Repaid = $10M.

Formula Expression
FCFF = NI + NCC + Int × (1 − t) − FCInv − WCInv = CFO + Int × (1 − t) − FCInv

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: WCInv = ΔAR + ΔInv − ΔAP = 12 + 8 − 5 = +$15M. Step 2: CFO = NI + NCC − WCInv = $140M + $45M − $15M = $170M. Step 3: FCFF = CFO + Int×(1 − t) − FCInv = $170M + $20M(0.75) − $65M = $170M + $15M − $65M = $120M. Step 4: Net Borrowing = $30M − $10M = +$20M. FCFE = CFO − FCInv + Net Borrowing = $170M − $65M + $20M = $125M (verified as FCFF $120M − After-tax Interest $15M + Net Borrowing $20M = $125M).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: FCFF ($120M) adds back the after-tax interest ($15M) deducted inside US GAAP CFO, whereas FCFE ($125M) reflects the +$20M net debt inflow.

Worked Example 2: Adjusting FCFF When an IFRS Company Classifies Interest Paid in Financing (CFF)

Problem & Given Scenario: Suppose an identical company reporting under IFRS places its $20M Interest Paid inside Financing Cash Flow (CFF), so its reported IFRS CFO is $190M ($20M higher than US GAAP CFO of $170M). Compute FCFF.

Formula Expression
FCFE = CFO − FCInv + Net Borrowing = FCFF − Int × (1 − t) + Net Borrowing

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

When Interest Paid is already excluded from CFO (placed in CFF), do NOT add back Int×(1−t) to CFO; instead, note that the tax shield ($20M × 0.25 = $5M) remains inside CFO taxes paid, or compute directly from NI: FCFF = NI ($140M) + NCC ($45M) + Int(1−t) ($15M) − FCInv ($65M) − WCInv ($15M) = $120M.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Regardless of whether a firm uses IFRS or US GAAP cash flow classification, economic FCFF is strictly invariant ($120M).

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A US GAAP company reports Operating Cash Flow (CFO) of $250 million, Interest Expense of $40 million, a 25% tax rate, Capital Expenditures of $90 million, and Net Debt Repayment of $15 million. Its Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF) is closest to:

  • [A] $160 million

  • [B] $190 million

  • [C] $200 million

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: FCFF = CFO + Interest × (1 − Tax Rate) − FCInv = $250M + $40M × (1 − 0.25) − $90M = $250M + $30M − $90M = $190 million.

Practice Question 2

Question: Using the same data (CFO = $250M, CapEx = $90M, Net Debt Repayment = $15M), the company's Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE) is closest to:

  • [A] $145 million

  • [B] $160 million

  • [C] $175 million

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: FCFE = CFO − FCInv + Net Borrowing = $250M − $90M + (−$15M) = $145 million.

Practice Question 3

Question: When converting Operating Cash Flow (CFO) from the indirect method to the direct method, which of the following is NOT a line item reported on a direct-method CFO statement?

  • [A] Cash collected from customers

  • [B] Depreciation and amortization expense

  • [C] Cash paid to suppliers and employees

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Depreciation and amortization are non-cash expenses; they appear only on an indirect-method reconciliation and never on a direct-method cash flow statement.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF) from NI and CFO.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE).

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
