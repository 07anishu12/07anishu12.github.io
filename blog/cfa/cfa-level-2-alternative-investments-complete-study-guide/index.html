Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: REIT Net Asset Value Per Share (NAVPS)

Formula Expression
NAVPS = [ (Next-12-Month NOI / Market Cap Rate) + Cash & AR + Land/CIP − Total Debt − Preferred Stock ] / Diluted Shares

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Values a REIT's existing real estate portfolio using private-market Cap Rates, adds non-rental assets, deducts debt, and divides by shares to test whether the REIT trades at a premium or discount to NAV.

Formula 2: Relationship Between Cap Rate, Discount Rate (r), and Growth Rate (g)

Formula Expression
Cap Rate (R_0) = Discount Rate (r) − Constant NOI Growth Rate (g) => Property Value V_0 = NOI_1 / (r − g) = NOI_1 / Cap Rate

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: The Going-In Cap Rate equals the required total return (r) minus the expected perpetual growth rate of NOI (g). Terminal Cap Rate R_N is typically higher than Going-In Cap Rate R_0 because the building is older and future growth g_N is lower!.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Calculating REIT Net Asset Value Per Share (NAVPS) and Premium/Discount to NAV

Problem & Given Scenario: A Healthcare REIT reports trailing 12-month NOI of $200.0M, which is expected to grow by 4.0% over the next 12 months. Private-market appraisals indicate a market Cap Rate of 6.50% for comparable healthcare properties. The REIT also holds $40.0M in Cash & Equivalents, $80.0M in Construction-in-Progress (at book value), $1,120.0M in Total Mortgage Debt, and has 50.0M common shares trading at $41.00 per share.

Formula Expression
NAVPS = [ (Next-12-Month NOI / Market Cap Rate) + Cash & AR + Land/CIP − Total Debt − Preferred Stock ] / Diluted Shares

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1 (Forward Next-12-Month NOI): NOI_1 = $200.0M × 1.04 = $208.0M (ALWAYS grow trailing NOI to forward NOI_1 before dividing by the Cap Rate!). Step 2 (Estimated Market Value of Operating Real Estate): $208.0M / 0.0650 = $3,200.0M. Step 3 (Total Net Asset Value): $3,200.0M + $40.0M (Cash) + $80.0M (CIP) − $1,120.0M (Debt) = $2,200.0M. Step 4 (NAV per Share): $2,200.0M / 50.0M shares = $44.00 per share. Step 5 (Discount to NAVPS): ($41.00 − $44.00) / $44.00 = −6.82% discount.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: The #1 exam trap in REIT NAVPS vignettes is dividing trailing NOI ($200M) by the cap rate instead of first growing it by (1 + g) into forward 12-month NOI ($208M)!

Worked Example 2: Comparing Going-In Cap Rate vs. Terminal (Going-Out) Cap Rate in Real Estate DCF

Problem & Given Scenario: An office building is acquired at a Going-In Cap Rate of 5.50% (r = 8.0%, initial g = 2.5%). Why does the analyst assume a higher Terminal Cap Rate of 6.25% at Year 5 exit?

Formula Expression
Cap Rate (R_0) = Discount Rate (r) − Constant NOI Growth Rate (g) => Property Value V_0 = NOI_1 / (r − g) = NOI_1 / Cap Rate

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

At Year 5, the building is 5 years older (requiring higher ongoing maintenance CapEx), long-term interest rate uncertainty is higher, and expected perpetual rent growth g_T beyond Year 5 is lower (e.g., 1.75% instead of 2.50%), so Terminal Cap Rate = r − g_T = 8.00% − 1.75% = 6.25% > 5.50%.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Terminal (going-out) cap rates are almost always set 50 to 100 bps higher than going-in cap rates to reflect property aging and slower terminal rent growth.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A Retail REIT generated $150 million of Net Operating Income (NOI) over the past 12 months, and NOI is expected to grow by 3.0% next year. If the market capitalization rate for retail properties is 6.0%, the REIT has $50 million in cash and other tangible assets, $825 million in total debt, and 35 million shares outstanding (and zero non-cash rent), its Net Asset Value per share (NAVPS) is closest to:

  • [A] $49.29

  • [B] $51.43

  • [C] $75.00

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Step 1: Forward NOI_1 = $150M × 1.03 = $154.50M. Step 2: Operating Real Estate Value = $154.50M / 0.060 = $2,575.0M. Step 3: Total NAV = $2,575.0M + $50.0M − $825.0M = $1,800.0M. Step 4: NAVPS = $1,800.0M / 35M shares = $51.43. (Option A forgets to grow NOI by 3%).

Practice Question 2

Question: When calculating Net Asset Value per Share (NAVPS) for a REIT that reports 'straight-line rent' adjustments (non-cash rent) inside its GAAP revenue, how should the analyst treat non-cash rent before capitalizing NOI?

  • [A] Deduct non-cash rent from reported NOI so that only actual cash NOI is capitalized (and then grow cash NOI by 1 + g)

  • [B] Add non-cash rent twice

  • [C] Capitalize Goodwill at the cap rate

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: In the CFA Level II REIT reading, first subtract non-cash (straight-line) rent from trailing NOI to isolate cash NOI, grow cash NOI by (1 + g), and divide by the market Cap Rate (note: Goodwill and deferred financing costs are given $0 value in NAVPS!).

Practice Question 3

Question: Why is Price-to-AFFO (Adjusted Funds From Operations) generally considered superior to Price-to-FFO when comparing an Office REIT (high tenant improvement and leasing commission outlays) against a Self-Storage REIT (low maintenance CapEx)?

  • [A] Because AFFO deducts recurring maintenance capital expenditures, tenant improvements, and leasing commissions as well as straight-line rent adjustments, reflecting true economic free cash flow

  • [B] Because FFO deducts depreciation twice

  • [C] Because AFFO adds back mortgage interest

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: FFO ignores the heavy recurring capital expenditures (roofs, HVAC, tenant improvements, leasing commissions) required to maintain occupancy in office/retail properties; AFFO deducts them.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in REIT Net Asset Value Per Share (NAVPS).

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Relationship Between Cap Rate, Discount Rate (r), and Growth Rate (g).

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
