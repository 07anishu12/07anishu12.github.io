Introduction

Coupon Rate vs Current Yield vs Yield to Maturity: Key Differences is a core Level I Fixed Income topic (LM 5: Yield and Yield Spread Measures for Fixed-Rate Bonds — Current yield, simple yield, YTM, and ordering across discount, par, and premium bonds) focusing on comparing nominal coupon rate, current yield (income return), simple yield, and YTM across discount, par, and premium bonds. This guide covers the governing equations (Current Yield & Simple Yield Formulas and Strict Yield Ordering Inequality), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, comparing nominal coupon rate, current yield (income return), simple yield, and YTM across discount, par, and premium bonds links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Fixed Income. Current Yield & Simple Yield Formulas (Current Yield = Annual Cash Coupon / Flat Price ; Simple Yield = [Annual Coupon + (Par − Flat Price) / T] / Flat Price) establishes the primary relationship (Current yield captures annual coupon income divided by clean price (ignoring pull-to-par); Simple yield adds straight-line amortization of discount/premium.), while Strict Yield Ordering Inequality (Discount Bond: Coupon Rate < Current Yield < YTM ; Premium Bond: Coupon Rate > Current Yield > YTM) governs Relates promised coupon rate, income yield, and total internal rate of return based on whether price is below or above par.

Formula Expression

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Comparing Coupon Rate, Current Yield, Simple Yield, and YTM for a Discount Bond

Problem & Given Scenario: A 6-year, 4.50% semiannual coupon bond ($100 par) trades at a flat price of $92.50.

Formula Expression
Current Yield = Annual Cash Coupon / Flat Price

Simple Yield = [Annual Coupon + (Par − Flat Price) / T] / Flat Price

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

  • Step 1. Coupon Rate = 4.50%.

  • Step 2. Current Yield = $4.50 / $92.50 = 4.8649% ≈ 4.86%.

  • Step 3. Simple Yield = [$4.50 + ($100 − $92.50)/6] / $92.50 = ($4.50 + $1.25) / $92.50 = $5.75 / $92.50 = 6.2162% ≈ 6.22%.

  • Step 4. Exact Semiannual YTM (N = 12, PV = −92.50, PMT = 2.25, FV = 100): periodic = 2.999% -> Annual YTM = 6.00%.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Notice the strict ordering for a discount bond ($92.50 < $100): Coupon Rate (4.50%) < Current Yield (4.86%) < YTM (6.00%).

Worked Example 2: Comparing Yield Metrics for a Premium Bond

Problem & Given Scenario: A 5-year, 7.00% semiannual coupon bond ($100 par) trades at a flat price of $108.40.

Formula Expression
Discount Bond: Coupon Rate < Current Yield < YTM

Premium Bond: Coupon Rate > Current Yield > YTM

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

  • Step 1. Coupon Rate = 7.00%.

  • Step 2. Current Yield = $7.00 / $108.40 = 6.4576% ≈ 6.46%.

  • Step 3. Exact Semiannual YTM (N = 10, PV = −108.40, PMT = 3.50, FV = 100): periodic = 2.543% -> Annual YTM = 5.09%.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: For a premium bond ($108.40 > $100), amortizing the $8.40 capital loss at maturity pulls YTM (5.09%) below Current Yield (6.46%), which in turn sits below the Coupon Rate (7.00%).

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A 10-year corporate bond with a 5.50% annual coupon trades at a flat price of $104.80 (par = $100). Which statement regarding its yield measures is most accurate?

  • [A] Its current yield is 5.25% and its yield to maturity is less than 5.25%

  • [B] Its current yield is 5.25% and its yield to maturity is between 5.25% and 5.50%

  • [C] Its yield to maturity is greater than 5.50%

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Current Yield = $5.50 / $104.80 = 5.248% ≈ 5.25%. Because it is a premium bond ($104.80 > $100), Coupon Rate (5.50%) > Current Yield (5.25%) > YTM (actual YTM = 4.88% < 5.25%).

Practice Question 2

Question: What major component of fixed-income return does the Current Yield ignore that Yield to Maturity incorporates?

  • [A] Annual coupon payments

  • [B] The time value of money and the capital gain or loss as the bond's price converges to par at maturity

  • [C] The clean flat price of the bond

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Current Yield (= Annual Coupon / Flat Price) looks only at current cash income and ignores the pull-to-par capital gain (on discount bonds) or loss (on premium bonds).

Practice Question 3

Question: A 4-year bond with a 3.60% coupon ($100 par) trades at a flat price of $96.00. Its Japanese-style Simple Yield is closest to:

  • [A] 3.75%

  • [B] 4.60%

  • [C] 4.79%

Tip: Correct Answer: Option C.

Explanation: Simple Yield = [Annual Coupon + (Par − Flat Price) / Years] / Flat Price = [$3.60 + ($100 − $96)/4] / $96.00 = ($3.60 + $1.00) / $96.00 = $4.60 / $96.00 = 4.7917% ≈ 4.79%.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Current Yield & Simple Yield Formulas.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Strict Yield Ordering Inequality.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
