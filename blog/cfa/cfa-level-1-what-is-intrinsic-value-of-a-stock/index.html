Introduction

What Is Intrinsic Value of a Stock? How to Calculate It is a core Level I Equity Investments topic (LM 8: Equity Valuation: Concepts and Basic Tools — Intrinsic value vs market price, present value models (DDM/FCFE), asset-based valuation, mispricing alpha) focusing on distinguishing fundamental intrinsic value from market price, selecting among DDM, FCFE, and Asset-Based valuation models, and holding-period expected return decomposition. This guide covers the governing equations (Intrinsic Value vs. Market Price Mispricing Signal and Asset-Based Equity Valuation), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, distinguishing fundamental intrinsic value from market price, selecting among DDM, FCFE, and Asset-Based valuation models, and holding-period expected return decomposition links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Equity Investments. Intrinsic Value vs. Market Price Mispricing Signal (Mispricing = V_0 − P_0 ; If V_0 > P_0 => Undervalued (Buy) ; If V_0 < P_0 => Overvalued (Sell/Short)) establishes the primary relationship (Compares analyst-estimated present value of cash flows (V_0) against observed market price (P_0).), while Asset-Based Equity Valuation (V_Asset_Based = Fair Value of Total Assets − Fair Value of Total Liabilities − Fair Value of Preferred Stock) governs Adjusts book values of tangible assets, receivables, inventory, and debt to current market/liquidation fair values.

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Intrinsic Value vs. Market Price Mispricing Signal

Formula Expression
Mispricing = V_0 − P_0

If V_0 > P_0 => Undervalued (Buy)

If V_0 < P_0 => Overvalued (Sell/Short)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Compares analyst-estimated present value of cash flows (V_0) against observed market price (P_0).

Formula 2: Asset-Based Equity Valuation

Formula Expression
V_Asset_Based = Fair Value of Total Assets − Fair Value of Total Liabilities − Fair Value of Preferred Stock

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Adjusts book values of tangible assets, receivables, inventory, and debt to current market/liquidation fair values.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: One-Year Holding Period Intrinsic Valuation vs. Market Price

Problem & Given Scenario: An analyst forecasts that a stock will pay a year-end dividend of D_1 = $2.40 and trade at a terminal price of P_1 = $66.00 in 1 year. The stock's CAPM beta is 1.25, the risk-free rate is 4.0%, and the equity risk premium is 5.6%. The stock currently trades at P_0 = $58.00.

Formula Expression
Mispricing = V_0 − P_0

If V_0 > P_0 => Undervalued (Buy)

If V_0 < P_0 => Overvalued (Sell/Short)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: Required return k_e = 4.0% + 1.25(5.6%) = 4.0% + 7.0% = 11.0%. Step 2: Intrinsic Value V_0 = ($2.40 + $66.00) / (1 + 0.11) = $68.40 / 1.11 = $61.62. Step 3: Since V_0 ($61.62) > Market Price P_0 ($58.00), the stock is undervalued by $3.62 per share (Expected 1-yr return at $58 purchase = [$68.40 − $58.00]/$58.00 = 17.93% > 11.0% k_e).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Whenever Intrinsic Value V_0 exceeds Market Price P_0, the stock's expected return (17.93%) exceeds its required return (11.0%).

Worked Example 2: Asset-Based Valuation of a Natural Resource / Holding Company

Problem & Given Scenario: A regional bank/holding firm reports Book Assets = $500M and Book Liabilities = $360M (Book Equity = $140M across 10M shares = $14.00/share). Fair value adjustments reveal: Real estate holdings are worth $35M above book carrying value, loan portfolio requires an additional $10M fair value markdown, and off-balance-sheet environmental liabilities have a fair value of $5M.

Formula Expression
V_Asset_Based = Fair Value of Total Assets − Fair Value of Total Liabilities − Fair Value of Preferred Stock

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Adjusted Fair Value of Equity = $140M + $35M − $10M − $5M = $160M. Intrinsic Asset-Based Value per share = $160M / 10M shares = $16.00 per share.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Asset-based valuation works best for holding companies, financial firms, or distressed liquidations with readily determinable tangible asset values.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: An analyst estimates a stock's intrinsic value at $48.00 per share while the stock trades in the market at $52.50 per share. Based on this analysis, the stock is:

  • [A] Undervalued and its expected return exceeds its required return

  • [B] Overvalued and its expected return is less than its required return

  • [C] Fairly valued if its beta equals 1.0

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Since V_0 ($48.00) < P_0 ($52.50), the stock is overvalued by $4.50, and paying $52.50 will generate an expected return below the required return k_e.

Practice Question 2

Question: For which of the following companies would an asset-based valuation model be LEAST appropriate?

  • [A] A closed-end equity investment fund

  • [B] A pharmaceutical company whose value derives primarily from unrecorded internally generated R&D and brand intangibles

  • [C] A distressed manufacturing company facing imminent liquidation

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Asset-based valuation fails when a firm possesses large unrecorded intangible assets, human capital, or synergies whose fair values cannot be reliably measured separately.

Practice Question 3

Question: An investor expects a stock to pay dividends of $1.50 at Year 1 and $1.80 at Year 2, followed by a sale price of $42.00 at the end of Year 2. At a 10.0% required return, the stock's intrinsic value today is closest to:

  • [A] $36.07

  • [B] $37.56

  • [C] $39.82

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: V_0 = $1.50 / (1.10)^1 + ($1.80 + $42.00) / (1.10)^2 = $1.3636 + ($43.80 / 1.21) = $1.3636 + $36.1983 = $37.5619 ≈ $37.56.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Intrinsic Value vs. Market Price Mispricing Signal.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Asset-Based Equity Valuation.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
