Introduction

Formula 1: The Six Tenets of the CFA Code of Ethics

Formula Expression
1. Act with integrity, competence, diligence, respect

2. Place integrity of profession & clients above personal interest

3. Use reasonable care & independent judgment

4. Practice and encourage others to practice ethically

5. Promote integrity and viability of global capital markets

6. Maintain and improve professional competence

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: The Code of Ethics sets the overarching moral principles ('who we aspire to be'), whereas the Standards set the enforceable rules of conduct ('what we must do').

Formula 2: Four-Step CFA Ethical Decision-Making Framework

Formula Expression
Step 1: Identify (facts, stakeholders, duties, conflicts) -> Step 2: Consider (situational influences, behavioral biases, guidance) -> Step 3: Decide and Act -> Step 4: Reflect

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: A cyclic, non-linear decision framework designed to overcome short-term situational pressures and conformity bias.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Distinguishing Code of Ethics Principles from Specific Standards Violations

Problem & Given Scenario: Compare two scenarios: (1) A charterholder neglects continuing financial education for 10 years, relying on outdated valuation methods that are not technically illegal. (2) A charterholder copies a competitor's spreadsheet model without attribution.

Formula Expression
1. Act with integrity, competence, diligence, respect

2. Place integrity of profession & clients above personal interest

3. Use reasonable care & independent judgment

4. Practice and encourage others to practice ethically

5. Promote integrity and viability of global capital markets

6. Maintain and improve professional competence

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Scenario 1 violates Tenet 6 of the Code of Ethics ('Maintain and improve their professional competence and strive to maintain and improve the competence of other investment professionals') as well as Standard V(A) Diligence. Scenario 2 violates both the Code of Ethics (integrity) and Standard I(C) Misrepresentation (plagiarism).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Exam questions frequently test the exact wording of the Six Tenets of the Code of Ethics—especially Tenet 6 (maintaining and improving both one's own competence AND the competence of other investment professionals).

Worked Example 2: Applying the Ethical Decision-Making Framework to Situational Pressures

Problem & Given Scenario: A junior equity analyst is pressured by the head of Investment Banking to upgrade a 'Sell' rating to 'Buy' so the firm can win a $15M underwriting mandate, arguing 'every other bank on the street has a Buy rating on them.'

Formula Expression
Step 1: Identify (facts, stakeholders, duties, conflicts) -> Step 2: Consider (situational influences, behavioral biases, guidance) -> Step 3: Decide and Act -> Step 4: Reflect

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

  • Step 1. Identify: Duty of loyalty to investing clients and independence/objectivity vs pressure from employer IB division.

  • Step 2. Consider: Recognize the behavioral conformity/loyalty bias ('everyone else does it') and short-term bonus temptation.

  • Step 3. Decide & Act: Refuse to alter the independent research conclusion (or move the stock to a restricted list showing only factual data without a recommendation).

  • Step 4. Reflect: Assess whether firm-wide firewall procedures between Research and IB need strengthening.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Placing a client issuer on a 'Restricted List' that reports only objective factual information is the proper compliance step when independence is compromised.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: According to the fifth tenet of the CFA Institute Code of Ethics, Members and Candidates must promote the integrity and viability of the global capital markets for the ultimate benefit of:

  • [A] Their employing investment firms

  • [B] High-net-worth institutional clients

  • [C] Society

Tip: Correct Answer: Option C.

Explanation: The exact phrasing of the fifth tenet of the Code of Ethics is to 'Promote the integrity and viability of the global capital markets for the ultimate benefit of society.'

Practice Question 2

Question: Which of the following is one of the six tenets of the CFA Institute Code of Ethics?

  • [A] Guaranteeing that client portfolios outperform their benchmarks over rolling five-year periods

  • [B] Maintaining and improving their professional competence and striving to maintain and improve the competence of other investment professionals

  • [C] Requiring all investment firms to adopt the Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS)

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Tenet 6 explicitly requires members and candidates to maintain and improve both their own competence and the competence of other investment professionals. (GIPS compliance is voluntary, and performance guarantees are prohibited).

Practice Question 3

Question: Why are rule-based legal and regulatory compliance systems alone insufficient without ethical principles in the investment profession?

  • [A] Because laws are always retroactive

  • [B] Because laws and regulations often lag behind financial innovation, can be narrow or ambiguous, and set only minimum baseline compliance rather than fiduciary excellence

  • [C] Because regulators do not enforce securities laws

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Laws typically react after market failures occur and can be gamed via technical loopholes; ethical principles guide behavior in novel gray areas before laws catch up.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in The Six Tenets of the CFA Code of Ethics.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Four-Step CFA Ethical Decision-Making Framework.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
