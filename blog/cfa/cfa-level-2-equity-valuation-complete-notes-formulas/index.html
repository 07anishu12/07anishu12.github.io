Introduction

CFA Level 2 Equity Valuation: Complete Notes and Formulas is a core Level II Equity Investments topic (LM 1–8: Equity Valuation Applications and Processes, Return Concepts, Industry and Company Analysis, Discounted Dividend Valuation, Free Cash Flow Valuation, Market-Based Valuation, Residual Income Valuation, Private Company Valuation) focusing on choosing among DDM, FCFF/FCFE, Residual Income, and Multiples across corporate lifecycles, equity risk premium estimation (historical vs forward-looking Gordon/Macro), and control/marketability adjustments. This guide covers the governing equations (Macroeconomic (Ibbotson-Chen) Supply-Side Equity Risk Premium and Pastor-Stambaugh Liquidity-Adjusted Cost of Equity & Control Premium), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, choosing among DDM, FCFF/FCFE, Residual Income, and Multiples across corporate lifecycles, equity risk premium estimation (historical vs forward-looking Gordon/Macro), and control/marketability adjustments links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Equity Investments. Macroeconomic (Ibbotson-Chen) Supply-Side Equity Risk Premium (ERP = {[1 + E(I)] × [1 + E(g_EPS)] × [1 + E(ΔP/E)] − 1} + E(Dividend Yield) − Nominal Risk-Free Rate) establishes the primary relationship (Forecasts the forward-looking Equity Risk Premium from expected inflation, real EPS growth, repricing (P/E change), and dividend yield minus the long-term government bond yield.), while Pastor-Stambaugh Liquidity-Adjusted Cost of Equity & Control Premium (r_e = R_F + β_M(ERP) + β_Size(SMB) + β_Value(HML) + β_Liquidity(LIQ) ; DLOC = 1 − [1 / (1 + Control Premium)]) governs Adds an illiquidity factor beta to Fama-French 3-factor required return, and converts a Control Premium into a Discount for Lack of Control (DLOC).

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Macroeconomic (Ibbotson-Chen) Supply-Side Equity Risk Premium

Formula Expression
ERP = {[1 + E(I)] × [1 + E(g_EPS)] × [1 + E(ΔP/E)] − 1} + E(Dividend Yield) − Nominal Risk-Free Rate

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Forecasts the forward-looking Equity Risk Premium from expected inflation, real EPS growth, repricing (P/E change), and dividend yield minus the long-term government bond yield.

Formula 2: Pastor-Stambaugh Liquidity-Adjusted Cost of Equity & Control Premium

Formula Expression
r_e = R_F + β_M(ERP) + β_Size(SMB) + β_Value(HML) + β_Liquidity(LIQ)

DLOC = 1 − [1 / (1 + Control Premium)]

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Adds an illiquidity factor beta to Fama-French 3-factor required return, and converts a Control Premium into a Discount for Lack of Control (DLOC).

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Calculating the Forward-Looking Macroeconomic (Supply-Side) Equity Risk Premium

Problem & Given Scenario: An equity strategist forecasts for an economy: Expected Inflation E(I) = 2.50%, Expected Real EPS Growth E(g_EPS) = 3.00%, Expected P/E Repricing E(ΔP/E) = −0.50% per year, Expected Dividend Yield = 2.10%, and 10-year Nominal Treasury Yield (R_F) = 4.20%.

Formula Expression
ERP = {[1 + E(I)] × [1 + E(g_EPS)] × [1 + E(ΔP/E)] − 1} + E(Dividend Yield) − Nominal Risk-Free Rate

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: Compound the three growth/repricing terms: (1.0250) × (1.0300) × (0.9950) − 1 = 1.05047 − 1 = 5.047% ≈ 5.05%. Step 2: Add Expected Dividend Yield (2.10%) to get Expected Market Return E(R_M) = 5.05% + 2.10% = 7.15%. Step 3: Subtract Nominal Risk-Free Rate (4.20%): Supply-Side ERP = 7.15% − 4.20% = 2.95%.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: The Ibbotson-Chen supply-side model is favored over historical averages when valuation multiples (P/E) have expanded to unsustainable levels.

Worked Example 2: Applying Blume Adjusted Beta for Forward-Looking Equity Valuation

Problem & Given Scenario: A utility stock's raw historical OLS regression beta is β_raw = 0.70. Compute its Blume-adjusted forward beta used in equity valuation.

Formula Expression
r_e = R_F + β_M(ERP) + β_Size(SMB) + β_Value(HML) + β_Liquidity(LIQ)

DLOC = 1 − [1 / (1 + Control Premium)]

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Blume Adjusted Beta = (2/3 × β_raw) + (1/3 × 1.00) = (0.6667 × 0.70) + 0.3333 = 0.4667 + 0.3333 = 0.80.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Blume's adjustment shrinks raw historical betas one-third of the way toward the market mean of 1.00 to account for long-run beta mean reversion.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: Using the Blume adjustment method, if a technology stock's raw historical regression beta is 1.45, its adjusted forward-looking beta is closest to:

  • [A] 1.15

  • [B] 1.30

  • [C] 1.45

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Adjusted Beta = (2/3 × 1.45) + (1/3 × 1.00) = 0.9667 + 0.3333 = 1.30.

Practice Question 2

Question: When estimating the historical Equity Risk Premium (ERP) from long-term index data, if failed companies that went bankrupt were removed from the historical index database, the estimated historical ERP will be:

  • [A] Biased upward due to survivorship bias

  • [B] Biased downward

  • [C] Unbiased

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Survivorship bias inflates historical index returns by excluding delisted/bankrupt companies, overstating the historical ERP unless corrected.

Practice Question 3

Question: Which absolute valuation model is LEAST sensitive to errors in estimating the distant terminal value because a large portion of intrinsic value is anchored in current t = 0 accounting Book Value?

  • [A] Two-stage Dividend Discount Model

  • [B] Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF) Model

  • [C] Residual Income (RI) Model

Tip: Correct Answer: Option C.

Explanation: In the Residual Income model, V_0 = B_0 + PV(Future Residual Income), so current Book Value B_0 often accounts for 50%–70% of total value at t = 0.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Macroeconomic (Ibbotson-Chen) Supply-Side Equity Risk Premium.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Pastor-Stambaugh Liquidity-Adjusted Cost of Equity & Control Premium.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
