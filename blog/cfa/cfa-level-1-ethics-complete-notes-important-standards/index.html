Introduction

CFA Ethics Level 1: Complete Notes and Important Standards is a core Level I Ethics and Professional Standards topic (LM 1–5: Ethics and Trust in the Investment Profession, Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct (Standards I–VII), Guidance and Application, GIPS Introduction) focusing on the 6 components of the Code of Ethics, the 7 Standards of Professional Conduct (22 sub-sections), the Ethics adjustment border-line rule, and Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS). This guide covers the governing equations (Stricter-of-Law Rule (Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law) and Ethical vs. Legal Matrix & Fiduciary Hierarchy), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Worked Example 1: Applying the Stricter-of-Law Principle Across Jurisdictions

Problem & Given Scenario: An analyst resides in Country X (which allows soft-dollar research rebates without disclosure), manages accounts for clients domiciled in Country Y (which requires annual summary disclosure of all commission rebates), and holds the CFA designation (Standard VI(A) requires full, plain-language prior disclosure of all conflicts and compensation arrangements).

Formula Expression
Governing Rule = max_strictness(Applicable Local Law, Country Law where Business Conducted, CFA Code and Standards)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Between Country X law, Country Y law, and the CFA Standards, the CFA Code and Standards (and Country Y law) are strictest. The analyst must follow the strictest rule—providing full, clear disclosure of the soft-dollar arrangements to clients.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Never violate local law, and whenever local law is less strict than (or silent compared to) the CFA Code and Standards, you MUST follow the CFA Code and Standards.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A CFA charterholder lives in a developing country where insider trading is not prohibited by local securities law, and local market custom permits trading on material nonpublic information. Under Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law, the charterholder must:

  • [A] Follow local market custom and trade on the information

  • [B] Follow the stricter standard—the CFA Institute Code and Standards—and refrain from trading on or causing others to trade on material nonpublic information

  • [C] Resign from the CFA Program

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: When applicable law is silent or less strict than the Code and Standards, Members and Candidates must adhere to the stricter CFA Code and Standards.

Practice Question 2

Question: Who is legally permitted to claim compliance with the Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS)?

  • [A] Individual portfolio managers and research analysts

  • [B] Only investment management firms (or asset owners) that manage actual discretionary assets on a firm-wide basis; individuals and software packages can NEVER claim GIPS compliance

  • [C] Financial software vendors that calculate time-weighted returns

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Compliance with GIPS is strictly a firm-wide (or asset-owner-wide) claim—either a firm is 100% compliant on a firm-wide basis or it is not; individual managers or software tools cannot claim compliance.

Practice Question 3

Question: Under the CFA Institute Disciplinary Review Process, who has the authority to investigate alleged violations of the Code and Standards and impose sanctions ranging from cautionary letters to revocation of the CFA charter?

  • [A] The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)

  • [B] The CFA Institute Professional Conduct Program and Disciplinary Review Committee (DRC)

  • [C] The candidate's local university

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: The CFA Institute Professional Conduct staff and peer-led Disciplinary Review Committee (DRC) enforce the Code and Standards globally.
