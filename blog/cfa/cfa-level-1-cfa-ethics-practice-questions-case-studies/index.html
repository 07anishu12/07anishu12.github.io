3. Fair Dealing ≠ Equal Dealing

4. Mosaic = Public + Non-Material Nonpublic

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

  • Step 1. Law vs Standards conflict -> Follow stricter rule (I(A)).

  • Step 2. Supervisor ignores illegal act -> Dissociate; resign if needed (I(A)).

  • Step 3. Issuer pays flight to remote mine -> OK only if no commercial transport; pay own hotel (I(B)).

  • Step 4. Issuer-paid research -> Flat fee only, disclosed, never stock/warrants (I(B)).

  • Step 5. Client gives past-performance gift -> Notify employer; future bonus needs advance written consent (I(B)/IV(B)).

  • Step 6. Citing Fed/Govt data without footnote -> Permitted; copying peer report -> Plagiarism (I(C)).

  • Step 7. Guaranteeing Treasury STRIP -> Permitted; guaranteeing fund return -> Violation (I(C)).

  • Step 8. Arrested at peaceful protest -> No violation; expense fraud -> Violation (I(D)).

  • Step 9. CEO leaks M&A in elevator -> MNPI, cannot trade (II(A)).

  • Step 10. Counting trucks at factory + public filings -> Mosaic Theory, permitted (II(A)).

  • Step 11. Wash trades to boost volume -> Manipulation violation (II(B)).

  • Step 12. Tax-loss selling at year-end -> Permitted (II(B)).

  • Step 13. Soft dollars for Bloomberg terminal research -> Permitted; for office carpet -> Violation (III(A)).

  • Step 14. Pension plan client -> Plan beneficiaries, not company management (III(A)).

  • Step 15. Hot IPO oversubscribed -> Allocate pro-rata by order size; exclude family/personal accounts (III(B)).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Reviewing these 15 core rulings builds instant pattern recognition for Session 1 Ethics questions.

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

  • Step 17. Porting past firm track record -> Allowed only if manager was sole/primary decision-maker AND has supporting records (III(D)).

  • Step 18. Disclosing client data -> Only if illegal activity or CFA PCP investigation (III(E)).

  • Step 19. Registering new LLC before resigning -> Permitted; copying models/client lists -> Violation (IV(A)).

  • Step 20. Soliciting former clients after leaving (no non-compete) -> Permitted from public info/memory (IV(A)).

  • Step 21. Inadequate compliance system -> Decline supervisory role in writing until fixed (IV(C)).

  • Step 22. Outvoted on investment committee -> May keep name on report if consensus has reasonable basis (V(A)).

  • Step 23. Fact vs opinion -> Price targets are always opinions, never guaranteed facts (V(B)).

  • Step 24. Record retention default -> 7 years if no local law (V(C)).

  • Step 25. Fee-paying family account (no manager ownership) -> Trade pro-rata WITH regular clients (VI(B)).

  • Step 26. Beneficial-interest spouse account -> Trade AFTER clients and employer (VI(B)).

  • Step 27. Referral fees -> Disclose cash & non-cash value BEFORE contract signed (VI(C)).

  • Step 28. Discussing tested/untested exam topics -> Violation of VII(A).

  • Step 29. Unpaid CFA annual dues -> Cannot use 'CFA' designation (VII(B)).

  • Step 30. GIPS compliance -> Firm-wide only; include all actual, fee-paying, discretionary accounts; external verification is voluntary and firm-wide (GIPS).

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A portfolio manager leaves Firm A to join Firm B. At Firm A, she was the sole decision-maker for a mid-cap equity strategy that beat the benchmark by 300 bps annually over 5 years. At Firm B, she wants to include that 5-year track record in her marketing brochure, and reconstructs the performance from publicly available mutual fund filings and Bloomberg data. Has she violated Standard III(D) Performance Presentation?

  • [A] Yes, because past performance always stays with the previous employer and can never be cited

  • [B] No, provided she was the sole decision-maker at Firm A, clearly discloses that the performance was achieved at her prior firm, and backs up the record with publicly available data without taking proprietary Firm A files

  • [C] Yes, unless Firm A gives written consent

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: A manager may present a portable track record from a prior firm if she was the sole (or primary) decision-maker, clearly discloses that the record was earned at the prior firm, and supports it using public records without stealing confidential employer files.

Practice Question 2

Question: Under the Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS), which of the following statements regarding independent third-party verification is accurate?

  • [A] Verification is mandatory for any firm claiming GIPS compliance

  • [B] Verification is voluntary, must be performed on a firm-wide basis (never on a single composite alone), and must be conducted by an independent third party

  • [C] A firm can self-verify its own GIPS compliance

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: GIPS verification is voluntary (though strongly recommended), must be performed by an independent third party, and applies strictly to the entire firm—there is no such thing as partial or single-composite GIPS verification.

Practice Question 3

Question: An analyst visits a retail chain's flagship store as an ordinary shopper, notices that shelves are half-empty and checkout lines are nonexistent, and combines these observations with the retailer's public 10-Q inventory turnover ratios to issue a 'Sell' recommendation. This action:

  • [A] Violates Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information

  • [B] Is a proper application of the Mosaic Theory and complies with the Code and Standards

  • [C] Violates Standard III(E) Confidentiality

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Observing store operations open to the public is non-material nonpublic (or public) information; combining it with public financial filings is the essence of the Mosaic Theory.
