Introduction

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, straight-line, double-declining balance (DDB), and units-of-production depreciation schedules, salvage value floors, and average asset age estimation links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Financial Statement Analysis. Straight-Line & Units-of-Production Depreciation (Depr_SL = (Cost − Residual Value) / Useful Life ; Depr_UOP = (Cost − Residual) × (Output_t / Total Output)) establishes the primary relationship (Allocates depreciable cost evenly over time (SL) or proportionally to physical usage (UOP).), while Double-Declining Balance (DDB) & Age Metrics (Depr_DDB_t = (2 / Useful Life) × Carrying Amount_{t−1} (floored at Residual Value) ; Avg Age = Acc. Depr. / Annual SL Depr.) governs Applies 2× the straight-line rate to beginning net book value without subtracting residual value initially, but stops depreciating once NBV hits residual value.

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Straight-Line & Units-of-Production Depreciation

Formula Expression
Depr_SL = (Cost − Residual Value) / Useful Life

Depr_UOP = (Cost − Residual) × (Output_t / Total Output)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Allocates depreciable cost evenly over time (SL) or proportionally to physical usage (UOP).

Formula 2: Double-Declining Balance (DDB) & Age Metrics

Formula Expression
Depr_DDB_t = (2 / Useful Life) × Carrying Amount_{t−1} (floored at Residual Value)

Avg Age = Acc. Depr. / Annual SL Depr.

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Applies 2× the straight-line rate to beginning net book value without subtracting residual value initially, but stops depreciating once NBV hits residual value.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: 3-Year Multi-Method Depreciation Comparison (SL vs. DDB)

Problem & Given Scenario: A machine costs $120,000, has an estimated residual value of $15,000, and a useful life of 5 years.

Formula Expression
Depr_SL = (Cost − Residual Value) / Useful Life

Depr_UOP = (Cost − Residual) × (Output_t / Total Output)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Straight-Line (SL): Annual Depr = ($120,000 − $15,000) / 5 = $21,000/year; Year 3 Ending NBV = $120,000 − 3($21,000) = $57,000. Double-Declining Balance (DDB): Rate = 2 / 5 = 40.0%. Year 1 Depr = 40% × $120,000 = $48,000 (End NBV = $72,000). Year 2 Depr = 40% × $72,000 = $28,800 (End NBV = $43,200). Year 3 Depr = 40% × $43,200 = $17,280 (End NBV = $25,920).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: In Years 1 and 2, DDB charges higher depreciation ($48,000 and $28,800) than SL ($21,000), but by Year 3 DDB depreciation ($17,280) crosses below SL ($21,000).

Worked Example 2: Estimating Average Age, Total Useful Life, and Remaining Life of PP&E

Problem & Given Scenario: A firm using straight-line depreciation reports Gross PP&E = $900M (assuming zero salvage), Accumulated Depreciation = $360M, and Annual Depreciation Expense = $60M.

Formula Expression
Depr_DDB_t = (2 / Useful Life) × Carrying Amount_{t−1} (floored at Residual Value)

Avg Age = Acc. Depr. / Annual SL Depr.

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Total Useful Life = Gross PP&E / Annual Depr = $900M / $60M = 15.0 years. Average Age = Acc. Depr / Annual Depr = $360M / $60M = 6.0 years. Remaining Useful Life = Net PP&E / Annual Depr = ($900M − $360M) / $60M = $540M / $60M = 9.0 years.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Checking that Average Age (6.0 yrs) + Remaining Life (9.0 yrs) = Total Useful Life (15.0 yrs) alerts analysts to upcoming CapEx replacement cycles.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A piece of equipment is purchased for $200,000 with an estimated residual value of $30,000 and a useful life of 4 years. Under the double-declining balance (DDB) method, depreciation expense in Year 3 is closest to:

  • [A] $20,000

  • [B] $25,000

  • [C] $30,000

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: DDB rate = 2 / 4 = 50%. Year 1 Depr = 50% × $200,000 = $100,000 (End NBV = $100,000). Year 2 Depr = 50% × $100,000 = $50,000 (End NBV = $50,000). In Year 3, 50% × $50,000 would be $25,000, which would drop NBV to $25,000—below the $30,000 residual value floor! Thus Year 3 depreciation is capped at $50,000 − $30,000 = $20,000.

Practice Question 2

Question: If management increases the estimated useful life of existing manufacturing equipment from 8 years to 12 years, this change is accounted for as a:

  • [A] Retrospective restatement of prior-period financial statements

  • [B] Change in accounting estimate applied prospectively to current and future periods

  • [C] Correction of a prior-period accounting error

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Changes in useful life or residual value are changes in accounting estimates and are applied prospectively without restating prior years.

Practice Question 3

Question: Compared to expensing an expenditure immediately, capitalizing and depreciating a capital expenditure in its first year results in:

  • [A] Higher operating cash flow (CFO), lower investing cash flow (CFI), and higher net income

  • [B] Lower operating cash flow (CFO) and higher investing cash flow (CFI)

  • [C] Identical operating cash flow (CFO) and higher total cash flow

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Capitalizing classifies the cash outflow in CFI instead of CFO (boosting CFO) and expenses only 1 year's depreciation instead of 100% of the outlay (boosting Year 1 Net Income).

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Straight-Line & Units-of-Production Depreciation.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Double-Declining Balance (DDB) & Age Metrics.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
