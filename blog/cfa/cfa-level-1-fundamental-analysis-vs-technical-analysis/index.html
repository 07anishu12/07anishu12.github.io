Introduction

Fundamental Analysis vs Technical Analysis: CFA Explained is a core Level I Equity Investments topic (LM 3 & LM 6–8: Market Efficiency, Company Analysis, and Equity Valuation — Top-down/bottom-up fundamental analysis vs price/volume technical analysis) focusing on comparing fundamental intrinsic valuation (financial statements, macro/industry moat, DCF) against technical price/volume chart analysis, EMH implications, and behavioral finance. This guide covers the governing equations (Fundamental Valuation vs. Relative Strength Ratio and Moving Average Crossover & Put-Call Sentiment Ratio), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, comparing fundamental intrinsic valuation (financial statements, macro/industry moat, DCF) against technical price/volume chart analysis, EMH implications, and behavioral finance links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Equity Investments. Fundamental Valuation vs. Relative Strength Ratio (Fundamental Alpha = (V_Intrinsic − P_Market) / P_Market ; Relative Strength = Stock Price_t / Benchmark Index_t) establishes the primary relationship (Fundamental analysis targets convergence of market price toward intrinsic value; technical analysis tracks price momentum and supply/demand trends.), while Moving Average Crossover & Put-Call Sentiment Ratio (SMA_N = (1/N) ∑ P_{t−i} ; Put-Call Ratio = Put Volume / Call Volume) governs Technical indicators use moving averages to identify trend support/resistance and options volume to gauge contrarian sentiment extremes.

Formula 1: Fundamental Valuation vs. Relative Strength Ratio

Formula Expression

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Contrasting Fundamental vs. Technical Signals on an Industrial Stock

Problem & Given Scenario: Stock X trades at $45.00 (down from $60.00). Fundamental Audit: Net cash per share = $12.00, FCFE = $4.20/share growing at 5%, required return = 10% -> Fundamental Intrinsic Value V_0 = $4.20 / (0.10 − 0.05) = $84.00 (46% undervalued). Technical Audit: 50-day SMA ($48.50) just crossed below the 200-day SMA ($51.00) ('death cross') on heavy volume, breaking $46.00 support.

Formula Expression
Fundamental Alpha = (V_Intrinsic − P_Market) / P_Market

Relative Strength = Stock Price_t / Benchmark Index_t

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

A fundamental value manager buys at $45.00 due to the deep discount below $84.00 intrinsic value, whereas a trend-following technical trader waits for price stabilization and a positive moving-average crossover before entering.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Institutional managers often combine fundamental analysis (to decide WHAT to buy) with technical analysis (to time WHEN to execute).

Worked Example 2: Evaluating Market Efficiency (EMH) Implications for Technical vs. Fundamental Analysis

Problem & Given Scenario: In Market A (Weak-Form Efficient), serial correlation of daily returns is 0.00, but stocks with low P/B and positive earnings surprises earn +4.5% annual alpha. In Market B (Semi-Strong Form Efficient), neither historical price rules nor public financial statement ratios generate positive risk-adjusted alpha after transaction costs.

Formula Expression
SMA_N = (1/N) ∑ P_{t−i}

Put-Call Ratio = Put Volume / Call Volume

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

In Market A, Technical Analysis fails (since weak-form efficiency prices in all historical price/volume data), but Fundamental Analysis succeeds. In Market B, both Technical and Fundamental Analysis fail to beat passive indexing.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Weak-form efficiency invalidates technical analysis; semi-strong-form efficiency invalidates both technical and public-data fundamental analysis.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: If a capital market is weak-form efficient but NOT semi-strong-form efficient, which active investment approach can consistently generate positive abnormal risk-adjusted returns?

  • [A] Technical analysis based on past price and volume patterns

  • [B] Fundamental analysis of publicly available financial statements and industry reports

  • [C] Neither technical nor fundamental analysis

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Weak-form efficiency means past prices and volume are already fully reflected (ruling out technical analysis), but because the market is not yet semi-strong efficient, fundamental analysis of public filings can still earn abnormal returns.

Practice Question 2

Question: Unlike fundamental analysis, technical analysis can be applied directly to assets such as spot currencies and non-income-producing commodities because:

  • [A] Technical analysis relies solely on observable market price and trading volume rather than discounted future cash flows or financial statements

  • [B] Commodities have higher intrinsic dividend yields

  • [C] Currency markets have zero transaction costs

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Assets without cash flows or financial statements (such as gold bullion or spot FX pairs) lack DCF inputs, making technical supply-and-demand price analysis widely used by macro traders.

Practice Question 3

Question: In technical analysis, when a stock's price breaks decisively below a well-established 'support level' on high volume, that former support price level typically becomes:

  • [A] A new resistance level on subsequent upward price rebounds

  • [B] The stock's fundamental book value

  • [C] An arbitrage-free floor

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: By the polarity principle of technical analysis, once a support floor is breached, trapped buyers looking to exit at break-even turn that price level into overhead resistance.
