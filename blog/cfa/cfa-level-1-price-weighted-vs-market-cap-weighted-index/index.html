Introduction

Price Weighted vs Market Cap Weighted Index: Numerical Examples is a core Level I Equity Investments topic (LM 2: Security Market Indexes — Price-weighted, equal-weighted, market-capitalization-weighted, float-adjusted, and fundamental-weighted indexes; divisor adjustments and rebalancing) focusing on computing index levels, constituent weights, and period returns across price-weighted, equal-weighted, and float-adjusted market-cap-weighted indexes, plus stock split divisor adjustment. This guide covers the governing equations (Price-Weighted Index & Split Divisor Adjustment and Float-Adjusted Market-Cap-Weighted & Equal-Weighted Return), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, computing index levels, constituent weights, and period returns across price-weighted, equal-weighted, and float-adjusted market-cap-weighted indexes, plus stock split divisor adjustment links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Equity Investments. Price-Weighted Index & Split Divisor Adjustment (Index_PW = (∑ P_i) / Divisor ; w_i_PW = P_i / (∑ P_j) ; New Divisor = (Post-Split ∑ P_i) / Pre-Split Index Level) establishes the primary relationship (Weights each stock by its per-share price (equivalent to holding 1 share of each constituent); requires divisor adjustment after stock splits.), while Float-Adjusted Market-Cap-Weighted & Equal-Weighted Return (w_i_MC = (P_i × Float_Shares_i) / ∑ (P_j × Float_Shares_j) ; R_Equal_Weighted = (1/N) ∑ R_i) governs Market-cap indexes automatically adjust for price changes without trading; equal-weighted indexes require periodic rebalancing.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Comparing Price-Weighted, Equal-Weighted, and Market-Cap-Weighted Index Returns

Problem & Given Scenario: A 3-stock index contains: Stock A (P_0 = $100, P_1 = $110 [+10%], Shares = 10M, MktCap_0 = $1,000M); Stock B (P_0 = $50, P_1 = $45 [−10%], Shares = 40M, MktCap_0 = $2,000M); Stock C (P_0 = $10, P_1 = $12 [+20%], Shares = 500M, MktCap_0 = $5,000M).

Formula Expression
Index_PW = (∑ P_i) / Divisor

w_i_PW = P_i / (∑ P_j)

New Divisor = (Post-Split ∑ P_i) / Pre-Split Index Level

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

  • Step 1. Price-Weighted Return: Sum P_0 = 100 + 50 + 10 = $160; Sum P_1 = 110 + 45 + 12 = $167; R_PW = (167 − 160)/160 = +4.375% ≈ +4.38% (dominated by high-priced Stock A at 62.5% weight).

  • Step 2. Equal-Weighted Return: R_EW = (+10% − 10% + 20%) / 3 = +6.67%.

  • Step 3. Market-Cap-Weighted Return: Total MktCap_0 = $8,000M; Total MktCap_1 = $1,100M + $1,800M + $6,000M = $8,900M; R_MC = (8,900 − 8,000)/8,000 = +11.25% (dominated by mega-cap Stock C at 62.5% weight).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: The exact same 3 stocks yield +4.38% (Price-Weighted), +6.67% (Equal-Weighted), and +11.25% (Market-Cap-Weighted) due to weighting methodology.

Worked Example 2: Adjusting the Price-Weighted Index Divisor for a 2-for-1 Stock Split

Problem & Given Scenario: Using the 3 stocks at t = 0 (P_A = $100, P_B = $50, P_C = $10) with an initial divisor of d_0 = 3.00 (Index Level = 160 / 3 = 53.3333), suppose Stock A executes a 2-for-1 split (dropping P_A from $100 to $50). Compute the new divisor d_new.

Formula Expression
w_i_MC = (P_i × Float_Shares_i) / ∑ (P_j × Float_Shares_j)

R_Equal_Weighted = (1/N) ∑ R_i

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Post-split sum of prices = $50 + $50 + $10 = $110. To keep the index level unchanged at 53.3333: d_new = $110 / 53.3333 = 2.0625 (or $110 × 3 / $160 = 2.0625).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Stock splits reduce the divisor of a price-weighted index and decrease the split stock's weight (Stock A drops from 62.5% to 50/110 = 45.45%).

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: A price-weighted index consists of three stocks priced at $90, $60, and $30, with a current divisor of 2.0 (Index Level = 90.0). If the $90 stock undergoes a 3-for-1 stock split (reducing its price to $30), the new divisor required to prevent the index level from changing is:

  • [A] 1.33

  • [B] 1.50

  • [C] 2.00

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Pre-split index = (90 + 60 + 30) / 2.0 = 180 / 2.0 = 90.0. Post-split sum of prices = 30 + 60 + 30 = 120. Solve 120 / d_new = 90.0 => d_new = 120 / 90.0 = 1.3333 ≈ 1.33.

Practice Question 2

Question: Which index weighting methodology requires the highest portfolio turnover to maintain target constituent weights as constituent stock prices fluctuate?

  • [A] Float-adjusted market-capitalization weighting

  • [B] Price weighting

  • [C] Equal weighting

Tip: Correct Answer: Option C.

Explanation: In an equal-weighted index, any divergence in stock returns immediately causes winners to exceed 1/N weight and losers to fall below 1/N weight, requiring periodic selling of winners and buying of losers.

Practice Question 3

Question: Compared to a market-capitalization-weighted index, an index weighted by fundamental company metrics (such as book value, cash flow, and dividends) will exhibit a systematic:

  • [A] Momentum bias toward high P/E growth stocks

  • [B] Value tilt, because stocks whose market cap has risen above their fundamental share weight are trimmed during rebalancing

  • [C] Bias toward high-share-price companies

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Fundamental indexes weight stocks by earnings/book/dividends rather than market price, creating a contrarian value tilt that sells high-multiple stocks and buys low-multiple stocks.
