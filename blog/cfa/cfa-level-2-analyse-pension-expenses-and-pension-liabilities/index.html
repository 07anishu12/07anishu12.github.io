Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Funded Status & Total Periodic Pension Cost (TPPC) (Funded Status = Fair Value of Plan Assets − PBO ; TPPC = Current Service Cost + Past Service Cost + Interest Cost − Actual Return on Plan Assets + Actuarial Loss (or = Ending Net Liability − Beg Net Liability + Employer Contributions)) and IFRS (IAS 19) vs. US GAAP (ASC 715) Income Statement Pension Expense (IFRS P&L = Service Cost + Past Service Cost + [Net Pension Liability × Discount Rate r] ; US GAAP P&L = Service Cost + Interest Cost (PBO×r) − Expected Return (Assets×E(R)) + Amortized Past Service & Actuarial Losses) govern Defined Benefit Funded Status (Plan Assets − PBO), Total Periodic Pension Cost (TPPC), IFRS vs US GAAP income statement vs OCI classification, actuarial assumption sensitivities, and CFO/CFF cash flow adjustments.

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Funded Status & Total Periodic Pension Cost (TPPC)

Formula Expression
Funded Status = Fair Value of Plan Assets − PBO

TPPC = Current Service Cost + Past Service Cost + Interest Cost − Actual Return on Plan Assets + Actuarial Loss (or = Ending Net Liability − Beg Net Liability + Employer Contributions)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Economic TPPC equals the true change in the plan's net liability excluding employer cash contributions.

Formula 2: IFRS (IAS 19) vs. US GAAP (ASC 715) Income Statement Pension Expense

Formula Expression
IFRS P&L = Service Cost + Past Service Cost + [Net Pension Liability × Discount Rate r]

US GAAP P&L = Service Cost + Interest Cost (PBO×r) − Expected Return (Assets×E(R)) + Amortized Past Service & Actuarial Losses

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:

  • IFRS uses a single Net Interest rate (Discount Rate r on both PBO and Plan Assets) and puts Remeasurements permanently in OCI.

  • US GAAP uses Expected Return E(R) on Plan Assets and amortizes OCI corridors into P&L.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Calculating Economic TPPC, IFRS P&L Pension Cost, and US GAAP P&L Pension Cost

Problem & Given Scenario: A Defined Benefit plan has: Beginning PBO = $500M, Beginning Plan Assets = $400M (Beginning Net Pension Liability = $100M). During the year: Current Service Cost = $22M, Past Service Cost = $4M, Discount Rate r = 5.0%, Expected Return on Plan Assets E(R) = 7.0%, Actual Return on Plan Assets = $34M, Actuarial Loss on PBO = $10M, and Employer Contributions = $30M (zero prior OCI amortization).

Formula Expression
Funded Status = Fair Value of Plan Assets − PBO

TPPC = Current Service Cost + Past Service Cost + Interest Cost − Actual Return on Plan Assets + Actuarial Loss (or = Ending Net Liability − Beg Net Liability + Employer Contributions)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

  • Step 1. Economic Total Periodic Pension Cost (TPPC): Service ($22M) + Past Service ($4M) + Interest Cost ($500M × 5% = $25M) − Actual Return ($34M) + Actuarial Loss ($10M) = $27.0M.

  • Step 2. IFRS (IAS 19) Income Statement Expense: Service ($22M) + Past Service ($4M) + Net Interest ($100M Net Liability × 5.0% = $5M) = $31.0M (while OCI records Remeasurement = Actual Return $34M − Interest Income $20M − Actuarial Loss $10M = +$4.0M gain!).

  • Step 3. US GAAP Income Statement Expense: Current Service ($22M) + Interest ($25M) − Expected Return ($400M × 7.0% = $28M) = $19.0M (Past Service $4M goes to OCI and is amortized over future service!).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Notice how the exact same pension plan reports $31.0M P&L expense under IFRS vs $19.0M under US GAAP because US GAAP credits a 7.0% Expected Return ($28M) on Plan Assets and defers Past Service Cost ($4M) in OCI!

Worked Example 2: Reclassifying Operating vs. Financing Cash Flows When Employer Contribution Exceeds TPPC

Problem & Given Scenario: In the example above, Economic TPPC = $27.0M, while the company made a cash Employer Contribution of $42.0M (all deducted inside CFO), and the corporate tax rate is 25%.

Formula Expression
IFRS P&L = Service Cost + Past Service Cost + [Net Pension Liability × Discount Rate r]

US GAAP P&L = Service Cost + Interest Cost (PBO×r) − Expected Return (Assets×E(R)) + Amortized Past Service & Actuarial Losses

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Because the $42.0M contribution exceeded the $27.0M economic pension cost by $15.0M, the firm effectively paid down $15.0M of its debt-like pension liability! After-tax excess contribution = $15.0M × (1 − 0.25) = $11.25M. Analyst Adjustment: Add +$11.25M back to Operating Cash Flow (CFO) and subtract −$11.25M from Financing Cash Flow (CFF).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: When Employer Contribution > TPPC, the after-tax excess is economically a debt repayment (reclassify from CFO to CFF); when Contribution < TPPC, the shortfall is economic borrowing (reclassify from CFF to CFO).

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: If a US GAAP reporting company increases its assumed Expected Rate of Return on Pension Plan Assets from 6.0% to 8.0% while holding the discount rate and all other actuarial assumptions constant, what is the immediate impact on its financial statements?

  • [A] Its balance sheet Funded Status improves and its PBO decreases

  • [B] Its balance sheet Funded Status and PBO are completely unaffected, but its reported US GAAP Income Statement pension expense decreases (increasing reported Net Income)

  • [C] Its IFRS income statement pension expense decreases

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Funded Status = Fair Value of Plan Assets − PBO (neither of which uses the Expected Return assumption!). Raising E(R) merely inflates the expected return credit on a US GAAP income statement, artificially boosting GAAP Net Income.

Practice Question 2

Question: Under IFRS (IAS 19), how is Past Service Cost (arising from a plan amendment) recognized, compared to US GAAP?

  • [A] Under IFRS, Past Service Cost is expensed immediately in Profit or Loss (P&L) in the period of the amendment; under US GAAP, it is recorded in Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) and amortized into P&L over the remaining service life of employees

  • [B] Under IFRS, it is deferred in OCI

  • [C] Both expense it immediately

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: IAS 19 expenses Past Service Cost immediately in P&L, whereas US GAAP defers prior service cost in OCI and amortizes it over future service years.

Practice Question 3

Question: A company's Total Periodic Pension Cost (TPPC) for the year is $40 million, and it makes a cash contribution to the pension plan of $60 million. Given a 25% marginal tax rate, how should an analyst adjust the company's reported Operating Cash Flow (CFO) and Financing Cash Flow (CFF) to reflect economic reality?

  • [A] Increase CFO by $15 million and decrease CFF by $15 million

  • [B] Decrease CFO by $15 million and increase CFF by $15 million

  • [C] Increase CFO by $20 million

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: The $20M excess contribution ($60M − $40M) is economically equivalent to paying down a pension debt liability. After tax ($20M × [1 − 0.25] = $15M), reclassify $15M out of CFO (increasing CFO by +$15M) and into CFF (decreasing CFF by −$15M).

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Funded Status & Total Periodic Pension Cost (TPPC).

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in IFRS (IAS 19) vs. US GAAP (ASC 715) Income Statement Pension Expense.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
