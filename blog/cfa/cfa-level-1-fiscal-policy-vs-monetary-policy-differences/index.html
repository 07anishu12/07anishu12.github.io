Introduction

Fiscal Policy vs Monetary Policy: Differences With Examples is a core Level I Economics topic (LM 3 & LM 4: Fiscal Policy & Monetary Policy — Spending vs tax multipliers, balanced budget multiplier, automatic stabilizers, crowding out, Ricardian equivalence, 4 policy interaction matrix) focusing on comparing government fiscal policy (G and T multipliers, automatic stabilizers, crowding out, Ricardian equivalence) against central bank monetary policy and their 2x2 interaction matrix. This guide covers the governing equations (Government Spending Multiplier vs. Tax Multiplier and Balanced-Budget Fiscal Impact (ΔG = ΔT)), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, comparing government fiscal policy (G and T multipliers, automatic stabilizers, crowding out, Ricardian equivalence) against central bank monetary policy and their 2x2 interaction matrix links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Economics. Government Spending Multiplier vs. Tax Multiplier (Spending Multiplier M_G = 1 / [1 − MPC × (1 − t)] ; Tax Multiplier M_T = −MPC / [1 − MPC × (1 − t)]) establishes the primary relationship (Direct government spending G has a larger multiplier than an equal lump-sum tax cut because 100% of ΔG is spent in round 1, whereas (1 − MPC) of a tax cut is saved.), while Balanced-Budget Fiscal Impact (ΔG = ΔT) (ΔY_Balanced = (M_G + M_T) × ΔG = [(1 − MPC) / (1 − MPC(1 − t))] × ΔG > 0) governs Increasing G and lump-sum taxes T by the same dollar amount is still net expansionary.

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Government Spending Multiplier vs. Tax Multiplier

Formula Expression
Spending Multiplier M_G = 1 / [1 − MPC × (1 − t)]

Tax Multiplier M_T = −MPC / [1 − MPC × (1 − t)]

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Direct government spending G has a larger multiplier than an equal lump-sum tax cut because 100% of ΔG is spent in round 1, whereas (1 − MPC) of a tax cut is saved.

Formula 2: Balanced-Budget Fiscal Impact (ΔG = ΔT)

Formula Expression
ΔY_Balanced = (M_G + M_T) × ΔG = [(1 − MPC) / (1 − MPC(1 − t))] × ΔG > 0

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Increasing G and lump-sum taxes T by the same dollar amount is still net expansionary.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Calculating the Fiscal Multiplier and Aggregate Demand Impact

Problem & Given Scenario: An economy has a Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC) of 0.75 and an effective net tax rate of t = 20.0%. The government enacts a $100 billion infrastructure spending package (ΔG = +$100B).

Formula Expression
Spending Multiplier M_G = 1 / [1 − MPC × (1 − t)]

Tax Multiplier M_T = −MPC / [1 − MPC × (1 − t)]

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: Effective marginal propensity to spend out of national income = MPC × (1 − t) = 0.75 × (1 − 0.20) = 0.60. Step 2: Fiscal Multiplier M_G = 1 / (1 − 0.60) = 1 / 0.40 = 2.50x. Step 3: Total Aggregate Demand expansion = 2.50 × $100B = +$250 billion.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: A $100B increase in government spending expands equilibrium Aggregate Demand by $250B through successive rounds of consumer respending.

Worked Example 2: Analyzing the 2x2 Monetary and Fiscal Policy Interaction Matrix

Problem & Given Scenario: Evaluate the impact on Aggregate Demand, Interest Rates, and Private vs. Public Sector Growth under all 4 combinations of Expansionary (Easy) and Contractionary (Tight) Fiscal and Monetary Policy.

Formula Expression
ΔY_Balanced = (M_G + M_T) × ΔG = [(1 − MPC) / (1 − MPC(1 − t))] × ΔG > 0

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

  • Step 1. Easy Fiscal + Easy Monetary: Highly expansionary AD; public & private sectors both grow; interest rate impact ambiguous.

  • Step 2. Tight Fiscal + Tight Monetary: Highly contractionary AD; both sectors shrink.

  • Step 3. Easy Fiscal + Tight Monetary: Higher interest rates (crowding out private investment I); public sector share of GDP expands.

  • Step 4. Tight Fiscal + Easy Monetary: Lower interest rates; private investment I expands while public sector share shrinks.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: The 2x2 Fiscal-Monetary policy interaction table is one of the highest-frequency conceptual test items on CFA Level I Economics.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: In an economy where the marginal propensity to consume (MPC) is 0.80 and the income tax rate (t) is 25%, the fiscal spending multiplier is:

  • [A] 2.50x

  • [B] 4.00x

  • [C] 5.00x

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Multiplier = 1 / [1 − MPC × (1 − t)] = 1 / [1 − 0.80 × (1 − 0.25)] = 1 / (1 − 0.60) = 1 / 0.40 = 2.50x.

Practice Question 2

Question: Which policy combination will most reliably cause domestic real interest rates to rise and the public sector's share of GDP to increase at the expense of private-sector capital investment?

  • [A] Expansionary fiscal policy combined with contractionary monetary policy

  • [B] Contractionary fiscal policy combined with expansionary monetary policy

  • [C] Expansionary fiscal policy combined with expansionary monetary policy

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Expansionary fiscal policy increases government borrowing demand (pushing rates up and growing G), while contractionary monetary policy restricts credit supply (pushing rates up further and crowding out private investment I).

Practice Question 3

Question: In discretionary fiscal policy, the delay between the time an economic recession begins and the time economists and policymakers recognize the downturn in official GDP data is called the:

  • [A] Action lag

  • [B] Impact lag

  • [C] Recognition lag

Tip: Correct Answer: Option C.

Explanation: The three fiscal policy lags are: Recognition lag (identifying the downturn in lagging data), Action/Legislative lag (passing a bill in parliament/Congress), and Impact lag (spending taking effect).

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Government Spending Multiplier vs. Tax Multiplier.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Balanced-Budget Fiscal Impact (ΔG = ΔT).

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
