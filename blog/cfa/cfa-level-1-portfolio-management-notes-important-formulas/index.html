Introduction

CFA Portfolio Management: Complete Notes and Important Formulas is a core Level I Portfolio Management topic (LM 1–7: Portfolio Management Overview, IPS, Portfolio Risk and Return (Part I & II), Basics of Portfolio Planning, Behavioral Biases, Introduction to Risk Management) focusing on the three-step portfolio management process (Planning, Execution, Feedback), Investment Policy Statement (IPS) components, indifference utility curves, and risk governance. This guide covers the governing equations (Investor Utility Function & Indifference Curve and Capital Allocation Line (CAL) Expected Return & Volatility), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, the three-step portfolio management process (Planning, Execution, Feedback), Investment Policy Statement (IPS) components, indifference utility curves, and risk governance links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Portfolio Management. Investor Utility Function & Indifference Curve (U = E(R) − (0.5 × A × σ^2)) establishes the primary relationship (E(R) = expected return (in decimals or % consistently); σ^2 = return variance; A = risk aversion coefficient (A > 0 risk-averse, A = 0 risk-neutral, A < 0 risk-seeking).), while Capital Allocation Line (CAL) Expected Return & Volatility (E(R_C) = R_F + [(E(R_P) − R_F) / σ_P] × σ_C ; Slope = Sharpe Ratio of Risky Portfolio P) governs Combines the risk-free asset with a risky portfolio P; the optimal risky portfolio is the Tangency Market Portfolio M on the Capital Market Line (CML).

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Investor Utility Function & Indifference Curve

Formula Expression
U = E(R) − (0.5 × A × σ^2)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:

  • E(R) = expected return (in decimals or % consistently).

  • σ^2 = return variance.

  • A = risk aversion coefficient (A > 0 risk-averse, A = 0 risk-neutral, A < 0 risk-seeking).

Formula 2: Capital Allocation Line (CAL) Expected Return & Volatility

Formula Expression
E(R_C) = R_F + [(E(R_P) − R_F) / σ_P] × σ_C

Slope = Sharpe Ratio of Risky Portfolio P

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:

  • Combines the risk-free asset with a risky portfolio P.

  • the optimal risky portfolio is the Tangency Market Portfolio M on the Capital Market Line (CML).

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Ranking Portfolios Using the CFA Investor Utility Function

Problem & Given Scenario: An institutional investor has a risk aversion coefficient of A = 4.0. Evaluate Portfolio 1 (E(R_1) = 10.0% = 0.10, σ_1 = 15.0% = 0.15) vs. Portfolio 2 (E(R_2) = 13.0% = 0.13, σ_2 = 22.0% = 0.22). Also find the certainty-equivalent risk-free rate for Portfolio 1.

Formula Expression
U = E(R) − (0.5 × A × σ^2)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Using decimals: Utility U_1 = 0.10 − (0.5 × 4.0 × 0.15^2) = 0.10 − (2.0 × 0.0225) = 0.10 − 0.0450 = 0.0550 (5.50%). Utility U_2 = 0.13 − (0.5 × 4.0 × 0.22^2) = 0.13 − (2.0 × 0.0484) = 0.13 − 0.0968 = 0.0332 (3.32%). For a risk-free asset (σ = 0), U_RF = R_F, so the investor is indifferent between Portfolio 1 and a guaranteed 5.50% risk-free return.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Always convert E(R) and σ into decimals (0.10 and 0.15) before squaring σ in U = E(R) − 0.5×A×σ^2!

Worked Example 2: Constructing a Leveraged Position on the Capital Market Line (CML)

Problem & Given Scenario: The risk-free rate is R_F = 4.0%, and the Market Portfolio M has E(R_M) = 10.0% and σ_M = 15.0%. An investor borrows 40% of their initial equity at the 4.0% risk-free rate to invest w_M = 140% (1.40) in the Market Portfolio (and w_RF = −0.40).

Formula Expression
E(R_C) = R_F + [(E(R_P) − R_F) / σ_P] × σ_C

Slope = Sharpe Ratio of Risky Portfolio P

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Expected Return E(R_P) = (−0.40 × 4.0%) + (1.40 × 10.0%) = −1.60% + 14.00% = 12.40% (or 4.0% + 1.40×6.0% = 12.40%). Portfolio Standard Deviation σ_P = |w_M| × σ_M = 1.40 × 15.0% = 21.00%. Portfolio Sharpe Ratio = (12.40% − 4.0%) / 21.00% = 8.40% / 21.00% = 0.40 (identical to Market Sharpe 6.0% / 15.0% = 0.40).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Points to the left of M on the CML (0 ≤ w_M < 1) represent lending portfolios; points to the right of M (w_M > 1) represent leveraged borrowing portfolios.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: An investor with a risk aversion coefficient of A = 3.0 evaluates a portfolio with an expected return of 11.0% (0.11) and a standard deviation of 20.0% (0.20). The investor's utility score (in decimal/percentage terms) is:

  • [A] 5.00%

  • [B] 8.00%

  • [C] 17.00%

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Using decimals: U = E(R) − 0.5 × A × σ^2 = 0.11 − (0.5 × 3.0 × 0.20^2) = 0.11 − (1.5 × 0.04) = 0.11 − 0.06 = 0.0500 = 5.00%.

Practice Question 2

Question: In an Investment Policy Statement (IPS), the five standard investment constraints (often remembered by the acronym LLTTU) are:

  • [A] Leverage, Liquidity, Turnover, Tracking Error, and Underwriting

  • [B] Liquidity, Legal and Regulatory, Time Horizon, Tax Concerns, and Unique Circumstances

  • [C] Liability, Longevity, Timing, Taxes, and Utility

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: The five IPS constraints are Liquidity requirements, Legal/Regulatory factors, Time horizon, Tax concerns, and Unique circumstances (such as ESG exclusions).

Practice Question 3

Question: For a risk-neutral investor (A = 0), how are indifference curves plotted on an Expected Return (vertical axis) vs. Standard Deviation (horizontal axis) graph?

  • [A] Upward-sloping convex curves

  • [B] Flat horizontal straight lines, because utility depends solely on Expected Return regardless of volatility

  • [C] Downward-sloping curves

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: When A = 0, U = E(R) − 0 = E(R), so the investor cares only about expected return and is indifferent to standard deviation, producing horizontal indifference lines.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Investor Utility Function & Indifference Curve.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Capital Allocation Line (CAL) Expected Return & Volatility.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
