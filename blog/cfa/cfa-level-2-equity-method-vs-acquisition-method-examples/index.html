Introduction

Equity Method vs Acquisition Method: Accounting Examples covers comparing the Equity Method (20%–50% significant influence) and Acquisition Method (>50% control) across Revenue, Net Income, Assets, Equity, and financial ratios (Net Margin, ROE, Leverage) in Level II Financial Statement Analysis (LM 1: Intercorporate Investments — Equity method (one-line consolidation, excess purchase price amortization, upstream/downstream unrealized profit) vs Acquisition method (100% consolidation + Non-Controlling Interest), ratio comparisons), with verified formulas, two worked examples, and three practice MCQs.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

Equity Method Investment Carrying Value Roll-Forward (Ending Investment = Initial Cost + %×(Associate Net Income) − %×(Dividends Received) − %×(Amortization of Fair Value > Book Value) − %×(Unrealized Inventory Profit)) and Acquisition Method vs. Equity Method Financial Ratio Invariance & Divergence (Net Income_Parent = IDENTICAL under both ; Equity_Parent (excl NCI) = IDENTICAL ; Revenue, Total Assets & Total Liabilities = HIGHER under Acquisition Consolidation) govern comparing the Equity Method (20%–50% significant influence) and Acquisition Method (>50% control) across Revenue, Net Income, Assets, Equity, and financial ratios (Net Margin, ROE, Leverage).

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Equity Method Investment Carrying Value Roll-Forward

Formula Expression
Ending Investment = Initial Cost + %×(Associate Net Income) − %×(Dividends Received) − %×(Amortization of Fair Value > Book Value) − %×(Unrealized Inventory Profit)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Dividends received from an associate are a return of capital that REDUCES the balance sheet investment account and NEVER enters the investor's income statement!.

Formula 2: Acquisition Method vs. Equity Method Financial Ratio Invariance & Divergence

Formula Expression
Net Income_Parent = IDENTICAL under both

Equity_Parent (excl NCI) = IDENTICAL

Revenue, Total Assets & Total Liabilities = HIGHER under Acquisition Consolidation

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Because Net Income is identical while Consolidated Revenue and Assets are larger, the Equity Method always reports a HIGHER Net Profit Margin and HIGHER ROE/ROA than Consolidation!.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Full Equity Method Calculation with Fair Value PP&E Step-Up and Upstream Inventory Profit

Problem & Given Scenario: On Jan 1, Company P buys 30% of Company S for $180M when S's Book Value of Net Assets is $500M (so 30% × $500M = $150M book share). The $30M excess purchase price is attributable to $20M of undervalued Equipment (10-year remaining life -> $2.0M/yr extra depreciation) and $10M of Goodwill. During the year, S reports Net Income = $80M, pays Total Cash Dividends = $30M, and sells inventory to P that includes $10M of unrealized after-tax profit still sitting in P's year-end warehouse.

Formula Expression
Ending Investment = Initial Cost + %×(Associate Net Income) − %×(Dividends Received) − %×(Amortization of Fair Value > Book Value) − %×(Unrealized Inventory Profit)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: Base Share of S's Net Income = 30% × $80M = $24.0M. Step 2: Deduct PP&E Fair Value Excess Amortization = −$2.0M ($20M / 10 yrs). Step 3: Deduct 30% of Unrealized Upstream Inventory Profit = −30% × $10M = −$3.0M. Step 4: Net Equity Income recognized on P's Income Statement = $24.0M − $2.0M − $3.0M = +$19.0M. Step 5: Cash Dividends Received = 30% × $30M = $9.0M (reduces Investment account; enters CFO). Step 6: Ending Balance Sheet Investment in S = $180.0M + $19.0M − $9.0M = $190.0M.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Under the Equity Method, both upstream (S to P) and downstream (P to S) unrealized intercompany profits are eliminated proportionally (30%) against equity income.

Worked Example 2: Comparing Financial Ratios Under Equity Method vs. Acquisition Consolidation

Problem & Given Scenario: Suppose Parent P (standalone Revenue = $1,000M, NI = $100M, Assets = $800M, Equity = $500M) holds a 50% stake in Joint Venture V (Revenue = $400M, NI = $40M, Assets = $300M, Liabilities = $100M, Equity = $200M, purchased at book value $100M). Compare Net Profit Margin, ROA, and ROE under (A) Equity Method vs. (B) Acquisition Consolidation.

Formula Expression
Net Income_Parent = IDENTICAL under both

Equity_Parent (excl NCI) = IDENTICAL

Revenue, Total Assets & Total Liabilities = HIGHER under Acquisition Consolidation

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

  • Step 1. Equity Method: Revenue = $1,000M (excludes V's revenue!); Net Income = $100M + 50%($40M) = $120M; Total Assets = $800M (already includes $100M investment); Equity = $500M -> Net Margin = $120M / $1,000M = 12.00%; ROA = $120M / $800M = 15.00%; ROE = $120M / $500M = 24.00%.

  • Step 2. Acquisition Consolidation: Revenue = $1,000M + $400M = $1,400M; Net Income Attributable to P = $120M (Total NI = $140M including $20M NCI); Total Assets = $800M − $100M + $300M = $1,000M; Total Equity (including $100M NCI) = $600M -> Net Margin (P) = $120M / $1,400M = 8.57%; ROA = $140M / $1,000M = 14.00%; Total ROE = $140M / $600M = 23.33%.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Memorize this Level II rule: Equity Method gives the SAME Parent Net Income and Parent Equity as Consolidation, but LOWER Revenue, LOWER Assets, and LOWER Liabilities—meaning Equity Method reports HIGHER Net Margin, HIGHER Asset Turnover, HIGHER ROA, and LOWER Leverage!

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: Company A owns 25% of Company B and accounts for the investment using the equity method. During 2026, Company B reports Net Income of $60 million and pays total cash dividends of $20 million. Assuming no purchase price excess amortization or intercompany transactions, how do these events affect Company A's 2026 Income Statement and Balance Sheet carrying value?

  • [A] Income statement recognizes $20M; Balance sheet increases by $10M

  • [B] Income statement recognizes $15M (25% × $60M); Balance sheet carrying value increases by $10M ($15M income minus $5M dividends received)

  • [C] Income statement recognizes $15M; Balance sheet increases by $15M

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Under the equity method, A recognizes 25% × $60M = $15M on its income statement, while the 25% × $20M = $5M dividend reduces the investment account, for a net balance sheet increase of +$10M.

Practice Question 2

Question: Compared to the Acquisition (consolidation) method, accounting for a profitable joint venture using the Equity Method will result in:

  • [A] Higher reported Total Revenue and higher Total Assets

  • [B] Identical Net Income attributable to parent shareholders, but higher Net Profit Margin and higher Return on Assets (ROA)

  • [C] Higher reported Debt-to-Equity ratio

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: The Equity Method reports the same parent Net Income over smaller Revenue (parent only) and smaller Total Assets (net investment only), resulting in higher Net Profit Margin and higher ROA.

Practice Question 3

Question: Under the Equity Method, when is an impairment loss recognized, and can an Equity Method impairment loss subsequently be reversed under IFRS or US GAAP?

  • [A] Recognized when fair value falls below carrying value permanently (other-than-temporary); subsequent reversal of an equity method impairment is prohibited under US GAAP

  • [B] Never recognized

  • [C] Reversed through OCI every quarter

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: Under US GAAP, if the fair value of an equity method investment drops below carrying value on an other-than-temporary basis, it is written down on the income statement and reversals are prohibited.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
