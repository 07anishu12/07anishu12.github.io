Introduction

Purchasing Power Parity: How to Calculate Exchange Rates is a core Level I Economics topic (LM 7 & LM 8: Foreign Exchange Rates — Price/Base quote conventions, real exchange rates, cross rates, forward points, Interest Rate Parity (IRP), and Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)) focusing on Price/Base FX quote notation, real vs nominal exchange rates, cross-rate triangular multiplication, Covered Interest Rate Parity (CIRP), and Purchasing Power Parity. This guide covers the governing equations (Real Exchange Rate & Relative Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) and Covered Interest Rate Parity (No-Arbitrage Forward Rate)), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, Price/Base FX quote notation, real vs nominal exchange rates, cross-rate triangular multiplication, Covered Interest Rate Parity (CIRP), and Purchasing Power Parity links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Economics. Real Exchange Rate & Relative Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) (Real FX Rate (P/B) = S_{P/B} × (CPI_Base / CPI_Price) ; E(S_{1,P/B}) ≈ S_{0,P/B} × [1 + (π_Price − π_Base)]) establishes the primary relationship (Adjusts the nominal spot rate S_{P/B} for relative price levels; under Relative PPP, the currency with higher inflation depreciates by the inflation differential.), while Covered Interest Rate Parity (No-Arbitrage Forward Rate) (F_{P/B} = S_{P/B} × [(1 + r_Price × Days/360) / (1 + r_Base × Days/360)]) governs Locks in the arbitrage-free forward exchange rate: the currency with the higher interest rate always trades at a forward discount.

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: Real Exchange Rate & Relative Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)

Formula Expression
Real FX Rate (P/B) = S_{P/B} × (CPI_Base / CPI_Price)

E(S_{1,P/B}) ≈ S_{0,P/B} × [1 + (π_Price − π_Base)]

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:

  • Adjusts the nominal spot rate S_{P/B} for relative price levels.

  • under Relative PPP, the currency with higher inflation depreciates by the inflation differential.

Formula 2: Covered Interest Rate Parity (No-Arbitrage Forward Rate)

Formula Expression
F_{P/B} = S_{P/B} × [(1 + r_Price × Days/360) / (1 + r_Base × Days/360)]

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Locks in the arbitrage-free forward exchange rate: the currency with the higher interest rate always trades at a forward discount.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Calculating the Real Exchange Rate and PPP-Implied Currency Depreciation

Problem & Given Scenario: The USD/EUR spot rate (Price = USD, Base = EUR) is S_0 = 1.0800 USD per EUR. Over the next year, US inflation is expected to be π_USD = 4.50% and Eurozone inflation is expected to be π_EUR = 2.00%. Compute (1) the PPP-expected spot rate in 1 year, and (2) the Real USD/EUR exchange rate if the actual nominal spot rate instead stays frozen at 1.0800.

Formula Expression
Real FX Rate (P/B) = S_{P/B} × (CPI_Base / CPI_Price)

E(S_{1,P/B}) ≈ S_{0,P/B} × [1 + (π_Price − π_Base)]

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1 (Relative PPP Expected Spot Rate): E(S_1) = 1.0800 × (1.045 / 1.020) = 1.0800 × 1.02451 = 1.1065 USD/EUR (EUR appreciates +2.45% and high-inflation USD depreciates). Step 2 (Real Exchange Rate if S_1 stays 1.0800): Real Rate = 1.0800 × (102.0 / 104.5) = 1.0542 USD/EUR (real depreciation of the EUR by 2.39%, making Eurozone exports more competitive).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Always keep Price currency in the numerator and Base currency in the denominator for both PPP and IRP!

Worked Example 2: Calculating Cross Rates and Covered Interest Rate Parity Forward Points

Problem & Given Scenario: Spot JPY/USD = 150.00 and Spot USD/GBP = 1.2600. 1-year interest rates (Actual/360, 360 days) are r_JPY = 0.50% and r_USD = 4.50%. Calculate (1) the JPY/GBP cross rate, and (2) the 1-year JPY/USD forward rate and forward points.

Formula Expression
F_{P/B} = S_{P/B} × [(1 + r_Price × Days/360) / (1 + r_Base × Days/360)]

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1 (Cross Rate JPY/GBP): (JPY / USD) × (USD / GBP) = 150.00 × 1.2600 = 189.00 JPY per GBP. Step 2 (1-Year Forward JPY/USD, where Price = JPY, Base = USD): F_{JPY/USD} = 150.00 × (1 + 0.0050) / (1 + 0.0450) = 150.00 × (1.0050 / 1.0450) = 144.2584 ≈ 144.26 JPY/USD. Forward Points = F − S = 144.26 − 150.00 = −5.74 JPY (Base currency USD trades at a forward discount because r_USD > r_JPY).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Because USD has the higher interest rate (4.50% > 0.50%), USD must trade at a forward discount (−5.74 JPY) to prevent covered interest arbitrage.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: Using CFA Institute Price/Base notation, the spot exchange rate is 1.3500 CAD/USD (Price = CAD, Base = USD). If the 1-year risk-free rate is 3.0% in Canada and 5.0% in the United States, the 1-year arbitrage-free forward rate (CAD/USD) is closest to:

  • [A] 1.3243 CAD/USD

  • [B] 1.3500 CAD/USD

  • [C] 1.3762 CAD/USD

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: F_{CAD/USD} = S_{CAD/USD} × (1 + r_CAD) / (1 + r_USD) = 1.3500 × (1.03 / 1.05) = 1.324286 ≈ 1.3243 CAD/USD.

Practice Question 2

Question: Given spot quotes of USD/EUR = 1.0800 and CHF/USD = 0.8800, the CHF/EUR cross exchange rate is closest to:

  • [A] 0.8148 CHF/EUR

  • [B] 0.9504 CHF/EUR

  • [C] 1.2273 CHF/EUR

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Multiply so USD cancels: (CHF / USD) × (USD / EUR) = 0.8800 × 1.0800 = 0.9504 CHF/EUR.

Practice Question 3

Question: Under the Marshall-Lerner condition, a depreciation of the domestic currency will improve a country's trade balance (assuming initial trade balance = 0) if and only if:

  • [A] The sum of the price elasticities of demand for exports and imports exceeds 1.0 (ε_X + ε_M > 1)

  • [B] Export and import demand are completely inelastic

  • [C] Domestic inflation exceeds foreign inflation

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: When export and import demand are sufficiently elastic (ε_X + ε_M > 1), the volume surge in exports and drop in import volume outweigh the higher domestic cost per unit imported.

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in Real Exchange Rate & Relative Purchasing Power Parity (PPP).

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in Covered Interest Rate Parity (No-Arbitrage Forward Rate).

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
