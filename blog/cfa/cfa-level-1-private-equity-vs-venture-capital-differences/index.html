Introduction

Private Equity vs Venture Capital: Differences and Examples is a core Level I Alternative Investments topic (LM 3: Investments in Private Capital: Equity and Debt — Venture capital stages (pre-seed, seed, early/Series A, later/growth), LBO buyout mechanics, exit strategies, and private debt (direct lending, mezzanine, distressed)) focusing on comparing Venture Capital startup financing stages (pre-seed to late stage) against Leveraged Buyouts (LBOs), private debt strategies, J-curve cash flows, and exit routes. This guide covers the governing equations (LBO Equity Multiple (MOIC) & IRR Relationship and PE Fund Multiples: PIC, DPI, RVPI, and TVPI), two verified numerical examples, a comparison table, and three original CFA-style practice questions.

Concept Explanation & Institutional Framework

From first principles, comparing Venture Capital startup financing stages (pre-seed to late stage) against Leveraged Buyouts (LBOs), private debt strategies, J-curve cash flows, and exit routes links theoretical valuation and risk mechanics to practical decisions in Alternative Investments. LBO Equity Multiple (MOIC) & IRR Relationship (MOIC (Multiple on Invested Capital) = Total Exit Equity Proceeds / Initial Sponsor Equity ; IRR = (MOIC)^(1 / Holding Years) − 1 (if zero interim dividends)) establishes the primary relationship (Measures total cash-on-cash multiple and annualized compound return in a leveraged buyout or VC exit.), while PE Fund Multiples: PIC, DPI, RVPI, and TVPI (DPI = Cumulative Distributions / Paid-In Capital ; RVPI = Residual NAV / Paid-In ; TVPI = DPI + RVPI) governs DPI measures realized cash-on-cash return; RVPI measures unrealized paper value; TVPI is Total Value to Paid-In Capital.

Core Formulas & Mathematical Equations

Note: The following analytical equations govern problem-solving and numerical questions across this curriculum reading.

Formula 1: LBO Equity Multiple (MOIC) & IRR Relationship

Formula Expression
MOIC (Multiple on Invested Capital) = Total Exit Equity Proceeds / Initial Sponsor Equity

IRR = (MOIC)^(1 / Holding Years) − 1 (if zero interim dividends)

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown: Measures total cash-on-cash multiple and annualized compound return in a leveraged buyout or VC exit.

Formula 2: PE Fund Multiples: PIC, DPI, RVPI, and TVPI

Formula Expression
DPI = Cumulative Distributions / Paid-In Capital

RVPI = Residual NAV / Paid-In

TVPI = DPI + RVPI

Institutional Context & Variable Breakdown:

  • DPI measures realized cash-on-cash return.

  • RVPI measures unrealized paper value.

  • TVPI is Total Value to Paid-In Capital.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Calculating LBO Sponsor Equity Return (MOIC and IRR) with Debt Paydown and EBITDA Growth

Problem & Given Scenario: A Private Equity buyout firm acquires a mature industrial company for an Enterprise Value of $500M (10.0x Year-0 EBITDA of $50M), financed with $350M of Debt (70%) and $150M of Sponsor Equity (30%). Over 5 years, EBITDA grows to $75M, and the company's free cash flow pays down $150M of debt (leaving $200M Ending Debt). At Year 5, the PE firm exits at the same 10.0x EV/EBITDA multiple.

Formula Expression
MOIC (Multiple on Invested Capital) = Total Exit Equity Proceeds / Initial Sponsor Equity

IRR = (MOIC)^(1 / Holding Years) − 1 (if zero interim dividends)

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: Exit Enterprise Value at Year 5 = 10.0 × $75M = $750M. Step 2: Subtract remaining Debt ($200M) to find Exit Equity Value = $750M − $200M = $550M. Step 3: Sponsor MOIC = $550M / $150M = 3.67x cash-on-cash. Step 4: 5-Year Annualized Gross IRR = (550 / 150)^(1/5) − 1 = (3.6667)^0.20 − 1 = 29.67%.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Notice the two engines of LBO equity value creation (+$400M gain on $150M equity): +$250M came from EBITDA growth ($50M -> $75M @ 10x) and +$150M came from debt paydown ($350M -> $200M) with zero multiple expansion!

Worked Example 2: Evaluating VC Portfolio Power-Law Returns vs. LBO Loss Rates

Problem & Given Scenario: A $100M early-stage Venture Capital fund invests $10M each into 10 Series A startups. Over 7 years: 6 startups fail completely ($0), 3 return 1.5x ($45M total), and 1 'home run' unicorn returns 20.0x ($200M).

Formula Expression
DPI = Cumulative Distributions / Paid-In Capital

RVPI = Residual NAV / Paid-In

TVPI = DPI + RVPI

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Total Fund Exit Value = $0 + $45M + $200M = $245M (TVPI = 2.45x; 7-Year Gross IRR = 2.45^(1/7) − 1 = 13.65%).

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: In Venture Capital, returns follow an extreme right-skewed power law (1 startup generated 81.6% of the fund's return), whereas LBOs rely on steady cash-flowing companies with low failure rates.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: At which stage of the Venture Capital financing lifecycle is capital typically provided to a newly formed company that has a developed prototype and business plan and is beginning initial commercial production and sales (often called Series A)?

  • [A] Pre-seed (angel) stage

  • [B] Early-stage (start-up / Series A) financing

  • [C] Mezzanine / pre-IPO stage

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Pre-seed/seed funds idea validation and product development; Early-stage (start-up/Series A) funds initial commercial production and early sales; Later-stage funds expansion of an already revenue-generating company.

Practice Question 2

Question: Which private equity exit strategy typically achieves the highest valuation multiple due to strategic operating synergies and provides an immediate 100% cash exit without public lock-up periods?

  • [A] Trade sale to a strategic corporate acquirer

  • [B] Initial Public Offering (IPO)

  • [C] Management liquidation

Tip: Correct Answer: Option A.

Explanation: A trade sale to a strategic buyer in the same industry often commands a synergy premium and provides immediate liquidity without IPO underwriting fees or 180-day insider lock-ups.

Practice Question 3

Question: A Private Equity fund has $200 million inCommitted Capital, of which $160 million has been called (Paid-In Capital). To date, the fund has distributed $120 million in cash to LPs, and the remaining portfolio has a Net Asset Value (NAV) of $140 million. The fund's Total Value to Paid-In (TVPI) multiple is:

  • [A] 1.30x

  • [B] 1.625x

  • [C] 1.75x

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: DPI = $120M / $160M = 0.75x; RVPI = $140M / $160M = 0.875x; TVPI = DPI + RVPI = ($120M + $140M) / $160M = $260M / $160M = 1.625x. (Dividing by $200M committed instead of $160M paid-in is the trap answer 1.30x).

Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Periodicity Mismatch: Failing to align compounding frequency in LBO Equity Multiple (MOIC) & IRR Relationship.

  • Timing Errors: Mixing beginning and ending balances in PE Fund Multiples: PIC, DPI, RVPI, and TVPI.

  • Distractor Traps: Stopping at an unadjusted intermediate step.
