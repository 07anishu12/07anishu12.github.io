Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Eliminating Numerical Distractors Without Full Calculation Using Economic Bounds

Problem & Given Scenario: Consider this Fixed Income question: 'A 6-year, 5.0% annual coupon bond ($100 par) trades at a flat price of $94.20. Its Yield to Maturity is closest to: (A) 4.75% ; (B) 5.00% ; (C) 6.20%.' Solve in 5 seconds without touching your calculator.

Formula Expression
Option A = Exact Correct Formula

Option B = Common 1-Step Omission Trap (e.g., forgetting ×(1−t) or ×(1+g) or ×0.5)

Option C = Sign-Flip or Inverse Ratio Trap

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

Step 1: The bond's price ($94.20) is LESS than Par ($100.00)—it is a Discount Bond! Step 2: By the strict yield inequality for a Discount Bond, YTM MUST be strictly GREATER than the 5.0% Coupon Rate! Step 3: Eliminate Option A (4.75% < 5.0%) and Eliminate Option B (5.00% = Par yield). Only Option C (6.20% > 5.0%) is economically possible—solved in 5 seconds with zero calculator keystrokes!

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Before pressing a single calculator button on Bond Pricing/YTM, Portfolio Volatility (must be ≤ weighted average σ), or Cross-Rates, check whether economic bounds eliminate 1 or 2 options immediately!

Worked Example 2: Handling 'LEAST Likely' Stems Using the T/F/F Margin Method

Problem & Given Scenario: Demonstrate how to avoid the #1 exam-day reading error on 'Which of the following statements is LEAST likely correct?' questions.

Formula Expression
P(Correct | 3 Options) = 33.33%

P(Correct | Eliminate 1 Option) = 50.00% (+16.67% jump!)

P(Correct | Eliminate 2 Options) = 100.00%

Step-by-Step Substitution & Calculation:

When tired in Hour 4 of the exam, candidates read a 'LEAST likely' stem, spot a true statement in Option A, and click Option A by mistake! To prevent this: Write 'T / F' next to A, B, and C on your scratch pad. On a 'LEAST likely correct' question, you must find TWO True statements (T, T) and ONE False statement (F)—and select the lone 'F'! Furthermore, eliminate any option containing extreme absolutes ('always', 'never', 'guaranteed') unless it states a mathematical identity.

Tip: Financial Interpretation & Exam Takeaway: Writing T / T / F for 'Least Likely' and F / F / T for 'Most Likely' prevents accidental inversion under time pressure.

CFA Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

Practice Question 1

Question: Without performing a full multi-step calculation, a candidate sees a question asking for the standard deviation of a 50/50 portfolio combining Asset A (σ_A = 14%) and Asset B (σ_B = 22%) when their correlation is ρ = +0.20. The three options are: (A) 14.3% ; (B) 18.0% ; (C) 20.5%. How can the candidate identify the correct answer immediately?

  • [A] By picking the middle number

  • [B] At ρ = +1.0, the maximum possible portfolio standard deviation is the weighted average 0.5(14%) + 0.5(22%) = 18.0%. Since ρ = +0.20 < +1.0, diversification guarantees that σ_P MUST be strictly less than 18.0%—eliminating both B (18.0%) and C (20.5%) and leaving A (14.3%) as the only possible answer

  • [C] By averaging the variances

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Whenever correlation ρ < +1.0, portfolio standard deviation is strictly less than the weighted average of individual standard deviations (18.0%), instantly eliminating Options B and C.

Practice Question 2

Question: Why do CFA exam item writers construct numerical distractors (incorrect options) by omitting a single step—such as using D_0 instead of D_1 = D_0(1+g) in the Gordon Growth Model, or omitting the 0.5 multiplier on Convexity?

  • [A] By coincidence

  • [B] To test whether candidates truly understand the complete formula rather than just recognizing a number that appears on their calculator screen mid-calculation

  • [C] To make all options equal

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: Because every distractor corresponds to a common partial or flawed calculation, seeing your calculator's number in the answer choices does NOT guarantee you didn't fall for a trap—always verify the final step!

Practice Question 3

Question: When answering a conceptual CFA question with the stem 'Which of the following actions is LEAST likely a violation of the Code and Standards?', the candidate is looking for:

  • [A] The two options that are violations

  • [B] The ONE option that is compliant (NOT a violation), while the other two options are violations

  • [C] An option that violates local law

Tip: Correct Answer: Option B.

Explanation: 'LEAST likely a violation' is a double-negative stem meaning 'Which action is COMPLIANT (permitted)?'—two options will be violations and one option will be compliant.
