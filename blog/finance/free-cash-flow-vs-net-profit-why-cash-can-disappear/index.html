Understand working capital, capital expenditure and the gap between profit and usable cash.

Net profit records income and expenses under accounting rules; free cash flow estimates cash left after defined operating and investment needs. A profitable company can face a cash shortage when customers pay late, inventory rises, capital expenditure is heavy or debt repayments fall due. Define which free-cash-flow measure you mean before comparing it with profit.

Separate recognition from collection

A company may recognise revenue before collecting the customer's payment, subject to the applicable accounting requirements. The unpaid amount becomes a receivable. Profit therefore need not equal cash received during the same period.

Depreciation moves in the opposite direction: it reduces accounting profit without being a new cash payment in that period. The underlying asset may have been bought earlier or may require future replacement. Adding depreciation back does not mean capital assets never consume cash.

The IFRS Foundation's IAS 7 overview explains adjusting profit for non-cash items and accruals in the indirect cash-flow approach. Apply the entity's actual reporting standards and classification rules when analysing a real company.

Build a cash bridge from profit

Consider a synthetic business with net profit ₹100 lakh and depreciation ₹20 lakh. Receivables rise ₹40 lakh, inventory rises ₹30 lakh and operating payables rise ₹10 lakh. Ignoring other adjustments, operating cash is 100+20-40-30+10, or ₹60 lakh.

If cash capital expenditure is ₹80 lakh, a simple Operating cash flow - Capital expenditure measure gives negative ₹20 lakh. The business is profitable but has invested more cash in operations and assets than it generated under this simplified presentation.

Now add a ₹25 lakh principal repayment. That is a financing cash use rather than an operating expense in the example, and it creates an additional funding need. The business may need opening cash, new finance, reduced spending or better collections to meet obligations.

This illustration omits detailed tax, interest-classification and other accounting effects to isolate the mechanism. It is not a complete reporting template. Identify those items explicitly when moving from the classroom bridge to actual financial statements.

Distinguish the common free-cash-flow definitions

A commonly reported simple FCF measure is operating cash flow less capital expenditure, but definitions vary. FCFF is cash available to capital providers before financing payments, often modelled as after-tax EBIT plus non-cash depreciation less capital spending and additional operating working capital.

FCFE starts from equity economics and includes relevant debt financing effects. Damodaran's cash-flow chapter sets out net income, reinvestment and net borrowing in the equity cash-flow framework. Do not compare FCFF with equity value or FCFE with enterprise value without the appropriate framework.

Read company-defined FCF reconciliations. Some measures exclude acquisitions, leases, restructuring or other items. That can serve a purpose, but the exclusions must be understood. A favourable label does not establish that the entire amount is available for dividends without affecting the business.

Diagnose the reason before judging the business

Negative FCF can reflect growth investment, a temporary timing issue or a deteriorating operation. Investigate whether receivables are collectible, inventory can be sold, and new assets can earn adequate returns. Spending is not value creation merely because management calls it growth capex.

Positive FCF can also be flattered by stretching suppliers, selling receivables, reducing necessary maintenance or releasing inventory that cannot keep falling indefinitely. Examine several periods and compare cash generation with operational conditions. One strong year does not establish sustainable cash conversion.

Build a review table showing profit, non-cash adjustments, working-capital movements, operating cash, capital expenditure and financing obligations. Reconcile it to reported statements and identify unusual items. Ratios such as operating cash divided by profit can help, but are difficult to interpret with low or negative profit and should not replace the bridge.

The practical question is whether the company can fund necessary operations, investment and obligations under plausible downside conditions. Cash quality and timing matter alongside total earnings. No company has been investigated in this guide, and the synthetic examples do not support an investment recommendation.

Quick questions, clear answers

Is positive free cash flow always better than growth spending?

No. A valuable expansion can temporarily reduce FCF, while cutting essential investment can temporarily increase it. Assess expected returns, execution risk and financing capacity. The sign of one cash-flow measure does not establish whether management is creating or destroying value.

Can a profitable company default?

Yes. Debt service and other payments require available cash when due, while accounting profit may be tied up in receivables or assets. Liquidity facilities, maturity schedules and cash forecasts matter. Profitability does not guarantee the ability to meet every near-term obligation.

Does depreciation add-back create cash?

No. It reverses a non-cash accounting expense in a cash-flow reconciliation. Cash generation comes from actual receipts and payments. The business may still need capital expenditure to maintain productive assets, so inspect replacement needs before treating depreciation add-back as surplus cash.
