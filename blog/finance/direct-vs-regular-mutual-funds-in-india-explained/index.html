Direct and regular plans of the same mutual fund scheme generally share the investment portfolio and fund manager, but differ in distribution arrangements and expenses. Direct plans exclude distributor commissions from the plan's expense structure. A lower cost helps returns, but investors still need to choose a suitable scheme and manage their decisions responsibly.

Understand what actually differs

A regular plan is purchased through a distributor or intermediary. Its expense ratio includes the relevant distribution cost. A direct plan is purchased without that distribution arrangement. SEBI's explanation of regular and direct plans identifies this cost distinction while noting that the underlying portfolio remains the same.

The plans have separate NAVs because their expenses differ. A higher NAV does not mean a plan is more expensive in the way that a higher-priced physical product is. The number of units adjusts to the purchase amount. Compare percentage returns, expenses and suitability rather than the rupee level of NAV.

Direct and regular are also separate from growth and income-distribution options. A direct growth option and a regular distribution option differ along two dimensions. Match both plan and option when analysing expenses or historical outcomes.

Put the cost difference into rupees

Consider a hypothetical ₹2,00,000 investment held for ten years. Assume an identical portfolio return of 10% each year before expenses. For a simplified illustration, use an annual net return of 9.5% for the direct plan and 8.5% for the regular plan.

The resulting values are approximately ₹4,95,646 and ₹4,52,197. The difference, about ₹43,449, comes from the assumed one-percentage-point annual return gap compounding over time. These are invented teaching assumptions, not actual plan expense ratios or projected fund returns.

In Excel, calculate =200000*(1+9.5%)^10 and =200000*(1+8.5%)^10. Real expenses accrue through the NAV rather than as a single year-end deduction; actual market returns and expense ratios vary. This model isolates the compounding mechanism without claiming that the annual arithmetic is exact for every fund.

Decide what service you need

A distributor may help with paperwork, transactions, explanations and maintaining an investment routine. Examine the actual service rather than assuming that every regular plan includes comprehensive financial planning. Ask how the distributor is compensated and what assistance continues after purchase.

A direct investor takes responsibility for researching schemes, selecting an allocation, monitoring suitability and avoiding behavioural mistakes. Paid advice can be obtained separately where appropriate. Check the professional's regulatory status and the agreed scope, fees and conflicts; do not assume a platform's advertising label tells the whole story.

AMFI's direct-plan material confirms that the two plans belong to the same scheme and share the portfolio and manager. That makes a same-scheme comparison useful for isolating expenses. Comparing unrelated schemes introduces manager choices, portfolio risk and market exposure into the result.

Check the mechanics before switching

Do not treat a regular-to-direct switch as a costless account-setting change. Establish whether the transaction involves redemption and fresh purchase, which can create an exit load or tax consequence under the rules applicable to the specific holding. Current taxation and scheme conditions require checking before an actual switch.

Collect the folio statement, acquisition dates, plan names, option names and relevant scheme documents. Compare the expected ongoing cost saving with immediate transaction consequences. Small annual savings may take time to recover a meaningful initial cost, and future returns are uncertain.

For new contributions, confirm the final order screen explicitly says the intended plan and option. Save the transaction confirmation. Review nominee details and contact information through the authorised service channel. The cheapest intended plan is of little help if transactions are accidentally placed into another plan or the account cannot be accessed when needed.

Quick questions, clear answers

Does a direct plan hold different shares?

For the same scheme, direct and regular plans share the portfolio. Different NAVs reflect the separate plan expenses. Compare the precise scheme and option names: similarly named products from different fund houses are not the same portfolio and cannot isolate the distribution-cost effect.

Is a regular plan automatically unsuitable?

No. Assess the value of the service received against its continuing cost and your ability to manage independently. Costs matter, but poor allocation or emotionally driven decisions can also be expensive. The appropriate arrangement depends on the investor's needs and actual support provided.

Can I estimate the saving from my statement?

Use the current plan-specific expense ratios and a transparent holding-period assumption. Treat the answer as a scenario because expenses, balances and returns change. Before switching existing units, separately calculate any applicable exit load and tax rather than subtracting only future annual costs.
