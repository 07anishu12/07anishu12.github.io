Read an investment-banking pitchbook as a structured argument for a transaction or advisory mandate. Identify the decision, evidence, assumptions and incentives behind each page. Company profiles and valuation ranges may be useful, but a polished presentation is not independent proof that a deal is attractive or that its forecasts will occur.

Begin with the mandate and proposed decision

Establish who prepared the book, who received it and what it seeks to achieve. A sell-side mandate pitch, financing proposal and acquisition discussion have different purposes. The title page, date, confidentiality marking and disclaimer help define the context but do not replace substantive review.

Look for a clear proposed action: explore a sale, raise capital, acquire a target or appoint an adviser. Ask whether the recommendation follows from the business's needs or mainly from the presenter's available product. A useful reading starts with the decision rather than absorbing every chart as equally important.

CFA Institute's investment-banker profile identifies capital raising, mergers and acquisitions, and pitchbook communication as relevant work. There is no mandatory universal pitchbook sequence; the pages described here are a practical reading framework.

Interpret the company and market pages

A company overview should explain products, customers, geography, revenue drivers and financial history. Check units, reporting periods and the distinction between audited results, management figures and forecasts. A revenue chart without cash flow or leverage can leave important financial risks out of view.

Market pages may describe industry size, competition and growth. Trace material figures to original sources and dates. A global market estimate is not automatically the addressable market for one Indian business, and expected industry growth does not guarantee the company's share or profitability.

Strategy and transaction-rationale pages should connect proposed action to a measurable business need. For an acquisition, separate cost synergies, revenue synergies, integration costs and execution risks. Avoid treating a large synergy total as realised value merely because it appears in a highlighted box.

Rebuild the valuation pages

Trading comparables show how selected listed peers are priced. Precedent transactions show prices in past deals. A DCF estimates value from forecast cash flows. Each method uses different evidence and assumptions, so the resulting ranges should not be averaged without understanding the differences.

In a hypothetical valuation page, EBITDA ₹50 crore at 8×–10× implies enterprise value ₹400–₹500 crore. If net debt is ₹100 crore with no other adjustments, equity value is ₹300–₹400 crore. A reader should be able to rebuild this bridge rather than infer shareholder value directly from the enterprise-value chart.

Check whether multiples use trailing or forecast earnings, and whether target and peers use the same basis. Inspect exclusions, normalisation adjustments, lease treatment and forecast sources. A “valuation football field” is a visual comparison of ranges, not a confidence interval unless statistical evidence explicitly supports that interpretation.

For DCF, inspect revenue, margins, reinvestment, WACC and terminal assumptions. A narrow chart can conceal a highly sensitive model. Ask how the range changes if the operating case disappoints, rather than only examining small changes to the discount rate.

Evaluate process, credentials and conflicts

Process pages should explain proposed stages, information needs, responsibilities and dependencies. An indicative timetable depends on diligence, financing, approvals and counterparties; it is not a guaranteed completion date. Clarify which steps are within the adviser's control.

Credentials pages describe prior work, but the relevance and actual team involvement matter more than the number of logos. Check whether examples match the sector, transaction type and geography. Do not assume a firm's involvement in one historic transaction proves that the proposed team delivered every element.

CFA Institute's communication standard stresses distinguishing facts from opinions and communicating material limitations. Apply the same reading discipline to forecasts, indicative prices and claims about likely buyers or financing availability.

Finish with an issues list: unsupported market numbers, valuation assumptions, missing cash-flow evidence, unresolved risks and incentives requiring explanation. A pitchbook can support a conversation, but investment or board decisions require underlying evidence, appropriate advice and formal approvals. This guide reviews no confidential pitchbook and makes no claim about a specific bank's practices.

Quick questions, clear answers

Is a pitchbook the same as a research report?

No. A pitchbook commonly supports a transaction or advisory discussion, while research has a different purpose and audience. Both require careful source review. Understand who commissioned the work and the presenter's incentives before interpreting recommendations or valuation ranges.

Does a long buyer list mean a sale is likely?

No. A list may identify theoretical candidates rather than parties that have expressed interest or obtained funding. Ask how each candidate fits, what evidence supports likely interest and whether any contact has occurred. Do not treat potential interest as an offer.

Should I trust a valuation range because several methods agree?

Agreement can be informative, but the methods may share the same optimistic earnings forecast or peer assumptions. Examine independence of inputs and downside cases. Multiple charts repeating one assumption do not create multiple independent pieces of evidence.
