Royal Enfield Classic 350 vs Royal Enfield Hunter 350: Engine Specs, Weight & Claimed vs Real Mileage

Evaluating Royal Enfield Classic 350 against Royal Enfield Hunter 350 requires separating official ARAI claims from independent road tests and owner averages. The Royal Enfield Classic 350 uses a 349.34cc engine (20.2 bhp @ 6,100 rpm, 27.0 Nm @ 4,000 rpm) in a 195 kg chassis with a claimed 41.55 km/l.

The Royal Enfield Hunter 350 features a 349.34cc powertrain (20.2 bhp @ 6,100 rpm, 27.0 Nm @ 4,000 rpm) weighing 181 kg and claiming 36.2 km/l. Their 14 kg weight difference and gearing (5-speed vs 5-speed constant mesh) drive their real-world efficiency gap.

City Traffic Tractability, Highway Touring Range and Performance

In urban traffic, the Royal Enfield Classic 350 leverages Counter-balanced J-series long-stroke engine, 19-inch front / 18-inch rear wheel stability, twin downtube spine frame, and relaxed 4,000 rpm torque peak to return 31–35 km/l, while the Royal Enfield Hunter 350 uses 14 kg lighter than Classic 350 (181 kg vs 195 kg), 17-inch front and rear wheels, shorter 1,370mm wheelbase, and brisk urban throttle map to achieve 32–35 km/l.

On open highways, the Royal Enfield Classic 350 reaches 114 km/h and cruises best at 80–95 km/h (36–39 km/l, ~442 km tank range), whereas the Royal Enfield Hunter 350 reaches 114 km/h and cruises cleanly at 80–95 km/h (36–39 km/l, ~455 km tank range).

Conditional Verdict: Which Motorcycle Suits You Best?

Choose the Royal Enfield Classic 350 (₹1,87,434 – ₹2,30,000) if you prefer its 349.34cc torque delivery, 13.0L tank range, and Counter-balanced J-series long-stroke engine, 19-inch front / 18-inch rear wheel stability, twin downtube spine frame, and relaxed 4,000 rpm torque peak. Choose the Royal Enfield Hunter 350 (₹1,49,900 – ₹1,74,655) if your riding benefits more from its 181 kg weight, 33–37 km/l real-world efficiency, and 14 kg lighter than Classic 350 (181 kg vs 195 kg), 17-inch front and rear wheels, shorter 1,370mm wheelbase, and brisk urban throttle map.
