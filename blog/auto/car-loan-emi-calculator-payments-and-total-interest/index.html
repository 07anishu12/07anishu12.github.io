Car-loan EMI depends on the financed amount, periodic interest rate and number of payments. A larger down payment reduces the loan, while a longer tenure usually lowers EMI but increases total interest at the same rate. Compare the all-in borrowing cost as well as the monthly instalment; an affordable-looking EMI can conceal a long repayment commitment.

Use the reducing-balance formula

For equal monthly payments with a constant rate, EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1). Here P is principal, r the monthly interest rate as a decimal, and n the number of monthly payments. If the quoted nominal annual rate is 10%, use r = 0.10/12 under the monthly-rate assumption.

The formula assumes payments at the end of each month and no irregular first period, fees financed separately, rate resets or balloon balance. Real repayment schedules can differ. At a zero interest rate, the mathematical special case is P/n rather than the expression's zero-over-zero form.

In Excel, the equivalent illustrative formula is =-PMT(10%/12,60,800000). The minus sign expresses the payment as a positive outgoing amount in this presentation. Keep percentage and period units consistent; entering an annual rate for monthly payments produces the wrong result.

Work through a hypothetical car loan

Assume an ₹8,00,000 loan at a constant nominal annual rate of 10% over 60 months. The calculated EMI is approximately ₹16,997.64. Total scheduled repayments are about ₹10,19,858.15 and interest about ₹2,19,858.15 before fees and any lender rounding.

These figures are mathematical illustrations, not a current lender offer. The first month's interest under this assumption is ₹8,00,000 × 10%/12 = ₹6,666.67. About ₹10,330.97 of the first instalment reduces principal. Later interest falls as the outstanding balance declines.

This explains reducing balance: interest is calculated on the remaining principal, not the original amount forever. A flat-rate quote applies a different method and cannot be compared with the same numerical reducing-balance percentage without converting the cash flows.

See what changing tenure actually does

Keep principal at ₹8,00,000 and nominal annual rate at 10%, but extend the illustrative tenure to 84 months. EMI falls to approximately ₹13,280.95. Total interest rises to about ₹3,15,599.57, around ₹95,741.42 more than the 60-month example.

The lower EMI can help cash flow, but it does not make the loan cheaper. It also keeps the repayment obligation in place longer, potentially overlapping with rising maintenance costs or a desire to replace the car. Consider whether you can afford the shorter term without draining essential savings.

Do not assume a future refinance or large salary increase will solve an uncomfortable loan. Test the repayment against current dependable income and other commitments. Build a contingency for income disruption before treating the maximum sanctioned amount as your spending target.

Use down payment without exhausting savings

Reduce the hypothetical principal to ₹6,00,000 at the same 10% rate over 60 months. EMI becomes about ₹12,748.23 and interest about ₹1,64,893.61. Compared with borrowing ₹8 lakh, the additional ₹2 lakh upfront reduces interest by approximately ₹54,964.54.

That saving is useful, but using every rupee of accessible savings can create a different risk. Retain a practical emergency reserve and funds for insurance, registration or other charges not financed. Separate the desired car price from the amount a lender will actually fund.

A dealer's advertised down payment may exclude fees, insurance or accessories. Ask for the precise initial cash requirement and a full amortisation schedule. A balloon or residual payment must be included in the comparison rather than hidden behind a small monthly number.

Compare APR, fees and repayment terms

RBI's annual-report discussion explains Key Facts Statements and communication of all-in loan costs. Request the applicable KFS and compare APR, processing charges, taxes on fees, late-payment terms and any prepayment or foreclosure conditions.

APR incorporates relevant borrowing costs under its stated calculation and may differ from the nominal rate used in the simple EMI formula. Keep loan-only costs separate from optional products, and ask whether any insurance or service package has been added to principal.

Before signing, reconcile sanctioned amount, net disbursal, down payment, number of instalments and total repayment. Use the lender's final schedule for the contract and the calculator to understand it. Educational arithmetic cannot establish approval or the rate offered to a particular borrower.

Quick questions, clear answers

Is a flat 5% rate cheaper than reducing 10%?

The percentages use different methods, so the numbers alone do not answer that. Compare actual disbursal and repayment cash flows, fees and the disclosed APR. Ask for written schedules rather than relying on a dealer's verbal comparison.

Does paying extra automatically reduce tenure?

That depends on the lender's terms and how the payment is applied. Confirm permitted prepayment, charges and whether EMI or tenure changes. Obtain an updated schedule after a payment; do not assume an extra transfer necessarily reduces principal as intended.

Can the calculator predict approval?

No. It calculates payments for assumed inputs. Approval and pricing depend on lender policy, income, obligations, credit profile and documentation. Obtain a formal offer and check the actual conditions before committing to a car purchase based on estimated finance.
