1. Introduction

In one of the most sensational surprises of EICMA 2026, Honda has officially unveiled the all-new Honda CBR500R FOUR, reviving the golden era of high-revving, small-displacement inline-four supersport machinery. In an era where competing manufacturers have largely abandoned multi-cylinder complexity in favor of utilitarian parallel-twins, Honda made a bold, enthusiast-driven declaration by engineering a cleansheet 500cc inline-four screamer. Co-existing alongside the sensible twin-cylinder CBR500R, the new CBR500R FOUR is developed for purists who crave spine-tingling acoustic theatre, an astronomical 15,000 rpm redline, and race-bred supersport ergonomics. Equipped with Honda's revolutionary electronic E-Clutch technology and track-grade Showa suspension, the CBR500R FOUR redefines middleweight performance.

2. Launch and Availability

The Honda CBR500R FOUR celebrated its official world premiere at EICMA 2026 on November 3, 2026, at the Honda exhibition stand in Milan. Honda executives confirmed that the motorcycle has concluded final development testing and will enter commercial production at Honda's Kumamoto factory in Japan during early 2027. European showroom rollouts are scheduled for June 2027, offered in both unrestricted and A2-licencerestricted formats. For the Indian market, Honda Motorcycle and Scooter India (HMSI) is evaluating a limited CBU/CKD allocation through BigWing Topline showrooms for late 2027 or early 2028.

3. Engine and Performance

The crown jewel of the CBR500R FOUR is its all-new 499cc, liquid-cooled, DOHC, 16-valve inline-four engine. Featuring a short-stroke bore and stroke ratio, ultra-lightweight forged aluminum pistons, and finger-follower valvetrain actuation, the engine revs freely to an exhilarating 15,200 rpm redline. In unrestricted international trim, the powerplant produces a thrilling 78 hp (57.4 kW) at 13,500 rpm and 48 Nm of peak torque at 10,500 rpm. For European A2 licensing, an electronically restricted 47.6 hp (35 kW) variant will be offered that can be seamlessly uncorked once riders obtain full certification. The motorcycle is paired with a six-speed transmission equipped with Honda's breakthrough E-Clutch system, allowing clutchless starts, stops, and gearshifts without ever touching the handlebar clutch lever.

4. Design and hardware

Visually, the CBR500R FOUR borrows aggressive aerodynamic cues directly from the CBR600RR and CBR1000RR-R Fireblade. The full front fairing integrates functional aerodynamic winglets that provide highspeed stability through fast bends. Twin LED projector headlights frame a central high-pressure ram-air intake that boosts manifold pressure at top speed. The chassis features a lightweight aluminum twin-spar perimeter frame paired with an aluminum swingarm, keeping total kerb weight down to an agile 182 kg. Suspension is commanded by 41mm Showa Separate Function Fork-Big Piston (SFF-BP) inverted forks, while braking is handled by dual 296mm floating discs with radial-mount Nissin calipers.

5. Features and technology

The CBR500R FOUR is outfitted with Honda's latest generation electronics. The standard Honda E-Clutch system uses two electronic actuators inside the clutch cover, giving riders the choice of operating the manual clutch lever traditionally or shifting gear levers seamlessly without the clutch. A 5.0-inch full-colour TFT display offers Honda RoadSync smartphone connectivity, turn-by-turn navigation, and lap-timing telemetry. The electronic package, governed by a multi-axis IMU, includes 3-level Honda Selectable Torque Control (HSTC), Cornering ABS, and three riding modes: Sport, Standard, and Rain.

6. Expected price and market positioning

Given its complex four-cylinder engineering and standard E-Clutch technology, the CBR500R FOUR will sit at a premium compared to twin-cylinder middleweights. In European showrooms, retail pricing is expected to sit between €8,500 and €9,200. In India, if introduced via the CKD route, the anticipated ex-showroom price is estimated between Rs 6.50 lakh and Rs 7.20 lakh. Honda will position the machine as an aspirational, highperformance icon for passionate sportbike purists.

If local assembly can be secured at the Manesar plant, an Indian market debut in late 2027 or early 2028 is commercially feasible.

8. Rivals and alternatives

The Honda CBR500R FOUR occupies a distinct niche, with its most immediate rival being the Kawasaki Ninja ZX-4R / ZX-4RR. Compared to the Ninja ZX-4R, the Honda offers a larger 499cc displacement, stronger lowto-midrange torque, standard Honda E-Clutch convenience, and superior everyday road comfort, while delivering the same spine-tingling four-cylinder acoustic rush.

9. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How many cylinders does the Honda CBR500R FOUR have?

It is powered by an all-new 499cc, liquid-cooled, 16-valve inline-four engine capable of revving to 15,200 rpm.

Q: How much horsepower does the CBR500R FOUR produce?

In full unrestricted specification, it generates 78 hp at 13,500 rpm, alongside an A2-compliant 47.6 hp restricted version.

Q: Does it come with Honda E-Clutch as standard?

Yes, the motorcycle features Honda's breakthrough E-Clutch system as standard equipment, allowing effortless clutchless riding.

Q: Will the CBR500R FOUR launch in India?

Launch status for India is possible but unconfirmed, with potential CKD introduction being evaluated by HMSI for late 2027.

10. Conclusion

The Honda CBR500R FOUR unveiled at EICMA 2026 is a triumphant celebration of motorcycling passion. In an era dominated by homogenized parallel-twins, Honda's decision to engineer an authentic 15,000-rpm inlinefour supersport with E-Clutch technology is an extraordinary gift to enthusiasts. It stands as a modern masterpiece of mechanical soul, acoustic drama, and accessible track capability.
