Under ₹15 lakh on-road in India (including the full battery outright), the two standout all-round family EVs are the MG Windsor EV Excite / Exclusive (38 kWh battery, ~260–280 km real range, and class-leading rear lounge legroom) and the Tata Punch EV Long Range Adventure / Empowered (35 kWh battery, ~265–290 km real range, 190 mm ground clearance, and 5-star Bharat NCAP safety). If you want an affordable second car strictly for city errands under ₹9.5 lakh, pick the Tata Tiago EV Long Range (24 kWh, ~180–200 km real range) or MG Comet EV.

Rule #1 When Shopping EVs Under ₹15 Lakh: Always Buy the 'Long Range' Battery

To advertise low starting prices, automakers offer two battery sizes in models like the Tiago EV and Punch EV: a smaller Medium Range (19.2–25 kWh) pack and a larger Long Range (24–35 kWh) pack.

Because healthy EV battery practice means keeping a 15%–20% reserve buffer (never planning to drive down to 2% on the road), a 25 kWh battery with a 190 km real range only gives you ~150 km of stress-free usable range between charges.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Why do all EVs in this segment use LFP (Lithium Iron Phosphate) battery chemistry?

LFP batteries are ideally suited to India's 45°C summer temperatures because they have a much higher thermal-runaway safety threshold than NMC (Nickel Manganese Cobalt) batteries and can be charged regularly to 100% State of Charge (which is actually recommended once a week on LFP packs to keep the BMS cell-balancing calibrated) with minimal cycle degradation.

Q2. Should I buy the Tata Nexon EV Medium Range (30 kWh) or Tata Punch EV Long Range (35 kWh) for ₹13.5 lakh?

Even though the Nexon EV has a slightly wider body shell, the Punch EV Long Range (35 kWh) is the smarter EV buy at ₹13.5 lakh: it sits on Tata's newer acti.ev platform, carries a 5 kWh larger battery that delivers 55–60 km more real-world range, and charges faster.

Q3. Why shouldn't I buy the Citroen eC3 if I take highway trips or live in a hot city?

While the Citroen eC3 has fantastic suspension comfort, its 29.2 kWh battery uses air cooling rather than active liquid cooling. In 42°C Indian summers or during back-to-back DC fast charging, liquid-cooled batteries (used in the Tiago EV, Punch EV, Nexon EV, and Windsor EV) maintain battery health and fast-charging speeds much better.

Q4. Does the MG Windsor EV's glass roof or huge touchscreen have physical buttons for basic controls?

In the MG Windsor EV Exclusive/Essence, several functions—including wing-mirror adjustment and headlamp controls—are integrated into the 15.6-inch touchscreen menu, though physical HVAC shortcut switches remain below the screen. Test the ergonomics during a showroom drive.

Q5. Do I need a 7.2 kW fast wallbox charger at home for a 24–38 kWh EV?

Not at all! Unless you drain the entire 35–38 kWh battery from 10% to 100% every single day (260+ km/day), the standard 3.3 kW portable/wall charger plugged into a normal 15-Amp geyser/AC socket adds ~18–20 km of range per hour—easily replenishing a 60 km daily commute in just 3 hours overnight without paying your electricity board for a 7 kW load upgrade!
