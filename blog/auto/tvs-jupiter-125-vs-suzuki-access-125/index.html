TVS Jupiter 125 vs Suzuki Access 125: Specifications & Claimed vs Real Mileage

Comparing TVS Jupiter 125 and Suzuki Access 125 requires separating ARAI lab claims from independent road tests and owner averages. The TVS Jupiter 125 pairs a 124.8cc engine (8.04 bhp @ 6,500 rpm, 10.5 Nm @ 4,500 rpm) with a 108 kg chassis and claims 57.2 km/l.

The Suzuki Access 125 uses a 124.0cc powertrain (8.58 bhp @ 6,750 rpm, 10.0 Nm @ 5,500 rpm) weighing 103 kg with a claimed 52.5 km/l. Their 5 kg mass difference and gearing (CVT Automatic vs CVT Automatic) shape their real-world fuel gap.

City Traffic Usability and Highway Cruising Behaviour

In stop-and-go city traffic, kerb weight and idle-saving tech determine fuel burn. The TVS Jupiter 125 features Floorboard-mounted fuel tank freeing 33-litre twin-helmet under-seat storage, IntelliGO stop-start, and peak torque at 4,500 rpm, returning 48–52 km/l in urban use. The Suzuki Access 125 counters with Light 103 kg kerb weight paired with brisk 8.58 bhp SEP engine, Eco Drive Illumination ring, and external tail-mounted fuel filler, achieving 48–52 km/l. Related reading: Suzuki Access 125 standalone city and highway mileage review.

On open highways, the TVS Jupiter 125 reaches 90 km/h and cruises cleanly at 50–65 km/h (52–56 km/l), whereas the Suzuki Access 125 tops out at 90 km/h and cruises comfortably at 50–65 km/h (51–55 km/l).
