Quick Answer: The Bajaj Pulsar NS125 (124.45cc) delivers a real-world mileage of 45–49 km/l in city traffic, 49–53 km/l on highways, and an owner-reported mixed average of 44–50 km/l, against a claimed figure of 46.9 km/l. At 48 km/l and an illustrative petrol price of ₹105/litre, its running cost is ₹2.19/km (₹2.13/km in Delhi).

Claimed Mileage vs Real-World Efficiency of Bajaj Pulsar NS125

Evaluating bajaj pulsar ns125 mileage requires separating five distinct figures: ARAI-certified laboratory claims, independent city/highway road tests, owner-reported mixed averages, dashboard readouts, and calculated petrol costs. Under standardized Indian Driving Cycle (IDC) dynamometer testing, the Bajaj Pulsar NS125 is certified at 46.9 km/l. Because lab rollers eliminate wind drag, gradients, pillion weight, and signal idling, on-road efficiency is typically 10% to 18% lower than the brochure rating. City vs Highway Performance and Full-Tank Verification

In dense metro commutes, frequent signal stops and 1st/2nd gear crawling hold the Bajaj Pulsar NS125 to 45–49 km/l. Using its efficiency tech (Pressed-steel perimeter frame, Nitrox monoshock, 4-valve top-end breathing, and 144 kg kerb weight) trims idle fuel waste by 4%–7%. On highways at 65–80 km/h, top-gear cruising raises economy to 49–53 km/l.

Because digital console economy readouts typically overestimate by 3%–6%, verify your bike's true mileage via the full-tank auto-cut method: fill the 12.0L tank to auto-cut, ride 100+ km, refill to auto-cut at the same nozzle, and divide distance by litres added.
