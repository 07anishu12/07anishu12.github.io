1. Introduction

Yamaha made history at EICMA 2026 by launching its brand-new 'RW' crossover adventure scooter family with two distinct displacements: the twin-cylinder flagship Yamaha RW700 and the middleweight singlecylinder Yamaha RW350. By introducing these machines simultaneously, Yamaha has mounted a comprehensive challenge to the adventure scooter market, giving riders a choice between a 73.4 hp twincylinder behemoth equipped with automated manual Y-AMT transmission, and an agile 29.2 hp singlecylinder commuter equipped with a seamless twist-and-go CVT. For riders eager to join the adventurescooter revolution in 2027, an exhaustive comparative breakdown between the RW700 and RW350 reveals which machine delivers the optimal balance of power, weight, practicality, and financial value.

2. Launch and Availability

Both adventure scooters were unveiled during Yamaha's global premiere at EICMA on November 3, 2026, in Milan. Commercial rollouts will commence across European and Australian markets in spring 2027, with the RW700 launching in May 2027 and the RW350 following in June 2027. For the Indian market, India Yamaha Motor is evaluating customer response to determine whether to introduce the accessible RW350 as a localized CKD offering or the flagship RW700 as a CBU halo machine in late 2027.

3. Engine and Performance

The mechanical divide between the two models defines their riding character. The RW700 is powered by Yamaha's legendary 689cc, liquid-cooled, DOHC parallel-twin CP2 engine with a 270-degree crossplane crankshaft, generating an impressive 73.4 hp at 8,750 rpm and 68 Nm of peak torque at 6,500 rpm. The RW350 utilizes a newly engineered 327cc liquid-cooled single-cylinder SOHC Blue Core engine producing 29.2 hp at 7,500 rpm and 31.5 Nm of torque at 5,750 rpm. The transmission technologies are equally divergent: the RW700 utilizes Yamaha's advanced Y-AMT automated manual transmission with electronic clutch actuators and paddle-shift capability, while the RW350 employs a traditional, fuss-free V-belt continuously variable transmission (CVT).

4. Design and hardware

Chassis dimensions and weight dictate their handling dynamics. The RW700 is a substantial transcontinental touring machine with a kerb weight of 224 kg. It features premium off-road hardware: wire-spoked tubeless wheels (17-inch front, 15-inch rear) with Pirelli Scorpion Rally STR tires, 43mm inverted KYB forks with 160mm of travel, and twin 298mm front brake discs with radial four-piston calipers. The RW350 is significantly lighter and nimbler, weighing just 184 kg (a massive 40 kg lighter). It rides on 15-inch front and 14-inch rear alloy wheels, with 145mm of suspension travel, making it far easier to filter through dense city gridlock and park in tight spaces.

5. Features and technology

Both cockpits feature cutting-edge electronics. The RW700 features a 7.0-inch TFT screen with full-screen Garmin navigation, cruise control, heated grips, Yamaha Ride Control (YRC) engine mapping, and a 34-litre underseat compartment. The RW350 features a 5.0-inch TFT display with Y-Connect smartphone navigation, traction control, keyless smart-fob ignition, and a larger 38-litre underseat storage bay that swallows two fullface helmets—a feat the twin-cylinder RW700 cannot match due to engine packaging.

6. Expected price and market positioning

Pricing creates a clear demographic separation. In Europe, the RW350 retails between €6,700 and €7,200, making it an accessible daily commuter for A2 licence holders. The flagship RW700 commands €11,800 to €12,900, targeting affluent tourers cross-shopping the Honda X-ADV (€13,200). If evaluated for India, the RW350 could retail around Rs 4.50 lakh via CKD, while the RW700 would command Rs 12.50 lakh to Rs 14.00 lakh via CBU import.

8. Rivals and alternatives

The RW700 battles the Honda X-ADV 750 and BMW F 900 XR. The RW350 competes with the Honda ADV350, Kymco DT X360, and BMW C 400 X.

9. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Which scooter has more underseat storage, RW700 or RW350?

Surprisingly, the single-cylinder RW350 has a larger 38-litre underseat storage bay that fits two helmets, compared to 34 litres on the twin-cylinder RW700.

Q: How do their transmissions differ?

The RW700 uses the Y-AMT automated manual transmission with electronic paddle shifting, while the RW350 uses a traditional twist-and-go automatic CVT.

Q: Which scooter is better for long-distance highway touring?

The RW700 is vastly superior for long highways due to its 73.4 hp CP2 twin engine, standard cruise control, and 160 km/h cruising composure.

Q: Which model makes more sense for city commuting?

The RW350 makes significantly more sense for city commuting; it is 40 kg lighter, more fuel-efficient (29.4 km/l), and easier to handle in traffic.

10. Conclusion

Deciding between the Yamaha RW700 and RW350 comes down to your primary riding environment. If your riding consists of 80 percent city commuting and weekend suburban gravel trails where low weight, fuel economy, and cavernous helmet storage are paramount, the RW350 makes far more sense. But if you demand transcontinental two-up touring power, 73.4 hp crossplane twin thrills, automated paddle shifting, and serious off-road spoked wheels, the flagship RW700 is the ultimate crossover machine.
