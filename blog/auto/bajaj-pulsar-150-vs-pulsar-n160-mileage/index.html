Bajaj Pulsar 150 vs Bajaj Pulsar N160: Specifications & Claimed vs Real Mileage

Comparing Bajaj Pulsar 150 and Bajaj Pulsar N160 requires separating ARAI lab claims from independent road tests and owner averages. The Bajaj Pulsar 150 pairs a 149.5cc engine (13.8 bhp @ 8,500 rpm, 13.25 Nm @ 6,500 rpm) with a 148 kg chassis and claims 50.0 km/l.

The Bajaj Pulsar N160 uses a 164.82cc powertrain (15.68 bhp @ 8,750 rpm, 14.65 Nm @ 6,750 rpm) weighing 152 kg with a claimed 51.6 km/l. Their 4 kg mass difference and gearing (5-speed vs 5-speed) shape their real-world fuel gap. Related reading: Bajaj Pulsar NS160 mileage and top speed guide.

City Traffic Usability and Highway Cruising Behaviour

In stop-and-go city traffic, kerb weight and idle-saving tech determine fuel burn. The Bajaj Pulsar 150 features 15-litre long-range tank, upright ergonomics, twin-shock rear suspension, and low spare-part costs, returning 44–48 km/l in urban use. The Bajaj Pulsar N160 counters with Broad torque band with 85% peak torque available across the mid-range, 14-litre tank, and dual-channel ABS, achieving 38–43 km/l.

On open highways, the Bajaj Pulsar 150 reaches 112 km/h and cruises cleanly at 70–85 km/h (47–51 km/l), whereas the Bajaj Pulsar N160 tops out at 115 km/h and cruises comfortably at 75–90 km/h (44–48 km/l).

Conditional Verdict: Which Model Fits Your Commute?

Choose the Bajaj Pulsar 150 (₹1,10,419 – ₹1,15,418) if you value its 44–48 km/l real-world efficiency, 148 kg weight, and 15-litre long-range tank, upright ergonomics, twin-shock rear suspension, and low spare-part costs.
