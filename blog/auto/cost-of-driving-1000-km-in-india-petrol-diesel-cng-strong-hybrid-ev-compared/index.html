Using October 2026 New Delhi reference energy prices (Petrol ₹102.12/L, Diesel ₹95.20/L, CNG ₹86.98/kg, Home Electricity ₹8.00/kWh) for a comparable Compact-to-Mid-Size SUV in real Indian city traffic with the AC on, driving 1,000 km costs approximately: ₹9,725 in a Turbo-Petrol Automatic, ₹7,565 in a Naturally Aspirated Petrol, ₹6,140 in a Diesel, ₹4,540 in a Strong Hybrid, ₹4,220 in a Factory CNG car (including start-up petrol), and ₹1,350 in a Home-Charged EV.

Declared Assumptions: How to Compare Apples to Apples

To prevent misleading comparisons (such as comparing a tiny 800 kg hatchback against a 1,500 kg SUV), every figure below is calculated for a comparable sub-4m to 4.3m family SUV/crossover (e.g., Nexon / Brezza / Creta / Grand Vitara class) carrying 2–3 occupants with the air conditioner running.

Declared Energy Price Baseline (New Delhi, October 2026):

  • Petrol: ₹102.12 per litre (Note: varies from ₹94.72 to ₹111.21 across Indian cities; adjust by ±6% for your state).
  • Diesel: ₹95.20 per litre.
  • CNG: ₹86.98 per kg (plus ₹350 per 1,000 km for morning cold-start petrol).
  • Electricity (for EV benchmark): ₹8.00/kWh domestic slab (including 12% AC wallbox charging loss) or ₹16.00/kWh on Highway DC Fast Chargers.

4 Eye-Opening Takeaways from the 1,000 km Math

  1. Strong Hybrid Matches CNG in the City Without Pump Queues: Driving 1,000 km in city traffic costs ₹4,538 in a Strong Hybrid vs ₹4,216 in a CNG car—a difference of just ₹322 per 1,000 km! Yet the Strong Hybrid fills up at any regular petrol pump in 3 minutes and has an automatic gearbox.
  2. Diesel Beats Strong Hybrid on Open Highways: Once you hit the expressway for a 1,000 km road trip, a 1.5L Diesel SUV drops to ₹4,760 per 1,000 km (at 20 km/l), beating the Strong Hybrid's ₹5,106 per 1,000 km (at 20 km/l on pricier petrol).
  3. Turbo-Petrol Automatics Are the Priciest to Run in City Jams: At 10.5 km/l in dense traffic, a 1.0L/1.2L/1.5L Turbo-Petrol DCT/AT costs ₹9,726 per 1,000 km—more than double the running cost of a Strong Hybrid or CNG car.
  4. Highway DC Fast Charging Makes EVs Cost Almost as Much as CNG: While home charging an EV costs just ₹1,355 per 1,000 km, plugging into ₹16/kWh + 18% GST highway DC fast chargers at 100 km/h (where EV efficiency drops to ~5.8 km/kWh) costs roughly ₹3,255 per 1,000 km.

How to Convert This Table to Your Own City in 10 Seconds

Use this simple formula for any car and any city:

Cost per 1,000 km = (1,000 ÷ Your Real Mileage) × Local Fuel Price per Litre/Kg.

  • Choose CNG or Home-Charged EV if: You want the lowest possible ₹1,350–₹4,200 cost per 1,000 km under a ₹10–₹15 lakh car budget.
  • Choose a Strong Hybrid if: You want ₹4,500/1,000 km city running costs with automatic comfort and zero charging/queue hassle.
  • Choose Diesel if: More than 60% of your kilometres are racked up on intercity national highways.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. How much less does 1,000 km cost if I drive a small hatchback like a Maruti Celerio or WagonR instead of an SUV?

Because a lightweight hatchback achieves ~18.5 km/l on petrol and ~26 km/kg on CNG in the city, its 1,000 km city cost drops to roughly ₹5,520 on Petrol and ₹3,600 on CNG (and ~₹4,440 on Petrol / ~₹3,050 on CNG on highways).

Q2. Why did we include ₹350 of petrol in the 1,000 km CNG city calculation?

Most factory CNG cars start on petrol until the coolant warms up and periodically use small amounts of petrol when a CNG cylinder empties a few kilometres before a station. Ignoring that 3–3.5 litres of petrol per 1,000 km understates real-world bi-fuel bills.

Q3. Why does an EV cost more than twice as much per 1,000 km on highways compared to home charging?

Two factors combine on highways: first, commercial DC fast-charging networks charge ₹15–₹18/kWh plus 18% GST (versus ₹6–₹9/kWh on domestic home meters); second, pushing an SUV through wind resistance at 100 km/h without frequent braking regeneration reduces EV efficiency from ~6.6 km/kWh down to ~5.6–5.8 km/kWh.

Q4. Does this 1,000 km fuel cost include oil changes, tyres, or vehicle depreciation?

No. The figures above represent direct energy/fuel cost per 1,000 km. Routine servicing adds roughly ₹600–₹900 per 1,000 km (₹6,000–₹9,000 per 10,000 km service), and tyre wear adds roughly ₹700–₹900 per 1,000 km.

Q5. Which powertrain has the most predictable cost per 1,000 km over the next 5 years?

Home-charged Electric Vehicles (especially for house owners with rooftop solar) and Strong Hybrids offer the highest insulation against volatile global crude-oil spikes.
