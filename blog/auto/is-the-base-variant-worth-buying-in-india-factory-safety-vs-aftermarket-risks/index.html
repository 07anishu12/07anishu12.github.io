Yes, a base variant is worth buying in India only when it already includes your non-negotiable factory hardware—specifically six airbags, Electronic Stability Program (ESP), all four power windows, central locking, and a rear defogger if you drive in rain or fog. Models like the Maruti Baleno Sigma, Maruti Brezza LXi, and Mahindra XUV 3XO MX1 are outstanding base-trim bargains, whereas stripped fleet trims missing AC or power steering should be avoided.

Why 2026 Base Variants Are Very Different from 5 Years Ago

Historically, Indian carmakers treated base trims purely as price-leader billboards—omitting passenger airbags, ABS, power windows, and even body-coloured bumpers. Today, tightened safety norms and fierce competition mean the base variant of many ₹6 lakh to ₹15 lakh cars shares the exact same crash structure, engine, suspension, six airbags, ESP, Hill-Hold Assist, and ISOFIX mounts as the top-end trim costing ₹4 lakh more.

Because state road tax (8%–18%) is calculated as a percentage of the ex-showroom invoice, buying a base variant also saves you tax on every feature you skip.

The Golden Rule: What You Can Add Later vs What Must Be Factory-Fitted

Before booking any base model, divide missing features into two buckets. Never buy a base variant expecting an accessory shop to install structural or high-amperage electrical systems:

  • Safe to Add at the Dealership or Reputed Shop (Coupler-to-Coupler): Infotainment head unit + speakers (using plug-and-play ISO wiring harnesses without wire cutting), rear parking camera, seat covers (strictly 6-airbag compatible with tear-away side seams!), floor mats, wheel covers or alloy wheels, and parcel trays.
  • Impossible or Unsafe to Retrofit Aftermarket: Six airbags, ESP/Hill-Hold, rear windshield defogger and rear wash-wiper, height-adjustable driver's seat, electrically foldable ORVMs (requires door panel cutting and BCM wiring), 60:40 split rear seats, and automatic climate control.

How to Protect Your Electrical Warranty When Upgrading a Base Model

Every Indian automaker's warranty booklet states that cutting, splicing, or tapping into factory wiring harnesses voids the electrical warranty. If you buy a base trim like the Baleno Sigma or Brezza LXi, either order the official dealership accessory infotainment/camera package (which preserves your factory warranty on invoice) or insist on a strict coupler-to-coupler (socket-to-socket) installation with zero wire stripping. Additionally, if your car has six airbags, never fit standard stitched seat covers over the front backrests—they block the side-thorax airbags placed inside the seat bolsters.

Decision Checklist: Should You Book the Base Variant?

  • Buy the Base Variant if: You are comfortable without a height-adjustable driver seat or rear wiper, the base trim includes 6 airbags + ESP + 4 power windows + central locking, and a ₹30,000 dealer audio/camera upgrade gives you everything you need while saving ₹1.0+ lakh.
  • Skip the Base Variant and Buy the Second Trim if: The base model lacks front/rear power windows, remote central locking, or a height-adjustable seat that a shorter driver in your family needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Why do dealerships often claim base variants have a 3-to-6 month waiting period?

Automakers allocate lower production volumes to lower-margin base trims, and dealers earn higher incentives and accessory margins on mid and top variants. Always insist on a formal booking receipt with an order number, which forces the factory allocation system to queue your car.

Q2. Does installing an aftermarket touchscreen and rear camera void my new car's warranty?

If the installer uses a vehicle-specific plug-and-play (coupler-to-coupler) harness without cutting or splicing any factory wire, your warranty remains intact. Cutting factory wires or tapping into fuse boxes with bare wires can void electrical warranty claims.

Q3. Can I put normal seat covers on a base variant that comes with 6 airbags?

No. Cars with 6 airbags house two side-thorax airbags inside the outer bolsters of the front driver and passenger seats. You must either leave the fabric uncovered, use backrest-free seat cushions, or buy certified airbag-compatible covers from the automaker.

Q4. Is it cheaper to buy alloy wheels from the showroom on a base model than buying the mid variant?

Yes. A set of four genuine 15-inch or 16-inch OEM alloy wheels at the dealership counter typically costs ₹26,000 to ₹38,000, whereas jumping two variants solely for alloy wheels often costs ₹1.20 lakh to ₹1.80 lakh.

Q5. Does a base variant have lower resale value after 5 years?

Base variants actually lose less money in absolute rupees because their initial purchase price and road tax were lowest, provided you have fitted a clean audio system and central locking.
