How Dual-Cylinder Technology Solved the Biggest CNG Flaw

For over a decade, buying a CNG hatchback meant surrendering your entire luggage boot to a single 55-to-60-litre cylinder mounted across the floor. Even carrying a medium suitcase required folding down the rear passenger bench.

Tata Motors (with Twin-Cylinder iCNG) and Hyundai (with Hy-CNG Duo on the Exter and Grand i10 Nios) split that storage into two smaller cylinders tucked beneath a flat floor tray. The result is 200 to 210 litres of clean, usable boot space above the floor—enough for two cabin trolley bags and backpacks—while Maruti Suzuki's S-CNG hatchbacks still use single cylinders that prioritise spare-wheel access or maximum km/kg economy over luggage room.

Performance, Refuelling Range & Everyday Ownership Reality

  • Power Drop on CNG (12% to 15%): Because CNG has a slower flame propagation speed and displaces intake air in naturally aspirated engines, peak output drops from ~86 PS in petrol mode to ~73 PS in CNG mode (or ~67 PS to ~57 PS in 1.0L engines). In flat city traffic, modern dual-ECU systems mask this well, but on steep flyovers with four adults and the AC on, you will need to downshift a gear earlier.
  • Real Range Per Fill (180–220 km): Although a 60-litre tank is rated for ~10–12 kg of gas, Indian city dispensing pressure usually pushes in 7.5 to 8.5 kg per fill. At 23–26 km/kg in city traffic, expect to visit a CNG station every 180 to 220 km.
  • Direct Start in CNG: Tata iCNG models can start directly in CNG mode even when the engine is cold, whereas Maruti and Hyundai systems start on petrol for a few seconds before switching over automatically—so always keep at least 5–7 litres of petrol in the tank to protect the fuel pump.

Which CNG Car Under ₹10 Lakh Should You Buy?

  • Choose the Tata Punch iCNG Pure/Adventure or Hyundai Exter Hy-CNG Duo if: You want 185+ mm ground clearance AND a usable 210-litre flat luggage boot for family trips.
  • Choose the Tata Tiago iCNG XZA (AMT) if: You commute in heavy city traffic and want the convenience of an automatic gearbox paired with CNG running costs (~₹3.50–₹3.80/km).
  • Choose the Maruti WagonR S-CNG or Maruti Dzire S-CNG if: You want the highest real-world fuel efficiency (25–27 km/kg) and fastest resale liquidity.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Why should I buy a factory-fitted CNG car instead of installing an aftermarket kit?

Factory CNG cars have reinforced rear suspension springs to handle the 65–75 kg tank weight, hardened engine valve seats, crash-tested integrated piping, and full manufacturer warranty. An aftermarket kit voids your new-car engine and electrical warranty.

Q2. Where is the spare wheel placed in twin-cylinder CNG cars like the Tata Punch and Hyundai Exter?

Because the twin CNG cylinders occupy the traditional spare-wheel well under the boot floor, the spare tyre is mounted underneath the rear underbody (similar to larger SUVs) or secured vertically in a compact recess.

Q3. Do CNG cars require more frequent maintenance than petrol cars?

Yes, slightly. Spark plugs in a CNG engine should be inspected every 10,000 km and replaced every 20,000–30,000 km, the low-pressure CNG filter cartridge needs periodic replacement, and the cylinder requires mandatory hydro-testing every 3 years.

Q4. Can I drive a CNG car on long highway trips where CNG stations are scarce?

Yes. Every factory CNG car retains its full 35-to-37-litre petrol tank. Even when the CNG tank runs empty after ~200 km, the car seamlessly switches to petrol for another 500–600 km of highway range.

Q5. Does insurance cost more for a factory CNG car?

Yes, by a modest margin. Because the invoice price is ₹90,000–₹1,00,000 higher and the policy includes a bi-fuel endorsement, annual insurance is roughly ₹1,500–₹2,500 higher than the equivalent petrol trim.
