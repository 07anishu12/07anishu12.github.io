To afford a car in India without straining your household investments, judge affordability by the car's 'All-In Monthly Outflow' (5-year Loan EMI with 25% down payment PLUS monthly fuel, insurance, and service reserve): a ₹10 lakh on-road car costs ~₹24,500/month all-in; a ₹15 lakh car costs ~₹35,500/month all-in; and a ₹20 lakh car costs ~₹46,500/month all-in. For a household paying rent or a home-loan EMI, your comfortable monthly in-hand take-home income should be approximately ₹95,000–₹1,20,000 for a ₹10 lakh car, ₹1,45,000–₹1,80,000 for a ₹15 lakh car, and ₹1,95,000–₹2,40,000 for a ₹20 lakh car (about 20%–25% lower if you live in a debt-free family home).

Step 1: Know the True 'All-In Monthly Cost' of a ₹10L, ₹15L & ₹20L Car

A ₹15,600 EMI on a ₹10 lakh car is only ~63% of what the car actually pulls out of your bank account each month! Assuming a healthy 25% Down Payment, a 5-Year (60-Month) Loan at 9.1% p.a., and 1,000 km/month of city driving:

  • ₹10 Lakh On-Road Car (₹2.50L Down, ₹7.50L Loan): ₹15,604 EMI + ₹6,200 Petrol + ₹1,400 Insurance/mo + ₹1,100 Service/Tyre Reserve = ₹24,304 / month Total Outflow.
  • ₹15 Lakh On-Road Car (₹3.75L Down, ₹11.25L Loan): ₹23,406 EMI + ₹7,800 Petrol + ₹2,000 Insurance/mo + ₹1,600 Service/Tyre Reserve = ₹34,806 / month Total Outflow.
  • ₹20 Lakh On-Road Car (₹5.00L Down, ₹15.00L Loan): ₹31,208 EMI + ₹9,200 Fuel + ₹2,700 Insurance/mo + ₹2,100 Service/Tyre Reserve = ₹45,208 / month Total Outflow.

Why You Should Never Let Bank 'Loan Eligibility' Dictate Your Car Budget

Indian banks use a metric called FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio)—allowing up to 50% to 60% of your monthly net income to go toward EMIs! That means if your in-hand salary is ₹75,000/month and you have no home loan, a bank will gladly sanction a ₹35,000/month EMI for a ₹20 lakh SUV.

If you take that loan, ₹35,000 EMI + ₹13,000 fuel/insurance = ₹48,000/month (64% of your entire take-home pay!) vanishes into a depreciating car, leaving ₹27,000 for food, utilities, parents' healthcare, and ₹0 for mutual fund SIPs!

How to Safely Buy One Segment Higher Without Hurting Monthly Cashflow

If your heart is set on a ₹15 lakh car on a ₹1.10 lakh salary, do NOT stretch the loan to 7 years at 10% down. Instead, increase your Down Payment from 25% to 45%–50% (by saving for 10 extra months or exchanging an existing car). Putting ₹7.00 lakh down on a ₹15 lakh car drops the loan amount to ₹8.00 lakh—bringing your monthly EMI right down to ₹16,640 (the exact same monthly burden as a ₹10 lakh car!).

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Why do we always use 'Monthly In-Hand Take-Home Salary' instead of 'Annual CTC'?

Because Annual CTC (Cost to Company) includes Employer PF, Gratuity, variable bonuses, ESOPs, and 15%–30% Income Tax—money that never hits your monthly bank account to pay an EMI! A ₹20 lakh CTC typically puts only ₹1.25–₹1.38 lakh/month into your bank account.

Q2. What is a healthy rule of thumb for Car On-Road Price vs Annual In-Hand Income?

In India, keeping your car's On-Road Price between 60% and 85% of your Annual Net In-Hand Household Income (roughly 7 to 10 months of take-home pay) keeps ownership stress-free. Once a car exceeds 12 months (100%) of your annual net take-home pay, it noticeably slows down your long-term wealth creation.

Q3. How does a company Corporate Car Lease policy change these salary numbers?

If your employer offers an official Corporate Car Lease where the lease rental, fuel, insurance, and driver allowance are deducted from your pre-tax gross salary, a buyer in the 30% income-tax bracket saves ~31.2% tax on the entire EMI and running cost—allowing you to comfortably step up by ₹3–₹4 lakh on the same salary!

Q4. Should I combine my spouse's income when calculating the salary needed for our family car?

Yes! If both spouses are earning and sharing one primary household car, use your Combined Net Household In-Hand Income (minus your combined rent/home EMI and mandatory 25% investment SIPs) to check the table.

Q5. What if I am paying 100% Cash for the ₹15 lakh car with zero loan EMI?

If you have already accumulated ₹15 lakh in dedicated car savings (separate from your emergency fund and retirement corpus) and take zero loan, your ongoing monthly outflow is only the ₹11,400/month in fuel, insurance, and service—which is comfortable even on a ₹75,000–₹90,000 monthly in-hand income.
