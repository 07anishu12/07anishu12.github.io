Debating between a 10%, 20%, or 40% car down payment? See how down payment size affects your EMI, total interest, negative equity, and emergency savings.

For a brand-new car in India, your ideal down payment is 20% to 30% of the total On-Road Price (never less than 20%), paired with a loan tenure of 4 to 5 years. Putting at least 20%–25% down covers the non-refundable state RTO road tax, insurance, and immediate day-one depreciation—ensuring you never owe the bank more than the car's market value ('negative equity'). For a used car—where loan interest rates are 12.5% to 15%—increase your down payment to 35% to 50% to avoid overpaying on high-interest debt, while always keeping 6 months of household expenses untouched in your emergency fund.

Why 'Zero Down Payment / 90% On-Road Loan' Is a Financial Trap

Suppose you buy a ₹12.00 lakh on-road car with only ₹1.00 lakh down (borrowing ₹11.00 lakh over 6 or 7 years). The moment the car leaves the showroom, ₹1.60 lakh of road tax + insurance is gone, and its market resale value after 12 months is ~₹9.40 lakh.

Meanwhile, after 12 months of paying EMI on a 6-year loan, your remaining loan principal is still ₹9.65 lakh! You are in Negative Equity ('Underwater'): even if you face a job change or medical emergency and want to sell the car in Year 1 or Year 2, selling the car won't even pay off the bank loan—you would have to pay ₹30,000–₹60,000 out of pocket just to hand the keys over!

New Car (9.1% Loan) vs Used Car (14% Loan): Why the Strategy Changes

  • On a New Car (~9.0%–9.2% Interest): Paying 25% to 30% down (₹3.00–₹3.60 lakh on a ₹12L car) hits the sweet spot. Every ₹1,00,000 of extra down payment reduces your 5-year monthly EMI by ₹2,080/month and saves ₹24,830 in guaranteed tax-free interest.
  • On a Used Car (~13.5%–15.0% Interest): No conservative bank investment beats a guaranteed 14% post-tax interest saving! When buying a used car, put down 40% to 50% (plus keep your ₹50k repair buffer in cash) and cap the loan tenure at 36 to 48 months.

The Golden Boundary: Never Touch Your 6-Month Emergency Fund

Before increasing your down payment from 25% to 50% to save interest, check one hard boundary: a car is an illiquid, depreciating asset. Once you hand ₹6 lakh in cash to a car dealer, you cannot pull ₹2 lakh back out of the car's bonnet next month if someone in the family is hospitalised.

The Sequence:

  1. Keep 6 months of mandatory household expenses + health insurance deductibles untouched in a liquid FD/sweep account.
  2. Keep ₹30,000–₹50,000 for accessories/buffer.
  3. Use the remaining dedicated car-purchase savings for your 25%–35% Down Payment.

Clear Decision Rule

  • If You Cannot Afford 20% Down Without Touching Emergency Savings: Your target car is one segment too expensive—step down to a variant or model costing ₹2–₹3 lakh less.
  • Target 25%–30% Down on New Cars (and 40%+ on Used Cars): Keeps your EMI comfortable and protects your equity from Day 1.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What does '100% Ex-Showroom Finance' mean when advertised by Indian banks?

When a bank advertises '100% Finance', they almost always mean 100% of the Ex-Showroom Price—NOT the On-Road Price. You still have to pay the state RTO road tax, registration, insurance, and FASTag (roughly 13% to 18% of the total on-road cost) out of your own pocket as the down payment.

Q2. Can I use my old car's exchange value as my full down payment?

Yes! This is one of the easiest ways to reach a 25%–35% down payment without touching your bank savings. If your 6-year-old hatchback fetches ₹3.20 lakh in exchange (plus a ₹20,000 exchange bonus), that ₹3.40 lakh is credited directly as your down payment on a ₹12 lakh new car.

Q3. Is it better to make a 30% down payment on a 5-year loan or a 20% down payment on a 4-year loan?

Both save significant interest! Shortening the loan tenure from 5 years (60 months) to 4 years (48 months) on a ₹9.60 lakh loan saves ₹49,500 in interest, while increasing the down payment from 20% to 30% on a 5-year loan saves ₹29,760 and keeps your monthly EMI ₹4,500 lower for cashflow safety.

Q4. Should I break a 7% Fixed Deposit to make a bigger down payment on a 9.2% New-Car Loan?

Yes—provided it is surplus FD money and NOT your 6-month emergency fund! A 7% FD actually yields only ~4.9% after 30% income tax, whereas reducing a 9.2% car loan gives you a guaranteed 9.2% post-tax saving—making debt reduction nearly twice as rewarding as keeping surplus cash in a taxable FD.

Q5. Why do banks demand at least 25%–35% down payment on used cars?

Because banks lend up to 75%–80% of their internal depreciated valuation grid, which is usually 10% lower than the retail dealer price. That valuation gap plus the 20% margin means a used-car buyer naturally pays 28%–35% out of pocket.
