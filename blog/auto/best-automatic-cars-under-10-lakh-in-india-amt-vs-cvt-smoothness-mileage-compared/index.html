AMT vs CVT Under ₹10 Lakh: Why Gearbox Type Changes Your Daily Drive

Not all 'two-pedal' cars under ₹10 lakh work the same way beneath the floor. Knowing which gearbox is fitted prevents buyer's remorse:

  • AMT (Automated Manual Transmission — called AGS by Maruti, Smart Auto by Hyundai): Uses a standard manual gearbox and clutch operated by electro-hydraulic or electric actuators. AMTs cost only ₹50,000–₹65,000 more than a manual and deliver nearly identical fuel efficiency. However, you will feel a brief pause ('head-nod') during 1st-to-2nd and 2nd-to-3rd upshifts under hard throttle.
  • CVT / IVT (Continuously Variable / Intelligent Variable Transmission): Uses two variable-diameter pulleys connected by a steel belt or chain instead of fixed gear cogs. There are zero gear changes and zero shift shocks in bumper-to-bumper traffic. A CVT costs ₹90,000–₹1,20,000 more than a manual and typically consumes 1–2 km/l more petrol in dense city crawls.

Why Hill-Hold Assist Is Non-Negotiable in an AMT

Unlike a traditional torque converter or CVT that maintains gentle hydraulic forward pressure on slopes, an AMT disengages its clutch when your foot is on the brake. Without Electronic Stability Program (ESP) and Hill-Hold Assist (HHA), an AMT car will roll backward for a split second when you move your right foot from brake to accelerator on a mall ramp or flyover. Always choose a variant that includes factory Hill-Hold Assist—now standard on the Baleno AGS, Dzire AGS, Exter AMT, Punch Accomplished AMT, and Amaze CVT.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. How can I reduce the gear-shift jerk ('head-nod') when driving an AMT car?

Ease off the accelerator pedal slightly for half a second just as the car reaches 2,000–2,200 RPM between 1st and 2nd gear, exactly as you would in a manual car. This lets the actuator complete the shift smoothly without lurching.

Q2. Is an AMT clutch expensive to replace compared to a manual clutch?

The mechanical clutch plate and pressure plate inside an AMT wear out just like a manual clutch (typically lasting 45,000–70,000 km) and cost roughly ₹6,000–₹10,000 to replace. However, avoid holding the car stationary on slopes using the accelerator, which overheats the clutch.

Q3. Should I shift an automatic into Neutral (N) at long Indian traffic signals?

Yes. If you are stopped at a red light for more than 20–30 seconds, shifting to Neutral (N) and engaging the handbrake releases actuator/transmission load and reduces brake pedal fatigue.

Q4. Why are there no Torque Converter or DCT automatics under ₹10 lakh on-road?

Torque converter (AT) and dual-clutch (DCT) gearboxes are paired with higher-taxed turbo-petrol engines in cars like the Venue DCT, Sonet DCT, Brezza AT, and XUV 3XO AT, whose automatic trims start between ₹11.50 lakh and ₹13.20 lakh on-road.

Q5. Does a CVT rubber-band effect matter in city driving?

No. The CVT 'rubber-band' sensation—where engine RPM rises before road speed catches up—only occurs when you floor the accelerator for sudden highway overtakes. In gentle city driving, a CVT is exceptionally smooth and quiet.
