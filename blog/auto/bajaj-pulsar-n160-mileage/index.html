Claimed Mileage vs Real-World Efficiency of Bajaj Pulsar N160

Evaluating bajaj pulsar n160 mileage requires separating five distinct figures: ARAI-certified laboratory claims, independent city/highway road tests, owner-reported mixed averages, dashboard readouts, and calculated petrol costs. Under standardized Indian Driving Cycle (IDC) dynamometer testing, the Bajaj Pulsar N160 is certified at 51.6 km/l. Because lab rollers eliminate wind drag, gradients, pillion weight, and signal idling, on-road efficiency is typically 10% to 18% lower than the brochure rating.

Driven by a 164.82cc (Single-cylinder, 4-stroke, SOHC, 2-valve, oil-cooled, FI) motor producing 15.68 bhp @ 8,750 rpm and 14.65 Nm @ 6,750 rpm through a 5-speed transmission, the Bajaj Pulsar N160 weighs 152 kg. Key efficiency hardware includes Broad torque band with 85% peak torque available across the mid-range, 14-litre tank, and dual-channel ABS. Across variants (Dual-Channel ABS (Standard Telescopic), Dual-Channel ABS with USD Forks & Ride Modes (Road, Rain, Off-Road)), core engine mapping remains identical.

Speed-Wise Fuel Economy, Top Speed (115 km/h) and Gearing

Aerodynamic drag rises with the square of speed, rapidly increasing fuel demand above 65 km/h. At 35–45 km/h in 4th or top gear, the Bajaj Pulsar N160 operates under light load and returns 46–48 km/l unless traffic forces frequent downshifts.

Its peak thermal efficiency window is 75–90 km/h in top gear, delivering 48–50 km/l on open roads. Pushing toward its 115 km/h flat-road top speed triggers rich fuel mapping, dropping economy to 41–43 km/l at 80 km/h and 35–37 km/l near full throttle.

What is the real-world mileage of Bajaj Pulsar N160 in 2026?
