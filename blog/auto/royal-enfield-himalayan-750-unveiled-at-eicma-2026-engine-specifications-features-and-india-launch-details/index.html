1. Introduction

Royal Enfield has electrified the global adventure touring fraternity at EICMA 2026 by unveiling the all-new Royal Enfield Himalayan 750. Representing the brand's largest, most technologically sophisticated motorcycle to date, the Himalayan 750 evolves the acclaimed overland philosophy of the Himalayan family into the heavy middleweight multi-cylinder category. Constructed around a newly developed 750cc liquid-cooled paralleltwin engine, this flagship machine is purpose-built to conquer intercontinental expeditions with two-up touring comfort, generous luggage capacities, and uncompromised rough-road resilience. Unveiled during Royal Enfield's 125th-anniversary celebrations at Milan, the Himalayan 750 signals the iconic manufacturer's ambition to challenge established global heavyweights on the international stage.

2. Launch and Availability

The global unveiling took place at EICMA 2026 on November 3, 2026, in Milan, Italy. Royal Enfield showcased production-ready units across multiple adventure trims, confirming that commercial rollouts will commence in mid-2027. European and North American markets are scheduled to receive the Himalayan 750 during the third quarter of 2027, meeting Euro 5+ emissions and full A2/unrestricted licensing categories. For the Indian domestic market, Royal Enfield confirmed that pre-bookings will officially open in mid-2027, with customer deliveries beginning shortly thereafter from Royal Enfield's state-of-the-art Vallam Vadagal facility in Tamil Nadu.

3. Engine and Performance

Propelling the Himalayan 750 is an all-new 750cc, liquid-cooled, four-stroke parallel-twin engine featuring a 270-degree crankshaft configuration that produces an engaging, muscular exhaust note and tractive power delivery. The engine utilizes double overhead camshafts (DOHC) and four valves per cylinder, producing a verified 58 bhp at 7,500 rpm and 65 Nm of peak torque at 5,500 rpm. Significantly, over 80 percent of maximum torque is available from just 3,000 rpm, ensuring effortless hill-climbing prowess and low-speed tractability on challenging alpine terrain. The motor is paired with a heavy-duty six-speed transmission featuring a bi-directional quickshifter and a slip-and-assist clutch, ensuring fatigue-free gear shifting across cross-country marathons.

4. Design and hardware

Engineered for brutal expeditions, the Himalayan 750 is built around a twin-spar steel tubular frame codeveloped with Harris Performance. The chassis is paired with a bolt-on steel subframe engineered to carry heavy hard panniers and a top box without flexing. Front suspension is handled by 43mm Showa upsidedown cartridge forks with 200mm of travel, while the rear features a Showa linkage monoshock offering 200mm of wheel travel and a hydraulic remote preload adjuster. Tubeless cross-spoked rims—21-inch front and 17-inch rear—allow puncture repairs without wheel removal. Twin 320mm front discs clamped by radially mounted ByBre calipers provide reassuring stopping power for the 212 kg motorcycle.

5. Features and technology

The Himalayan 750 marks Royal Enfield's most technologically advanced production cockpit. It features an updated circular 5.0-inch TFT TripperDash display with full-map navigation powered by Google Maps, music controls, and ride telemetry. An advanced six-axis Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) governs lean-sensitive Cornering ABS and multi-level Traction Control. Riders can toggle between four riding modes: Eco, Performance, Rain, and Off-Road (which deactivates rear ABS and traction intervention). Additional touring luxuries include integrated cruise control, heated grips, an electrically adjustable windscreen, and a 20-litre fuel tank delivering a cruising range exceeding 450 kilometers.

6. Expected price and market positioning

Royal Enfield intends to disrupt the premium middleweight adventure category with competitive pricing. In Europe, the model is anticipated to retail between €8,500 and €9,200. In India, local assembly and deep domestic component sourcing will yield an estimated ex-showroom price between Rs 4.20 lakh and Rs 4.60 lakh. At this price point, the Himalayan 750 will undercut twin-cylinder European competitors significantly, offering adventure tourers twin-cylinder refinement at an unprecedented value threshold.

8. Rivals and alternatives

The Royal Enfield Himalayan 750 will rival the Yamaha Ténéré 700, Aprilia Tuareg 660, BMW F 800 GS, and Suzuki V-Strom 800DE. In India, its closest price and capability alternatives include the single-cylinder KTM 390 Adventure, the Kawasaki Versys 650, and Royal Enfield's own Himalayan 450. The Himalayan 750 differentiates itself through its authentic long-range overland focus, tubeless spoked wheels, accessible lowend torque, and unmatched price-to-displacement quotient.

9. Frequently Asked Questions

It is powered by an all-new 750cc, liquid-cooled, DOHC parallel-twin engine producing 58 bhp and 65 Nm of torque.

Q: Does the Himalayan 750 get tubeless spoked wheels?

Yes, the motorcycle features 21-inch front and 17-inch rear cross-spoked tubeless wheels as standard equipment.

Q: When will the Himalayan 750 launch in India?

Royal Enfield has scheduled the commercial launch in India for mid-to-late 2027.

Q: What is the expected price of the Himalayan 750 in India?

10. Conclusion

The Royal Enfield Himalayan 750 unveiled at EICMA 2026 is a monumental milestone for the Indian motorcycling industry. By successfully combining a refined 750cc parallel-twin engine with authentic 21-inch off-road chassis hardware, sophisticated IMU electronics, and long-distance touring ergonomics, Royal Enfield has engineered an overland motorcycle capable of traversing continents. It represents the definitive evolution for passionate Himalayan owners and promises to redefine global middleweight adventure touring.
