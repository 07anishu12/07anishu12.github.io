1. Introduction

Royal Enfield's 125th-anniversary celebration at EICMA 2026 reached its crescendo with the simultaneous global premiere of two flagship motorcycles constructed on its brand-new liquid-cooled 750cc parallel-twin platform: the Royal Enfield Himalayan 750 and the Royal Enfield Continental GT 750. While both machines share the same architectural twin-cylinder foundation developed at the brand's UK and Chennai R&D centers, they represent diametrically opposed motorcycling philosophies. The Himalayan 750 is an uncompromising, transcontinental overland expedition tourer engineered with 21-inch wheels and long-travel Showa suspension to cross continents and unpaved mountain passes. The Continental GT 750 is an aggressive, asphalt-carving café racer honoring the 1960s Ton-Up boy culture with clip-on handlebars, rearset footpegs, and dual front disc brakes. For enthusiasts anticipating their 2027 arrival, understanding their key differences is essential.

2. Launch and Availability

Both motorcycles were officially unveiled to the global motorcycling community on November 3, 2026, at EICMA in Milan. Royal Enfield confirmed that series production of the 750cc platform will begin at its Vallam Vadagal manufacturing plant in Tamil Nadu, India, in mid-2027. The Himalayan 750 will roll out first in European and Indian showrooms in late summer 2027, followed shortly by the Continental GT 750 during the high-spending Indian festive season in autumn 2027.

3. Engine and Performance

Underneath their distinct styling, both models utilize Royal Enfield's clean-sheet 750cc, liquid-cooled, fourstroke parallel-twin engine featuring a 270-degree crankshaft, double overhead camshafts (DOHC), and four valves per cylinder. However, Royal Enfield engineers have applied distinct state-of-tune calibrations to suit their contrasting missions. The Himalayan 750 is tuned for heavy overland tractor-like torque, producing 58 bhp at 7,500 rpm and 65 Nm of peak torque at 5,500 rpm, with over 80 percent of torque accessible from just 3,000 rpm. The Continental GT 750 features a sportier camshaft profile, higher compression, and freerbreathing exhausts, generating 62 bhp at 7,800 rpm and 68 Nm at 5,600 rpm, delivering a more aggressive top-end rush for high-speed road riding.

4. Design and hardware

The chassis architecture and ergonomics represent total divergence. The Himalayan 750 features an upright, commanding touring stance with wide handlebars, an electrically adjustable windscreen, an 835mm rally seat, and genuine 21/17-inch tubeless spoked wheels with 200mm of Showa suspension travel, achieving 220mm of ground clearance. In contrast, the Continental GT 750 adopts a crouched, aggressive ergonomic posture with low clip-on handlebars clamped below the triple tree, rearset footpegs, a 14.5-litre sculpted fuel tank with knee recesses, an 800mm seat height with removable solo cowl, and 17-inch wheels shod in sticky road tires.

5. Features and technology

Both motorcycles feature Royal Enfield's latest generation electronics governed by a six-axis IMU. The Himalayan 750 features a 5.0-inch full-colour circular TripperDash display with Google Maps full-screen navigation, four riding modes (Eco, Performance, Rain, Off-Road), and switchable rear ABS. The Continental GT 750 features a compact 4.0-inch circular TFT gauge housed in an authentic retro aluminum casing, offering three road modes (Sport, Urban, Rain), lean-sensitive Cornering ABS, and traction control.

6. Expected price and market positioning

Manufactured at Royal Enfield's high-volume industrial facilities in Tamil Nadu, both models will undercut imported multi-cylinder alternatives significantly. The Continental GT 750 is expected to launch at an exshowroom price between Rs 3.80 lakh and Rs 4.20 lakh. The heavier, more complex Himalayan 750, with its Showa suspension and larger 20-litre tank, will sit slightly higher, between Rs 4.20 lakh and Rs 4.60 lakh exshowroom. In Europe, the models are priced between €7,800 and €9,200.

8. Rivals and alternatives

The Himalayan 750 confronts the Yamaha Ténéré 700, Aprilia Tuareg 660, and Suzuki V-Strom 800DE. The Continental GT 750 rivals the Kawasaki Z650RS, Triumph Speed 400, and Moto Guzzi V7.

9. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Do both bikes use the exact same engine?

They share the same 750cc liquid-cooled parallel-twin architecture, but the Continental GT 750 is tuned for more peak power (62 bhp vs 58 bhp), while the Himalayan 750 prioritizes low-end torque.

Q: Which bike is better for long-distance highway touring?

The Himalayan 750 is vastly superior for long distances due to its upright seating, large windscreen, plush 200mm Showa suspension, and 20-litre fuel tank.

Q: Does the Continental GT 750 have dual front disc brakes?

Yes, the Continental GT 750 addresses previous 650cc braking limitations by featuring dual 300mm front floating disc brakes with radial calipers.

Q: What is the price difference between the two models in India?

The Himalayan 750 is expected to cost approximately Rs 30,000 to Rs 40,000 more than the Continental GT 750.

10. Conclusion

The Royal Enfield Himalayan 750 and Continental GT 750 prove the immense versatility of Royal Enfield's new liquid-cooled twin platform. If your heart yearns for transcontinental expeditions, carrying luggage, and traversing broken mountain passes in supreme comfort, the Himalayan 750 is an unbeatable overland weapon. But if you crave the romantic soul of a 1960s café racer, sharp canyon-carving agility, and pure retro style, the Continental GT 750 is the definitive modern-classic.
