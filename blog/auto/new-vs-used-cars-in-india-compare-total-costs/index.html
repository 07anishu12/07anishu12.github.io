Compare new and used cars using depreciation, loan costs, repairs and ownership risk.

A used car can cost less after depreciation, but higher financing costs, repairs or weak history can erase the advantage. A new car can offer clearer provenance and a fresh warranty, yet that does not automatically justify its premium. Compare equivalent use over the same ownership period using transparent assumptions.

Match usefulness, not just the badge

Choose vehicles that meet your passenger, luggage, safety and transmission needs. A five-year-old larger car and a new smaller car may both fit the budget but create different expenses and practical compromises. State those differences instead of calling one universally better value.

For the used option, record age, kilometres, ownership history, service evidence and remaining warranty. Confirm the exact generation and variant; a familiar model name can span substantial changes. For the new option, obtain the actual allotted model year and full on-road quotation.

Use the same annual distance and holding period. If you plan to keep the used car for three years and the new one for seven, the comparison needs a common time horizon or an explicit replacement scenario. Otherwise the totals answer different questions.

Build an illustrative depreciation comparison

Suppose a hypothetical new car costs ₹12 lakh and is sold after five years for ₹6 lakh. Its depreciation is ₹6 lakh. A suitable used car costs ₹7 lakh and is sold five years later for ₹3 lakh, producing ₹4 lakh depreciation.

The assumed used-car advantage is ₹2 lakh before interest, repairs and operating differences. These are teaching values, not present market prices or predicted resale outcomes. A valuation platform estimate or dealer asking price is not a guaranteed future sale receipt.

Stress-test the result. If the used car eventually sells for only ₹2 lakh, depreciation becomes ₹5 lakh and the initial advantage falls to ₹1 lakh. Keep that uncertainty visible rather than projecting today's apparent resale strength with false precision.

Add financing on the real amount borrowed

Compare actual sanctioned terms for each car, including rate, tenure, processing fees and valuation conditions. Do not assume a used-car loan receives the same rate or loan-to-value treatment as new finance. A smaller principal may still offset a higher rate, but calculate rather than guessing.

Suppose the hypothetical new-car finance costs ₹1.4 lakh in interest and fees while used finance costs ₹1.1 lakh. Adding these to depreciation gives ₹7.4 lakh versus ₹5.1 lakh before running expenses. The used advantage becomes ₹2.3 lakh under those assumptions.

Use RBI's KFS discussion as context for requesting all-in loan-cost information. Avoid double counting principal: if full purchase cost and resale are already in your model, add interest and fees, not all EMI payments again.

Give the used car a realistic repair budget

Obtain an independent inspection and a dated estimate for immediate work. Tyres, brakes, fluids, suspension wear and neglected servicing can create cash demands soon after purchase. A clean exterior and low odometer reading do not establish mechanical condition.

Suppose inspection identifies ₹45,000 of initial work and you allow ₹75,000 for additional repairs over five years. The hypothetical ₹1.2 lakh repair allowance reduces the earlier ₹2.3 lakh advantage to ₹1.1 lakh, before insurance, fuel and service differences.

A contingency is not proof that all the money will be spent. Show both expected and adverse scenarios. For a technically complex vehicle with weak history, inspection limitations and repair exposure may justify paying more for a better-documented alternative.

Resolve documents and choose with a cash buffer

Verify the seller's authority, RC details, insurance and recorded finance. Parivahan's ownership guidance identifies the ordinary ownership-transfer forms and Form 35 for hypothecation termination. Confirm the exact state and transaction requirements rather than assuming possession completes the transfer.

Compare insurance with consistent coverage using the principles in IRDAI's motor guide. A lower IDV or missing add-on can make a used-car premium look cheaper without providing equivalent protection. Check local age or use restrictions before valuing the remaining ownership period.

Prefer used when verified condition, documents and conservative total cost provide a worthwhile saving after a repair reserve. Prefer new when you value its documented specification and warranty enough to pay the premium, or when available used examples carry unacceptable uncertainty. Whichever you choose, retain funds beyond the purchase and down payment.

Quick questions, clear answers

Does a certified used car remove inspection risk?

No certification can guarantee future reliability. Read who inspected it, what was checked and the actual warranty exclusions. Independent assessment can still be useful, particularly for accident repairs, flood history or expensive components outside a limited commercial warranty.

Is depreciation always highest in the first year?

Do not apply a universal percentage to every vehicle and market. Prices, availability, condition and demand vary. Use real comparable transactions where available, and label future resale scenarios as assumptions rather than claiming a fixed depreciation curve.

Should I buy an older premium car instead?

Only after pricing its actual maintenance, parts, insurance and repair exposure. A lower used purchase price does not turn premium components into budget-car components. Compare the whole ownership model and inspection evidence, not just the badge obtainable for the money.
