Most motorcycle owners in India can garage and insure only one machine. That single bike must slip through weekday rush-hour gridlock without roasting the rider's legs, and then cruise securely at 95–105 km/h on weekend expressway getaways without feeling breathless. Achieving this dual mandate requires a distinct engineering compromise: sufficient displacement (160cc to 250cc) for high-speed passing reserves, dual-channel ABS for highway braking confidence, and compliant ergonomics that prevent lower back strain after three hours in the saddle. This review assesses the five most capable dual-purpose commuters available in 2026.

Quick Answer & At-a-Glance Buying Verdict

For Indian riders needing one versatile machine for both city commuting and weekend highway touring, the top recommendation is the Bajaj Pulsar N250 (ex-showroom Delhi: ₹1.51 lakh; on-road: ~₹1.74 lakh). Its torquey 249cc oil-cooled engine cruises comfortably at 100 km/h with low vibration, while delivering 35–38 km/l in city traffic. Riders favoring lighter weight and sharper handling should look at the TVS Apache RTR 160 4V, while those wanting relaxed retro touring should select the Royal Enfield Hunter 350.

Detailed Model-by-Model Evaluation & Official Links

1. Bajaj Pulsar N250 / Pulsar NS200

Built by Bajaj Auto, the Pulsar N250 produces 24.5 PS and 21.5 Nm of torque. Its standout attribute for highway trips is the wide torque curve: you can hold 90–100 km/h in top gear without stressing the motor, while assist-and-slipper clutch tech keeps lever pull light in bumper-to-bumper city commuting. Official Link: Bajaj Pulsar N250 / Pulsar NS200 Official Page

2. TVS Apache RTR 160 4V (USD & Dual-Channel ABS)

TVS Motor Company's 159.7 cc oil-cooled motor generates 17.55 PS in Sport mode. Weighing 146 kg with Showa-tuned suspension and ride modes, it is easily maneuverable in weekday traffic and remains stable during weekend highway runs up to 100 km/h. Official Link: TVS Apache RTR 160 4V Official Page

3. Royal Enfield Hunter 350

The Hunter 350 uses Royal Enfield's J-series 349 cc engine (20.2 bhp / 27 Nm). With 17-inch alloy wheels and a nimble 790 mm seat height, it is the most agile Royal Enfield in town, while the relaxed low-end torque allows stress-free cruising on rural highways. Official Link: Royal Enfield Hunter 350 Official Page

4. Yamaha MT-15 Version 2.0

Yamaha's MT-15 delivers high-revving performance from its 155 cc liquid-cooled VVA engine (18.4 PS). Its ultra-light 141 kg curb weight makes it brilliant for slicing through urban gridlock, and the Deltabox chassis provides highway stability, although windblast is noticeable above 100 km/h. Official Link: Yamaha MT-15 Version 2.0 Official Page

5. Suzuki V-Strom SX 250 / Royal Enfield Himalayan 450

For riders who spend serious time on broken highways, the Suzuki V-Strom SX 250 (26.5 PS oil-cooled) and Himalayan 450 (40 PS liquid-cooled) provide long-travel suspension, wind protection screens, and upright seating ergonomics that eliminate fatigue on 500 km touring days. Official Link: Suzuki V-Strom SX Official Page

Buyer-Specific Recommendations & Use-Case Verdict

  1. Balanced 50/50 City & Highway Commuters: Pick the Bajaj Pulsar N250 for effortless 100 km/h cruising, tractable city torque, and 35 km/l real-world mileage.
  2. Budget-Conscious Dual-Use Commuters: Pick the TVS Apache RTR 160 4V for the lowest purchase cost and 42+ km/l efficiency.
  3. Relaxed Weekend Tourers: Choose the Royal Enfield Hunter 350 for classic exhaust thump, comfortable ergonomics, and strong highway presence.
  4. Adventure Tourers: Choose the Suzuki V-Strom SX 250 for highway wind protection and high ground clearance over rough roads.

Ownership Cost, City On-Road Variations & Financing

State RTO levies range from 7%–10% in North India to 15%–18% in Maharashtra and Karnataka. For a rider covering 1,500 km per month (1,000 km city + 500 km highway), fuel expenses for a 250cc bike returning 35 km/l total roughly ₹4,070/month at ₹95/L. Annual maintenance at authorized service points runs ₹2,200 to ₹3,500.

Pre-Purchase Showroom Checklist & Frequently Asked Questions

Pre-Delivery Checklist:

  1. Check the 17-digit VIN in daylight to verify fresh assembly within 60 days.
  2. Confirm dual-channel ABS functionality during your pre-delivery test spin.
  3. Demand an itemized quote to ensure dealer handling charges are omitted.

Frequently Asked Questions:

  • Q1: Is a 160cc bike adequate for highway riding in India?
    Yes, a 160cc machine like the Apache RTR 160 4V cruises comfortably between 85–95 km/h, though 200–250cc motorcycles provide superior reserve acceleration for passing trucks on 4-lane highways.
  • Q2: Which dual-use bike has the lowest maintenance?
    The Pulsar N250 and Apache RTR 160 4V have widespread parts availability and highly affordable consumable costs across India.

Summary

When selecting one motorcycle for both city commuting and weekend highway trips, the Bajaj Pulsar N250 offers the most compelling blend of passing power, relaxed cruising, and manageable running costs. For budget-conscious riders, the TVS Apache RTR 160 4V is a superb alternative.
