Bajaj Pulsar 150 vs TVS Apache RTR 160 2V: Specifications & Claimed vs Real Mileage

Comparing Bajaj Pulsar 150 and TVS Apache RTR 160 2V requires separating ARAI lab claims from independent road tests and owner averages. The Bajaj Pulsar 150 pairs a 149.5cc engine (13.8 bhp @ 8,500 rpm, 13.25 Nm @ 6,500 rpm) with a 148 kg chassis and claims 50.0 km/l.

The TVS Apache RTR 160 2V uses a 159.7cc powertrain (15.82 bhp @ 8,750 rpm (Sport) / 13.14 bhp (Urban/Rain), 13.85 Nm @ 7,000 rpm (Sport) / 12.7 Nm (Urban/Rain)) weighing 138 kg with a claimed 47.0 km/l. Their 10 kg mass difference and gearing (5-speed vs 5-speed with Glide Through Technology (GTT)) shape their real-world fuel gap. City Traffic Usability and Highway Cruising Behaviour

In stop-and-go city traffic, kerb weight and idle-saving tech determine fuel burn. The Bajaj Pulsar 150 features 15-litre long-range tank, upright ergonomics, twin-shock rear suspension, and low spare-part costs, returning 44–48 km/l in urban use. The TVS Apache RTR 160 2V counters with 138 kg light kerb weight, three electronic throttle/ABS ride modes, and Glide Through Technology for crawl traffic, achieving 43–47 km/l.

On open highways, the Bajaj Pulsar 150 reaches 112 km/h and cruises cleanly at 70–85 km/h (47–51 km/l), whereas the TVS Apache RTR 160 2V tops out at 107 km/h and cruises comfortably at 70–85 km/h (46–50 km/l).
