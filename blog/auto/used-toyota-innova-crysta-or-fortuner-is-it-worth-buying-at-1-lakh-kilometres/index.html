Yes, a well-maintained used Toyota Innova Crysta (2017–2022) or Toyota Fortuner (2017–2022) with 70,000 to 1,20,000 genuine kilometres is worth buying—PROVIDED you live outside Delhi NCR's 10-year diesel restriction and verify the complete Toyota dealership service history before paying a rupee. While their 2.4L (2GD-FTV) and 2.8L (1GD-FTV) diesel engines easily run 3,00,000+ km, they are the most frequently odometer-tampered vehicles in India, and a neglected 1,20,000 km example can still hit you with ₹1.20 lakh to ₹1.80 lakh in injector, suspension, tyre, and brake rotor bills.

The #1 Rule: How to Catch the '2,20,000 km Rolled Back to 68,000 km' Scam

Because an Innova or Fortuner cabin wears so well, unscrupulous brokers buy 5-year-old highway cars with 2,20,000 km, fit a ₹3,000 steering wheel leather wrap and ₹400 new rubber brake pedal pad, and flash the digital odometer back to 68,000 km.

Never trust the dashboard screen alone:

  1. Pull the Complete Toyota DMS National Service History: Every Toyota dealer in India shares one centralised cloud database. Take the registration and chassis number to any authorised Toyota service advisor (or insist the seller open the Toyota i-Connect app in front of you).
  2. Match Tyre DOT Codes & Driver Seat Bolster Foam: A genuine 65,000 km car is either on its late factory tyres or its first replacement set; collapsed outer driver-seat foam and shiny seatbelt webbing point to 1,50,000+ km.

4 Expensive Wear Items to Check Between 90,000 km and 1,40,000 km

Even on a genuine 1,00,000 km Toyota, budget ₹80,000–₹1,20,000 if these four items haven't been done:

  1. Diesel Fuel Injector Feedback Values (OBD Scan): Ask a Toyota technician to read the Injector Compensation / Feedback Values on Toyota Techstream (must be within ±3.0 mm³/stroke). Four new OEM diesel injectors cost ₹95,000–₹1,25,000.
  2. 6-Speed Automatic Transmission Fluid (ATF): Though marketed as 'lifetime fill', flushing the WS-grade ATF every 60,000–80,000 km (₹9,000) prevents torque-converter shudder.
  3. Front Lower Control Arm Bushes, Ball Joints & Hydraulic Steering Rack: Check for power-steering fluid leaks around the rubber rack boots (₹32,000–₹42,000 for a new rack).
  4. EGR Valve & Intake Manifold Soot Cleaning: Restores lost pickup and mileage at 1,00,000 km.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Do the 2.4L and 2.8L Innova Crysta and Fortuner engines have a rubber timing belt that needs changing at 1,00,000 km?

No! Unlike the older pre-2016 2.5L/3.0L KD-series engines (which used a rubber timing belt that required mandatory replacement every 1,00,000–1,50,000 km), the 2016+ 2.4L (2GD) and 2.8L (1GD) engines use a durable metal timing chain designed to last the life of the engine (only the external serpentine accessory belt and water pump need periodic inspection).

Q2. Why are 2018–2022 Diesel Automatic Innova Crystas so expensive in the used market?

Because Toyota discontinued the 6-speed Automatic transmission on the Innova Crysta in 2023 (selling the new Crysta strictly as a 5-speed Manual). Buyers who want a ladder-frame Diesel Automatic Innova have to buy a well-kept 2018–2022 pre-owned example.

Q3. Is it worth paying Toyota ₹2,500–₹3,500 for an official Pre-Purchase Inspection (PPI)?

Yes—it is the best ₹3,000 you will ever spend. Book a general check-up at an authorised Toyota dealership with the seller: they will verify the true odometer history across India, hoist the vehicle to check chassis/frame repairs, and run a Techstream diagnostic scan on the injectors, ABS, airbags, and DPF.

Q4. Why can you find 2017–2018 Delhi-registered Fortuners and Crystas at lower prices?

Because Delhi NCR strictly enforces the 10-year ban on diesel vehicles. A 2018 DL/HR26/UP16 diesel Fortuner has only 2 years of legal life left inside NCR, so owners sell them to out-of-state buyers (in Punjab, Rajasthan, UP, MP, or South India) using an RTO No Objection Certificate (NOC).

Q5. Should I buy a used Petrol Innova Crysta 2.7 AT if I live in Delhi NCR?

While a used 2.7L Petrol Automatic Innova Crysta gets the full 15-year NCR lifespan and sells for ₹2–₹3 lakh less than the diesel, it returns only 6.5 to 8.0 km/l in city traffic. Unless your monthly running is under 600 km, stretching for a newer Innova Hycross Hybrid is more economical.
