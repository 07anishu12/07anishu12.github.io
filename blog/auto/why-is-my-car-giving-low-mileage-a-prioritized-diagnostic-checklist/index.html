If your car's verified tank-to-tank mileage has dropped by 2 to 4 km/l, diagnose it in three cost-ordered stages rather than buying expensive fuel additives. First, check zero-cost external causes: under-inflated tyres (24 PSI instead of 33 PSI), a change in average traffic speed or short <4 km cold trips, and roof racks or boot clutter. Second, inspect ₹350–₹1,800 dusty-climate wear items: a choked engine air filter, worn spark plugs, or overdue engine oil. Third, if mileage remains low, have a qualified technician inspect for a dragging brake caliper, slipping clutch, stuck-open coolant thermostat, or faulty oxygen (O2)/MAF sensor.

First: Confirm It Is a Real Drop (Not a Route or MID Illusion)

Before opening the bonnet, verify two baselines:

  1. Check Your Average Speed Display: If road construction or monsoon waterlogging reduced your commute's average speed on the MID from 24 km/h down to 13 km/h this month, a 2.5–3.5 km/l mileage drop is 100% traffic-induced—not a car fault.
  2. Verify via Full-Tank Auto-Cut-Off: Confirm the drop across two full tanks at a reputable COCO fuel station to rule out pump short-dispensing.

The 3-Tier Low-Mileage Diagnostic Table (Ranked From Cheapest to Mechanical)

Work through the table below from top to bottom. Never pay a workshop for Tier 3 repairs before verifying Tier 1 and Tier 2 items.

Tier 1 & Tier 2: The Indian Dust and Tyre-Pressure Culprits

  • Under-Inflated Tyres (6–8 PSI Low): Driving on 25 PSI instead of the manufacturer's recommended 32–35 PSI flexes the rubber sidewalls continuously, increasing rolling resistance by ~15% and cutting 1.0 to 1.8 km/l off your mileage. Always check tyre pressure cold (within 2 km of starting in the morning).
  • Choked Engine Air Filter in Dusty Indian Cities: Due to construction dust across Indian cities, an air filter scheduled for replacement at 20,000 km often clogs completely by 8,000 to 10,000 km.

Tier 3: 4 Mechanical Faults That Require a Qualified Mechanic

If tyres, filters, and traffic are normal, ask a qualified technician to check these four specific systems:

  1. Dragging Brake Caliper or Over-Tight Handbrake Cable: After driving 5 km without heavy braking, carefully hold the back of your hand near (do NOT touch!) each wheel hub. If one wheel is radiating intense heat or smells burnt, a seized caliper slide pin is holding the brake pad against the disc constantly.
  2. Slipping Clutch (Manual / AMT): If engine RPM climbs from 2,000 to 3,200 RPM when you press the accelerator in 4th gear on a flyover without a matching surge in road speed, your clutch plate is worn out and wasting engine power as friction heat.
  3. Faulty Upstream Oxygen (O2) or MAF Sensor: A lazy O2 sensor or dust-coated Mass Air Flow sensor tricks the ECU into injecting a permanently rich fuel mixture. An OBD-II scanner reads Short-Term and Long-Term Fuel Trims (STFT/LTFT) in 5 minutes.
  4. Stuck-Open Coolant Thermostat: Keeps the engine below its 88°C–92°C operating temperature, trapping the ECU in rich cold-start fuel enrichment.

What to Refuse at the Service Workshop

Politely refuse unverified 'engine flush', 'fuel additive cans', or 'nitrogen air packages' added to your job card. Stick to clean OEM air filters, correct-viscosity synthetic oil (e.g., 0W-20 / 5W-30 as specified in your manual), spark plugs at recommended intervals, and wheel alignment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. How can I do a quick 30-second test to see if my brakes are dragging?

On a quiet, nearly flat driveway with only a gentle 1-degree slope, put the car in Neutral (N) and release the handbrake. A healthy car with properly inflated tyres will begin rolling smoothly on its own. If it stays stuck or stops abruptly without touching the brake pedal, have a mechanic check for a dragging brake pad or tight handbrake cable.

Q2. Does cleaning the throttle body improve mileage?

If carbon build-up around the throttle butterfly valve after 30,000–40,000 km is causing an unstable idle RPM, cleaning the throttle body (costing ₹500–₹900) restores smooth airflow and idle stability, recovering ~0.5–1.0 km/l.

Q3. Why did my diesel SUV's mileage drop during a DPF regeneration cycle?

To burn accumulated soot out of a BS6 Diesel Particulate Filter (DPF), the ECU intentionally injects extra post-combustion diesel fuel to raise exhaust temperatures to 600°C for 15–20 minutes. During that active regeneration window, instantaneous mileage drops noticeably before returning to normal.

Q4. Can wheel alignment affect fuel mileage?

Yes. If hitting a deep pothole knocked your front wheels out of toe alignment, the tyres scrub sideways slightly down every kilometre of road—pulling the steering to one side, feathering the tyre edges, and trimming 0.8 to 1.2 km/l off your fuel economy.

Q5. Should I use fuel injector cleaner additives every service?

Modern Indian BS6 petrol and diesel fuels already contain detergent packages. You only need injector cleaning if an OBD scan or cylinder balance test confirms a partially clogged injector causing a misfire or lean/rich trim.
