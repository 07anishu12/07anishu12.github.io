Rizta deserves early consideration when its documented 34-litre boot and family-oriented package fit your belongings; iQube offers a wider listed spread of current battery capacities. Choose the exact versions around daily distance, rider/pillion fit and charging access. This guide does not establish an independently tested comfort, range or reliability winner.

Compare the current variant families

The Ather Rizta specification table separates S and Z, lists 34 litres of boot space for both and gives 123/161 km IDC figures by configuration. The current iQube table lists battery families from 2.3 through 5.3 kWh, with corresponding IDC claims from 114 to 212 km.

Those are manufacturer-published figures, not range measured here. The iQube's family maximum should not be compared with the smallest Rizta as though price and equipment were equal. Choose matched quotations that meet your minimum daily planning range first.

Ather's current online capacity table and an older downloadable brochure show different capacity labels. Do not silently select whichever looks most attractive. Have the dealer identify the exact battery, usable/installed basis and current brochure for your order before publishing a capacity-specific comparison.

Test storage and passenger fit together

Rizta's 34-litre figure gives a concrete storage shortlist reason, but helmet fit remains shape-dependent. TVS's page identifies a 32-litre storage image with an ST qualification; do not assign that value automatically to every iQube variant or compare inconsistent configurations.

Bring helmets, rainwear and typical bags. Check that the seat latches without pressing on the contents and that any optional front storage is priced separately. Loose bags must not obstruct steering or foot movement, regardless of apparent floorboard room.

Ask the regular pillion to mount and sit on both, checking footrests, grab support and seat shape. A showroom photograph of a broad saddle does not establish comfort for your family. Include the effort of pushing backwards and parking, particularly for the shortest regular rider.

Identify the functions included in the price

Ather's Rizta page differentiates S and Z equipment, including navigation-related presentation and Magic Twist availability in the shown packages. Confirm exact pack dependency and operating instructions. A demonstrated premium function should not be assumed standard on the entry quote.

TVS's current table distinguishes LCD, TFT and touchscreen arrangements across variants. Match the display you are paying for and check readability in daylight. Connected functions are useful only when compatible with your phone, affordable to retain and operated without distracting the rider.

For either scooter, ask about any software pack, subscription and introductory period. Obtain a list of what continues after the paid or promotional period ends. Separate vehicle hardware, software access and warranty products in the quote.

Match charging to the household routine

Ather's table identifies different home-charger powers and charging times by configuration. TVS gives variant-specific 10–80% times. Compare the same state-of-charge window and charger rather than declaring a winner from unrelated figures.

Establish a suitable circuit, parking permission and cable route with qualified advice. If relying on workplace or public charging, confirm permission and compatibility. Do not assume an electric scooter can use any public car charger or that a mapped location is always working.

For a hypothetical daily energy use of 1.5 kWh and wall tariff of ₹10/kWh, daily electricity costs ₹15 before other charges. These are planning assumptions, not measured Rizta/iQube consumption. Include charging losses and the actual tariff mix in a personal budget.

Choose the useful battery margin

For a hypothetical 35 km daily commute, start by planning an adequate reserve and occasional detours. A substantially larger pack may reduce charging frequency, but its purchase premium is not automatically recovered through lower electricity use. Bigger capacity is mainly an energy-availability benefit, not free energy.

Prioritise Rizta when the verified 34-litre package and selected S/Z functions solve your storage and family needs. Prioritise iQube when its documented broader capacity range offers a better fit between budget and required margin. Test the actual variants before treating either direction as a final purchase verdict.

Compare battery warranty, charger coverage, service location and insurance. Ather's table distinguishes standard and optional extended coverage; lifetime or promotional language from another year should not be assumed. Ask both providers for current written terms and a dated all-in quotation.

Quick questions, clear answers

Does the larger boot guarantee helmet fit?

No. Litres do not describe the full shape of a helmet shell or the available opening. Bring your actual equipment and check seat closure gently. Compare storage in the quoted variants rather than a premium demonstration with optional accessories.

Should I buy the maximum-range version?

Only if its useful reserve, charging frequency or trips justify the extra cost. Establish conservative daily needs first. Certified range is not guaranteed road range, and unused capacity may provide little benefit to a short commuter with dependable overnight charging.

Why do Ather battery labels need checking?

The current online table and older brochure inspected in this research use different capacity labels. They may concern different configurations or revisions, but that explanation was not established here. Confirm the exact ordered pack and measurement basis rather than merging the sources.
