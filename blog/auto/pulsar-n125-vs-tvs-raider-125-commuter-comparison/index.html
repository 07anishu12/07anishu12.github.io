The Pulsar N125 versus Raider 125 decision is now strongly variant-dependent. Bajaj documents a 124.58 cc N125 and distinct LED Disc/LED Disc BT versions; TVS's current Raider material describes top variants with dual discs and single-channel ABS. An older blanket claim that the Raider has no ABS would be misleading for this shortlist.

Start with the documented engine differences

Bajaj's current N125 page lists 124.58 cc, 12 PS and a 9.5-litre tank. TVS's October 2026 variant explanation identifies the Raider's 124.8 cc three-valve family. These capacities are effectively the same broad class; a fractional displacement difference should not decide the purchase.

The N125 page describes its engine as a two-valve design, while TVS describes three valves. Valve count is a real engineering difference, but it does not independently establish acceleration, efficiency or durability. Compare current output units carefully: PS, bhp and kW are not interchangeable labels.

Older Raider launch material describes earlier equipment and tuning. It is historical context, not authority to overwrite a current brochure. Record manufacturing year and full trim before comparing specifications.

Put braking ahead of display size

TVS's current article describes a top Raider version with dual discs and single-channel ABS. That gives buyers seeking front-wheel anti-lock assistance a specific variant to investigate. It does not imply every Raider has that package or that a rear disc also has rear-wheel ABS.

Bajaj's inspected N125 page differentiates LED Disc and LED Disc BT by connectivity, tyre and starting-system equipment. It did not establish equivalent ABS coverage for this guide. Verify the current homologated braking specification before concluding that the two quotes have equal safety equipment.

Ask for warning-light and brake-system explanations at delivery. A combined-braking label, a front disc and ABS perform different roles. Do not infer an anti-lock function from a sporty model name or the presence of a rear disc.

Compare the upgrades you will actually use

Bajaj states that LED Disc BT adds Bluetooth, a wider rear tyre and an integrated starter generator relative to LED Disc. These are concrete variant differences worth pricing. A wider tyre is not a universal grip guarantee; compound, condition and approved fitment matter.

TVS's described top package includes a TFT screen, connected functions and assistance features. Check which are present on your quote and whether phone compatibility or paid services affect them. Demonstrate navigation and settings while parked, keeping attention on riding when moving.

For both bikes, ask how the starting or idle-stop features behave and what conditions apply. A specification list cannot establish how much fuel a function saves in your route. Avoid paying a large premium on the assumption that a promotional efficiency improvement is guaranteed.

Test the commuter basics before financing

Try low-speed starts, turning and parking in suitable riding gear. Check clutch reach, knee room and whether the seat supports you without pushing your weight onto the bars. Bring the daily pillion to inspect mounting, footrest reach and available seat space.

Assess a normal slope and gentle acceleration at legal speeds. This guide contains no timed performance test, so Bajaj or TVS superlatives are not adopted as findings. A peak-output figure also cannot describe the exact low-speed response that matters in traffic.

Ask the local workshop for service intervals, common wear items and parts lead times. Compare written estimates over the same distance. Nationwide brand familiarity or isolated complaints do not establish comparative failure rates for either model.

Use a premium calculation to choose

Suppose, hypothetically, an ABS-equipped preferred trim costs ₹12,000 more. Over a planned four-year holding period, that is ₹250 monthly before financing and resale. Decide whether its specific equipment and fit justify that cost; do not pretend an unmeasured fuel saving automatically pays it back.

At 800 km monthly and an assumed ₹2/km fuel cost, annual fuel spending is ₹19,200. A hypothetical 5% reduction saves ₹960 annually, showing why a small efficiency difference may be less important than the upfront trim choice. These are examples, not results for the N125 or Raider.

Prioritise the relevant Raider ABS trim when verified anti-lock equipment is central. Prioritise the N125 when its actual quoted configuration meets your requirements and its cost or demonstrated fit is preferable. Confirm brake equipment and current variants before treating either as the better overall value.

Quick questions, clear answers

Does every Raider have ABS now?

The current manufacturer article describes ABS-equipped top versions, not universal fitment across every variant. Confirm the exact ordered trim and year. Older articles can remain indexed after equipment changes, so use the current brochure and invoice.

Is N125 LED Disc BT just a screen upgrade?

Bajaj's inspected page also identifies a wider rear tyre and integrated starter generator alongside Bluetooth. Verify the current trim sheet and price each useful benefit. Do not assume that the same changes apply to earlier production or another Pulsar model.

Which gives better mileage?

No matched independent mileage test was performed here. Compare evidence for the same model years and riding conditions, then budget conservatively. Manufacturer slogans or a single owner's refill cannot establish a universal winner for your commute.
